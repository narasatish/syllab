What is a Polynomial?

  • A special type of algebraic expression.
  • Variables have only non-negative integer exponents.
  • No division by variables, no fractional exponents.

🎙️ Now, a polynomial is a specific kind of algebraic expression. The key rule is that the powers of the variables must always be whole numbers – no fractions, no negatives. This makes them predictable and easy to work with.

Parts of a Polynomial

  • Terms: Parts separated by + or -.
  • Coefficients: Numbers multiplying variables.
  • Variables: Letters representing unknown values.
  • Exponents: Powers of the variables.

🎙️ Every polynomial has distinct components. For example, in 3x² + 2x - 5, 3x², 2x, and -5 are terms. 3 and 2 are coefficients, x is the variable, and 2 is an exponent.

Real-Life Polynomial: Area of a Rectangle

  • Width = x units
  • Area = Length × Width = x(x + 3) = x² + 3x

🎙️ Polynomials are everywhere! Imagine a rectangle where the length is 3 units more than its width. If the width is 'x', the area becomes x² + 3x, which is a quadratic polynomial. We use them to describe shapes and spaces.

The Degree of a Polynomial

  • Highest exponent of the variable in a polynomial.
  • Determines the polynomial's type.

🎙️ The degree is like the polynomial's 'rank'. It's simply the largest power you see on any variable in the expression. This single number tells us a lot about the polynomial's behavior and shape.

Types of Polynomials by Number of Terms

Finding Zeroes: Linear Polynomials

  • Set the polynomial equal to zero.
  • Solve the resulting linear equation.
  • Example: For P(x) = 2x - 6, set 2x - 6 = 0, so 2x = 6, x = 3.

🎙️ Finding zeroes for linear polynomials is straightforward. You just set the expression to zero and solve for 'x'. For P(x) = 2x - 6, the zero is 3, because 2(3) - 6 equals zero.

1Set P(x) = 0
2Isolate the variable
3Solve for x

How Many Zeroes Can There Be?

  • A polynomial of degree 'n' has at most 'n' zeroes.
  • Linear (degree 1): At most 1 zero.
  • Quadratic (degree 2): At most 2 zeroes.
  • Cubic (degree 3): At most 3 zeroes.

🎙️ An important theorem states that a polynomial can have at most as many zeroes as its degree. So, a quadratic polynomial can have at most two zeroes, while a cubic polynomial can have at most three. This gives us an upper limit.

Zeroes and Coefficients: Quadratic Polynomials

  • For ax² + bx + c, let zeroes be α and β.
  • Sum of zeroes (α + β) = -b/a
  • Product of zeroes (αβ) = c/a

🎙️ For quadratic polynomials, there's a beautiful relationship between their zeroes and their coefficients. The sum of the zeroes is always -b/a, and their product is c/a. This allows us to find one if we know the other without solving the full quadratic.

Worked Example: Quadratic Zeroes

  • Polynomial: x² - 5x + 6
  • Zeroes are 2 and 3 (since (2)²-5(2)+6=0, (3)²-5(3)+6=0).
  • Sum: 2+3=5. From formula: -(-5)/1 = 5. (Matches!)
  • Product: 2×3=6. From formula: 6/1 = 6. (Matches!)

🎙️ Let's see this in action with x² - 5x + 6. Its zeroes are 2 and 3. When we use the formulas, the sum is -(-5)/1 = 5, and the product is 6/1 = 6. Both match the actual sum and product of the zeroes, confirming the relationships.

2Find zeroes (factor/formula)
3Verify sum (-b/a)
4Verify product (c/a)

Division Algorithm for Polynomials

  • Similar to dividing numbers (Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder).
  • If P(x) is divided by G(x), then P(x) = G(x)Q(x) + R(x).
  • Degree of R(x) is always less than degree of G(x).

🎙️ Just like with numbers, we can divide polynomials. The division algorithm states that if you divide a polynomial P(x) by another polynomial G(x), you get a quotient Q(x) and a remainder R(x). The remainder's degree will always be smaller than the divisor's.

Worked Example: Polynomial Division

  • Divide (x² + 3x + 2) by (x + 1).
  • Steps: Divide leading terms, multiply divisor, subtract, bring down next term, repeat.
  • Result: Quotient (x + 2), Remainder (0).

🎙️ Let's perform a simple polynomial division. Dividing x² + 3x + 2 by x + 1 gives us a quotient of x + 2 and a remainder of 0. This means (x + 1) is a factor of x² + 3x + 2.

1Set up long division
2Divide leading terms
3Multiply and subtract
4Repeat until remainder degree is less than divisor degree
