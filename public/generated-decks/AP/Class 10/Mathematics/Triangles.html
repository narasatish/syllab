What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides.
  • It has three vertices (corners).
  • It has three interior angles.
  • The sum of its interior angles is always 180 degrees.

🎙️ A triangle is one of the simplest closed shapes you can draw. Remember, it always has exactly three straight sides, three points where the sides meet called vertices, and three angles inside it. A key property is that if you add up those three angles, you'll always get 180 degrees.

Elements of a Triangle

  • Sides: The three line segments forming the triangle.
  • Vertices: The three points where the sides meet.
  • Angles: The three angles formed inside at the vertices.

🎙️ Let's quickly review the parts of a triangle. The lines are called sides, the corners are called vertices, and the openings at the vertices are called angles. Understanding these basic elements is crucial before we move on to more complex concepts.

Types of Triangles (Based on Sides)

  • Equilateral: All three sides are equal.
  • Isosceles: Two sides are equal.
  • Scalene: All three sides are different.

🎙️ Triangles can be classified based on the lengths of their sides. An equilateral triangle is perfectly balanced with all sides equal. An isosceles triangle has two equal sides, and a scalene triangle has all sides of different lengths. This classification helps us understand their unique properties.

Types of Triangles (Based on Angles)

  • Acute: All three angles are less than 90°.

An obtuse triangle has one angle larger than 90 degrees. Remember, a triangle can only have one right or one obtuse angle.

Congruence vs. Similarity

  • Congruent: Same shape AND same size.
  • Similar: Same shape, but different sizes.
  • All congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent.

🎙️ This is a crucial distinction. Congruent triangles are identical copies of each other – same shape, same size. Think of two identical coins. Similar triangles, however, are like enlarged or reduced versions of each other. They have the same shape, but their sizes can be different. Imagine a photograph and its smaller printout.

Similar Triangles: The Concept

  • Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal.
  • Their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).
  • Similarity is denoted by the symbol '~'.

🎙️ When we say triangles are similar, it means one is a perfect 'scaled-up' or 'scaled-down' version of the other. All their angles match up, and if you divide the length of a side in one triangle by the length of the corresponding side in the other, you'll always get the same number. This ratio is called the scale factor.

Conditions for Similarity: AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle)

  • If all three corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar.
  • Actually, AA (Angle-Angle) is sufficient because the third angle will automatically be equal.

🎙️ The AAA criterion is one of the most straightforward ways to prove similarity. If you can show that all three angles of one triangle are equal to the corresponding three angles of another triangle, then they must be similar. Remember, because the sum of angles in a triangle is 180 degrees, showing just two pairs of angles are equal is enough.

1Angle 1 = Angle 1'
2Angle 2 = Angle 2'
3Angle 3 = Angle 3'

Conditions for Similarity: SSS (Side-Side-Side)

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.
  • This means the ratio of corresponding sides is constant.

🎙️ The SSS criterion for similarity states that if the ratio of all three pairs of corresponding sides is the same, then the triangles are similar. This means one triangle is simply an enlargement or reduction of the other, maintaining its shape. Check all three ratios carefully!

1Side 1 / Side 1' = k
2Side 2 / Side 2' = k
3Side 3 / Side 3' = k

Conditions for Similarity: SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle,
  • AND the sides including these angles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.

🎙️ The SAS criterion is a powerful tool. It means you don't need to check all three angles and all three sides. If you have an equal angle between two sides, and those two sides are proportional in both triangles, then you've proven similarity. Make sure the angle is *between* the proportional sides.

1Angle A = Angle D
2AB/DE = AC/DF

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem)

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle,
  • It intersects the other two sides at distinct points.
  • Then it divides the other two sides in the same ratio.

🎙️ The Basic Proportionality Theorem, also known as Thales Theorem, is fundamental. It tells us that when you draw a line inside a triangle that's parallel to one of its sides, that line will cut the other two sides into segments that are proportional to each other. This is extremely useful for finding unknown lengths.

BPT: Visual Explanation

  • In triangle ABC, if DE || BC (DE is parallel to BC),
  • Then AD/DB = AE/EC.
  • This ratio holds true for any parallel line segment.

🎙️ Let's visualize this. Imagine a triangle ABC. Now draw a line DE inside it, such that D is on AB, E is on AC, and DE is parallel to BC. The theorem states that the ratio of AD to DB will be exactly equal to the ratio of AE to EC. This means the line DE 'cuts' the sides proportionally.

Worked Example 1: Using BPT

  • In ΔABC, DE || BC. AD = 3 cm, DB = 6 cm, AE = 2 cm.
  • Find EC.
  • Using BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC
  • 3/6 = 2/EC => 1/2 = 2/EC => EC = 4 cm.

🎙️ Let's apply BPT to a problem. We have a triangle with a line parallel to one side. We are given three lengths and need to find the fourth. By setting up the proportion AD/DB = AE/EC, we can easily solve for the unknown length EC. It's a straightforward application once you know the theorem.

Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio,
  • Then the line must be parallel to the third side.
  • This is the opposite of the BPT.

🎙️ Just like many theorems have converses, BPT does too. The converse says: if you find a line segment inside a triangle that divides the two sides in the same ratio, then you can conclude that this line segment *must* be parallel to the third side. It's useful for proving lines are parallel.

Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles
  • Is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • Area(ΔABC) / Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (AC/DF)²

🎙️ This theorem provides a powerful link between similarity and area. If two triangles are similar, their areas don't just scale linearly with their sides. Instead, the ratio of their areas is the *square* of the ratio of their corresponding sides. This means if sides double, the area becomes four times larger!

Worked Example 2: Areas of Similar Triangles

  • ΔABC ~ ΔDEF. AB = 4 cm, DE = 8 cm.
  • Area(ΔABC) = 16 cm².
  • Find Area(ΔDEF).
  • Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² => 16/Area(ΔDEF) = (4/8)² = (1/2)² = 1/4.
  • Area(ΔDEF) = 16 * 4 = 64 cm².

🎙️ Let's apply the area theorem. We are given two similar triangles, the lengths of a pair of corresponding sides, and the area of one triangle. By using the formula, we can set up the ratio and solve for the unknown area. Notice how the area increased significantly because the side ratio was squared.

Pythagoras Theorem (Recap)

  • Applies ONLY to right-angled triangles.
  • The square of the hypotenuse (longest side) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse).

🎙️ You've learned Pythagoras Theorem in earlier classes, but it's crucial for Class 10, especially when dealing with right triangles. Always remember it applies *only* to right-angled triangles. The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle, and it's always the longest side. This theorem helps us find unknown side lengths.

Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

  • If the square of one side of a triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides,
  • Then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.
  • This helps determine if a triangle is a right-angled triangle.

🎙️ Similar to BPT, Pythagoras also has a converse. If you are given the three side lengths of a triangle, and you find that the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then you can confidently say that the triangle is a right-angled triangle. This is a great way to check for right angles!

Recap: Key Takeaways on Triangles

  • Triangles are fundamental shapes with unique properties.
  • Similarity means same shape, proportional sides, equal angles.
  • AAA, SSS, SAS are criteria for similarity.
  • BPT and its converse deal with parallel lines and proportional segments.
  • Area of similar triangles relates to the square of side ratios.
  • Pythagoras Theorem is vital for right-angled triangles.
