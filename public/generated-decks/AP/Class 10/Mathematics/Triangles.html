What is a Triangle? (A Quick Revision)

  • A polygon with three sides.
  • It has three vertices and three angles.
  • The sum of its interior angles is always 180°.

🎙️ Let's start with the basics. Remember, a triangle is a closed shape made of three straight lines. It has three corners, called vertices, and three angles inside. A fundamental property is that if you add up all three angles, you'll always get 180 degrees. This property is key to many concepts we'll discuss.

Recalling Types of Triangles

  • Based on sides: Equilateral, Isosceles, Scalene.
  • Based on angles: Acute, Right, Obtuse angled.
  • Each type has unique properties.

🎙️ Just like people, triangles come in different types! We can classify them by their sides – if all sides are equal (equilateral), two sides are equal (isosceles), or no sides are equal (scalene). We can also classify them by their angles – if all angles are less than 90° (acute), one angle is 90° (right), or one angle is greater than 90° (obtuse). These distinctions are important.

Defining Similar Polygons

  • Two polygons are similar if:
  • 1. Their corresponding angles are equal.
  • 2. Their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).

🎙️ To be more precise, for any two polygons to be similar, two conditions must be met. First, all their corresponding angles must be exactly equal. Second, the ratio of their corresponding sides must be the same. This means if one side is twice as long as its counterpart, then all other corresponding sides must also be twice as long. Both conditions are crucial.

This means one triangle is simply a scaled-up or scaled-down version of the other. Understanding this definition is the first step to mastering similar triangles.

Criteria for Similarity: AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle)

  • If corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then they are similar.
  • This is a powerful shortcut!
  • If ∠A=∠P, ∠B=∠Q, and ∠C=∠R, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

🎙️ This is our first major criterion! The AAA Similarity criterion states that if all three corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar. You don't even need to check the sides! This is because if the angles are the same, the shape is fixed, and the sides will automatically be proportional. It's like having two maps of the same region, one large and one small – the angles between roads remain the same.

Worked Example 2: Using AAA Similarity

  • Given ΔABC with ∠A=30°, ∠B=70°.
  • Given ΔDEF with ∠D=30°, ∠E=70°.
  • Are ΔABC and ΔDEF similar? How?

🎙️ Let's apply AAA. In ΔABC, ∠C = 180° - 30° - 70° = 80°. In ΔDEF, ∠F = 180° - 30° - 70° = 80°. So, ∠A=∠D=30°, ∠B=∠E=70°, and ∠C=∠F=80°. Since all corresponding angles are equal, by AAA similarity criterion, ΔABC is similar to ΔDEF. Simple, right?

AA Similarity: A Shorter Path!

  • If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.
  • This is a direct consequence of AAA.
  • If ∠A=∠P and ∠B=∠Q, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

🎙️ This is an even quicker way! If you know two pairs of corresponding angles are equal, the third pair must also be equal (because the sum of angles in a triangle is 180°). So, if you can prove just two angles are equal, you've automatically satisfied the AAA condition. This makes proving similarity much easier in many problems. It's a very commonly used criterion.

Criteria for Similarity: SSS (Side-Side-Side)

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then they are similar.
  • Ratio of sides must be constant.
  • If AB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RP, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

🎙️ Our second major criterion is SSS Similarity. This states that if the ratio of all three pairs of corresponding sides is the same, then the triangles are similar. Imagine you're baking a cake and you scale the recipe up or down. If you double all the ingredients, the cake will have the same shape, just a different size. Similarly, if all sides are scaled by the same factor, the triangles are similar.

Worked Example 3: Using SSS Similarity

  • ΔABC has sides 2cm, 3cm, 4cm.
  • ΔPQR has sides 4cm, 6cm, 8cm.
  • Are they similar? If so, how?

🎙️ Let's check the ratios: AB/PQ = 2/4 = 1/2. BC/QR = 3/6 = 1/2. CA/RP = 4/8 = 1/2. Since all corresponding side ratios are equal (1/2), by SSS similarity criterion, ΔABC is similar to ΔPQR. Remember, you must match the corresponding sides carefully, usually by ordering them from smallest to largest.

Criteria for Similarity: SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other triangle,
  • AND the sides including these angles are proportional, then they are similar.
  • If ∠A=∠P and AB/PQ = CA/RP, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

🎙️ Our third and final criterion is SAS Similarity. This one is a mix of angles and sides. It says that if you have an equal angle in both triangles, AND the two sides that form that angle are proportional, then the triangles are similar. The angle *must* be included between the two proportional sides. It's like having a specific corner of a house, and then scaling up or down the two walls connected to that corner proportionally.

Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔPQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)².
  • This is a powerful relationship!

🎙️ This is a very important theorem! It tells us how the areas of similar triangles are related. If you have two similar triangles, and one has sides twice as long as the other, its area won't just be twice as large – it will be four times as large (2 squared)! This is because area involves two dimensions, so the scaling factor is squared. Remember this for your exams!

Worked Example 5: Calculating Area Ratio

  • If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR and AB/PQ = 2/3.
  • What is the ratio of their areas?
  • Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔPQR) = ?

🎙️ Let's apply the theorem. We are given that the ratio of corresponding sides AB/PQ is 2/3. According to the theorem, the ratio of their areas will be the square of this ratio. So, Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔPQR) = (2/3)² = 4/9. This means if the sides are in a 2:3 ratio, the areas are in a 4:9 ratio. Understand this concept well.

Revisiting Pythagoras Theorem

  • For a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse).
  • Often used in problems involving similar right triangles.

🎙️ You've learned Pythagoras Theorem in earlier classes. It's crucial for right-angled triangles. It states that if you have a right triangle, the square of the longest side (hypotenuse) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides. This theorem frequently appears in problems involving similar triangles, especially when dealing with altitudes or medians within them.

Worked Example 6: Pythagoras and Similarity

  • In a right triangle ABC, ∠B=90°, BD ⊥ AC.
  • Prove ΔADB ~ ΔABC.
  • Use this to show AB² = AD * AC.

🎙️ This is a classic problem. In ΔADB and ΔABC: ∠ADB = ∠ABC = 90° (given). ∠A is common to both triangles. By AA similarity, ΔADB ~ ΔABC. Now, since they are similar, their sides are proportional: AD/AB = AB/AC. Cross-multiplying gives AB² = AD * AC. This shows how similarity helps derive important geometric relationships, including those related to Pythagoras.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem)

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points,
  • then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
  • If DE || BC, then AD/DB = AE/EC.

🎙️ This is a fundamental theorem often called Thales Theorem. It's incredibly useful in geometry. Imagine a triangular field, and you draw a straight fence parallel to one side, cutting across the other two. This theorem says that the fence will divide those two sides into segments that are in the same proportion. This theorem is also a direct consequence of similar triangles.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mixing up congruence and similarity.
  • Incorrectly matching corresponding vertices/sides.
  • Forgetting to square the ratio for areas.
  • Applying criteria incorrectly (e.g., SAS without included angle).

🎙️ Students often make a few common errors. Remember, congruent means identical, similar means same shape, different size. Always make sure you're comparing corresponding parts – angle A with angle P, side AB with side PQ, not just any random side. And for areas, don't forget that crucial squaring step! Pay close attention to the conditions for each similarity criterion.
