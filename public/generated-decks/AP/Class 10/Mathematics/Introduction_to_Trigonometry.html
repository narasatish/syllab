What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of triangles.
  • Comes from Greek words 'Trigonon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'triangle measurement'. It's a fascinating field that helps us understand how the lengths of a triangle's sides relate to its angles. It's a key part of geometry and algebra.

Sides of a Right-Angled Triangle

  • Hypotenuse: Longest side, opposite the 90° angle.
  • Perpendicular (Opposite): Side opposite the reference angle.
  • Base (Adjacent): Side next to the reference angle (not hypotenuse).

🎙️ Let's quickly recall the names of the sides. The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle. The other two sides, perpendicular and base, depend on which acute angle we are focusing on.

Reference Angle (θ)

  • We choose one of the two acute angles as our reference angle (often denoted by θ or A).
  • The 'Opposite' and 'Adjacent' sides are defined relative to this angle.

🎙️ In trigonometry, we always pick one of the acute angles to work with. This is our 'reference angle'. The names 'opposite' and 'adjacent' sides will change if you pick a different reference angle.

The Idea of Trigonometric Ratios

  • Ratios compare the lengths of two sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • These ratios remain constant for a given angle, regardless of triangle size.

🎙️ Imagine scaling a triangle up or down; the side lengths change, but their ratios for a specific angle stay the same. This constant relationship is the core idea behind trigonometric ratios.

Remembering the Ratios: SOH CAH TOA

  • A helpful mnemonic to recall the three main ratios.
  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ This is your secret weapon! SOH CAH TOA will help you remember the definitions of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent. Let's break down each one.

Sine (Sin): Opposite / Hypotenuse

  • Sin θ = Length of side Opposite to θ / Length of Hypotenuse.
  • Example: If Opposite = 3, Hypotenuse = 5, then Sin θ = 3/5.

🎙️ Sine tells us how tall the 'opposite' side is compared to the hypotenuse. Think of it as the 'vertical' component of the angle's reach. It's always a value between 0 and 1 for acute angles.

⬆️
Opposite Side
10 / 25

Cosine (Cos): Adjacent / Hypotenuse

  • Cos θ = Length of side Adjacent to θ / Length of Hypotenuse.
  • Example: If Adjacent = 4, Hypotenuse = 5, then Cos θ = 4/5.

🎙️ Cosine tells us how long the 'adjacent' side is compared to the hypotenuse. Think of it as the 'horizontal' component. Like Sine, it's also between 0 and 1 for acute angles.

➡️
Adjacent Side
11 / 25

Tangent (Tan): Opposite / Adjacent

  • Tan θ = Length of side Opposite to θ / Length of side Adjacent to θ.
  • Also, Tan θ = Sin θ / Cos θ.
  • Example: If Opposite = 3, Adjacent = 4, then Tan θ = 3/4.

🎙️ Tangent gives us the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side. It's like measuring the 'steepness' or slope of the angle. It can be any positive value for acute angles.

↗️
Slope Ratio

Introduction to Reciprocal Ratios

  • Each main ratio has a reciprocal (inverse) ratio.
  • These are Cosecant, Secant, and Cotangent.
  • They are simply 1 divided by the primary ratio.

🎙️ Just like 2 has a reciprocal of 1/2, our trigonometric ratios also have reciprocals. These are not new concepts but simply different ways of expressing the same side relationships.

🔄
Inverse Relationship
17 / 25

Cosecant (Csc): Hypotenuse / Opposite

  • Csc θ = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • It is the reciprocal of Sine: Csc θ = 1 / Sin θ.

🎙️ Cosecant is the flip of Sine. If Sine tells us Opposite/Hypotenuse, then Cosecant tells us Hypotenuse/Opposite. It's useful in certain calculations and identities.

⬆️
Hypotenuse over Opposite
18 / 25

Secant (Sec): Hypotenuse / Adjacent

  • Sec θ = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.
  • It is the reciprocal of Cosine: Sec θ = 1 / Cos θ.

🎙️ Similarly, Secant is the reciprocal of Cosine. If Cosine is Adjacent/Hypotenuse, then Secant is Hypotenuse/Adjacent. Notice the pattern – each reciprocal ratio flips the original fraction.

➡️
Hypotenuse over Adjacent
19 / 25

Cotangent (Cot): Adjacent / Opposite

  • Cot θ = Adjacent / Opposite.
  • It is the reciprocal of Tangent: Cot θ = 1 / Tan θ.

🎙️ And finally, Cotangent is the reciprocal of Tangent. It gives us the ratio of the adjacent side to the opposite side. It's also Cosine divided by Sine.

↗️
Adjacent over Opposite
