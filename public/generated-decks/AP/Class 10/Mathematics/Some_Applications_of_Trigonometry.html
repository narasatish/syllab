Angle of Elevation

  • Formed when you look UP at an object.
  • Between the line of sight and the horizontal line.

🎙️ When you look up at a kite or a tall building, the angle your line of sight makes with the ground, or the horizontal, is called the Angle of Elevation. It's an upward glance!

Angle of Depression

  • Formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • Between the line of sight and the horizontal line.

🎙️ Conversely, if you're on a cliff looking down at a boat, the angle between your horizontal view and your line of sight to the boat is the Angle of Depression. This is a downward glance.

  • A pole casts a shadow 15m long.
  • The angle of elevation of the sun is 60°.
  • Find the height of the pole.

🎙️ Let's try our first problem. We have a flagpole, its shadow, and the sun's angle. This forms a right-angled triangle. Which ratio connects opposite (height) and adjacent (shadow)?

10 / 25

Solution 1: Using Tangent

  • Diagram: Right triangle with height 'h', base 15m, angle 60°.
  • tan(60°) = Opposite/Adjacent = h/15.
  • h = 15 * tan(60°) = 15 * √3 ≈ 25.98m.

🎙️ Since we know the adjacent side and want the opposite side, Tangent is the perfect choice! tan(60°) is √3, so multiplying by 15 gives us the height of the flagpole.

Worked Example 2: Ship's Distance

  • From a lighthouse 75m high, the angle of depression of a ship is 30°.
  • Find the distance of the ship from the lighthouse.

🎙️ Now, let's use the Angle of Depression. We're on a lighthouse, looking down at a ship. Remember, the angle of depression is equal to the angle of elevation from the ship to the lighthouse!

Distance Measurement
12 / 25

Solution 2: Alternate Angles

  • Diagram: Lighthouse height 75m. Angle of depression 30°.
  • Angle of elevation from ship to lighthouse is also 30°.
  • distance = 75 / tan(30°) = 75√3 ≈ 129.9m.

🎙️ By using the property of alternate interior angles, the angle of elevation from the ship to the lighthouse is also 30°. Then, we use tangent again to find the horizontal distance, which is 75 times √3.

Worked Example 3: Balloon Height

  • A girl observes a balloon at 60° elevation.
  • After some time, it moves, and the angle becomes 30°.
  • If the girl is 1.2m tall and the balloon is 88.2m from the ground, find the distance the balloon traveled.

🎙️ This problem involves a moving object and two different angles of elevation. Remember to account for the girl's height in the initial setup of the triangle for the balloon's height above her eye level.

🎈
Balloon
15 / 25

Solution 3: Two Triangles

  • Height of balloon above girl's eye = 88.2 - 1.2 = 87m.
  • Use tan(60°) and tan(30°) for two triangles.
  • Calculate horizontal distances for both positions.
  • Subtract to find the distance traveled.

🎙️ We form two right triangles with the balloon's height above the girl's eye level. By using tan for both 60° and 30°, we find the two horizontal distances and then subtract them to get the balloon's horizontal travel.
