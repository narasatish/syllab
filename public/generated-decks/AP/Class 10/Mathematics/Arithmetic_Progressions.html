Defining an AP

  • A sequence of numbers
  • Difference between consecutive terms is constant
  • This constant difference is key

🎙️ An Arithmetic Progression (AP) is a sequence of numbers where the difference between any two consecutive terms is constant. This constant difference is very important!

The "Common Difference" (d)

  • The constant difference between terms
  • Denoted by the letter 'd'
  • Calculated as (succeeding term - previous term)

🎙️ This fixed difference between consecutive terms is called the common difference, denoted by 'd'. You find it by subtracting any term from its succeeding term.

➖
Constant Subtraction

Let's Find 'd'!

  • Sequence: 2, 5, 8, 11, ...
  • 5 - 2 = 3
  • 8 - 5 = 3
  • 11 - 8 = 3

🎙️ For example, in the sequence 2, 5, 8, 11..., d = 5-2 = 3. Also, 8-5 = 3. Since the difference is constant, it's an AP, and 'd' is 3.

1Pick two consecutive terms
2Subtract previous from next
3Repeat to confirm

The General Form of an AP

  • Starts with 'a'
  • Each term is 'previous term + d'
  • a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, ...

🎙️ With 'a' and 'd', we can write any AP as: a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, and so on. Each term is simply the previous term plus 'd'.

Building an AP

  • Given: a = 5, d = 3
  • 1st term: 5
  • 2nd term: 5 + 3 = 8
  • 3rd term: 8 + 3 = 11

🎙️ If a = 5 and d = 3, the AP would be: 5 (a), 5+3=8 (a+d), 8+3=11 (a+2d), 11+3=14 (a+3d)... The sequence is 5, 8, 11, 14...

What's the Nth Term?

  • 1st term (a1) = a + 0d
  • 2nd term (a2) = a + 1d
  • 3rd term (a3) = a + 2d

🎙️ If the 1st term is 'a', the 2nd is 'a+d' (1d), the 3rd is 'a+2d' (2d). Notice the pattern? The coefficient of 'd' is always one less than the term number.

11st term: a
22nd term: a+d
33rd term: a+2d
4Pattern: (n-1)d
16 / 30

The Formula for the Nth Term

  • an = a + (n-1)d
  • an: the nth term
  • a: the first term
  • n: the term number, d: common difference

🎙️ So, the formula to find any term (the nth term) in an AP is: an = a + (n-1)d. This powerful formula allows us to find any term without listing them all out!

📏
Measuring Any Term

Example: Find the 10th Term

  • AP: 2, 7, 12, ...
  • Here, a=2, d=5, n=10
  • a10 = 2 + (10-1)5
  • a10 = 2 + 9*5 = 47

🎙️ For the AP: 2, 7, 12, ... find the 10th term. Here, a=2, d=5, n=10. So, a10 = 2 + (10-1)5 = 2 + 9*5 = 2 + 45 = 47.

1Identify a, d, n
2Apply formula an = a + (n-1)d
3Calculate the value

Summing Up an AP: Gauss's Method

  • Gauss summed 1 to 100 quickly
  • Paired (1+100), (2+99), etc.
  • Each pair summed to 101
  • There were 50 such pairs (50 * 101 = 5050)

🎙️ When Gauss was a child, his teacher asked him to sum numbers from 1 to 100. He quickly found a trick: pair the first and last (1+100), second and second-last (2+99), etc. Each pair sums to 101, and there are 50 such pairs.

Building the Sum Formula

  • Write the sum (Sn) forwards
  • Write the sum (Sn) backwards
  • Add both equations
  • Simplify to derive Sn formula

🎙️ We can use a similar clever trick to derive the formula for the sum of 'n' terms of an AP. By writing the sum forwards and backwards and adding them, the common difference cancels out.

The Sum Formula: Sn

  • Sn = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
  • Sn: sum of 'n' terms
  • n: number of terms
  • a: first term, d: common difference

🎙️ The formula for the sum of the first 'n' terms of an AP is: Sn = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]. This helps us quickly find the total of many terms.

23 / 30

Sum Formula with Last Term (l)

  • Sn = n/2 [a + l]
  • l: the last term of the AP
  • This is an alternative, simpler formula
  • Remember, l is the same as an

🎙️ If you know the last term (l) of the AP, the sum formula becomes even simpler: Sn = n/2 [a + l]. Remember, the last term 'l' is essentially the 'nth' term, an.

Using the End

Example: Sum of First 10 Terms

  • AP: 2, 7, 12, ...
  • Here, a=2, d=5, n=10
  • Sn = 10/2 [2*2 + (10-1)5]
  • Sn = 5 [4 + 9*5] = 5 [4 + 45] = 5 * 49 = 245

🎙️ Find the sum of the first 10 terms of the AP: 2, 7, 12, ... Here, a=2, d=5, n=10. Substitute these values into the formula to get the sum.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing 'n' (term number) with 'an' (value of the term)
  • Calculation errors with signs when finding 'd'
  • Incorrectly calculating (n-1) in formulas
  • Using the wrong formula (nth term vs. sum)

🎙️ Students often confuse 'n' with 'an', or make calculation errors with signs when finding 'd'. Always double-check your common difference and ensure 'n-1' is correctly calculated.
