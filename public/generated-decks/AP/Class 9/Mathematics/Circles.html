What is a Circle?

  • A circle is a collection of all points in a plane.
  • These points are at a fixed distance from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.
  • The fixed distance is called the 'radius'.

🎙️ Imagine pinning one end of a string to a point and drawing with a pencil at the other end, keeping the string taut. The path traced by the pencil is a circle. The pin is the center, and the string's length is the radius.

Parts of a Circle: Center and Radius

  • Center (O): The fixed point inside the circle.
  • Radius (r): The distance from the center to any point on the circle.
  • All radii of the same circle are equal in length.

🎙️ The center is like the heart of the circle, and the radius is like an arm extending from the heart to any point on its boundary. Remember, a circle can have infinitely many radii, but they all have the same length.

Parts of a Circle: Diameter

  • Diameter (d): A line segment passing through the center.
  • It connects two points on the circle.
  • The diameter is twice the radius (d = 2r).

🎙️ The diameter is the longest chord in a circle. It cuts the circle exactly in half. If you know the radius, you can easily find the diameter by multiplying it by two, and vice versa.

Parts of a Circle: Chord

  • Chord: A line segment joining any two points on the circle.
  • A diameter is the longest chord of a circle.
  • Chords can be of different lengths.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a straight line inside the circle that touches two points on its boundary, but doesn't necessarily pass through the center. That's a chord. A diameter is a special type of chord that always passes through the center.

Parts of a Circle: Arc

  • Arc: A continuous piece of a circle.
  • It is a portion of the circumference.
  • Minor Arc: Shorter arc between two points.
  • Major Arc: Longer arc between the same two points.

🎙️ An arc is like a curved segment of the circle's boundary. Think of cutting a slice from a round cake; the crust of that slice is an arc. We classify arcs as minor or major based on their length relative to half the circle.

Parts of a Circle: Segment and Sector

  • Segment: Region between a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • Sector: Region between two radii and their corresponding arc.
  • Think of a 'slice' of pizza for a sector.

🎙️ A segment is the area enclosed by a chord and an arc. A sector, on the other hand, is like a piece of pie or a slice of pizza. It's the area enclosed by two radii and the arc between them.

Concentric Circles

  • Circles that share the same center point.
  • They have different radii.
  • Example: Target board, ripples in water.

🎙️ Concentric circles are like rings within rings, all originating from the same central point. Think of a dartboard or the ripples that form when you drop a stone into water. They are all concentric circles.

Circumference of a Circle

  • The perimeter or distance around the circle.
  • Formula: C = 2πr (where r is radius).
  • Alternatively: C = πd (where d is diameter).
  • π (pi) is a mathematical constant, approximately 3.14 or 22/7.

🎙️ The circumference is essentially the 'length' of the circle's boundary. If you were to cut a circle and straighten it out, its length would be the circumference. The constant pi (π) is crucial here, representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.

🏃‍♀️

Area of a Circle

  • The space enclosed by the circle.
  • Formula: A = πr² (where r is radius).
  • Units are always square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ The area tells us how much surface a circle covers. Imagine painting the inside of a circular disk; the amount of paint needed depends on its area. Remember the formula A = πr², and always express the area in square units.

Theorem 1: Perpendicular from Center to Chord

  • The perpendicular from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • If OM ⊥ AB, then AM = MB.
  • This means it divides the chord into two equal parts.

🎙️ This is a very important theorem. It tells us that if you draw a line from the center that hits a chord at a 90-degree angle, it will always cut that chord exactly in half. This property is often used in problems involving chords and distances.

Theorem 2: Equal Chords and their Distances

  • Equal chords of a circle (or of congruent circles) are equidistant from the center.
  • Conversely, chords equidistant from the center are equal.
  • If AB = CD, then OM = ON (where OM ⊥ AB, ON ⊥ CD).

🎙️ This theorem establishes a direct relationship between the length of a chord and its distance from the center. If two chords are of the same length, they will be the same distance away from the center. This works both ways, making it a powerful tool for proofs and calculations.

Angle Subtended by a Chord at the Center

  • A chord subtends an angle at the center by joining its endpoints to the center.
  • Equal chords subtend equal angles at the center.
  • If AB = CD, then ∠AOB = ∠COD.

🎙️ When you connect the two ends of a chord to the center of the circle, you form an angle. This angle is called the angle subtended by the chord at the center. A key property is that if chords are equal in length, the angles they form at the center will also be equal.

Angles in a Circle: Center vs. Circumference

  • The angle subtended by an arc at the center is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.
  • ∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB (where A, B are on circle, O is center, C is on circumference).

🎙️ This is a fundamental theorem regarding angles in a circle. It means that an angle formed at the center by an arc is always twice the size of any angle formed by the same arc at any other point on the circle's circumference. This relationship is incredibly useful in solving geometry problems.

Angles in the Same Segment

  • Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
  • If points C and D are in the same segment formed by chord AB,
  • Then ∠ACB = ∠ADB.

🎙️ Imagine drawing multiple angles from the same chord to different points on the circumference, as long as those points are in the same segment. This theorem states that all these angles will be equal. It's like looking at the same scene from different vantage points within a specific area.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on a circle.
  • The sum of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°.
  • ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°.

🎙️ A cyclic quadrilateral is a special type of quadrilateral where all its corners touch the circumference of a circle. The most important property to remember is that its opposite angles always add up to 180 degrees. This is a very common concept in geometry problems.
