Unveiling Heron's Formula

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • 'a', 'b', 'c' are the lengths of the triangle's sides
  • 's' is the semi-perimeter of the triangle

🎙️ Here it is! This formula might look a bit complex at first, with the square root and multiple terms, but we'll break it down step by step. The beauty is that it only requires the lengths of the three sides of the triangle.

✨
The Magic Formula
7 / 25

What is Semi-perimeter 's'?

  • It's half of the perimeter of the triangle
  • Perimeter = sum of all three side lengths (a + b + c)
  • Formula: s = (a + b + c) / 2

🎙️ Before applying Heron's formula, we first need to calculate something called the semi-perimeter. It's simply half the total length of all three sides of the triangle, an easy first step.

📏📏📏
Sum of Sides / 2
8 / 25

Decoding Heron's Formula

  • s: the semi-perimeter we just calculated
  • (s-a): the difference between 's' and side 'a'
  • (s-b): the difference between 's' and side 'b'
  • (s-c): the difference between 's' and side 'c'

🎙️ Each part inside the square root is important and represents a simple calculation. We multiply the semi-perimeter by the differences between the semi-perimeter and each of the three sides. This prepares us for the final step.

Step-by-Step

When is Heron's Formula Most Useful?

  • When only the three side lengths are known
  • No need to find or measure the perpendicular height
  • Works for all types of triangles (scalene, isosceles, equilateral)

🎙️ This formula is particularly handy when finding the height of the triangle is difficult or impossible. It's a universal tool for any triangle, as long as you have its side lengths, making it very versatile.

Worked Example 1: Calculate Semi-perimeter

  • Given sides: a=50m, b=80m, c=120m
  • Perimeter = 50 + 80 + 120 = 250m
  • Semi-perimeter (s) = 250 / 2 = 125m

🎙️ First, let's find the semi-perimeter. We add up all the side lengths of the park and then divide the sum by two. This gives us 's', which is 125 meters for this park.

1Add sides: 50 + 80 + 120 = 250
2Divide by 2: 250 / 2 = 125
12 / 25

Worked Example 1: Calculate (s-a), (s-b), (s-c)

  • s = 125m, a=50m, b=80m, c=120m
  • s - a = 125 - 50 = 75
  • s - b = 125 - 80 = 45
  • s - c = 125 - 120 = 5

🎙️ Next, we calculate the differences between the semi-perimeter 's' and each individual side length. These values will be used inside the square root in Heron's formula.

1s-a = 125 - 50 = 75
2s-b = 125 - 80 = 45
3s-c = 125 - 120 = 5
13 / 25

Worked Example 1: Apply Heron's Formula

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Area = √[125 × 75 × 45 × 5]
  • Area = √[2,109,375]
  • Area ≈ 1452.35 m²

🎙️ Now, we substitute all these calculated values into Heron's formula and calculate the product under the square root. Finally, we find the square root to get the area of the park, which is approximately 1452.35 square meters.

Special Case: Equilateral Triangle

  • For an equilateral triangle, all sides are 'a'
  • Semi-perimeter (s) = (a+a+a)/2 = 3a/2
  • Applying Heron's gives Area = (√3/4)a²

🎙️ For an equilateral triangle, where all sides are equal, Heron's formula simplifies to a well-known formula. You can derive this by substituting 'a' for all side lengths into Heron's formula.

Key Takeaways from Heron's Formula

  • Allows finding area without knowing height
  • Requires only the three side lengths (a, b, c)
  • First calculate semi-perimeter: s = (a+b+c)/2
  • Then apply: Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]

🎙️ Today, we learned a powerful tool for finding the area of any triangle. Remember the steps: first calculate 's', then the differences (s-a), (s-b), (s-c), and finally apply the main formula.
