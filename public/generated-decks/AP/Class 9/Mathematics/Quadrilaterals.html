What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides
  • Four vertices and four angles
  • Its name comes from 'quad' (four) and 'lateral' (sides)

🎙️ A quadrilateral is simply any closed shape with exactly four straight sides. Think of it as a four-sided polygon. It will always have four corners, which we call vertices, and four interior angles.

⏹️
A 4-sided Polygon

Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Four sides (e.g., AB, BC, CD, DA)
  • Four vertices (e.g., A, B, C, D)
  • Four interior angles (e.g., ∠A, ∠B, ∠C, ∠D)
  • Two diagonals (e.g., AC, BD)

🎙️ Every quadrilateral has these basic parts. The sides connect the vertices, and the angles are formed at each vertex. Diagonals are lines that connect non-adjacent vertices, cutting across the shape.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of all interior angles is 360°
  • Similar to a triangle's 180° sum
  • This property holds true for ALL quadrilaterals

🎙️ A very important property of any quadrilateral is that if you add up all its four interior angles, the total will always be 360 degrees. This is a fundamental rule we use to solve many problems involving quadrilaterals.

Introducing the Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides
  • Opposite sides are parallel to each other
  • It's a foundational type of quadrilateral

🎙️ Our first special quadrilateral is the parallelogram. The key feature here is that its opposite sides are always parallel. This single property leads to many other interesting characteristics.

↔️
Opposite Sides Parallel
9 / 25

Properties of a Parallelogram

  • Opposite sides are equal in length
  • Opposite angles are equal
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add up to 180°)
  • Diagonals bisect each other

🎙️ These are the four main properties that define a parallelogram. Remember, if a quadrilateral has any one of these properties, it is a parallelogram. For example, if its diagonals bisect each other, it must be a parallelogram.

✅
Sides, Angles, Diagonals

Meet the Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°
  • All rectangles are parallelograms
  • But not all parallelograms are rectangles

🎙️ The rectangle is a very familiar shape. It's a special type of parallelogram where all its corners are right angles, or 90 degrees. This means it inherits all the properties of a parallelogram and has some extra ones too.

🔲
All Angles 90°

Properties of a Rectangle

  • All properties of a parallelogram apply
  • Each interior angle is 90°
  • Diagonals are equal in length

🎙️ Since a rectangle is a parallelogram, its opposite sides are parallel and equal, and its diagonals bisect each other. Additionally, the defining feature is its right angles, and a unique property is that its diagonals are always the same length.

📏
Diagonals are Equal

Understanding the Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length
  • Often looks like a 'tilted' square
  • All rhombuses are parallelograms

🎙️ Next, we have the rhombus. This is another type of parallelogram where all four sides are equal in length. Imagine taking a square and pushing on two opposite corners; that's what a rhombus looks like.

💎
All Sides Equal
14 / 25

Properties of a Rhombus

  • All properties of a parallelogram apply
  • All four sides are equal
  • Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°)
  • Diagonals bisect the angles of the rhombus

🎙️ Besides the parallelogram properties, a rhombus has two very special features related to its diagonals. Not only do they bisect each other, but they do so at a right angle, and they also divide the corner angles into two equal parts.

✨
Perpendicular Diagonals

The Perfect Square

  • A quadrilateral with all sides equal AND all angles 90°
  • It is a rectangle, a rhombus, and a parallelogram!
  • The most 'perfect' regular quadrilateral

🎙️ The square is truly special because it combines the best features of both rectangles and rhombuses. It has all four sides equal and all four angles equal to 90 degrees. This makes it a highly symmetric and versatile shape.

📦
All Sides & Angles Equal
16 / 25

Properties of a Square

  • All properties of parallelograms, rectangles, and rhombuses apply
  • All sides are equal
  • All angles are 90°
  • Diagonals are equal, bisect each other at 90°, and bisect the angles

🎙️ Because a square is a rectangle and a rhombus, it inherits all their properties. This means its diagonals are equal and bisect each other at right angles. It truly is the 'master' quadrilateral, possessing all the key traits.

⭐

Exploring the Trapezium (Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides
  • The parallel sides are called bases
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs

🎙️ Now let's look at the trapezium, also known as a trapezoid. Unlike a parallelogram, it only has one pair of parallel sides. These parallel sides are usually drawn horizontally and are called the bases of the trapezium.

🚧

Soaring with the Kite

  • A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of equal-length adjacent sides
  • One diagonal is the perpendicular bisector of the other
  • The diagonals are perpendicular to each other

🎙️ The kite is named after the flying toy! Its unique feature is that it has two pairs of equal-length sides, but these sides are adjacent, meaning they share a common vertex. Its diagonals are always perpendicular.

🪁
Adjacent Sides Equal

A Special Trapezium: Isosceles Trapezium

  • A trapezium where the non-parallel sides are equal in length
  • Base angles are equal (angles along the parallel sides)
  • Diagonals are also equal in length

🎙️ Just like an isosceles triangle, an isosceles trapezium has two sides of equal length. In this case, it's the non-parallel sides that are equal. This leads to its base angles being equal and its diagonals also having equal lengths.

⛰️
Non-parallel sides equal

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • A quadrilateral has 4 sides and 4 angles (sum 360°)
  • Parallelograms: opposite sides parallel and equal
  • Rectangles: parallelogram with 90° angles, equal diagonals
  • Rhombus: parallelogram with equal sides, perpendicular diagonals
  • Square: all properties of rectangle and rhombus
  • Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides
  • Kite: two pairs of adjacent equal sides

🎙️ We've covered a lot today! Remember the basic definition of a quadrilateral and its angle sum property. Understand the specific properties that distinguish parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses, squares, trapeziums, and kites. Keep practicing to master these concepts!
