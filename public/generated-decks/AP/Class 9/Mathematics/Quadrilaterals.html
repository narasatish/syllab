What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • Has four vertices (corners).
  • Has four interior angles and two diagonals.

🎙️ Imagine a room with four walls. That's essentially a quadrilateral! It's a closed figure with four straight boundaries. Each corner where two walls meet is a vertex, and the angles are formed at these vertices.

The Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of all interior angles is 360°.
  • Can be proven by dividing into two triangles.
  • Each triangle's angles sum to 180°.

🎙️ Just like a triangle's angles always add up to 180 degrees, a quadrilateral has its own special sum. If you tear off the four corners of any quadrilateral and place them together, they will form a perfect circle, which is 360 degrees. This property is very useful for finding missing angles!

Full circle of angles

Worked Example 1: Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given three angles: 80°, 100°, 90°.
  • Let the fourth angle be 'x'.
  • Sum = 80° + 100° + 90° + x = 360°.

🎙️ Let's apply the Angle Sum Property. First, add up the three angles you know. Then, subtract that sum from 360 degrees. The remaining value will be your missing angle 'x'. In this case, 270° + x = 360°, so x = 90°.

1Add known angles
2Subtract from 360°
3Result is 'x'

Meet the Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with opposite sides parallel.
  • Think of a leaning bookshelf or a tilted window.
  • It's a very common type!

🎙️ Imagine a rectangle that has been pushed from one side, making it lean. The top and bottom sides are still parallel, and the left and right sides are also parallel. This is the essence of a parallelogram – two pairs of parallel sides.

Parallelogram Properties (Part 1)

  • Opposite sides are equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal in measure.
  • Adjacent angles are supplementary (add to 180°).

🎙️ In a parallelogram, there's a beautiful symmetry. If the top side is 5 cm, the bottom side will also be 5 cm. Similarly, if one angle is 70 degrees, the angle opposite to it will also be 70 degrees. Angles next to each other add up to 180 degrees.

⚖️
Balanced sides and angles
12 / 30

Parallelogram Properties (Part 2)

  • The diagonals bisect each other.
  • This means they cut each other into two equal halves.
  • The point of intersection is the midpoint of both diagonals.

🎙️ Draw a parallelogram and then draw its two diagonals. You'll notice they cross each other exactly in the middle. This bisection property is unique and very helpful when solving problems involving parallelograms.

✖️
Diagonals bisect

Worked Example 2: Using Parallelogram Properties

  • In parallelogram ABCD, AB = 5 cm, ∠A = 70°.
  • Find CD, BC, and ∠C, ∠B.
  • Apply properties of opposite sides and angles.

🎙️ Since opposite sides are equal, CD = AB = 5 cm. We can't find BC without more info, but we know BC will be equal to AD. For angles, ∠C = ∠A = 70°. And since adjacent angles are supplementary, ∠B = 180° - ∠A = 180° - 70° = 110°. Also, ∠D = ∠B = 110°.

1Opposite sides equal
2Opposite angles equal
3Adjacent angles supplementary

Introducing the Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • Think of a door, a book, or a TV screen.
  • It's a special type of parallelogram.

🎙️ A rectangle is essentially a 'neat' parallelogram. All the properties of a parallelogram apply to a rectangle, but with the added condition that all its corners are perfect right angles, making it a very stable and common shape in construction and design.

🚪
Everyday rectangle

Properties of a Rectangle

  • All properties of a parallelogram.
  • Each interior angle is 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal in length.

🎙️ Rectangles inherit all the parallelogram properties: opposite sides are parallel and equal, opposite angles are equal (which are all 90° here), and diagonals bisect each other. The key additional property is that both diagonals are of the same length, which is not true for all parallelograms.

Meet the Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.
  • Looks like a tilted square or a diamond shape.
  • Another special type of parallelogram.

🎙️ Imagine a square that has been pushed from two opposite corners, making it lean. All its sides are still equal, but its angles are no longer necessarily 90 degrees. This is a rhombus – a parallelogram where all four sides are congruent.

💎
Diamond shape

Properties of a Rhombus

  • All properties of a parallelogram.
  • All four sides are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).

🎙️ A rhombus has parallel and equal opposite sides, and equal opposite angles. Its diagonals also bisect each other. But here's the unique part: they cross each other at a perfect 90-degree angle. Also, the diagonals bisect the angles of the rhombus.

The Perfect Shape: The Square

  • A rectangle with all sides equal.
  • OR a rhombus with all angles equal to 90°.
  • It combines the best of both!

🎙️ The square is the superstar of quadrilaterals! It's both a rectangle AND a rhombus. This means it has all the properties of a parallelogram, all the properties of a rectangle, and all the properties of a rhombus. It's truly a 'perfect' four-sided figure.

⏹️
A perfect square

Properties of a Square

  • All four sides are equal.
  • All four angles are 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal and bisect each other at 90°.

🎙️ To summarize, a square has parallel opposite sides, all sides equal, all angles 90 degrees, and diagonals that are equal in length, bisect each other, and intersect at 90 degrees. It's the most specific and feature-rich quadrilateral among the parallelograms.

Introducing the Trapezium (Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.
  • Think of a bucket or a certain type of table.

🎙️ Unlike parallelograms which have two pairs of parallel sides, a trapezium only has one. Imagine a slice of cake that's wider at the bottom than the top, but with straight sides. The top and bottom are parallel, but the slanted sides are not.

⬆️
One pair of parallel sides

The Isosceles Trapezium

  • A special type of trapezium.
  • The non-parallel sides (legs) are equal in length.
  • Base angles are equal.

🎙️ An isosceles trapezium adds a touch of symmetry to the basic trapezium. Its non-parallel sides are equal, which means the base angles (angles on the same parallel side) are also equal. Think of it like an isosceles triangle, but with its top chopped off parallel to the base.

Discovering the Kite

  • A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of equal adjacent sides.
  • Looks exactly like a flying kite!
  • Not necessarily a parallelogram.

🎙️ A kite is a fun shape! It has two pairs of equal sides, but unlike a rhombus, these equal sides are adjacent to each other, not opposite. Imagine two isosceles triangles sharing a common base. That's a kite!

🪁
A flying kite

Properties of a Kite

  • Diagonals are perpendicular to each other.
  • One diagonal bisects the other diagonal.
  • One pair of opposite angles are equal.

🎙️ The diagonals of a kite have interesting properties. They intersect at a 90-degree angle, similar to a rhombus. Also, one of the diagonals is bisected by the other, and one pair of opposite angles are equal. This makes kites distinct from other quadrilaterals.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing rhombus and square properties.
  • Assuming all quadrilaterals are parallelograms.
  • Forgetting the Angle Sum Property of 360°.

🎙️ Students often get confused between a rhombus and a square, or forget that a trapezium is not a parallelogram. Always double-check the specific conditions and properties for each shape. And never forget that crucial 360-degree sum for all quadrilaterals!
