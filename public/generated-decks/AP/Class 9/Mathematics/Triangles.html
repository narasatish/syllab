What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides.
  • It has three vertices (corners).
  • It also has three interior angles.

🎙️ Simply put, a triangle is a closed shape made up of three straight lines. These lines meet at three distinct points called vertices, forming three angles inside the shape.

A basic triangle shape

Elements of a Triangle

  • Vertices: Points where sides meet (A, B, C).
  • Sides: Line segments connecting vertices (AB, BC, CA).
  • Angles: Formed by two sides meeting at a vertex (∠A, ∠B, ∠∠C).

🎙️ Every triangle has three main components: its vertices, its sides, and its angles. Understanding these elements helps us describe and work with different types of triangles.

Types of Triangles by Sides: Scalene

  • All three sides have different lengths.
  • All three angles have different measures.
  • No sides are equal.

🎙️ A scalene triangle is the 'unique' one, where every side has a different length. Consequently, all its interior angles will also be of different measures.

Unequal lengths

Types of Triangles by Sides: Isosceles

  • Exactly two sides are equal in length.
  • The angles opposite to the equal sides are also equal.
  • The third side is called the base.

🎙️ An isosceles triangle has two sides that are exactly the same length. A special property is that the angles opposite these equal sides are also equal to each other.

⚖️
Two sides are balanced

Types of Triangles by Sides: Equilateral

  • All three sides are equal in length.
  • All three angles are equal (each 60°).
  • It's a special type of isosceles triangle.

🎙️ The equilateral triangle is the most symmetrical. All its sides are equal, and all its angles are also equal, each measuring exactly 60 degrees. It's a perfect example of balance.

Types of Triangles by Angles: Acute-angled

  • All three interior angles are acute.
  • Each angle is less than 90°.
  • It looks 'sharp' or 'pointy'.

🎙️ In an acute-angled triangle, every single angle inside it measures less than 90 degrees. These triangles often look 'sharp' or 'pointy' at all their corners.

Types of Triangles by Angles: Right-angled

  • Exactly one interior angle is a right angle.
  • One angle measures exactly 90°.
  • The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.

🎙️ A right-angled triangle is easily identified by its one angle that measures exactly 90 degrees. This is a very important type of triangle, especially in trigonometry.

🎙️ An obtuse-angled triangle has one angle that is 'wide open', meaning it measures more than 90 degrees. Remember, a triangle can only have one obtuse angle.

Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

  • The sum of the three interior angles of any triangle is always 180°.
  • This is a fundamental property.
  • It holds true for all types of triangles.

🎙️ This is one of the most important properties you'll learn about triangles. No matter the shape or size of a triangle, if you add up its three inside angles, the total will always be exactly 180 degrees.

Exterior Angle Property of a Triangle

  • An exterior angle is formed by extending one side of a triangle.
  • It is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • It's always supplementary to the adjacent interior angle.

🎙️ When you extend one side of a triangle, the angle formed outside is called an exterior angle. This exterior angle has a special relationship: it's equal to the sum of the two interior angles that are not next to it.

Congruence of Triangles: What does it mean?

  • Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and size.
  • For triangles, it means they can be placed exactly on top of each other.
  • Corresponding sides and angles are equal.

🎙️ Congruence is a very important concept. Imagine having two identical twins; they are congruent. In geometry, it means two triangles are exact copies of each other, even if one is rotated or flipped.

Congruence Criteria: SSS (Side-Side-Side)

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to three sides of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • This is a powerful way to prove congruence.

🎙️ The SSS criterion states that if you can match all three sides of one triangle to all three sides of another, then those two triangles must be congruent. It's like having a perfect blueprint for both.

📏
Matching all sides
21 / 25

Congruence Criteria: SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to two sides and the included angle of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • The angle must be between the two sides.

🎙️ For SAS, we need two sides and the angle *between* them to match. This 'included' angle is crucial. If these three elements correspond, the triangles are congruent.

📐
Side, Angle, Side
22 / 25

Congruence Criteria: ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)

  • If two angles and the included side of one triangle are equal to two angles and the included side of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • The side must be between the two angles.

🎙️ Similar to SAS, ASA requires two angles and the side *between* them to be equal in both triangles. If you have this specific combination, you've got congruent triangles.

↔️
Angle, Side, Angle

Congruence Criteria: RHS (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side)

  • For right-angled triangles only.
  • If the hypotenuse and one side of a right triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one side of another right triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.

🎙️ RHS is a special criterion just for right-angled triangles. It means if the longest side (hypotenuse) and one other side are equal in two right triangles, they are congruent.
