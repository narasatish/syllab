How Chemicals Combine

  • Foundations for modern chemistry.
  • Established by Antoine Lavoisier and Joseph L. Proust.
  • Two important laws to understand reactions.

🎙️ These ancient ideas were philosophical. Later, scientists began to conduct experiments and discovered some fundamental laws that govern how elements combine to form compounds. These laws are the backbone of chemistry.

Law of Conservation of Mass

  • Mass can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products.
  • During a chemical reaction.

🎙️ This law states that in any chemical reaction, the total mass of the substances before the reaction is exactly equal to the total mass of the substances after the reaction. Nothing is lost, nothing is gained.

⚖️

Example: Burning Wood

  • Wood + Oxygen → Ash + Smoke + Gases.
  • Mass of wood + oxygen = Mass of ash + smoke + gases.
  • Mass is conserved, just transformed.

🎙️ When you burn wood, it seems like the wood disappears, but its mass is just converted into ash, smoke, and gases. If you could collect and weigh everything, you'd find the total mass remains the same.

Law of Constant Proportions

  • Also known as Law of Definite Proportions.
  • A chemical compound always contains elements.
  • In fixed proportions by mass, regardless of source.

🎙️ This law tells us that a pure chemical compound will always have the same elements combined in the same fixed ratio by mass, no matter where it comes from or how it was prepared.

Example: Water (H₂O)

  • Water always has Hydrogen and Oxygen.
  • Mass ratio of Hydrogen:Oxygen is 1:8.
  • Whether from a tap, a well, or a lab.

🎙️ Take water, for instance. Whether it's from a river, rain, or made in a lab, it will always be made of Hydrogen and Oxygen, and their mass ratio will always be 1:8. This is a powerful concept!

Dalton's Atomic Theory (Part 1)

  • Proposed by John Dalton in 1808.
  • Matter consists of indivisible atoms.
  • Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties.

🎙️ John Dalton took the ancient ideas and combined them with the laws of chemical combination to propose his revolutionary atomic theory. His first two postulates are about the nature of atoms themselves.

⚛️
Atom Symbol
11 / 25

Dalton's Atomic Theory (Part 2)

  • Atoms of different elements differ in mass and properties.
  • Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
  • Atoms cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction.

🎙️ Dalton further explained how atoms of different elements behave and how they combine. His theory helped explain the Laws of Chemical Combination we just discussed, like conservation of mass.

💡
Idea/Discovery
12 / 25

Limitations of Dalton's Theory

  • Atoms are now known to be divisible (protons, neutrons, electrons).
  • Isotopes: Atoms of same element can have different masses.
  • Does not explain why atoms combine.

🎙️ While groundbreaking, Dalton's theory had some limitations. With further scientific discoveries, we learned that atoms are not truly indivisible and that atoms of the same element can sometimes have different masses.

What Exactly is an Atom?

  • Smallest particle of an element.
  • May or may not exist independently.
  • Retains its chemical properties.

🎙️ So, after all this discussion, what is an atom? It's the smallest particle of an element that still shows all the properties of that element. It's the fundamental unit of matter.

How Small are Atoms?

  • Extremely small, invisible to the naked eye.
  • Measured in nanometres (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m).
  • Example: Hydrogen atom radius ~ 0.037 nm.

🎙️ Atoms are incredibly tiny! You cannot see them even with powerful optical microscopes. We measure their size in nanometres, which is a billionth of a meter. Imagine how many atoms fit in a single drop of water!

Naming Elements: Dalton's Symbols

  • Dalton proposed unique symbols for each element.

🎙️ Initially, scientists used pictorial symbols for elements. Dalton also proposed his own set of symbols, which were quite creative but became impractical as more elements were discovered.

Modern Symbols: IUPAC

  • International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry.
  • Uses one or two letters of the element's name.
  • First letter always capital, second letter always small.

🎙️ To standardize and simplify, the IUPAC developed a system. Most symbols are based on the English name, but some come from Latin or German names. This system is universally accepted.

Atomic Mass: Weighing Atoms

  • Mass of a single atom of an element.
  • Too small to measure directly in grams.
  • Expressed relative to a standard atom.

🎙️ Since atoms are so tiny, their individual masses are extremely small. We can't easily weigh a single atom. So, we use a relative scale to express their mass, comparing them to a standard.

The Atomic Mass Unit (amu)

  • Defined as 1/12th the mass of a Carbon-12 atom.
  • Also called 'unified mass unit' (u).
  • Makes atomic masses easier to work with.

🎙️ The internationally accepted standard for measuring atomic mass is the Carbon-12 atom. One atomic mass unit is defined as exactly one-twelfth of the mass of a single carbon-12 atom. This provides a convenient scale.

How Do Atoms Exist?

  • Most atoms do not exist independently.
  • They combine with other atoms.
  • Forming molecules or ions.

🎙️ Most atoms are not stable on their own. They prefer to combine with other atoms to achieve stability. This combination leads to the formation of molecules or charged particles called ions.

What is a Molecule?

  • A group of two or more atoms.
  • Chemically bonded together.
  • Can exist independently and show properties of the substance.

🎙️ When atoms join together, they form a molecule. A molecule is the smallest particle of an element or a compound that can exist freely and still retain all the properties of that substance.

Molecules of Elements

  • Atoms of the same element combine.
  • Monoatomic (He), Diatomic (O₂), Polyatomic (O₃, P₄).
  • Number of atoms in a molecule is its atomicity.

🎙️ Some elements exist as single atoms, like Helium. But many, like Oxygen, exist as diatomic molecules, meaning two atoms of Oxygen are bonded together. This number of atoms is called atomicity.

⚪⚪

Molecules of Compounds

  • Atoms of different elements combine.
  • In a fixed ratio by mass.
  • Examples: Water (H₂O), Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).

🎙️ When atoms of *different* elements combine in a fixed ratio, they form molecules of compounds. These compounds have properties entirely different from their constituent elements.
