What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • It has four vertices and four angles.
  • Formed by joining four non-collinear points in order.
  • The word 'quad' means four, 'lateral' means side.

🎙️ A quadrilateral is simply any closed shape that has four straight sides. Think of it as a basic building block in geometry. It always has four corners, which we call vertices, and four angles inside.

Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Sides: The four line segments.
  • Vertices: The four points where sides meet.
  • Angles: The four angles formed inside.
  • Diagonals: Lines joining opposite vertices.

🎙️ Every quadrilateral has these key parts: four sides, four vertices, four interior angles, and two diagonals. Understanding these elements helps us describe and classify different types of quadrilaterals. Imagine drawing a simple four-sided figure and labelling these parts.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of all interior angles is 360°.
  • Always true for any quadrilateral.
  • Similar to a triangle's 180° sum.

🎙️ This is a very important property! No matter what kind of quadrilateral it is, if you add up all its four interior angles, the total will always be 360 degrees. This property is fundamental for solving many problems related to angles in quadrilaterals.

🔄
Full Circle

Why 360°? A Simple Proof

  • Draw any quadrilateral, say ABCD.
  • Draw one diagonal, e.g., AC.
  • This divides it into two triangles: ABC and ADC.
  • Sum of angles in each triangle is 180°.

🎙️ To understand why the sum is 360 degrees, imagine drawing a diagonal inside any quadrilateral. This diagonal splits the quadrilateral into two triangles. Since each triangle's angles add up to 180 degrees, the sum of all angles in the quadrilateral becomes 180 + 180, which is 360 degrees.

1Quadrilateral ABCD
2Draw diagonal AC
3Two triangles formed
4180° + 180° = 360°

Types of Quadrilaterals: An Overview

  • Quadrilaterals have special names.
  • Based on their unique properties.
  • Parallelogram, Rectangle, Square, Rhombus, Trapezium, Kite.

🎙️ Just like how we have different types of triangles, there are also different types of quadrilaterals. Each type has its own special characteristics and properties. We will explore each of these in detail in the upcoming slides.

1. Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with opposite sides parallel.
  • Opposite sides are equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other.

🎙️ A parallelogram is a very common type of quadrilateral. Its defining feature is that both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. This parallelism leads to other important properties, such as opposite sides being equal and diagonals cutting each other exactly in half.

2. Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all angles 90°.
  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.
  • All four angles are right angles.
  • Diagonals are equal in length.

🎙️ A rectangle is a special kind of parallelogram. What makes it special is that all its interior angles are exactly 90 degrees. This means that besides having parallel and equal opposite sides, its diagonals are also equal in length, a property not found in all parallelograms.

3. Square

  • A rectangle with all sides equal.
  • All sides are equal and parallel.
  • All four angles are 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal and perpendicular bisectors.

🎙️ The square is the most 'perfect' of the quadrilaterals in many ways. It combines the properties of both a rectangle and a rhombus. All its sides are equal, and all its angles are 90 degrees. Its diagonals are not only equal but also cross each other at a right angle.

4. Rhombus

  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly.

🎙️ A rhombus is another special type of parallelogram, but instead of all angles being 90 degrees, it has all sides equal. Think of it as a square that has been pushed over, so its angles are no longer 90 degrees, but its sides remain equal. The diagonals are very special here, intersecting at 90 degrees.

5. Trapezium (or Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.
  • Angles between parallel lines add up to 180°.

🎙️ A trapezium is less 'symmetrical' than parallelograms, but still very important. It has only one pair of parallel sides. Think of a road that narrows or widens; the two edges might be parallel for a stretch, but the side paths might not be. The parallel sides are called bases.

6. Kite

  • Two distinct pairs of equal adjacent sides.
  • One diagonal is the perpendicular bisector of the other.
  • One pair of opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals intersect at 90°.

🎙️ A kite is a quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides that are equal. Unlike a rhombus where all sides are equal, here only *adjacent* pairs are equal. Its diagonals are perpendicular to each other, and one diagonal perfectly bisects the other. It's named after the flying toy!

Worked Example: Finding Angles in a Parallelogram

  • Given a parallelogram ABCD, angle A = 60°.
  • Find angles B, C, and D.
  • Opposite angles are equal; adjacent angles sum to 180°.

🎙️ Let's work through an example. In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal, and adjacent angles are supplementary (add up to 180 degrees). So, if angle A is 60 degrees, angle C will also be 60 degrees. Angle B will be 180 minus 60, which is 120 degrees, and so angle D will also be 120 degrees.

1Angle C = Angle A = 60° (Opposite angles)
2Angle A + Angle B = 180° (Adjacent angles)
3Angle B = 180° - 60° = 120°
4Angle D = Angle B = 120° (Opposite angles)

Worked Example: Diagonals of a Rhombus

  • In rhombus PQRS, diagonals PR and QS intersect at O.
  • If PR = 10 cm and QS = 24 cm, find the side length PQ.
  • Diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly.

🎙️ Here's a problem involving the diagonals of a rhombus. Remember that diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other perpendicularly. This means they form right-angled triangles inside. We can then use the Pythagorean theorem to find the side length, which is the hypotenuse of these triangles.

1PO = PR/2 = 5 cm; QO = QS/2 = 12 cm
2Triangle POQ is a right-angled triangle at O.
3Using Pythagoras: PQ² = PO² + QO²
4PQ² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169
5PQ = √169 = 13 cm

Recap: Quadrilateral Family Tree

  • Quadrilateral: 4 sides, 4 angles, sum 360°.
  • Parallelogram: Opposite sides parallel & equal, opposite angles equal, diagonals bisect.
  • Rectangle: Parallelogram + all angles 90°, diagonals equal.
  • Rhombus: Parallelogram + all sides equal, diagonals perpendicular bisectors.

🎙️ To summarize, we started with the general quadrilateral and then explored its specific types. Remember the key properties that define each shape. A square is the most specific, inheriting properties from both rectangles and rhombuses. This hierarchy helps us understand their relationships.
