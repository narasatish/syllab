Whole Numbers (W) - Adding Zero!

  • Natural numbers combined with zero.
  • Starts from 0: {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}.
  • No fractions or decimals.

🎙️ Whole numbers are just like natural numbers, but with one important addition: zero. If you have no items, you represent that with zero. So, whole numbers include 0 and all the natural numbers.

Integers (Z) - Including Negatives!

  • Whole numbers and their negatives.
  • {..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...}.
  • No fractions or decimals.

🎙️ Integers expand our number system to include negative values. Think of them as whole numbers stretching in both positive and negative directions from zero. They are essential for representing concepts like debt or temperatures below zero.

Rational Numbers (Q) - Fractions and Decimals!

  • Numbers that can be written as p/q.
  • Where p and q are integers, and q ≠ 0.
  • Includes terminating and repeating decimals.

🎙️ Rational numbers are numbers that can be expressed as a fraction. This means they include all integers, as well as fractions and decimals that either terminate or repeat. The denominator 'q' can never be zero.

Representing Rational Numbers on the Number Line

  • Every rational number has a unique point.
  • Divide segments between integers.
  • Locate the fraction or decimal.

🎙️ Just like integers, rational numbers can also be precisely located on a number line. We simply divide the segments between integers into the appropriate number of parts, based on the denominator of the fraction.

Worked Example: Representing 3/4 and -1.5

  • For 3/4: Divide 0-1 into 4 parts, take 3rd.
  • For -1.5: Locate between -1 and -2, exactly in middle.

🎙️ Let's see how this works. To represent 3/4, we divide the segment between 0 and 1 into four equal parts and mark the third point. For -1.5, we go halfway between -1 and -2 on the negative side.

Worked Example: Find 3 Rational Numbers Between 1/2 and 3/4

  • Common denominator: 1/2 = 2/4, 3/4.
  • Convert to 8ths: 4/8 and 6/8. So 5/8 is one.
  • Convert to 16ths: 8/16 and 12/16. So 9/16, 10/16, 11/16 are three.

Irrational Numbers (I) - Beyond Fractions!

  • Numbers that CANNOT be written as p/q.
  • Non-terminating, non-repeating decimals.
  • Examples: √2, π, √3.

🎙️ Now we move to irrational numbers, which are fascinating because they cannot be expressed as simple fractions. Their decimal representations go on forever without repeating a pattern.

🎙️ Even though they seem abstract, irrational numbers appear naturally in geometry and nature. For example, the diagonal of a square with side length 1 is exactly √2, and Pi is essential for anything circular.

Representing Irrational Numbers (e.g., √2) on Number Line

  • Use Pythagorean theorem for construction.
  • For √2: construct a right triangle with legs of 1 unit.
  • The hypotenuse length is √2. Transfer to line.

🎙️ Representing irrational numbers like √2 on a number line requires a geometric construction. We use the Pythagorean theorem to create a length equal to √2 and then transfer it to the number line using a compass.

1Draw line
2Construct 1x1 triangle
3Hypotenuse is √2
4Arc to number line

Real Numbers (R) - The Complete Picture!

  • The set of all rational and irrational numbers.
  • Covers the entire number line without gaps.
  • Every point on the number line is a real number.

🎙️ Real numbers are the grand union of both rational and irrational numbers. They fill up the entire number line completely, meaning every single point on the line corresponds to a unique real number.
