Heron's Formula: The Magic Equation

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)].
  • 'a', 'b', 'c' are the lengths of the triangle's sides.
  • 's' is the semiperimeter of the triangle.

🎙️ Here it is! The formula might look a bit complex at first, but we'll break it down. It involves the square root of a product of four terms. The most important new term here is 's', the semiperimeter.

➕
The Formula

Understanding Semiperimeter (s)

  • 'Semi' means half.
  • 'Perimeter' is the total length of all sides.
  • Semiperimeter (s) = (a + b + c) / 2.

🎙️ Before we use the main formula, we need to understand 's'. Semiperimeter is simply half of the perimeter. You add up all three side lengths and then divide by two. It's a crucial first step in using Heron's Formula.

📏
Half Perimeter

Calculating Semiperimeter - Example

  • Triangle sides: a=5cm, b=6cm, c=7cm.
  • Perimeter = 5+6+7 = 18cm.
  • Semiperimeter (s) = 18 / 2 = 9cm.

🎙️ Let's try an example. If a triangle has sides of 5, 6, and 7 centimeters, its perimeter is 18 cm. Therefore, its semiperimeter 's' would be 9 cm. Easy, isn't it?

Applying Heron's Formula - Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Find 's' (semiperimeter).
  • Step 2: Calculate (s-a), (s-b), (s-c).
  • Step 3: Multiply s * (s-a) * (s-b) * (s-c).
  • Step 4: Find the square root of the product.

🎙️ Here's a simple process to follow. First, calculate 's'. Then, find the difference between 's' and each side length. Multiply these four values together, and finally, take the square root of the result.

Worked Example 1: Basic Triangle Area

  • Sides: a=13m, b=14m, c=15m.
  • s = (13+14+15)/2 = 21m.
  • Area = √[21(21-13)(21-14)(21-15)].
  • Area = √[21 × 8 × 7 × 6] = √7056 = 84 m².

🎙️ Let's apply the formula to a triangle with sides 13, 14, and 15 meters. First, we find 's' as 21 meters. Then, we substitute all values into Heron's formula and calculate the square root, which gives us 84 square meters.

Area of Equilateral Triangle using Heron's

  • Side 'a'. s = (a+a+a)/2 = 3a/2.
  • Area = √[ (3a/2)(a/2)(a/2)(a/2) ]
  • Area = √[ 3a⁴/16 ] = (a²/4)√3.

🎙️ For an equilateral triangle with side 'a', the semiperimeter is 3a/2. When you substitute this into Heron's formula and simplify, you actually derive the standard formula for an equilateral triangle's area: (a²/4)√3.

Area of Isosceles Triangle using Heron's

🎙️ Similarly, for an isosceles triangle with two equal sides 'a' and base 'b', you can use Heron's formula. Calculate 's', then find the three terms (s-a), (s-a), and (s-b) before substituting them into the main formula.

Extending to Quadrilaterals

  • Heron's formula is for triangles.
  • But we can find the area of quadrilaterals too!
  • Divide the quadrilateral into two triangles.
  • Find the area of each triangle and add them together.

🎙️ Heron's Formula is specifically for triangles, but we can extend its use. If you have a quadrilateral, you can always divide it into two triangles by drawing a diagonal. Then, you can apply Heron's formula to each triangle.

Worked Example 4: Area of a Quadrilateral

  • Quadrilateral ABCD: AB=7m, BC=6m, CD=12m, DA=15m, diagonal AC=10m.
  • ΔABC (sides 7,6,10): s=11.5m, Area ≈ 20.68 m².
  • ΔADC (sides 15,12,10): s=18.5m, Area ≈ 59.71 m².
  • Total Area = 20.68 + 59.71 = 80.39 m².

🎙️ When finding the area of a quadrilateral, first draw a diagonal to split it into two triangles. Then, calculate the area of each triangle using Heron's Formula. Finally, add the two areas to get the total area of the quadrilateral.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Heron's Formula: Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)].
  • 's' is the semiperimeter: (a+b+c)/2.
  • Works when only side lengths are known.
  • Can be used for quadrilaterals by dividing them.

🎙️ So, what have we learned today? Heron's Formula is a powerful tool to find a triangle's area when only its sides are known. Remember the two main parts: calculating 's' and then applying the main formula.
