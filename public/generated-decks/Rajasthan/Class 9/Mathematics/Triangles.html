What is a Triangle?

  • A closed figure with three sides.
  • Formed by three non-collinear points.
  • The simplest polygon.

🎙️ A triangle is one of the most fundamental shapes in geometry. It's simply a closed figure made up of three straight line segments. Think of it as connecting three dots that don't lie on a single straight line.

Parts of a Triangle

  • Three Vertices (corners).
  • Three Sides (line segments).
  • Three Interior Angles.

🎙️ Every triangle has three key components: the points where the sides meet are called vertices, the lines connecting them are the sides, and the space between the sides at each vertex forms an angle.

The Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

  • The sum of all three interior angles.
  • Always equals 180 degrees.
  • A fundamental property for all triangles.

🎙️ This is a cornerstone property of triangles. No matter what kind of triangle you have – acute, obtuse, or right-angled – if you add up the measures of its three interior angles, the total will always be exactly 180 degrees.

Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given two angles: 60° and 70°.
  • Let the third angle be x.
  • Equation: 60° + 70° + x = 180°.
  • Solve for x: x = 50°.

Exterior Angle Property

  • An exterior angle is formed by extending one side.
  • It equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • Provides another way to find angles.

🎙️ When you extend one side of a triangle, the angle formed outside is called an exterior angle. This exterior angle has a special relationship with the two interior angles that are not adjacent to it. It's equal to their sum.

Using the Exterior Angle Property

  • Exterior angle = 120°.
  • One interior opposite angle = 70°.
  • Let the other interior opposite angle be y.
  • Equation: 120° = 70° + y.
  • Solve for y: y = 50°.

🎙️ This property is very useful for quickly finding unknown angles without needing to find all interior angles first. If you know the exterior angle and one of the interior opposite angles, you can easily find the other.

What is Congruence?

  • Two figures are congruent if they are exactly the same.
  • Same shape and same size.
  • One can be placed exactly over the other to coincide.

🎙️ In geometry, 'congruent' means identical. Imagine two identical cookies – they are congruent. For triangles, it means not only do they look the same, but all their corresponding sides and angles are equal.

SSS Congruence (Side-Side-Side)

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal.
  • To the three corresponding sides of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.

🎙️ The SSS criterion is one of the ways to prove triangles are congruent. If you can show that all three sides of one triangle are exactly the same length as the corresponding three sides of another, then the triangles must be identical.

SAS Congruence (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If two sides and the included angle.
  • Of one triangle are equal to.
  • Two sides and the included angle of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ For SAS, the angle *must* be between the two sides. If you have two triangles where two sides and the angle *formed by those two sides* are equal to their counterparts in the other triangle, then they are congruent.

ASA Congruence (Angle-Side-Angle)

  • If two angles and the included side.
  • Of one triangle are equal to.
  • Two angles and the included side of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ Similar to SAS, for ASA, the side *must* be between the two angles. If you can match two angles and the side that connects their vertices in two triangles, then those triangles are congruent.

AAS Congruence (Angle-Angle-Side)

  • If two angles and a non-included side.
  • Of one triangle are equal to.
  • Two angles and the corresponding non-included side of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ AAS is very similar to ASA. The key difference is that the side is *not* between the two angles. If you have two angles and any corresponding non-included side equal, the triangles are congruent. This is often derived from ASA and the angle sum property.

RHS Congruence (Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side)

  • Specific for right-angled triangles only.
  • If the hypotenuse and one side.
  • Of a right triangle are equal to.
  • The hypotenuse and one side of another right triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ RHS is a special congruence rule that applies *only* to right-angled triangles. If the longest side (hypotenuse) and one of the other two sides are equal in two right triangles, then they are congruent.

Properties of an Isosceles Triangle

  • Two sides are equal in length.
  • Angles opposite to the equal sides are also equal.
  • The angle between the equal sides is the vertex angle.

🎙️ Isosceles triangles have a beautiful symmetry. Not only do they have two equal sides, but the angles directly opposite those equal sides are also equal. This property is very useful in solving problems.

Understanding ASA Theorem (Concept)

  • If two angles and the included side of one triangle.
  • Are equal to two angles and the included side of another.
  • Then the triangles are congruent (ASA).
  • This is a fundamental theorem for congruence.

🎙️ The ASA congruence criterion isn't just a rule; it's a theorem that can be proven. It essentially states that if you 'build' a triangle with specific angles and the side between them, there's only one unique triangle you can form.

Triangle Inequalities

  • The side opposite the larger angle is longer.
  • The side opposite the smaller angle is shorter.
  • The sum of any two sides is greater than the third side.

🎙️ Triangles also have interesting inequality properties. For example, if you have a triangle with a very wide angle, the side across from it will always be the longest. Also, you can't form a triangle if one side is too long compared to the other two.
