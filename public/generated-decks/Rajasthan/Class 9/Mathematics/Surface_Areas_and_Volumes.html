Understanding Surface Area

  • The total area of all the faces of a 3D object.
  • Imagine wrapping a gift – the paper covers the surface.
  • Measured in square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ Surface area is simply the sum of the areas of all the flat or curved surfaces that make up a 3D object. Think about painting a box; you'd need enough paint to cover its entire outer surface. That's surface area!

Understanding Volume

  • The amount of space occupied by a 3D object.
  • Think of filling a container with water or sand.
  • Measured in cubic units (e.g., cm³, m³).

🎙️ Volume, on the other hand, tells us how much space an object takes up or how much a container can hold. If you fill a water bottle, the amount of water inside represents its volume. It's about the 'inside' space.

Units for Surface Area and Volume

  • Surface Area: square units (cm², m², km²).
  • Volume: cubic units (cm³, m³, km³).
  • Remember: Area is 2D, Volume is 3D!

🎙️ It's crucial to use the correct units. Since surface area involves two dimensions (length and width), its units are squared. Volume involves three dimensions (length, width, and height), so its units are cubed. Don't mix them up!

  • Opposite faces are identical.
  • Examples: brick, matchbox, book.

🎙️ Our first shape is the cuboid. It's like a stretched cube. Think of a brick or a shoebox. It has length, breadth, and height, and all its faces are rectangles. Can you spot any cuboids around you?

Calculating Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • Formula: 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • l = length, b = breadth, h = height.
  • Sum of areas of all 6 rectangular faces.

🎙️ To find the total surface area of a cuboid, we sum the areas of its six faces. Since opposite faces are identical, we can calculate the area of three unique faces (length x breadth, breadth x height, length x height) and multiply their sum by two. This gives us the formula 2(lb + bh + hl).

1Area of Top/Bottom: lb
2Area of Front/Back: lh
3Area of Sides: bh
4Total: 2(lb+lh+bh)
9 / 25

Example: Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • A box has l=10cm, b=5cm, h=3cm.
  • SA = 2(10x5 + 5x3 + 3x10)
  • SA = 2(50 + 15 + 30) = 2(95) = 190 cm².

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. If we have a box with these dimensions, we substitute the values into the formula. First, calculate the areas of the pairs of faces, then add them up, and finally multiply by two to get the total surface area. Remember the units!

Calculating Surface Area of a Cube

  • Formula: 6a²
  • a = side length of the cube.
  • Since all 6 faces are squares of area a².

🎙️ Because all six faces of a cube are identical squares, and the area of one square face is side × side (a²), the total surface area is simply 6 times the area of one face. This gives us the concise formula 6a².

⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜
Six Square Faces

Example: Surface Area of a Cube

  • A cube has a side length of 4 cm.
  • SA = 6 × (4 cm)²
  • SA = 6 × 16 cm² = 96 cm².

🎙️ Let's try an example. If a cube has a side length of 4 cm, we square the side length to get the area of one face, which is 16 cm². Then, we multiply that by 6 to find the total surface area of the cube. Easy, isn't it?

Calculating Volume of a Cuboid

  • Formula: l × b × h
  • l = length, b = breadth, h = height.
  • Area of base multiplied by height.

🎙️ The volume of a cuboid is found by multiplying its length, breadth, and height. Imagine stacking layers of unit cubes to fill the cuboid; the number of cubes in the base layer (length × breadth) times the number of layers (height) gives you the total volume.

📦

Example: Volume of a Cuboid

  • A room is 5m long, 4m wide, 3m high.
  • Volume = 5m × 4m × 3m
  • Volume = 60 m³.

🎙️ If you want to know how much air is in your classroom, you'd calculate its volume. For a room with these dimensions, simply multiply the length, breadth, and height together. The result is 60 cubic meters, representing the space inside the room.

Calculating Volume of a Cube

  • Formula: a³
  • a = side length of the cube.
  • Since l=b=h=a, it's a x a x a.

🎙️ For a cube, since all its sides are equal (length = breadth = height = 'a'), the volume formula simplifies to 'a' multiplied by itself three times, or a³. This makes sense as it's a special case of the cuboid volume.

Example: Volume of a Cube

🎙️ Let's take a small sugar cube. If its side is 1 cm, then its volume is 1 cm cubed. This means it occupies a very tiny space. If you had a larger box with side 5 cm, its volume would be 125 cm³.

Meet the Cylinder

  • A 3D shape with two circular bases and a curved surface.
  • Bases are parallel and congruent.
  • Examples: cold drink can, water pipe, pillar.

🎙️ Next, we have the cylinder. Think of a cold drink can or a water pipe. It has two flat circular ends and a smooth, curved surface connecting them. The height of the cylinder is the distance between its two circular bases.

🥤
A Cold Drink Can

Curved Surface Area (CSA) of a Cylinder

  • Formula: 2πrh
  • r = radius of the base, h = height.
  • Area of the 'label' around the can.

🎙️ Imagine peeling off the label from a can. It forms a rectangle. The length of this rectangle is the circumference of the cylinder's base (2πr), and its width is the cylinder's height (h). So, the curved surface area is 2πrh.

1Unroll the curved part
2It forms a rectangle
3Length = 2πr (circumference)
4Width = h (height)
5Area = 2πrh
20 / 25

Total Surface Area (TSA) of a Cylinder

  • Formula: 2πr(r + h)
  • CSA + Area of two circular bases.
  • 2πrh + 2(πr²) = 2πr(h + r).

🎙️ The total surface area of a cylinder includes its curved surface area PLUS the areas of its two circular bases. Each circular base has an area of πr². So, we add 2πr² to the curved surface area (2πrh) to get the total surface area: 2πr(h + r).

Example: Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • A cylinder has r=7cm, h=10cm.
  • CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 10 = 440 cm².
  • TSA = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7+10) = 44 × 17 = 748 cm².

🎙️ Let's calculate for a cylinder. Using the value of π as 22/7, we can find the curved surface area first. Then, for the total surface area, we use the combined formula. Notice how the units are always squared for surface area.

Calculating Volume of a Cylinder

  • Formula: πr²h
  • r = radius of the base, h = height.
  • Area of base multiplied by height.

🎙️ Similar to a cuboid, the volume of a cylinder is the area of its base multiplied by its height. Since the base is a circle, its area is πr². So, the volume of a cylinder is πr²h. Think of filling a cylindrical glass with water.

Example: Volume of a Cylinder

  • A water pipe has r=3.5cm, h=20cm.
  • Volume = (22/7) × (3.5)² × 20
  • Volume = (22/7) × 12.25 × 20 = 770 cm³.

🎙️ Let's find the volume of a water pipe. We plug in the radius and height into the formula. Remember to square the radius first, then multiply by π and the height. The result, 770 cubic centimeters, tells us how much water the pipe can hold.
