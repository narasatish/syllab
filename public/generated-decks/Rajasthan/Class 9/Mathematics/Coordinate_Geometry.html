  • Positive numbers to the right.

Introducing the Cartesian Plane

  • Two number lines, perpendicular to each other.
  • Intersect at their zero points.
  • Forms a flat surface or 'plane'.

🎙️ Imagine taking two number lines and crossing them at their zero points, forming a perfect 'plus' sign. This flat surface created by these two lines is called the Cartesian Plane, named after René Descartes.

The X-axis (Horizontal Axis)

  • The horizontal number line.
  • Values increase to the right (positive).
  • Values decrease to the left (negative).
  • Also called the 'Abscissa axis'.

🎙️ The horizontal line is traditionally called the X-axis. Just like a number line, positive numbers are to the right of the center, and negative numbers are to the left. Remember this direction as we plot points.

The Y-axis (Vertical Axis)

  • The vertical number line.
  • Values increase upwards (positive).
  • Values decrease downwards (negative).
  • Also called the 'Ordinate axis'.

🎙️ The vertical line is the Y-axis. Positive numbers are above the center, and negative numbers are below. Think of it like a building's floors – positive for above ground, negative for basements.

The Origin (0,0)

  • The point where the X-axis and Y-axis intersect.
  • Represents the zero point for both axes.
  • Its coordinates are (0, 0).

🎙️ The point where the two axes cross is very important. It's called the Origin, and it's our starting reference point for all locations. It has zero value on both the X and Y axes.

Coordinates of a Point (x, y)

  • Every point on the plane has a unique pair of numbers.
  • These are called its 'coordinates'.
  • Always written as an 'ordered pair' (x, y).
  • The x-value comes first, then the y-value.

🎙️ Just like a house number and street name give a unique address, 'x' and 'y' coordinates give a unique location for any point on the Cartesian plane. The order matters a lot, so always remember (x, y).

Abscissa and Ordinate

  • The x-coordinate is called the 'Abscissa'.
  • The y-coordinate is called the 'Ordinate'.
  • Example: In (5, 3), 5 is the abscissa, 3 is the ordinate.

🎙️ These are specific terms you'll hear often. The abscissa tells you how far left or right a point is from the Y-axis, and the ordinate tells you how far up or down it is from the X-axis.

Dividing the Plane: Quadrants

  • The X and Y axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • Each region is called a 'Quadrant'.
  • Numbered counter-clockwise starting from the top-right.

🎙️ The two axes create four distinct areas on the plane. We call these quadrants. Understanding which quadrant a point lies in can tell us a lot about its coordinates, especially their signs.

🧭
Four Directions
12 / 25

Quadrant I (First Quadrant)

  • Top-right region.
  • Both x and y coordinates are positive.
  • Example: (3, 5), (10, 2).

🎙️ In the first quadrant, both your movements from the origin are in the positive direction – right for x, and up for y. So, any point here will have both its coordinates as positive numbers.

😊
All Positive!

Quadrant II (Second Quadrant)

  • Top-left region.
  • x-coordinate is negative, y-coordinate is positive.
  • Example: (-2, 4), (-7, 1).

🎙️ To reach the second quadrant, you move left (negative x) and then up (positive y). So, points in this quadrant will always have a negative x-coordinate and a positive y-coordinate.

🤔
Left then Up!

Quadrant III (Third Quadrant)

  • Bottom-left region.
  • Both x and y coordinates are negative.
  • Example: (-6, -8), (-1, -5).

🎙️ In the third quadrant, you move left (negative x) and then down (negative y). This means both coordinates for any point in this quadrant will be negative numbers.

Quadrant IV (Fourth Quadrant)

  • Bottom-right region.
  • x-coordinate is positive, y-coordinate is negative.
  • Example: (4, -3), (9, -6).

🎙️ For the fourth quadrant, you move right (positive x) and then down (negative y). So, points here will have a positive x-coordinate and a negative y-coordinate.

Plotting a Point: Step 1 (X-coordinate)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move horizontally along the X-axis.
  • Move right for positive x, left for negative x.

🎙️ To plot a point, always begin at the origin. The first number, the x-coordinate, tells you how many units to move horizontally. Remember, right is positive, left is negative.

Plotting a Point: Step 2 (Y-coordinate)

  • From your X-position, move vertically.
  • Move up for positive y, down for negative y.
  • Mark the final position.

🎙️ Once you've moved to the correct x-position, the second number, the y-coordinate, tells you how many units to move vertically from that spot. Up is positive, down is negative. This is your point!

Worked Example 1: Plotting (3, 2)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 3 units right on the X-axis.
  • From there, move 2 units up parallel to the Y-axis.
  • Mark the point.

🎙️ Let's plot our first point. For (3, 2), both numbers are positive. So, we go 3 steps right, and then 2 steps up. This point lies in the First Quadrant.

Points on the X-axis

  • If a point lies on the X-axis, its y-coordinate is always 0.
  • It has the form (x, 0).
  • Example: (5, 0), (-3, 0).

🎙️ What if a point is exactly on one of the axes? If it's on the X-axis, it means you don't move up or down at all. So, its y-coordinate will always be zero.

↔️

Points on the Y-axis

  • If a point lies on the Y-axis, its x-coordinate is always 0.
  • It has the form (0, y).
  • Example: (0, 4), (0, -2).

🎙️ Similarly, if a point is on the Y-axis, it means you don't move left or right from the origin. Therefore, its x-coordinate will always be zero. The origin (0,0) is on both axes.
