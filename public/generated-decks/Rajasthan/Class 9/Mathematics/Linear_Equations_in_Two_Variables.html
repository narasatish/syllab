Definition: Linear Equation in Two Variables

  • An equation with two variables
  • Highest power of each variable is 1
  • No product of variables (like xy)

🎙️ A linear equation in two variables describes a direct relationship between two changing quantities. The 'linear' part means when you graph it, you'll get a straight line.

General Form: ax + by + c = 0

  • a, b, c are real numbers
  • a and b are NOT both zero
  • x and y are the variables

🎙️ This is the standard way we write these equations. 'a' and 'b' are coefficients of x and y, and 'c' is the constant term. It's important that 'a' and 'b' aren't both zero, otherwise, it wouldn't be a two-variable equation!

Solutions of an Equation

  • A pair of values (x, y)
  • Makes the equation true

🎙️ A solution isn't just one number; it's a pair of numbers, one for 'x' and one for 'y', that when substituted into the equation, makes both sides equal. It's like finding the right key for a lock.

Worked Example: Finding Solutions for 2x + y = 6

  • If x=0, then 2(0) + y = 6 => y = 6. Solution: (0, 6)
  • If y=0, then 2x + 0 = 6 => x = 3. Solution: (3, 0)
  • If x=1, then 2(1) + y = 6 => y = 4. Solution: (1, 4)

🎙️ Notice how each pair (x, y) makes the equation true. We can continue this process to find as many solutions as we need.

1Assume x=0
2Find y
3Assume y=0
4Find x

Real-Life Example: Distance & Time

  • A car travels at a constant speed of 60 km/hr
  • Let distance be 'd' km and time be 't' hours
  • Equation: d = 60t

🎙️ Here, 'd' and 't' are our two variables. If the car travels for 2 hours, d = 60 * 2 = 120 km. So (2, 120) is a solution. This equation is also a linear equation in two variables.

Graph of a Linear Equation

  • Every solution (x, y) is a point on the graph
  • All points on the graph are solutions
  • The graph is always a straight line

🎙️ Plotting these solutions on a coordinate plane reveals something amazing: they all line up! This is why they are called 'linear' equations.

Steps to Plot a Graph

  • 1. Find at least two solutions (x, y)
  • 2. Plot these points on a Cartesian plane
  • 3. Draw a straight line passing through them

🎙️ While two points are enough to draw a line, finding a third point can help you check for accuracy. If all three points don't lie on the same line, you've made a calculation error.

Special Cases: Parallel to Axes

  • Equation x = a: A vertical line parallel to the y-axis
  • Equation y = b: A horizontal line parallel to the x-axis

🎙️ These are special types of linear equations. For x=a, no matter what y is, x is always 'a'. Similarly for y=b. They are still linear equations in two variables, just with one coefficient being zero (e.g., x=3 is 1x + 0y - 3 = 0).
