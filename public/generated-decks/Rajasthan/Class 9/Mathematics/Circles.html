What is a Circle?

  • A circle is a set of all points in a plane.
  • These points are at a fixed distance from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the centre.

🎙️ Imagine a point on a paper. Now, take a compass and draw a curve keeping the compass point fixed. That curve is a circle! Every point on that curve is exactly the same distance from the fixed point in the middle.

The Centre of a Circle

  • It is the fixed point inside the circle.
  • All points on the circle are equidistant from the centre.
  • Usually denoted by 'O'.

🎙️ The most important point in a circle is its centre. It's like the heart of the circle. Every single point on the boundary of the circle is the same distance away from this central point.

Understanding the Radius

  • A line segment connecting the centre to any point on the circle.
  • It is the fixed distance from the centre to the circle.
  • All radii of a circle are equal in length.

🎙️ The radius is a line segment that starts from the centre and touches any point on the circle's boundary. It defines the 'size' of the circle. If you move the end point along the circle, the length of the radius remains constant.

Introducing the Diameter

  • A line segment passing through the centre.
  • Connects two points on the circle.
  • It is twice the length of the radius (D = 2r).

🎙️ The diameter is the longest chord in a circle. It always passes through the centre and connects two points on the circle's edge. Remember, the diameter is always double the length of the radius.

What is a Chord?

  • A line segment connecting any two points on the circle.
  • Does not necessarily pass through the centre.
  • The diameter is the longest chord.

🎙️ A chord is any straight line segment that connects two points on the circle. Think of it like a shortcut across the circle. The diameter is a special type of chord because it passes right through the centre.

Exploring an Arc

  • A continuous piece of a circle.
  • It's a part of the circumference.
  • Can be a minor arc (smaller) or a major arc (larger).

🎙️ An arc is simply a curved portion of the circle's boundary. Imagine cutting a piece out of a circular wire – that piece is an arc. We classify them as minor or major depending on their length.

The Circumference

  • The perimeter or boundary of a circle.
  • It's the total distance around the circle.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πD.

🎙️ The circumference is the total distance you would travel if you walked all the way around the circle. It's like the perimeter of a square, but for a circle. We use the constant 'pi' (π) to calculate it.

Interior and Exterior of a Circle

  • Interior: The region inside the circle.
  • Exterior: The region outside the circle.
  • On the circle: Points lying on the boundary.

🎙️ A circle divides the plane into three regions: the points inside the circle, the points outside the circle, and the points that are exactly on the circle's boundary. This helps us define different parts of the circle.

Understanding a Segment

  • The region bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • A chord divides a circle into two segments.
  • Minor segment (smaller) and Major segment (larger).

🎙️ When you draw a chord across a circle, it cuts the circle into two parts. Each of these parts is called a segment. Think of cutting a round pizza with a straight line – you get two segments.

Introducing a Sector

  • The region bounded by two radii and an arc.
  • Like a slice of pizza.
  • Minor sector (smaller) and Major sector (larger).

🎙️ A sector is formed by two radii and the arc between their endpoints. This is exactly like a slice of pizza or pie. The two radii meet at the centre, forming an angle.

Congruent Circles

  • Two circles are congruent if they have the same radius.
  • They are exact copies of each other.
  • Can be perfectly superimposed.

🎙️ Congruent circles are like identical twins. If two circles have the exact same radius, it means they are the same size and shape. You could place one directly on top of the other, and they would match perfectly.

Theorem: Equal Chords & Distances

  • Equal chords of a circle are equidistant from the centre.
  • If chord AB = chord CD, then distance from O to AB = distance from O to CD.
  • This means perpendicular distances.

🎙️ This theorem tells us something important about chords. If two chords within the same circle have the same length, then their perpendicular distances from the centre of the circle will also be equal. It's a fundamental property.

⚖️

  • Answer: 4 cm, based on the theorem.

Theorem: Perpendicular from Centre

  • The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • If OM ⊥ AB, then AM = MB.
  • This means it divides the chord into two equal parts.

🎙️ This theorem is very useful for calculations. If you draw a line from the centre that hits a chord at a 90-degree angle, that line will always cut the chord into two perfectly equal halves. It's a powerful tool.

✂️

Theorem: Chords Equidistant

  • Chords equidistant from the centre of a circle are equal in length.
  • This is the converse of Theorem 1.
  • If distance from O to AB = distance from O to CD, then chord AB = chord CD.

🎙️ This theorem is the opposite of the first one we discussed. If you find two chords that are exactly the same distance away from the centre of the circle, then you can be sure that those two chords must have the same length. It's a direct relationship.

↔️

Angle at the Centre

  • The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

🎙️ This is a very important theorem about angles in a circle. Imagine an arc. The angle it forms at the very centre of the circle is always twice as large as the angle it forms at any other point on the circle's boundary, as long as that point is not on the arc itself.

📐
Double the Angle

Angle on the Remaining Part

  • Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
  • This is a direct consequence of the previous theorem.
  • If an arc subtends angles at two different points in the same segment, those angles are equal.

🎙️ Following from the previous theorem, if you have an arc and pick any two points on the rest of the circle, the angles that the arc makes at those two points will always be identical. They 'see' the arc from the same perspective within that segment.

👥
Equal Views

Example: Angles in a Circle

  • In a circle, arc AB subtends ∠AOB = 80° at the centre.
  • Find the angle ∠ACB subtended by the same arc at point C on the remaining part.
  • Answer: ∠ACB = 80° / 2 = 40°.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on a circle.
  • The sum of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is 180°.
  • Example: A square or rectangle inscribed in a circle.

🎙️ A cyclic quadrilateral is a special type of four-sided shape where all its corners touch the circle's boundary. A key property is that its opposite angles always add up to 180 degrees. This is a very useful property for solving problems.
