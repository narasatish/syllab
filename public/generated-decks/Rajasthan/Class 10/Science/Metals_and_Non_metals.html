What are Elements?

  • Pure substances that cannot be broken down further
  • Building blocks of all matter
  • Each element has unique properties

🎙️ An element is the simplest form of matter. Think of them as the basic bricks that make up everything in the universe. There are over 118 known elements, each with its own special characteristics.

Classifying Elements: Metals, Non-metals, Metalloids

  • Elements are grouped based on their properties
  • Main categories: Metals and Non-metals
  • Some elements show properties of both: Metalloids

🎙️ To make sense of the many elements, scientists classify them. The two largest groups are metals and non-metals, which we'll focus on today. Metalloids are like a bridge, sharing traits from both sides.

Physical Properties of Metals: Luster & Hardness

  • Metals generally have a shiny surface (metallic luster)
  • They are typically hard, but some are soft
  • Example: Gold (shiny) and Iron (hard)

🎙️ One of the first things you notice about most metals is their shine, like a polished piece of gold or silver. They also tend to be tough and resistant to scratching, although exceptions like sodium exist.

Physical Properties of Metals: Malleability & Ductility

  • Malleability: Can be hammered into thin sheets
  • Ductility: Can be drawn into thin wires
  • Example: Aluminium foil (malleable) and Copper wire (ductile)

🎙️ Metals are incredibly versatile! Their malleability allows us to make things like cooking foil, while their ductility means we can stretch them into long, thin wires for electrical circuits. These properties make metals very useful.

🔨
Shape & Stretch
7 / 25

Physical Properties of Metals: Heat & Electrical Conductors

  • Good conductors of heat (thermal conductivity)
  • Excellent conductors of electricity (electrical conductivity)
  • Example: Copper in electrical wires, Aluminium in cooking utensils

🎙️ Metals are champions at moving energy. They quickly transfer heat, which is why pots and pans are made of metal. They also allow electricity to flow freely, making them essential for all our electronic devices.

⚡
Flow of Energy

Physical Properties of Metals: Sonorous & High M.P./B.P.

  • Sonorous: Produce a ringing sound when struck
  • High Melting Point (M.P.) and Boiling Point (B.P.)
  • Example: Temple bells (sonorous), Iron (high M.P.)

🎙️ Have you ever heard the clear ringing sound of a bell? That's because metals are sonorous. Also, most metals require a lot of heat to melt or boil, meaning they are strong and stable at high temperatures.

Physical Properties of Non-metals: Luster & Hardness

  • Generally dull in appearance (no metallic luster)
  • Mostly soft and brittle, easily breakable
  • Exception: Iodine (lustrous), Diamond (hardest substance)

🎙️ Unlike metals, non-metals usually lack a shiny surface and often appear dull. They are also typically soft and tend to break easily, like a piece of charcoal. Remember the rare exceptions like diamond's hardness!

Physical Properties of Non-metals: Brittle & Non-Ductile

  • Brittle: Break down into powder when hammered
  • Non-Ductile: Cannot be drawn into wires
  • Example: Sulfur powder (brittle), Carbon (cannot be drawn)

🎙️ Imagine trying to hammer a piece of coal into a sheet; it would just crumble. Non-metals are brittle, meaning they shatter when force is applied. They also cannot be stretched into wires, unlike metals.

💔
Breaks Easily
12 / 25

Physical Properties of Non-metals: Bad Conductors

  • Poor conductors of heat (thermal insulators)
  • Poor conductors of electricity (electrical insulators)
  • Exception: Graphite (form of carbon) conducts electricity

🎙️ Non-metals are generally the opposite of metals when it comes to conductivity. They don't easily allow heat or electricity to pass through them. This is why plastic handles are used on metal pots and pans.

🚫
No Flow
13 / 25

Physical Properties of Non-metals: Not Sonorous & Low M.P./B.P.

  • Not Sonorous: Do not produce a ringing sound when struck
  • Low Melting Point (M.P.) and Boiling Point (B.P.)
  • Example: Oxygen (gas at room temp), Sulfur (low M.P.)

🎙️ If you strike a non-metal, you won't hear a clear ringing sound; it'll likely just make a dull thud or break. Most non-metals also have relatively low melting and boiling points, often existing as gases or liquids at room temperature.

Non-metals Around Us

Chemical Properties of Metals: Reaction with Oxygen

  • Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides
  • Metal oxides are generally basic in nature
  • Example: Magnesium burns in air to form Magnesium Oxide (MgO)

🎙️ When metals come into contact with oxygen, especially when heated, they form metal oxides. These oxides often have basic properties, meaning they can react with acids. This is how rust forms on iron!

🔥
Oxidation
17 / 25

Chemical Properties of Metals: Reaction with Water

  • Metals react with water (cold/hot/steam) to form metal hydroxides/oxides
  • Hydrogen gas is often produced in these reactions
  • Example: Sodium reacts vigorously with cold water

🎙️ The reactivity of metals with water varies greatly. Some, like sodium, react violently even with cold water, while others, like iron, only react with steam at high temperatures. This reaction often releases hydrogen gas.

💧
Water Reaction
18 / 25

Chemical Properties of Metals: Reaction with Acids

  • Metals react with dilute acids to form a salt and hydrogen gas
  • More reactive metals react more vigorously
  • Example: Zinc reacts with dilute HCl to form Zinc Chloride and H₂ gas

🎙️ Many metals react with dilute acids to produce a salt and hydrogen gas. You might see bubbles forming, indicating the release of hydrogen. The speed of this reaction depends on how reactive the metal is.

🧪
Acid Attack

Chemical Properties of Non-metals: Reaction with Oxygen

  • Non-metals react with oxygen to form non-metal oxides
  • Non-metal oxides are generally acidic or neutral in nature
  • Example: Carbon burns in air to form Carbon Dioxide (CO₂), an acidic oxide

🎙️ Similar to metals, non-metals also react with oxygen, but they form non-metal oxides. These oxides often dissolve in water to form acids, like carbon dioxide forming carbonic acid in rainwater.

Gas Formation

Chemical Properties of Non-metals: Water & Acids

  • Non-metals generally do not react with water
  • Non-metals generally do not react with dilute acids
  • This is a key difference from metals

🎙️ Here's a major distinction: non-metals typically do not react with water or dilute acids. This is because they tend to gain electrons, and neither water nor dilute acids can provide them with the necessary electrons for a reaction.

The Reactivity Series of Metals

  • Arrangement of metals in decreasing order of their reactivity
  • More reactive metals can displace less reactive ones
  • Helps predict reaction outcomes

🎙️ The reactivity series is like a pecking order for metals. It tells us which metals are more eager to react than others. Metals higher in the series are more reactive and can 'push out' less reactive metals from their compounds.

Displacement Reactions

  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal
  • The less reactive metal is 'kicked out' of its compound
  • Example: Iron displaces copper from copper sulfate solution

🎙️ Displacement reactions are a direct application of the reactivity series. If you put a more reactive metal into a solution containing a less reactive metal's salt, the more reactive metal will take its place, forming a new compound.

Chapter Recap: Metals and Non-metals

  • Metals are generally shiny, hard, malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors.
  • Non-metals are generally dull, brittle, non-ductile, non-sonorous, poor conductors.
  • Chemical properties show distinct reactions with oxygen, water, and acids.
  • Reactivity series explains displacement reactions.
