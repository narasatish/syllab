What are Magnets?

  • Substances that attract iron and steel objects.
  • Have two poles: North (N) and South (S).
  • Like poles repel, unlike poles attract.

🎙️ You've all played with magnets, haven't you? They are special materials that can pull certain metals towards them. Remember, just like charges, magnet poles behave in opposite ways.

Magnetic Field

  • It's an invisible field, but its effects are visible.
  • It has both magnitude and direction.

🎙️ Just like a gravitational field around Earth, a magnetic field exists around every magnet. If you place a small iron filing in this region, it will experience a force and move towards the magnet.

Magnetic Field Lines

  • Imaginary lines representing the direction of the magnetic field.
  • Emerge from North pole, enter South pole (outside magnet).
  • Do not intersect each other.

🎙️ We use magnetic field lines to visualize the magnetic field. They show us the path a tiny North pole would take if placed in the field. The closer the lines, the stronger the field.

Magnetic Field due to a Straight Current-Carrying Conductor

  • Hans Christian Oersted discovered this effect.
  • A compass needle deflects when placed near a current-carrying wire.
  • Field lines are concentric circles around the wire.

🎙️ This was a groundbreaking discovery! Oersted accidentally found that an electric current produces a magnetic field around it. Imagine a straight wire, and around it, invisible magnetic circles are formed.

Right-Hand Thumb Rule

  • Helps determine the direction of the magnetic field.
  • Imagine holding the wire with your right hand.
  • Thumb points in current direction, fingers curl in field direction.

🎙️ This is a very important rule to remember! If your thumb points where the current goes, your curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field lines around the wire. Try it with your hand!

👍

Real-life Example: Magnetic Compass

  • A compass needle is a small magnet.
  • It aligns with the Earth's magnetic field.
  • Near a current-carrying wire, it aligns with the wire's field.

🎙️ When you bring a compass near a current-carrying wire, its needle will deflect. This shows that the wire is creating its own magnetic field, strong enough to influence the compass needle.

Magnetic Field due to a Circular Loop

  • Field lines are concentric circles near the wire.
  • At the center, lines are nearly straight and perpendicular to the loop.
  • Each segment of the loop contributes to the field.

🎙️ If you bend a straight wire into a circular loop, the magnetic field pattern changes. Near the wire, it's still circular, but at the very center of the loop, the field lines become almost straight.

Magnetic Field due to a Solenoid

  • A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire.
  • Behaves like a bar magnet when current passes through it.
  • Field inside is uniform, strong, and parallel to the axis.

🎙️ Imagine many circular loops stacked together. That's a solenoid! When current flows through it, it acts exactly like a bar magnet, with clear North and South poles, and a strong, uniform field inside.

🌀

Electromagnet

  • A temporary magnet formed by placing a soft iron core inside a solenoid.
  • Magnetic strength can be varied by changing current or turns.
  • Used in cranes, electric bells, medical imaging (MRI).

🎙️ Electromagnets are incredibly useful because you can switch their magnetism on and off, and even control its strength. They are essential in many modern technologies, from tiny doorbells to huge industrial cranes.

🎙️ The electric doorbell at your home uses an electromagnet! When you press the button, current flows, the electromagnet pulls a hammer, which strikes the gong, and you hear the ring. Simple yet brilliant!

Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor in a Magnetic Field

  • A current-carrying conductor experiences a force.
  • This force occurs when placed in an external magnetic field.
  • The direction of force depends on current direction and field direction.

🎙️ So far, we've seen current creating a magnetic field. Now, let's reverse it! If you put a wire with current in an existing magnetic field, the wire itself will experience a push or pull, a force.

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

  • Used to find the direction of force on a conductor.
  • Thumb: Direction of Force (Motion).
  • Forefinger: Direction of Magnetic Field.
  • Middle finger: Direction of Current.

🎙️ This rule helps us predict the direction of the force. Stretch your left hand's thumb, forefinger, and middle finger mutually perpendicular. Each finger points to one of the three directions: force, field, or current.

Electric Motor: Construction and Working

  • Armature coil rotates between strong magnet poles.
  • Split ring (commutator) reverses current direction every half turn.
  • Continuous rotation due to continuous force.

🎙️ An electric motor has a coil that spins in a magnetic field. The commutator is crucial; it ensures the force is always in the direction that keeps the coil rotating, making it useful for continuous work.

Worked Example: Force Direction

  • A current flows north in a wire.
  • It is placed in a magnetic field pointing east.
  • Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the force on the wire will be downwards.

🎙️ Let's apply the rule. Point your forefinger (Field) East, middle finger (Current) North. Your thumb (Force) will point downwards. This is how engineers design motors to get the desired motion.

Real-life Example: Electric Generator Principle

  • Works on electromagnetic induction.
  • Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.
  • Rotates a coil in a magnetic field to induce current.

🎙️ The power plants that supply electricity to our homes use generators. They spin huge coils within strong magnetic fields, inducing a current that is then transmitted to us. It's the inverse of a motor!

Electric Generator: Construction and Working

  • A coil rotates in a magnetic field (mechanical energy).
  • Magnetic flux changes, inducing current in the coil.
  • Slip rings (AC) or commutator (DC) collect the current.

🎙️ In a generator, you provide the mechanical energy to rotate the coil. This rotation causes a change in the magnetic field lines passing through the coil, which in turn generates an electric current. This is how we get AC or DC electricity.

Quick Question 1

  • The direction of magnetic field lines inside a solenoid is:
  • a) From North to South pole
  • b) From South to North pole
  • c) Perpendicular to the axis
  • d) Circular

🎙️ Think about how a solenoid acts like a bar magnet. Inside a bar magnet, the field lines move from the South pole to the North pole. So, the correct answer is 'b'.
