What is Surface Area?

  • The total area of all the faces of a 3D object.
  • Think of it as the 'skin' or outer covering.
  • Measured in square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ Imagine you want to paint a gift box. The amount of paint you need depends on the surface area of the box. It's the total area you can touch on the outside of the shape.

What is Volume?

  • The amount of space a 3D object occupies.
  • Think of it as the 'holding capacity' inside.
  • Measured in cubic units (e.g., cm³, m³).

🎙️ Now, if you want to fill that same gift box with small candies, the number of candies it can hold depends on its volume. Volume tells us how much 'stuff' can fit inside a shape.

Cuboid: Surface Area and Volume

  • Faces: 6 rectangles, Edges: 12, Vertices: 8.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • Volume = l × b × h

🎙️ A cuboid is like a brick or a shoebox. Remember 'l' for length, 'b' for breadth, and 'h' for height. The TSA formula covers all six rectangular faces, and the volume is simply multiplying its three dimensions.

Example: Painting a Cuboid Box

  • A box is 2m long, 1m wide, and 0.5m high.
  • Find the area to be painted (TSA).
  • TSA = 2(2x1 + 1x0.5 + 0.5x2) = 2(2 + 0.5 + 1) = 2(3.5) = 7 m².

🎙️ Here, we apply the TSA formula. Substitute the given dimensions (length, breadth, height) into the formula. The result, 7 square meters, tells us the total area that needs to be painted on the box.

Cube: Surface Area and Volume

  • A cuboid where all sides are equal (l=b=h=a).
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a²
  • Volume = a³

🎙️ A cube is a special type of cuboid where all its edges are of equal length, 'a'. Think of a dice or an ice cube. Since all 6 faces are identical squares, the TSA is 6 times the area of one square face.

Example: Filling a Cubic Tank

  • A cubic water tank has a side length of 2m.
  • Find its volume (capacity).
  • Volume = a³ = (2)³ = 8 m³.

🎙️ For this cubic tank, with each side measuring 2 meters, we simply cube the side length to find its volume. 8 cubic meters means it can hold 8000 liters of water (since 1 m³ = 1000 liters).

Cylinder: Surface Area and Volume

  • Has two circular bases and a curved surface.
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr(r + h)
  • Volume = πr²h

🎙️ A cylinder is like a tin can or a water pipe. 'r' is the radius of the base, and 'h' is the height. The CSA is the area of the curved part, and TSA adds the areas of the two circular bases.

🥫

Example: Water in a Cylindrical Tank

  • A cylindrical tank has radius 7m and height 10m.
  • Find its volume (capacity).
  • Volume = πr²h = (22/7) × 7² × 10 = 22 × 7 × 10 = 1540 m³.

🎙️ This problem asks for the volume of a cylindrical tank. We use the formula πr²h, substituting the given radius and height. The tank can hold 1540 cubic meters of water, which is a very large amount!

Cone: Surface Area and Volume

  • Has a circular base and a pointed top (vertex).
  • Slant height (l) = √(r² + h²)
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = πrl
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = πr(l + r)
  • Volume = (1/3)πr²h

🎙️ A cone is like an ice cream cone or a birthday cap. 'r' is the base radius, 'h' is the vertical height, and 'l' is the slant height. Notice the volume is one-third of a cylinder with the same base and height.

Example: Canvas for a Conical Tent

  • A conical tent has base radius 7m and height 24m.
  • Find the area of canvas needed (CSA).
  • l = √(7² + 24²) = √(49 + 576) = √625 = 25m.
  • CSA = πrl = (22/7) × 7 × 25 = 550 m².

🎙️ To find the canvas needed, we need the curved surface area. First, calculate the slant height 'l' using Pythagoras theorem. Then, substitute 'r' and 'l' into the CSA formula to get the required area.

Sphere & Hemisphere: Surface Area and Volume

  • Sphere: A perfectly round 3D object.
  • Sphere SA = 4πr², Volume = (4/3)πr³.
  • Hemisphere: Half of a sphere.
  • Hemisphere CSA = 2πr², TSA = 3πr², Volume = (2/3)πr³.

🎙️ A sphere is like a football or a globe, defined by its radius 'r'. A hemisphere is half a sphere, like a bowl. Note that the TSA of a hemisphere includes its circular base.

Example: Capacity of a Hemispherical Bowl

  • A hemispherical bowl has an inner radius of 3.5 cm.
  • Find the volume of liquid it can hold.
  • Volume = (2/3)πr³ = (2/3) × (22/7) × (3.5)³ ≈ 89.83 cm³.

🎙️ For this hemispherical bowl, we use the volume formula for a hemisphere. Substitute the radius 3.5 cm into the formula. The result tells us how much liquid the bowl can contain.

Frustum of a Cone: Introduction

  • Formed when a cone is cut by a plane parallel to its base.
  • The part remaining after removing the smaller cone.
  • Common examples: buckets, drinking glasses.

🎙️ Imagine a full cone. If you slice off its top part with a knife parallel to the base, the remaining lower part is called a frustum. It has two circular bases of different radii.

Frustum: SA and Volume Formulas

  • Let R and r be radii, h be height, l be slant height.
  • Slant height (l) = √[h² + (R-r)²]
  • CSA = πl(R + r)
  • TSA = πl(R + r) + πR² + πr²
  • Volume = (1/3)πh(R² + r² + Rr)

🎙️ These formulas might look complex, but they are derived from the cone formulas. 'R' is the radius of the larger base, and 'r' is the radius of the smaller base. 'h' is the vertical height.

Example: Volume of a Frustum Bucket

  • A bucket is a frustum with R=28cm, r=7cm, h=45cm.
  • Find its capacity (Volume).
  • Volume = (1/3)πh(R² + r² + Rr) = 48510 cm³.

🎙️ This example shows how to calculate the capacity of a bucket, which is a frustum. Carefully substitute the given radii and height into the volume formula. The result is its capacity in cubic centimeters.
