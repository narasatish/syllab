What is an Arithmetic Progression (AP)?

  • A sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant.
  • This constant difference is called the 'common difference'.
  • Example: 5, 10, 15, 20, ...

🎙️ An Arithmetic Progression, or AP, is a special kind of sequence. The key feature is that when you subtract any term from its next term, you always get the same number. This number is very important!

Key Terms of an AP

  • First term (a): The starting number of the AP.
  • Common difference (d): The constant difference between consecutive terms.

🎙️ To work with APs, we use two main terms. 'a' represents the very first number in our sequence. 'd' is the magic number that tells us how much we add (or subtract) to get from one term to the next. Remember these!

Finding the Next Term

  • To find any term, add the common difference to the previous term.
  • If the previous term is `a_n` and common difference is `d`, the next term is `a_n + d`.

🎙️ This is the simplest rule! Once you know the common difference 'd', you can extend your AP indefinitely. Just keep adding 'd' to the last term you found. Let's try an example together.

General Form of an AP

  • An AP can be written as: a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, ...
  • Here 'a' is the first term and 'd' is the common difference.

🎙️ This form helps us see the structure of any AP. Notice how the 'd' term increases by one for each successive term. The first term has 0 'd's, the second has 1 'd', the third has 2 'd's, and so on.

The nth Term Formula

  • To find any specific term (the 'nth' term) without listing all terms.
  • Formula: `a_n = a + (n-1)d`
  • Where `a_n` is the nth term, `a` is the first term, `n` is the term number, `d` is the common difference.

🎙️ This is one of the most powerful formulas in AP! Imagine if you needed to find the 100th term of an AP. You wouldn't want to list them all out! This formula lets you jump directly to any term you need.

Example: Finding the 10th Term

  • AP: 2, 7, 12, ...
  • Find the 10th term.

🎙️ Let's apply our new formula! Here, `a = 2`, `d = 7 - 2 = 5`. We want the 10th term, so `n = 10`. Substitute these values into the formula and solve. You'll find that `a_10 = 2 + (10-1)5 = 2 + 9*5 = 2 + 45 = 47`.

2Apply formula a_n = a + (n-1)d
3Calculate a_10

Example: Finding the Number of Terms

  • AP: 3, 7, 11, ..., 47
  • How many terms are there in this AP?

🎙️ Sometimes, we know the first term, the common difference, and the last term, but we don't know how many terms are in the sequence. We can use the same formula! Here `a=3`, `d=4`, and `a_n=47`. Solve `47 = 3 + (n-1)4` to find `n`.

Is it an AP?

  • Check if the difference between consecutive terms is constant.
  • Calculate `a2 - a1`, `a3 - a2`, etc.
  • If all differences are the same, it's an AP.

🎙️ Before you apply any AP formulas, always confirm if the given sequence is actually an AP! Just pick any two consecutive terms, subtract the first from the second. Do this for a few pairs. If the result is always the same, you have an AP.

Real-Life AP: Savings Plan

  • You save ₹100 in the first month.
  • Each subsequent month, you save ₹50 more than the previous month.
  • How much will you save in the 12th month?

🎙️ This is a great example of an AP. Your first term 'a' is ₹100. Your common difference 'd' is ₹50. You want to find the amount saved in the 12th month, so 'n' is 12. Use `a_n = a + (n-1)d` to find `a_12`.

How to Find the Sum (Sn)?

  • Legendary mathematician Gauss found a clever way!
  • He added the series forwards and backwards.
  • This method leads to a powerful formula.

🎙️ The story goes that young Gauss quickly summed the numbers from 1 to 100 by pairing them up (1+100, 2+99, etc.). This brilliant idea forms the basis for our sum formula. It's about finding symmetry in the sequence.

The Sum Formula for an AP

  • Formula 1: `S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]`
  • Formula 2 (if last term `a_n` is known): `S_n = n/2 [a + a_n]`
  • Where `S_n` is the sum of `n` terms.

🎙️ Here are the two main formulas for finding the sum of an AP. Choose the first one if you know 'a' and 'd', and the second one if you know the first term 'a' and the last term 'a_n'. Both will give you the correct sum!

Example: Sum of First 10 Terms

  • AP: 2, 4, 6, 8, ...
  • Find the sum of the first 10 terms.

🎙️ Let's put the sum formula into practice. Here `a=2`, `d=2`, and `n=10`. Plug these values into the first sum formula. `S_10 = 10/2 [2*2 + (10-1)2] = 5 [4 + 9*2] = 5 [4 + 18] = 5 * 22 = 110`.

1Identify a, d, n
2Apply S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
3Calculate S_10
