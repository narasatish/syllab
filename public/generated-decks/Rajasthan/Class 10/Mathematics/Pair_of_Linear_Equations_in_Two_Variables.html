What is a Linear Equation?

  • Variables have a power of 1.
  • No x², y³ terms.
  • Forms a straight line when graphed.

🎙️ A linear equation is special because its variables only have a power of one. This means when you plot it on a graph, it always forms a straight line. Think of a ruler, it's straight!

🎙️ A solution is like the correct answer to a puzzle. It's a specific pair of numbers for x and y that perfectly fit into the equation. For a single linear equation, there are actually countless solutions!

Graphical Method - Visualizing Solutions

  • Each equation forms a straight line.
  • Plot both lines on the same graph.
  • The intersection point (if any) is the solution.

🎙️ One powerful way to solve a pair of linear equations is by drawing them! Each equation creates a straight line on a graph. The point where these lines meet tells us the common solution.

📈
Plotting Lines

Intersecting Lines: Unique Solution

  • Lines cross at exactly one point.
  • This point is the unique solution (x, y).
  • Equations are called 'consistent'.

🎙️ When two lines cross each other, they intersect at only one spot. This means there's just one unique pair of (x, y) values that satisfies both equations. These systems are called consistent.

❌
One Meeting Point
13 / 25

Parallel Lines: No Solution

  • Lines never meet, always same distance apart.
  • No common point means no common solution.
  • Equations are called 'inconsistent'.

🎙️ Think of railway tracks; they run side-by-side but never meet. If your lines are parallel, they will never intersect, meaning there's no solution that works for both equations. Such systems are inconsistent.

🛤️
Never Meet
14 / 25

Coincident Lines: Infinitely Many Solutions

  • One line lies exactly on top of the other.
  • Every point on the line is a common solution.
  • Equations are 'dependent consistent'.

🎙️ Sometimes, both equations actually represent the exact same line! In this case, every single point on that line is a solution, giving us infinitely many solutions. These are called dependent consistent systems.

Overlap Perfectly

Algebraic Methods - Solving Precisely

  • Sometimes graphs aren't precise enough.
  • Algebraic methods give exact solutions.
  • Two main methods: Substitution and Elimination.

🎙️ While graphical methods are great for understanding, they can sometimes be tricky for exact answers. Algebraic methods use mathematical operations to find the precise values of x and y, leaving no room for error.

Method 1: Substitution - Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Express one variable in terms of the other.
  • Step 2: Substitute this expression into the second equation.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting single-variable equation.
  • Step 4: Substitute back to find the other variable.

🎙️ The substitution method is like replacing one piece of information with another. You isolate one variable, plug its expression into the other equation, and then solve. It's a systematic approach to finding the solution.

1Isolate a variable
2Substitute into 2nd eqn
3Solve for 1st variable
4Find 2nd variable
18 / 25

Worked Example: Substitution Method

  • Solve: x + y = 7 and x - y = 1
  • From (1): x = 7 - y
  • Substitute into (2): (7 - y) - y = 1 => 7 - 2y = 1
  • 2y = 6 => y = 3. Then x = 7 - 3 = 4.

🎙️ Let's walk through an example. We take one equation, express 'x' in terms of 'y', then substitute that into the second equation. This leaves us with just 'y', which we can easily solve for, and then find 'x'.

Method 2: Elimination - Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Make coefficients of one variable equal.
  • Step 2: Add or subtract equations to eliminate that variable.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting single-variable equation.
  • Step 4: Substitute back to find the other variable.

🎙️ The elimination method is about making one variable disappear! We multiply equations so that one variable has the same coefficient in both, then add or subtract to eliminate it, making the problem simpler.

1Equal coefficients
2Add/Subtract equations
3Solve for 1st variable
4Find 2nd variable
20 / 25

Worked Example: Elimination Method

  • Solve: 2x + 3y = 13 and 5x - 2y = 4
  • Multiply (1) by 2, (2) by 3: 4x + 6y = 26; 15x - 6y = 12
  • Add equations: 19x = 38 => x = 2
  • Substitute x=2 into (1): 2(2) + 3y = 13 => 4 + 3y = 13 => 3y = 9 => y = 3.

🎙️ Here, we aim to eliminate 'y'. We multiply the first equation by 2 and the second by 3 to get '+6y' and '-6y'. Adding these new equations eliminates 'y', allowing us to solve for 'x', then 'y'.
