What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of a triangle.
  • Comes from Greek words 'Trigon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's a powerful tool that helps us find unknown sides or angles of a triangle when we know some other parts. We'll mainly focus on right-angled triangles in this chapter.

Right-Angled Triangle Refresher

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The longest side is always opposite the right angle.

🎙️ Before diving into trigonometry, let's quickly recall what a right-angled triangle is. It's the most important type of triangle for our topic today, as all basic trigonometric ratios are defined based on it.

Identifying Sides of a Right Triangle

  • Hypotenuse: Side opposite the 90° angle (always the longest).
  • Opposite Side: Side opposite to the angle we are considering.
  • Adjacent Side: Side next to the angle we are considering (not the hypotenuse).

🎙️ For any acute angle in a right triangle, we name the other two sides relative to that angle. The hypotenuse stays the same, but the 'opposite' and 'adjacent' sides change depending on which acute angle we are looking at.

Introducing Trigonometric Ratios

  • These are ratios of the sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • They relate an angle to the lengths of its sides.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are simply fractions that compare the lengths of two sides of a right triangle. These ratios are constant for a given angle, no matter how big or small the triangle is, as long as the angle remains the same.

🔗

Sine (sin) Ratio

  • Sine of an angle (sin θ) = Opposite Side / Hypotenuse.
  • Often written as sin θ.

🎙️ The first ratio we'll learn is Sine. Think of 'opposite' as the side looking directly at the angle, and 'hypotenuse' as the side that never changes. So, sin θ tells us how tall the opposite side is compared to the hypotenuse.

⬆️
Opposite / Hypotenuse

Cosine (cos) Ratio

  • Cosine of an angle (cos θ) = Adjacent Side / Hypotenuse.
  • Often written as cos θ.

🎙️ Next is Cosine. The 'adjacent' side is the one right next to the angle, helping to form it, but not the hypotenuse. Cos θ gives us a ratio of this adjacent side to the hypotenuse.

➡️
Adjacent / Hypotenuse

Tangent (tan) Ratio

  • Tangent of an angle (tan θ) = Opposite Side / Adjacent Side.
  • Often written as tan θ.

🎙️ Finally, Tangent. This ratio compares the opposite side to the adjacent side. An interesting fact is that tan θ can also be found by dividing sin θ by cos θ!

Remember with SOH CAH TOA!

  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

Example: Finding Sine

  • Given a right triangle with Hypotenuse = 5, Opposite = 3.
  • Find sin θ.
  • sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse = 3 / 5.

🎙️ Let's apply what we've learned. If you have a right-angled triangle where the side opposite to angle θ is 3 units and the hypotenuse is 5 units, then sin θ is simply 3/5. Always remember to write the ratio as a fraction.

12 / 25

Example: Finding Cosine

  • Given a right triangle with Hypotenuse = 10, Adjacent = 8.
  • Find cos θ.
  • cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse = 8 / 10 = 4 / 5.

🎙️ For cosine, we look at the adjacent side. If the adjacent side is 8 and the hypotenuse is 10, then cos θ is 8/10, which simplifies to 4/5. Always simplify your fractions to their lowest terms.

✍️
Calculation

Example: Finding Tangent

  • Given a right triangle with Opposite = 12, Adjacent = 5.
  • Find tan θ.
  • tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent = 12 / 5.

🎙️ And for tangent, we use the opposite and adjacent sides. If the opposite side is 12 and the adjacent side is 5, then tan θ is 12/5. Notice that the hypotenuse is not directly involved in the tangent ratio.

Cosecant (cosec) Ratio

  • Cosecant of an angle (cosec θ) = Hypotenuse / Opposite Side.
  • It is the reciprocal of sin θ (cosec θ = 1/sin θ).

🎙️ Now, let's look at the other three ratios, which are reciprocals of the first three. Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine. This means if you know sin θ, just flip the fraction to get cosec θ.

⬇️

Secant (sec) Ratio

  • Secant of an angle (sec θ) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent Side.
  • It is the reciprocal of cos θ (sec θ = 1/cos θ).

🎙️ Secant is the reciprocal of cosine. So, once you calculate cos θ, simply invert that fraction to find sec θ. These reciprocal relationships make it easier to remember all six ratios.

⬅️
Hypotenuse / Adjacent

Cotangent (cot) Ratio

  • Cotangent of an angle (cot θ) = Adjacent Side / Opposite Side.
  • It is the reciprocal of tan θ (cot θ = 1/tan θ).

🎙️ And finally, Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent. If you know tan θ, just flip the fraction to get cot θ. You can also get cot θ by dividing cos θ by sin θ.

Example: Using Reciprocal Ratios

  • If sin θ = 3/5, find cosec θ.
  • cosec θ = 1 / sin θ = 1 / (3/5) = 5/3.
  • It's just the inverse of the fraction!

🎙️ Let's put the reciprocal relationship into practice. If we know sin θ is 3/5, then finding cosec θ is as simple as flipping that fraction to get 5/3. No need to look for sides again!

Real-Life: Height of a Tower

  • Problem: Find the height of a tower if you know your distance from it and the angle of elevation.
  • Method: Use the Tangent ratio (tan θ = Opposite/Adjacent).
  • Example: If you stand 100m away and angle is 30°, tower height = 100 * tan(30°).

🎙️ This is a classic example! Imagine you're standing on the ground, looking up at a tower. You know how far you are from it and the angle your line of sight makes with the ground. Using the tangent ratio, you can easily calculate the tower's height!

Real-Life: Distance Across a River

  • Problem: Find the width of a river without crossing it.
  • Method: Use the Tangent ratio by observing an object on the opposite bank and a known distance along your bank.
  • Example: If you walk 50m and the angle changes, you can find the width.

🎙️ How do engineers measure the width of a river without actually stretching a tape across it? They use trigonometry! By setting up a right-angled triangle using points on the river bank, they can calculate the distance using angles and known distances.
