Revisiting the Circle

  • A set of points equidistant from a central point
  • Radius (r): distance from center to boundary
  • Diameter (d): distance across center (d=2r)

🎙️ Before we dive into new concepts, let's quickly refresh our memory about the basics of a circle. Remember, a circle is perfectly round, and its size is defined by its radius or diameter.

Circumference of a Circle

  • The distance around the circle
  • It's like the perimeter of a polygon
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd

🎙️ The circumference is simply the total length of the boundary of the circle. Imagine unrolling the circle into a straight line – that length is the circumference. We use the constant π (pi) in its formula.

📏
Measuring Around
4 / 25

Area of a Circle

  • The space enclosed by the circle
  • The region inside the boundary
  • Formula: A = πr²

🎙️ While circumference measures the boundary, the area measures the entire space covered by the circle. Think of how much paint you'd need to cover a circular table top. That's the area!

✨

Understanding a Sector

  • A 'slice' of a circle
  • Bounded by two radii and an arc
  • Like a piece of pizza or cake

🎙️ Now, let's learn about parts of a circle. A sector is like taking a slice out of a round pizza. It's defined by the center, two radii, and the curved edge between them.

🍕
A Slice of Circle

Area of a Sector of a Circle

  • Area depends on the angle at the center
  • Formula: (θ/360°) × πr²
  • θ is the angle in degrees

🎙️ Since a sector is a part of the whole circle, its area is a fraction of the total area of the circle. This fraction is determined by the central angle, θ, compared to the full 360 degrees.

📐
Angle Matters

Understanding a Segment

  • A region bounded by a chord and an arc
  • The 'crust' part of a pizza slice if you cut off the tip
  • Can be minor (smaller) or major (larger)

🎙️ Another important part of a circle is a segment. Imagine drawing a straight line (a chord) inside a circle. The area enclosed by this chord and the curved arc is a segment.

🌙
Curved Section
11 / 25

Area of a Segment of a Circle

  • Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle
  • Triangle formed by radii and chord
  • For minor segment, subtract triangle from minor sector

🎙️ To find the area of a segment, we use a clever trick! We calculate the area of the sector that includes the segment, and then subtract the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.

➖
Sector minus Triangle

Example: Area of a Segment

  • Find the area of the minor segment of a circle with radius 14 cm and angle 90°. (Use π = 22/7)

🎙️ This example combines what we know about sectors and triangles. Remember, for a 90° angle, the triangle is a right-angled triangle, making its area calculation simpler: (1/2) * base * height.

1Find Area of Sector
2Find Area of Triangle (1/2 * r * r * sinθ)
3Subtract: Area of Sector - Area of Triangle
4Calculate the final area

Example: Area of a Circular Track

  • A circular track has an inner radius of 30 m and an outer radius of 35 m. Find the area of the track. (Use π = 22/7)

🎙️ This problem involves two concentric circles. The track's area is the difference between the area of the larger circle and the area of the smaller circle. It's like finding the area of a ring.

3Calculate Area of Inner Circle

Perimeters of Combinations of Plane Figures

🎙️ Not just areas, but perimeters of combined figures are also important. Remember, perimeter is the total length of the outer boundary. You need to carefully identify all the curved and straight edges that form the boundary.
