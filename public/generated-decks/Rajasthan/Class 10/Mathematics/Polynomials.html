What is an Algebraic Expression?

  • A combination of variables and constants.
  • Connected by mathematical operations (+, -, ×, ÷).
  • Example: 3x + 5, 2y² - 7.

🎙️ An algebraic expression is like a mathematical sentence using letters for unknown values and numbers for fixed values. They are combined using basic operations to form meaningful statements.

Terms in an Algebraic Expression

  • Parts of an expression separated by '+' or '-'.
  • Each term is a product of factors.
  • Example: In 4x² + 5x - 7, the terms are 4x², 5x, and -7.

🎙️ Think of terms as individual components of an algebraic expression. They are distinct pieces that are added or subtracted together to form the whole expression.

Understanding Variables

  • Symbols (usually letters) that represent unknown values.
  • Their value can change.
  • Commonly used: x, y, z, a, b, c.

🎙️ Variables are like placeholders for numbers that can change. Imagine you don't know the exact number of sweets in a box; you can use 'x' to represent that unknown quantity.

Understanding Constants

  • Symbols or numbers with a fixed value.
  • Their value does not change.
  • Examples: 5, -10, 0, π (pi).

🎙️ Constants are the opposite of variables; they are numbers whose values are always the same. For instance, in the expression 'x + 5', '5' is a constant because its value never changes.

What is a Polynomial?

  • An algebraic expression where exponents of variables are non-negative integers.
  • No variables in the denominator or under a radical sign.
  • Examples: 3x + 2, 5x² - 7x + 1, y³ + 4.

🎙️ A polynomial is a special type of algebraic expression that follows specific rules for its exponents. It's a fundamental building block for many higher-level mathematical concepts.

🏠
Building Block

🎙️ Polynomials aren't just abstract math! Engineers use them to design safe bridges, architects use them to create beautiful curves in buildings, and even game developers use them for character movements.

Degree of a Polynomial

  • The highest power of the variable in a polynomial.
  • Determines the type and behavior of the polynomial.
  • Example: In 5x³ + 2x² - 7, the degree is 3.

🎙️ The degree is a very important characteristic of a polynomial. It tells us about the 'strength' or 'complexity' of the polynomial and helps us classify it.

Types of Polynomials by Degree: Linear Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 1.
  • General form: ax + b, where a ≠ 0.
  • Example: 2x + 3, y - 5.

🎙️ Linear polynomials are the simplest type. When you graph them, they always form a straight line. Think of a simple equation like y = 2x + 3.

📏
Straight Line

Types of Polynomials by Degree: Quadratic Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 2.
  • General form: ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0.
  • Example: x² - 4x + 4, 3y² + 2y.

🎙️ Quadratic polynomials are very common. Their graphs are parabolas, which are U-shaped curves. You'll see these curves in many natural phenomena, like the path of a thrown ball.

🔄
Parabolic Curve

Types of Polynomials by Degree: Cubic Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 3.
  • General form: ax³ + bx² + cx + d, where a ≠ 0.
  • Example: x³ - 2x² + x - 1, 4y³ + 7.

🎙️ Cubic polynomials have a degree of three. Their graphs can have more complex curves than linear or quadratic polynomials, often showing two turns.

Complex Shape

Zeroes of a Polynomial

  • A real number 'k' is a zero if P(k) = 0.
  • It's the value of the variable that makes the polynomial equal to zero.
  • Also called 'roots' of the polynomial equation.

🎙️ Finding the zeroes of a polynomial is like finding the 'solution' or 'answer' to the polynomial equation. It's a very important concept with many applications.

Finding Zeroes of a Linear Polynomial (Worked Example)

  • Consider P(x) = 2x - 6.
  • To find zero, set P(x) = 0.
  • 2x - 6 = 0 => 2x = 6 => x = 3.

🎙️ For a linear polynomial, finding the zero is quite straightforward. You simply set the expression equal to zero and solve for the variable, just like solving a simple linear equation.

Geometric Meaning of Zeroes: Linear Polynomial

  • For a linear polynomial ax + b, its graph is a straight line.
  • The zero is the x-coordinate where the line intersects the x-axis.
  • A linear polynomial has exactly one zero.

🎙️ Visually, the zero of a linear polynomial is where its graph crosses the horizontal x-axis. Since a straight line can cross the x-axis at most once, a linear polynomial has only one zero.

↗️
X-axis Intercept

Geometric Meaning of Zeroes: Quadratic Polynomial

  • For a quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c, its graph is a parabola.
  • The zeroes are the x-coordinates where the parabola intersects the x-axis.
  • A quadratic polynomial can have 0, 1, or 2 distinct zeroes.

🎙️ A quadratic polynomial's graph, a parabola, can intersect the x-axis at two points, one point (if it just touches), or no points at all. This means it can have two, one, or no real zeroes.

📈
Parabola Intercepts

Geometric Meaning of Zeroes: Cubic Polynomial

  • For a cubic polynomial, its graph is a curve with up to two turns.
  • The zeroes are the x-coordinates where the curve intersects the x-axis.
  • A cubic polynomial can have 1, 2, or 3 distinct zeroes.

🎙️ The graph of a cubic polynomial can cross the x-axis up to three times. So, a cubic polynomial can have one, two, or three real zeroes, corresponding to these intersection points.

Relationship between Zeroes and Coefficients: Linear Polynomial

  • For P(x) = ax + b (a ≠ 0), the zero is x = -b/a.
  • Zero = -(Constant Term) / (Coefficient of x).
  • Example: For 2x + 6, zero = -6/2 = -3.

🎙️ There's a direct and simple relationship between the zero of a linear polynomial and its coefficients. You can find the zero just by looking at the constant term and the coefficient of x.

🤝
Direct Connection

Relationship between Zeroes and Coefficients: Quadratic Polynomial

  • For P(x) = ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0), let zeroes be α and β.
  • Sum of zeroes (α + β) = -b/a.
  • Product of zeroes (αβ) = c/a.

🎙️ For quadratic polynomials, the relationship is a bit more complex but equally powerful. The sum and product of the zeroes can be directly found from the coefficients a, b, and c.

🔗
Interlinked Properties

Worked Example: Zeroes and Coefficients (Quadratic)

  • Polynomial: x² + 5x + 6.
  • Zeroes are -2 and -3 (by factorization).
  • Sum: -2 + (-3) = -5. From formula: -b/a = -5/1 = -5. (Verified!)

🎙️ Let's put the relationship to the test. If we find the zeroes of x² + 5x + 6 by factoring, we get -2 and -3. When we compare their sum and product to the formulas, they match perfectly!

Recap: Key Takeaways from Polynomials

  • Polynomials are algebraic expressions with non-negative integer exponents.
  • Classified by degree (linear, quadratic, cubic) and terms (monomial, binomial, trinomial).
  • Zeroes are values that make the polynomial zero; they are x-intercepts on a graph.
  • Relationships exist between zeroes and coefficients for different polynomial types.
