What is a Circle?

  • A set of points equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.
  • The constant distance is called the 'radius'.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a perfect round shape. Every point on that curved line is exactly the same distance from the point you started drawing from. That fixed point is the center, and that constant distance is the radius.

Parts of a Circle: Center & Radius

  • Center (O): The fixed point.
  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on the circle.
  • All radii of a circle are equal.

🎙️ The heart of the circle is its 'center', usually denoted by 'O'. The 'radius' is like a bridge connecting the center to any point on the circle's boundary. Think of it as the 'reach' of the circle from its middle.

📍
Center and Stretch

Parts of a Circle: Diameter

  • A line segment passing through the center.
  • Connects two points on the circle.
  • It's the longest chord.
  • Diameter (d) = 2 × Radius (r).

🎙️ The 'diameter' is a special line that cuts right through the center of the circle, connecting two points on its edge. It's exactly twice the length of the radius and is the longest possible straight line you can draw inside a circle.

↔️
Across the Middle

Parts of a Circle: Chord

  • A line segment connecting any two points on the circle.
  • Does not necessarily pass through the center.
  • The diameter is the longest chord.

🎙️ A 'chord' is any straight line that connects two points on the circle's boundary. It's like a shortcut across the circle. Remember, the diameter is also a chord, but a very special one because it passes through the center!

Parts of a Circle: Arc

  • A continuous piece of a circle.
  • Part of the circumference.
  • Can be a minor arc (smaller) or major arc (larger).

🎙️ An 'arc' is simply a curved portion of the circle's edge. Think of it as a piece of the circle's boundary. We can have small arcs, called minor arcs, or larger ones, called major arcs.

Parts of a Circle: Sector & Segment

  • Sector: Area enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • Segment: Area enclosed by a chord and an arc.
  • Like a pizza slice (sector) vs. a piece of pie crust (segment).

🎙️ Imagine a pizza! A 'sector' is like a slice of pizza, defined by two radii and the crust. A 'segment' is a bit different; it's the area formed by a chord and the arc it cuts off. It's like the part of the pizza crust that's left after you cut a straight line across.

Circumference: The Boundary Length

  • The perimeter or distance around the circle.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd.
  • π (Pi) is a constant, approximately 3.14 or 22/7.

🎙️ The 'circumference' is simply the total distance around the circle, like the perimeter of a square. We calculate it using the famous constant 'Pi', which relates the circumference to the diameter. It's a fundamental value in circle calculations.

📏
Measuring Around

Area of a Circle: The Space Inside

  • The amount of surface enclosed by the circle.
  • Formula: A = πr².
  • Measured in square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ While circumference measures the boundary, the 'area' tells us how much flat space the circle covers. It's calculated using the radius squared, multiplied by Pi. This is crucial for understanding how much material is needed to cover a circular surface.

Space Covered

Worked Example: Circumference & Area

  • Problem: A circle has a radius of 7 cm.
  • Find its circumference.
  • Find its area. (Use π = 22/7)

🎙️ Let's apply our formulas! For a circle with a 7 cm radius: Circumference C = 2 * (22/7) * 7 = 44 cm. Area A = (22/7) * 7 * 7 = 154 cm². See how straightforward it is once you know the formulas?

Tangent to a Circle: Just Touching

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • The point of contact is called the 'point of tangency'.
  • Does not enter the interior of the circle.

🎙️ A 'tangent' is like a line that just kisses the circle, touching it at only one single point. It never crosses into the circle's inside. Imagine a car wheel touching the road – that's a tangent!

🖐️
Gentle Touch

Secant to a Circle: Cutting Through

  • A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.
  • Passes through the interior of the circle.
  • Contrast with a tangent which only touches.

🎙️ In contrast to a tangent, a 'secant' is a line that boldly cuts right through the circle, intersecting it at two different points. Think of it as slicing through a round cake.

Cutting Across

Tangent Property 1: Perpendicular Radius

  • The radius drawn to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle.
  • This is a fundamental property.

🎙️ This is a very important theorem! If you draw a radius to the exact point where a tangent touches the circle, that radius and the tangent will always form a perfect 90-degree angle. This property is used in many geometric proofs.

📐
Right Angle Rule
16 / 25

Tangent Property 2: Equal Lengths

  • The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
  • If P is an external point, and PA, PB are tangents, then PA = PB.

🎙️ Here's another powerful property: If you pick a point outside a circle and draw two tangents from it to the circle, those two tangent segments will always have the exact same length. This often helps us solve problems involving unknown lengths.

⚖️
Balanced Tangents

Worked Example: Tangent Lengths

  • Problem: From a point Q, the length of the tangent to a circle is 24 cm.
  • The distance of Q from the center is 25 cm.
  • Find the radius of the circle.

🎙️ Let's use the first tangent property here. We have a right-angled triangle formed by the radius, the tangent, and the line from the center to Q. Using Pythagoras theorem (r² + 24² = 25²), we find the radius r = 7 cm. This shows how useful these properties are!

Tangents from a Point: How Many?

  • Point inside the circle: Zero tangents.
  • Point on the circle: One tangent.
  • Point outside the circle: Two tangents.

🎙️ The number of tangents you can draw to a circle depends entirely on where your starting point is. If it's inside, no tangents. If it's on the circle, exactly one. If it's outside, exactly two tangents can be drawn.

Cyclic Quadrilateral: Inside a Circle

  • A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on the circumference of a circle.
  • Property: The sum of opposite angles is 180 degrees.
  • ∠A + ∠C = 180°, ∠B + ∠D = 180°.

🎙️ A 'cyclic quadrilateral' is a special type of four-sided shape where all its corners touch the circle's edge. Its most important property is that its opposite angles always add up to 180 degrees. This is a powerful tool for solving angle-related problems.

🔄
Angles Add Up

Worked Example: Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • Problem: In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = 70°.
  • Find the measure of ∠C.
  • If ∠B = 100°, find ∠D.

🎙️ Using the property that opposite angles sum to 180 degrees: If ∠A = 70°, then ∠C = 180° - 70° = 110°. If ∠B = 100°, then ∠D = 180° - 100° = 80°. Simple, right? This property makes solving such problems very easy.
