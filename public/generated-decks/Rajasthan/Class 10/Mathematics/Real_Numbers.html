Rational Numbers (Q)

  • Numbers that can be written as p/q.
  • p and q are integers, and q ≠ 0.
  • Example: 1/2, -3/4, 5 (which is 5/1).

🎙️ Rational numbers are essentially fractions. If you can express a number as a ratio of two integers, where the denominator is not zero, it's a rational number. This includes all integers, as they can be written with a denominator of 1.

Euclid's Division Lemma (EDL)

  • Given positive integers 'a' and 'b'.
  • There exist unique integers 'q' and 'r'.
  • Such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

🎙️ Euclid's Division Lemma is a fundamental statement about division. It simply says that when you divide one positive integer by another, you get a unique quotient and a unique remainder, and the remainder is always smaller than the divisor.

➗
Division rule

Euclid's Division Algorithm (EDA)

  • A technique to compute the HCF (Highest Common Factor) of two positive integers.
  • It applies Euclid's Division Lemma repeatedly.
  • The HCF is the divisor when the remainder becomes zero.

🎙️ Euclid's Division Algorithm is a practical method to find the HCF of two numbers. You keep applying the division lemma, using the previous divisor as the new dividend and the remainder as the new divisor, until you get a remainder of zero. The last non-zero divisor is your HCF.

1Apply EDL (a=bq+r)
2If r=0, b is HCF
3If r≠0, apply EDL to b and r
4Continue until r=0
12 / 25

Worked Example: HCF using EDA

  • Find HCF of 135 and 225.
  • Step 1: 225 = 135 × 1 + 90.
  • Step 2: 135 = 90 × 1 + 45.
  • Step 3: 90 = 45 × 2 + 0.

🎙️ Let's find the HCF of 135 and 225. We apply the algorithm: 225 divided by 135 gives a remainder of 90. Then, 135 divided by 90 gives a remainder of 45. Finally, 90 divided by 45 gives a remainder of 0. So, 45 is the HCF.

1225 = 135×1 + 90
2135 = 90×1 + 45
390 = 45×2 + 0
4HCF is 45

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (FTA)

  • Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes.
  • This factorisation is unique.
  • The order of prime factors does not matter.

🎙️ The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic is like a building block rule for numbers. It states that any composite number can be broken down into a unique set of prime numbers multiplied together. This is why prime factorization is so important.

Worked Example: Prime Factorization

  • Factorize 140 into prime factors.
  • 140 = 2 × 70
  • 140 = 2 × 2 × 35
  • 140 = 2 × 2 × 5 × 7

🎙️ Let's factorize 140. We start by dividing by the smallest prime, 2, repeatedly. Then we move to the next prime, 5, and finally 7. We get 140 as 2 multiplied by 2, by 5, and by 7. These are its unique prime factors.

1140 ÷ 2 = 70
270 ÷ 2 = 35
335 ÷ 5 = 7
4Prime factors: 2, 2, 5, 7

LCM and HCF using FTA

  • HCF: Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor.
  • LCM: Product of the greatest power of each prime factor.
  • For two numbers 'a' and 'b': HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b.

🎙️ The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic gives us a powerful way to find LCM and HCF. For HCF, we look at common prime factors and take their lowest powers. For LCM, we consider all prime factors and take their highest powers. Remember the useful product formula too!

HCF (common, min power)
LCM (all, max power)

Worked Example: LCM & HCF using FTA

  • Find LCM and HCF of 6 and 20.
  • Prime factorization: 6 = 2 × 3, 20 = 2² × 5.
  • HCF(6,20) = 2¹ = 2 (smallest power of common prime 2).
  • LCM(6,20) = 2² × 3¹ × 5¹ = 4 × 3 × 5 = 60 (greatest powers).

🎙️ Let's apply the FTA to 6 and 20. First, we find their prime factors. Then, for HCF, we pick the common prime factor (2) with the lowest power (2 to the power of 1). For LCM, we take all prime factors (2, 3, 5) with their highest powers. This gives us HCF of 2 and LCM of 60.

16 = 2 × 3
220 = 2² × 5
3HCF = 2
4LCM = 60

Decimal Expansions of Rational Numbers

  • Rational numbers have two types of decimal expansions.
  • Either terminating (ends after a finite number of digits).
  • Or non-terminating repeating (digits repeat in a pattern).

🎙️ When you convert a rational number (a fraction) to a decimal, it will always either stop (terminate) or continue infinitely with a repeating pattern. This is a defining characteristic of rational numbers.

Terminating Decimals

  • A rational number x = p/q (q ≠ 0) has a terminating decimal expansion.
  • If the prime factorization of 'q' is of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ.
  • Where n and m are non-negative integers.

🎙️ This theorem provides a quick way to check if a fraction will terminate. Just look at the prime factors of its denominator. If they are only 2s and/or 5s, the decimal will terminate. For example, 3/8 (denominator 8 = 2³) terminates.

It stops!

Worked Example: Terminating Decimal

  • Does 13/125 have a terminating decimal expansion?
  • Denominator q = 125.
  • Prime factorization of 125 = 5 × 5 × 5 = 5³.
  • Since 125 = 2⁰ × 5³, it is of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ. Yes, it terminates.

🎙️ Let's check 13/125. The denominator is 125, which is 5 cubed. Since this only contains prime factor 5 (which fits the 2ⁿ5ᵐ form where n=0), its decimal expansion will terminate. You can verify this by dividing: 13/125 = 0.104.

1Fraction: 13/125
2Denominator: 125
3Prime factors: 5³
4Form 2ⁿ5ᵐ? Yes.

Non-Terminating Repeating Decimals

  • A rational number x = p/q (q ≠ 0) has a non-terminating repeating decimal expansion.
  • If the prime factorization of 'q' is NOT of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ.
  • Meaning 'q' has prime factors other than 2 or 5.

🎙️ Conversely, if the denominator of a rational number has any prime factors other than 2 or 5, its decimal expansion will be non-terminating and repeating. For instance, 1/3 (denominator 3) is 0.333... and repeats.

It repeats!

Worked Example: Non-Terminating Decimal

  • Does 1/7 have a terminating decimal expansion?
  • Denominator q = 7.
  • Prime factorization of 7 = 7¹.
  • Since 7 is not of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ, it is non-terminating repeating.

🎙️ Consider the fraction 1/7. The denominator is 7, which is a prime number other than 2 or 5. Therefore, its decimal expansion will be non-terminating and repeating. If you divide 1 by 7, you'll see a repeating block of digits.

1Fraction: 1/7
2Denominator: 7
3Prime factors: 7¹
4Form 2ⁿ5ᵐ? No.
