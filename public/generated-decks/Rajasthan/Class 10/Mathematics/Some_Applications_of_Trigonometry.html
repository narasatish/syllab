Understanding the Line of Sight

  • The imaginary line from observer's eye to the object.
  • It's like a straight path your eyes follow.

🎙️ First, let's understand 'Line of Sight'. It's simply the straight line connecting your eye to the object you are looking at. Whether you look up, down, or straight, this line is your line of sight.

The Horizontal Line: Our Reference

  • A line parallel to the ground.
  • Passes through the observer's eye level.
  • It acts as our base for measuring angles.

🎙️ The horizontal line is crucial. Imagine a straight line going out from your eyes, perfectly parallel to the ground. This line is our reference point for defining angles of elevation and depression.

Angle of Elevation: Looking Up!

  • Formed when the object is ABOVE the horizontal level.
  • The observer has to look UP to see the object.
  • Angle between line of sight and horizontal line.

🎙️ When you look up at something, like a bird flying or the top of a building, the angle formed between your line of sight and the horizontal line is called the Angle of Elevation. Remember, you're elevating your gaze!

Example: Watching a Kite Fly High

  • You are on the ground, looking up.
  • The kite is above your horizontal line.
  • The angle formed is the Angle of Elevation.

🎙️ Imagine you're flying a kite. Your eyes are on the ground, and the kite is soaring high. The angle you make by lifting your head to see the kite is a perfect example of the Angle of Elevation.

Angle of Depression: Looking Down!

  • Formed when the object is BELOW the horizontal level.
  • The observer has to look DOWN to see the object.
  • Angle between line of sight and horizontal line.

🎙️ Now, what if you are on a height, like a building or a cliff, and you look down at something on the ground? The angle formed between your line of sight and the horizontal line (at your eye level) is the Angle of Depression.

Finding Heights using Trigonometry

🎙️ One of the most common applications is finding heights. Imagine needing to know how tall a flagpole is without climbing it. With an angle and a distance from its base, trigonometry gives us the answer!

Worked Example 4: Lighthouse and Two Ships

  • From a 75m high lighthouse, angles of depression to two ships are 30° and 45°.
  • The ships are on the same side of the lighthouse.
  • Find the distance between the two ships.

🎙️ Here, we use angles of depression. Remember that the angle of depression from the lighthouse to a ship is equal to the angle of elevation from the ship to the lighthouse. We'll find distances to each ship and then their difference.

1Draw diagram with horizontal line
2Use alternate interior angles for elevation
3Calculate distance to each ship
4Subtract to find distance between ships
