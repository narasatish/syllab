Direct Proof Example: Sum of Two Even Numbers

  • Statement: The sum of any two even numbers is always an even number.
  • Let two even numbers be 2m and 2n (where m, n are integers).
  • Their sum is 2m + 2n = 2(m+n).
  • Since (m+n) is an integer, 2(m+n) is an even number. Hence proven.

🎙️ Let's prove that the sum of any two even numbers is always an even number. We start by defining even numbers and then use algebraic manipulation to show the sum fits the definition of an even number.

Direct Proof Example: Sum of Two Odd Numbers

  • Statement: The sum of any two odd numbers is an even number.
  • Let two odd numbers be 2m+1 and 2n+1 (where m, n are integers).
  • Their sum is (2m+1) + (2n+1) = 2m + 2n + 2 = 2(m+n+1).
  • Since (m+n+1) is an integer, 2(m+n+1) is an even number. Hence proven.

Proof by Contradiction Example: If n² is even, then n is even

  • To prove: If n² is even, then n is even.
  • Assume the opposite: n² is even, but n is odd.
  • If n is odd, then n = 2k+1 for some integer k.
  • Then n² = (2k+1)² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1.
  • This means n² is odd, which contradicts our assumption that n² is even.
  • Therefore, our assumption (n is odd) must be false, so n must be even. Hence proven.

🎙️ Let's prove: 'If n² is even, then n is even'. We'll assume the opposite: that n is odd. If n is odd, can n² be even? Let's work it out and see if we find a contradiction!

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Assuming the conclusion: Starting with what you need to prove.
  • Using inductive reasoning for proof: Observations are not proofs.
  • Making logical jumps: Every step needs justification.
  • Not understanding definitions: Misinterpreting terms leads to errors.

🎙️ Students often make mistakes like assuming the conclusion, using inductive reasoning to prove a theorem, or making logical jumps without proper justification. Always be rigorous and check every step!
