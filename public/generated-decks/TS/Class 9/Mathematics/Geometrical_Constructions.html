What are Geometrical Constructions?

  • Understanding the principles behind shapes.

Essential Tools for Construction

  • Ruler/Straightedge: For drawing straight lines.
  • Compass: For drawing arcs and circles, and measuring distances.

Construction 1: Bisecting a Line Segment

  • Dividing a line segment into two equal parts.
  • The point that divides it is called the midpoint.
  • Each part will have the same length.

🎙️ Our first construction is bisecting a line segment. This means finding the exact middle point of a line and dividing it into two pieces of equal length.

↔️
Equal Halves

Steps: Bisecting a Line Segment

  • Draw line segment AB.
  • With A as center, draw arcs above and below AB (radius > half AB).
  • With B as center, draw arcs intersecting previous arcs at C and D.
  • Join C and D. CD bisects AB at M.

🎙️ Let's walk through the steps. First, draw your line. Then, using your compass, draw arcs from both ends of the line, making sure the radius is more than half the line's length. Where these arcs cross, draw a line. That line will bisect your original segment.

1Draw line AB
2Arcs from A
3Arcs from B
4Join intersection points

Worked Example 1: Bisecting an 8 cm Line Segment

  • Draw line segment AB = 8 cm.
  • Open compass more than 4 cm.
  • Draw intersecting arcs from A and B.
  • Join intersection points to find midpoint M.

🎙️ Imagine you have an 8 cm line. You'd set your compass to a radius greater than 4 cm. Then, from each end of the 8 cm line, you'd draw arcs. Connecting the points where these arcs intersect gives you the perpendicular bisector, and its intersection with the line is the midpoint.

1Draw AB = 8 cm
2Set compass > 4 cm
3Draw arcs from A & B
4Connect arc intersections

Construction 2: Bisecting an Angle

  • Dividing an angle into two equal angles.
  • The bisector is a ray that passes through the vertex.
  • Each new angle will have half the measure of the original.

🎙️ Next, we'll learn to bisect an angle. This means drawing a ray from the angle's vertex that splits the angle into two smaller angles of exactly equal measure.

Steps: Bisecting an Angle

  • Draw angle ABC.
  • With B as center, draw an arc intersecting BA and BC at E and D.
  • With E and D as centers, draw arcs intersecting at F.
  • Join B and F. BF is the angle bisector.

🎙️ To bisect an angle, first, draw an arc from the vertex that cuts both arms of the angle. Then, from these two intersection points, draw two more arcs that cross each other. Drawing a line from the vertex through this new intersection point gives you the angle bisector.

1Draw angle ABC
2Arc from B
3Arcs from E & D
4Join B to F

Worked Example 2: Bisecting a 60-degree Angle

  • Draw angle ABC = 60 degrees.
  • Draw an arc from B, intersecting AB at E, BC at D.
  • From E and D, draw arcs intersecting at F.
  • Join B to F. Angle ABF = Angle FBC = 30 degrees.

🎙️ If you have a 60-degree angle, after bisecting it, you will get two 30-degree angles. This is a very common construction and a good way to create angles like 30 degrees or 15 degrees.

1Draw 60° angle
2Arc from vertex
3Arcs from intersections
4Draw bisector

Construction 3: Perpendicular Bisector

  • A line that bisects a segment and is perpendicular to it.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle with the segment.
  • Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the segment's endpoints.

🎙️ This construction combines two ideas: bisecting a line segment and making a line perpendicular to it. The perpendicular bisector not only cuts the segment in half but also crosses it at a perfect 90-degree angle.

⟂
90° and Midpoint

Steps: Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

  • Draw line segment AB.
  • With A as center, draw arcs above and below AB (radius > half AB).
  • With B as center, draw arcs intersecting previous arcs at C and D.
  • Join C and D. CD is the perpendicular bisector of AB.

🎙️ The steps for drawing a perpendicular bisector are identical to bisecting a line segment. The line you draw connecting the intersecting arcs is, in fact, the perpendicular bisector.

1Draw line AB
2Arcs from A
3Arcs from B
4Join intersection points

Worked Example 3: Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

  • Draw a line segment PQ of length 7 cm.
  • Using P as center, draw arcs above and below PQ (radius > 3.5 cm).
  • Using Q as center, draw arcs intersecting the first ones.
  • Connect the two intersection points. This line is the perpendicular bisector.

🎙️ Let's say you have a 7 cm line. You'd open your compass to more than 3.5 cm. Then, from each end, draw arcs above and below the line. Connecting where these arcs cross gives you the perpendicular bisector.

1Draw PQ = 7 cm
2Arcs from P (radius > 3.5)
3Arcs from Q (intersecting)
4Draw perpendicular bisector

  • The perpendicular line passes through a specific point *on* the given line.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle at that point.

🎙️ Now, we'll learn to draw a perpendicular line from a point that lies *on* the original line. This creates a right angle at that exact point.

Steps: Perpendicular from a Point on a Line

  • Draw line L and mark point P on it.
  • With P as center, draw an arc intersecting L at A and B.
  • With A and B as centers, draw arcs intersecting at C (radius > AP).
  • Join P and C. PC is perpendicular to L.

🎙️ First, draw a line and mark a point on it. From that point, draw an arc that cuts the line at two places. Then, from these two new points, draw arcs above the line that intersect. Connect the original point to this new intersection, and you have your perpendicular line.

1Draw line L, point P
2Arc from P (cuts L)
3Arcs from A & B (intersect)
4Join P to intersection

Worked Example 4: Perpendicular from a Point on Line

  • Draw a line segment XY and mark point Z on it.
  • With Z as center, draw an arc intersecting XY at A and B.
  • With A and B as centers, draw arcs above XY intersecting at C.
  • Join Z and C. ZC is perpendicular to XY.

🎙️ Imagine you have a straight road and you want to build a path directly perpendicular from a specific spot on that road. This construction helps you achieve that precise 90-degree angle.

1Draw line XY, point Z
2Arc from Z (cuts XY)
3Arcs from A & B (intersect)
4Draw ZC perpendicular

🎙️ This construction is similar, but the point from which we draw the perpendicular is *not* on the line. This is very useful for finding the shortest distance from a point to a line.

Steps: Perpendicular from a Point Outside a Line

  • Draw line L and mark point P outside it.
  • With P as center, draw an arc intersecting L at A and B.
  • With A and B as centers, draw arcs intersecting below P at Q (radius > half AB).
  • Join P and Q. PQ is perpendicular to L.

🎙️ For this, you start by drawing an arc from the outside point that cuts the line at two places. Then, from these two intersection points, draw arcs on the *opposite* side of the line from your original point. Connect the original point to this new intersection.

1Draw line L, point P outside
2Arc from P (cuts L)
3Arcs from A & B (intersect)
4Join P to intersection

Worked Example 5: Perpendicular from a Point Outside a Line

  • Draw line segment MN and mark point R above it.
  • With R as center, draw an arc intersecting MN at A and B.
  • With A and B as centers, draw arcs below MN intersecting at S.
  • Join R and S. RS is perpendicular to MN.

🎙️ Imagine you have a point in a field and a straight road nearby. This construction helps you draw the shortest path from that point directly to the road, which will always be a perpendicular line.

1Draw line MN, point R above
2Arc from R (cuts MN)
3Arcs from A & B (intersect)
4Draw RS perpendicular
