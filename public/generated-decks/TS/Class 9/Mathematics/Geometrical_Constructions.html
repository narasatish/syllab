Steps to Bisect an Angle

🎙️ First, place your compass at the vertex and draw an arc that cuts both arms of the angle. Then, from these two intersection points, draw two more arcs that meet inside the angle. Finally, draw a line from the vertex to where these two new arcs meet. That's your angle bisector!

1Draw an arc from vertex, cutting both arms.
2From arc intersections, draw two more arcs inside.
3Draw a line from vertex through the new arc intersection.

Steps to Construct Perpendicular Bisector

🎙️ Take your compass, open it to more than half the length of your line segment. From one endpoint, draw an arc above and below the line. Repeat this from the other endpoint, making sure the arcs intersect. Connect these two intersection points, and you have your perpendicular bisector.

1From each endpoint, draw arcs above and below (radius > half length).
2Connect the intersection points of the arcs.

Steps to Copy an Angle

🎙️ First, draw an arc on your original angle. Then, draw a new ray where you want to copy the angle, and draw an arc of the *same radius*. Now, carefully measure the 'opening' of the original angle's arc with your compass and transfer that exact distance to your new arc. Connect the endpoint to this new intersection, and you've copied the angle!

1Draw an arc from vertex of original angle.
2Draw a base line and an arc of same radius from its endpoint.
3Measure arc length of original angle with compass.
4Transfer this length to the new arc, draw second arm.

Special Angles: Constructing 60° and 90°

  • Some angles are fundamental and can be constructed directly.
  • 60° comes from an equilateral triangle.
  • Steps for 60°: Draw ray, arc from endpoint, another arc from first arc intersection.

🎙️ We can construct 60 degrees directly. This is like forming an equilateral triangle. From this, we can build many other angles. Follow these steps carefully to get your first special angle.

1Draw a ray. From its endpoint, draw an arc.
2From arc-ray intersection, draw another arc (same radius).
3Draw line from endpoint through new intersection.

Constructing 90°, and Deriving 30° & 45°

  • 90° is a right angle, crucial for many shapes.
  • Steps for 90°: Construct 60° and 120° marks, then bisect the angle between them.
  • 30° is half of 60° (bisecting 60°); 45° is half of 90° (bisecting 90°).

🎙️ Once you have 90 degrees, you can easily bisect it for 45 degrees. Similarly, bisecting the 60-degree angle from the previous slide gives you 30 degrees. These are all interconnected and built upon each other!

1Draw ray, arc from endpoint. Mark 60° & 120° on arc.
2From 60° and 120° marks, draw arcs intersecting above.
3Draw line from endpoint through this intersection (90°).

Construction: SSS (Side-Side-Side) Triangle

  • Given the lengths of all three sides.
  • Analogy: Fixing three sticks to form a stable structure.
  • Example: Construct ΔABC with AB=5cm, BC=6cm, CA=7cm.

🎙️ If you know all three side lengths of a triangle, you can construct it! Start by drawing one side as the base. Then, use your compass to draw arcs from each end of the base, with radii equal to the other two side lengths. Where these arcs intersect is the third vertex of your triangle.

1Draw the base side (e.g., AB).
2From A, draw an arc with radius AC.
3From B, draw an arc with radius BC, intersecting the first arc.
4Connect the intersection point (C) to A and B.
24 / 29

Construction: SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Triangle

  • Given two sides and the included angle.
  • Analogy: Using a hinge (angle) to connect two fixed beams (sides).
  • Example: Construct ΔPQR with PQ=6cm, ∠P=60°, PR=5cm.

🎙️ When you have two sides and the angle between them, you can construct the triangle. Draw one side first. At one end of this side, construct the given angle. Then, mark the length of the second side along the ray of that angle. Finally, connect the endpoints to complete the triangle.

1Draw one side (e.g., PQ).
2At P, construct the given angle (∠P).
3Along the new ray, mark off the second side's length (PR).
4Connect the endpoint (R) to Q.
25 / 29

Construction: ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Triangle

  • Given two angles and the included side.
  • Analogy: Surveying land with known angles and a shared boundary.
  • Example: Construct ΔXYZ with XY=7cm, ∠X=45°, ∠Y=60°.

🎙️ When you have two angles and the side between them, you can construct the triangle. Draw the given side first. Then, at each end of this side, construct the given angles. The point where the two rays from these angles meet will be the third vertex of your triangle.

1Draw the included side (e.g., XY).
2At X, construct the first angle (∠X).
3At Y, construct the second angle (∠Y).
4Extend the rays until they intersect to form the third vertex (Z).

Construction: RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side) Triangle

  • Given a right angle, the hypotenuse, and one side.
  • Analogy: Setting up a tent pole perpendicular to the ground with a guy rope.
  • Example: Construct a right ΔDEF, with ∠E=90°, DF=8cm, EF=5cm.

🎙️ This criterion is specific to right-angled triangles. Start by drawing one of the given sides. At one end of this side, construct the 90-degree angle. Then, from the other end of the base, draw an arc with the length of the hypotenuse. Where it intersects the 90-degree ray is your third vertex.

1Draw one side (e.g., EF).
2At E, construct a 90° angle.
3From F, draw an arc with radius DF (hypotenuse), intersecting the 90° ray.
4Connect the intersection point (D) to F.

Construction: Triangle given Perimeter and Two Base Angles

  • Given the perimeter and two base angles.
  • This is a slightly more advanced construction.
  • Example: Construct ΔABC where AB+BC+CA = 12cm, ∠B=60°, ∠C=45°.

🎙️ This construction is a bit clever! You start by drawing a line segment equal to the total perimeter. Then, you construct half of each base angle at the ends of this perimeter line. By finding the perpendicular bisectors of the segments formed, you locate the other two vertices, effectively 'folding' the perimeter line into the desired triangle.

1Draw a line segment (e.g., XY) equal to the perimeter.
2At X, construct angle ∠B/2. At Y, construct angle ∠C/2.
3Let the rays intersect at A. This is the third vertex.
4Construct perpendicular bisectors of AX and AY to find B and C.

Common Mistakes and Exam Success Strategies

  • **Inaccuracy:** Not using sharp pencils or precise compass settings.
  • **Erasing too much:** Erasing construction lines makes it hard to retrace steps.
  • **Wrong tool usage:** Using ruler for measurement when not allowed, or compass incorrectly.
  • **Practice regularly:** Repetition builds muscle memory and accuracy.
  • **Show all construction lines:** They are part of your answer and carry marks.
  • **Use sharp pencil and good compass:** For neat and accurate diagrams.

🎙️ Many students lose marks due to small errors. Always use a sharp pencil for thin, clear lines. Don't erase your construction lines; they show your work! Practice is your best friend here. A neat diagram with clear steps will fetch you full marks. Sometimes, you'll be asked to write the steps too, so understand the logic behind each action.
