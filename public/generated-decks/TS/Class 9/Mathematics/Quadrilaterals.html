What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • It has four vertices and four angles.
  • Formed by joining four points in order, with no three points collinear.
  • A closed figure.

🎙️ Simply put, a quadrilateral is any closed shape that has exactly four straight sides. Think of it as a polygon with four corners. The word 'quad' means four, and 'lateral' means side.

📐
Four-sided polygon

Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Vertices: A, B, C, D (the corners)
  • Sides: AB, BC, CD, DA (the line segments)
  • Angles: ∠A, ∠B, ∠C, ∠D (inside the corners)
  • Diagonals: AC, BD (lines joining opposite vertices)

🎙️ Every quadrilateral has specific parts. The points where two sides meet are called vertices. The lines connecting these vertices are the sides, and the space between two sides at a vertex forms an angle. Diagonals connect opposite vertices.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of all interior angles of a quadrilateral is 360°.
  • This is a fundamental property for all quadrilaterals.
  • Just like a triangle's angles sum to 180°, a quadrilateral's sum to 360°.

🎙️ This property is incredibly useful! No matter what type of quadrilateral you have, if you add up the measures of its four interior angles, the total will always be 360 degrees. Remember this rule, it will help you solve many problems.

➕
Angles add up

Real-life Example: Angle Sum Property

  • Consider a window pane, a common quadrilateral.
  • Each corner of a rectangular window is 90 degrees.
  • 90° + 90° + 90° + 90° = 360°.
  • Even irregular shapes like a map outline follow this rule.

🎙️ Look around your house! A window, a blackboard, or even a floor tile are often quadrilaterals. If it's a perfect rectangle, each angle is 90 degrees, and they clearly add up to 360. Even if the shape is a bit distorted, the rule still holds true.

Worked Example 1: Finding a Missing Angle

  • Problem: A quadrilateral has angles 70°, 85°, and 110°.
  • Find the measure of the fourth angle.
  • Solution: Sum of angles = 360°.
  • 70° + 85° + 110° + x = 360°.

🎙️ Let's apply the angle sum property. First, add the three given angles. Then, subtract this sum from 360 degrees. The remaining value will be the measure of the fourth angle. This is a common type of problem you will encounter.

Types of Quadrilaterals

  • Quadrilaterals can be classified based on their sides and angles.
  • Common types include Trapezium, Parallelogram, Rectangle, Rhombus, and Square.
  • Each type has unique properties.

🎙️ Just like animals are classified into different species, quadrilaterals also have different categories. These categories depend on specific characteristics like whether sides are parallel, or if angles are equal. We'll explore each type in detail.

Trapezium (or Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.
  • If legs are equal, it's an isosceles trapezium.

🎙️ The defining feature of a trapezium is that it has at least one pair of opposite sides that are parallel. Imagine railway tracks; they are parallel. In a trapezium, only one pair of sides acts like that. The other two sides can be non-parallel.

Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral where both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  • This is a more specific type of quadrilateral than a trapezium.
  • Has several important properties.

🎙️ A parallelogram is a special kind of quadrilateral where not just one, but both pairs of opposite sides are parallel to each other. This parallelism gives it a unique set of properties that distinguish it from other quadrilaterals.

Properties of a Parallelogram 1

  • Opposite sides are equal in length.
  • Opposite sides are parallel (by definition).
  • Example: If one side is 5 cm, the opposite side is also 5 cm.

🎙️ One of the key properties of a parallelogram is that its opposite sides are not only parallel but also equal in length. This means if you measure one side, the side directly across from it will have the exact same length. This is very useful for calculations.

Properties of a Parallelogram 2

  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add up to 180°).
  • Example: If one angle is 70°, the opposite angle is also 70°.

🎙️ Another important property concerns its angles. Opposite angles in a parallelogram are always equal. Also, any two angles that are next to each other (consecutive angles) will add up to 180 degrees. This is due to the parallel lines property.

Properties of a Parallelogram 3

  • The diagonals bisect each other.
  • This means they cut each other into two equal parts.
  • The point of intersection is the midpoint of both diagonals.

🎙️ When you draw the two diagonals inside a parallelogram, they will always intersect exactly at their midpoints. So, each diagonal is divided into two equal segments by the other diagonal. This property is unique and very helpful for coordinate geometry problems.

Worked Example 2: Using Parallelogram Properties

  • Problem: In parallelogram ABCD, AB=6cm, BC=4cm, ∠A=80°.
  • Find CD, AD, ∠C, and ∠B.
  • Solution: CD=AB=6cm, AD=BC=4cm (opposite sides).
  • ∠C=∠A=80° (opposite angles), ∠B=180°-80°=100° (consecutive angles).

🎙️ Let's apply the properties we just learned. Since opposite sides are equal, CD will be 6cm and AD will be 4cm. Since opposite angles are equal, angle C is 80 degrees. And since consecutive angles are supplementary, angle B will be 180 minus 80, which is 100 degrees.

3Identify consecutive angles

Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • All properties of a parallelogram apply to a rectangle.
  • Additionally, its diagonals are equal in length.

🎙️ A rectangle is a very familiar shape. It's essentially a parallelogram where all the corners are perfectly square, meaning they are 90 degrees. Because it's a parallelogram, it inherits all its properties, plus it has the special property of equal diagonals.

🖼️
All corners 90°

Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.
  • All properties of a parallelogram apply to a rhombus.
  • Additionally, its diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).

🎙️ A rhombus is another special type of parallelogram. Its defining characteristic is that all four of its sides are equal in length. Think of it as a 'tilted' square. Its diagonals have a unique property: they meet at a perfect 90-degree angle.

💎
All sides equal

Square

  • A quadrilateral that is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
  • All four sides are equal, AND all four angles are 90°.
  • It possesses all properties of parallelograms, rectangles, and rhombuses.
  • Diagonals are equal and bisect each other at 90°.

🎙️ The square is the 'king' of quadrilaterals! It combines the best of both worlds: it has all sides equal like a rhombus, and all angles are 90 degrees like a rectangle. This makes it a highly symmetrical and versatile shape with many applications.

Summary and Recap

  • Quadrilaterals are four-sided polygons.
  • Angle sum property: all interior angles sum to 360°.
  • Types: Trapezium (one pair parallel), Parallelogram (both pairs parallel).
  • Special Parallelograms: Rectangle (90° angles), Rhombus (equal sides), Square (both).
