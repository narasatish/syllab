Introduction to Surface Area

  • The total area of all the faces (surfaces) of a 3D object.
  • It's like wrapping a gift – the amount of paper needed.

🎙️ Imagine painting a box. The amount of paint you need depends on the surface area. It's the sum of the areas of all the flat or curved parts that make up the object's exterior.

The Cuboid: A Common Shape

  • A 3D shape with 6 rectangular faces.
  • Examples: brick, book, matchbox.

🎙️ A cuboid is one of the most common 3D shapes we encounter. It has length, breadth, and height, and all its angles are right angles.

🧱

Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • Total Area = Sum of areas of all 6 faces.
  • Formula: 2(lb + bh + hl).

🎙️ A cuboid has three pairs of identical rectangular faces. We calculate the area of each pair and then add them up. Remember, 'l' is length, 'b' is breadth, and 'h' is height.

1Area of front/back: 2(lb)
2Area of top/bottom: 2(bh)
3Area of left/right: 2(hl)
4Total: 2(lb+bh+hl)

Real-Life Example: Painting a Room

  • A room is a cuboid.
  • We need to find the surface area of walls and ceiling to paint.

🎙️ When painting a room, you usually don't paint the floor. So, we'd calculate the lateral surface area (four walls) plus the area of the ceiling. This is a practical application of surface area.

Worked Example: Cuboid Surface Area

  • Find SA of a cuboid: l=5cm, b=3cm, h=2cm.
  • Solution: 2(5x3 + 3x2 + 2x5) = 2(15 + 6 + 10) = 2(31) = 62 cm².

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. Substitute the given values carefully into 2(lb + bh + hl). Don't forget the units – surface area is always in square units.

The Cube: A Special Cuboid

  • A cuboid where all sides are equal (l=b=h=a).
  • All 6 faces are identical squares.

🎙️ A cube is a special type of cuboid where all its dimensions are the same. This simplifies its surface area and volume calculations. Think of a Rubik's cube or a dice.

Surface Area of a Cube

  • Each face is a square with area a².
  • Total Surface Area = 6 × a².

🎙️ Since a cube has 6 identical square faces, and the area of one square is side × side (a²), the total surface area is simply 6 times a². This is a straightforward formula to remember.

⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜
Six square faces

Worked Example: Cube Surface Area

  • Find SA of a cube with side 4 cm.
  • Solution: 6 × (4 cm)² = 6 × 16 cm² = 96 cm².

🎙️ Here, 'a' is 4 cm. Just plug this value into the formula 6a². Make sure you square the side length first before multiplying by 6.

The Cylinder: Round and Smooth

  • A 3D shape with two circular bases and a curved surface.
  • Examples: water pipe, soft drink can.

🎙️ Cylinders are everywhere, from the pillars of buildings to storage tanks. They have a uniform circular cross-section throughout their height.

🥤
A can of drink
14 / 25

Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • Total SA = Area of two bases + Area of curved surface.
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh.
  • Total SA = 2πr(r + h).

🎙️ Imagine unrolling the curved part of a cylinder; it forms a rectangle. Its length is the circumference of the base (2πr) and its width is the height (h). Add the areas of the two circular bases.

1Area of top circle: πr²
2Area of bottom circle: πr²
3Area of curved part: 2πrh
4Total SA: 2πr(r+h)
15 / 25

Worked Example: Cylinder Surface Area

  • Find SA of a cylinder: r=7cm, h=10cm (use π=22/7).
  • Solution: 2(22/7)(7)(7+10) = 2(22)(17) = 748 cm².

🎙️ Be careful with calculations involving π. Sometimes it's better to use 22/7, sometimes 3.14, depending on the given radius or height to simplify. Here, 7 cancels out nicely.

Volume of a Cuboid

  • Volume = Area of base × Height.
  • Formula: V = l × b × h.

🎙️ Volume is about how much space is inside. For a cuboid, you multiply its length, breadth, and height. Imagine filling it with tiny unit cubes.

📦📦📦📦📦
Stacking boxes
18 / 25

Worked Example: Cuboid Volume

  • Find volume of a cuboid: l=5m, b=3m, h=2m.
  • Solution: 5m × 3m × 2m = 30 m³.

🎙️ Remember that volume is always expressed in cubic units. If the dimensions are in meters, the volume will be in cubic meters.

Volume of a Cube

  • Since l=b=h=a, Volume = a × a × a.
  • Formula: V = a³.

🎙️ For a cube, all sides are equal. So, the volume is simply the side length multiplied by itself three times, or 'a' cubed.

🧊🧊🧊🧊🧊🧊🧊🧊
Building with cubes

Worked Example: Cube Volume

  • Find volume of a cube with side 4 cm.
  • Solution: (4 cm)³ = 4 × 4 × 4 cm³ = 64 cm³.

🎙️ This is a straightforward application of the formula. Just cube the given side length. Make sure you don't confuse it with surface area.

Volume of a Cylinder

  • Volume = Area of circular base × Height.
  • Formula: V = πr²h.

🎙️ Similar to a cuboid, the volume of a cylinder is found by multiplying the area of its base (which is a circle, πr²) by its height. This formula is very useful for containers.

Worked Example: Cylinder Volume

  • Find volume of a cylinder: r=7cm, h=10cm (use π=22/7).
  • Solution: (22/7) × (7)² × 10 = (22/7) × 49 × 10 = 22 × 7 × 10 = 1540 cm³.

🎙️ Again, pay attention to the value of π. Square the radius first, then multiply by π and the height. The units will be cubic centimeters.

Surface Area vs. Volume: A Comparison

  • Surface Area: Amount of 'skin' or outer covering.
  • Volume: Amount of 'stuff' inside or space occupied.

🎙️ It's crucial not to confuse these two concepts. Surface area is about the exterior, like wrapping paper. Volume is about the interior, like how much water a bottle holds. Both are important in different real-world scenarios.
