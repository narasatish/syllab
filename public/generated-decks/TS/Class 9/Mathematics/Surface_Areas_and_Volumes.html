Covering the Cuboid: Surface Area

  • The total area of all its faces.
  • Like wrapping paper needed for a gift.
  • Formula: TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)

🎙️ Surface area is simply the sum of the areas of all the flat surfaces that make up the cuboid. Imagine unfolding the box – it would be a flat pattern of rectangles, and you'd calculate the area of each.

Practical Use: Painting a Room

  • Room dimensions: Length=5m, Breadth=4m, Height=3m.
  • We need to paint walls and ceiling (LSA + area of top).
  • Area = 2(l+b)h + lb = 2(5+4)3 + 5x4 = 54 + 20 = 74 sq m.

🎙️ Here, we're finding the Lateral Surface Area (LSA) for the four walls and adding the area of the ceiling. This helps a painter know how much paint to buy.

The Cube: A Perfect Square Box

  • All 6 faces are identical squares.
  • Length = Breadth = Height = 'a'.
  • A dice or a sugar cube are examples.

🎙️ A cube is a special type of cuboid where all sides are equal. This symmetry makes its calculations a bit simpler, but the concept remains the same.

Covering the Cube: Simple Calculations

  • Each face has area 'a²'.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a².
  • Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 4a² (four walls).

🎙️ Since all faces are squares and identical, finding the surface area is straightforward. Just multiply the area of one face by the number of faces you need to cover.

⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜⬜

Example: Decorating a Cubical Gift Box

  • Side of box (a) = 10 cm.
  • We need to cover all sides.
  • TSA = 6a² = 6 * (10 cm)² = 6 * 100 cm² = 600 cm².

🎙️ If you have a cubical gift box, knowing its surface area helps you buy exactly the right amount of decorative paper without any wastage.

The Right Circular Cylinder: The Can Shape

  • Two circular bases (top and bottom).
  • A curved surface connecting them.
  • Like a water bottle or a drum.

🎙️ Cylinders are everywhere! From the pipes carrying water to the cans of food in your kitchen. They are characterized by their smooth, curved side and circular ends.

Covering the Cylinder: Curved and Total

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh (area of the 'label').
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r).
  • The 2πr² accounts for the top and bottom circles.

🎙️ Imagine cutting a cylinder's curved surface and unrolling it – it forms a rectangle! The length is the circumference (2πr) and the width is the height (h). Don't forget the two circular bases for TSA!

🔄
Unrolling a Cylinder

Example: Painting a Cylindrical Tank

  • Radius (r) = 7m, Height (h) = 10m.
  • We need to paint the entire outer surface (TSA).
  • TSA = 2πr(h+r) = 2 * (22/7) * 7 * (10+7) = 44 * 17 = 748 sq m.

🎙️ This calculation is crucial for industries that build or maintain large cylindrical tanks, like those used for storing water or oil, to estimate painting costs.

Covering the Cone: Slant Height is Key!

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = πrl (where 'l' is slant height).
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = πrl + πr² = πr(l+r).
  • Slant height (l) = √(r² + h²).

🎙️ For a cone, we need to consider the slant height 'l', not just the vertical height 'h', when calculating the curved surface area. It's the distance from the apex to any point on the circumference of the base.

📐

Example: Making a Conical Tent

  • Radius (r) = 3m, Height (h) = 4m.
  • First, find slant height (l) = √(3²+4²) = 5m.
  • Material needed (CSA) = πrl = (22/7) * 3 * 5 = 330/7 ≈ 47.14 sq m.

🎙️ If you're making a conical tent, you only need material for the curved surface, not the base. This calculation helps determine how much fabric is required.

The Sphere: A Perfectly Round Shape

  • It has no flat faces, edges, or vertices.

Covering the Sphere: One Simple Formula

  • It only has one curved surface.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 4πr².
  • This formula applies to the entire outer surface.

🎙️ Interestingly, the surface area of a sphere is equal to the area of four circles with the same radius. It's a beautiful mathematical relationship!

The Hemisphere: Half the Fun!

  • Exactly half of a sphere.
  • Has a curved surface and a flat circular base.
  • Examples: A bowl, a half-cut orange.

🎙️ A hemisphere is simply a sphere cut in half. This creates a new flat circular surface in addition to the curved one, which affects its total surface area.

🥣
A Bowl
22 / 30

Covering the Hemisphere: Two Parts

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πr² (half of a sphere's SA).
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr² (curved) + πr² (base) = 3πr².
  • Remember the flat base for TSA!

🎙️ Be careful here! The curved surface area is indeed half of a sphere's. But for the total surface area, you must add the area of the circular base.

Example: Polishing a Hemispherical Bowl

  • Radius (r) = 7 cm.
  • We need to polish the outer curved surface (CSA).
  • CSA = 2πr² = 2 * (22/7) * 7² = 2 * (22/7) * 49 = 2 * 22 * 7 = 308 sq cm.

🎙️ If you're polishing the outside of a bowl, you're calculating its curved surface area. If you were painting the inside and outside, and the rim, you'd need the TSA.

Volume: How Much Can It Hold?

  • The amount of space a 3D object occupies.
  • Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³).
  • Think of filling a container with water or sand.

🎙️ While surface area is about covering the outside, volume is about how much 'stuff' can fit inside. It's the capacity of a container.

Volume of Cuboids and Cubes

  • Cuboid: Volume = Length × Breadth × Height (lwh).
  • Cube: Volume = Side × Side × Side = a³.
  • Simple multiplication of dimensions.

🎙️ These are the most basic volume formulas. For a cuboid, it's essentially the area of the base multiplied by its height. For a cube, all dimensions are the same, making it a³.

Volume of Cylinders and Cones

  • Cylinder: Volume = πr²h (Area of base × height).
  • Cone: Volume = (1/3)πr²h.
  • A cone's volume is exactly one-third of a cylinder with same base and height!

🎙️ This 1/3 relationship between a cone and a cylinder is a very important concept. Imagine filling a cylinder with water using a cone of the same base and height – you'd need three cones full!

Volume of Spheres and Hemispheres

  • Sphere: Volume = (4/3)πr³.
  • Hemisphere: Volume = (2/3)πr³ (exactly half of a sphere).
  • These involve the cube of the radius.

🎙️ For spheres and hemispheres, the volume depends on the cube of the radius. Notice that the volume of a hemisphere is simply half the volume of a full sphere.

Beware! Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing LSA with TSA.
  • Using wrong units (e.g., cm² for volume).
  • Forgetting 'l' vs 'h' in cone formulas.
  • Calculation errors, especially with π or squares/cubes.

🎙️ Many students lose marks due to these simple errors. Always double-check which surface area (LSA or TSA) is required, use correct units, and be careful with your calculations.
