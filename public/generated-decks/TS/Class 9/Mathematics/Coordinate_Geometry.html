  • Uses two perpendicular lines
  • Forms a plane

  • Positive to the right, negative to the left

The Starting Point: The Origin

  • Where X-axis and Y-axis meet
  • Its coordinates are (0, 0)
  • The central reference point

🎙️ The spot where the X-axis and Y-axis cross is super important. We call it the Origin, and it's our starting point for measuring all other locations.

How to Describe a Point: (x, y)

  • An ordered pair of numbers
  • First number is x-coordinate (abscissa)
  • Second number is y-coordinate (ordinate)

🎙️ Every point on our map has a unique address, written as two numbers in parentheses, like (x, y). The first number tells us the horizontal position, and the second tells us the vertical position.

Plotting Points: Finding the Spot

  • Start at the origin (0,0)
  • Move horizontally (x-value)
  • Then move vertically (y-value)

🎙️ Let's learn how to actually 'find' a point on our Cartesian plane. It's like following directions – first go left or right, then go up or down.

1Start at (0,0)
2Move x units
3Move y units
4Mark the point
10 / 25

Example: Plotting (3, 2)

  • Start at (0,0)
  • Move 3 units to the right (positive x)
  • Move 2 units up (positive y)
  • Mark the spot!

🎙️ For the point (3, 2), we first move 3 steps to the right from the origin. Then, from there, we move 2 steps upwards. That's where our point lies!

🔴
Point (3,2)

Dividing the Plane: Quadrants

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions
  • Each region is called a quadrant
  • Numbered counter-clockwise

🎙️ The X and Y axes don't just help us locate points; they also divide our entire plane into four distinct sections. We call these sections 'quadrants'.

4️⃣
Four Regions
12 / 25

Signs in Each Quadrant

  • Q1: (+, +) - Both positive
  • Q2: (-, +) - X negative, Y positive
  • Q3: (-, -) - Both negative
  • Q4: (+, -) - X positive, Y negative

🎙️ The signs of the x and y coordinates tell us exactly which quadrant a point will fall into. It's a quick way to know its general location without plotting.

➕➖
Direction Signs
13 / 25

Points on the Axes

  • If y=0, point is on X-axis (e.g., (3,0))
  • If x=0, point is on Y-axis (e.g., (0, -2))
  • These points are not in any quadrant

🎙️ What about points that lie directly on the X-axis or Y-axis? They have a special characteristic: one of their coordinates will always be zero. These points are considered to be *on* the axes, not *in* a quadrant.
