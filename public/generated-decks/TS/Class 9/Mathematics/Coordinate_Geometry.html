The Two Guiding Lines: Axes

  • Imagine two number lines intersecting each other.
  • One is horizontal, called the X-axis (or abscissa).
  • The other is vertical, called the Y-axis (or ordinate).

🎙️ These two perpendicular lines form the framework of our coordinate plane. The X-axis helps us measure horizontal distances, and the Y-axis helps us measure vertical distances. They are our fundamental reference lines.

➕
Intersecting axes

Our Starting Point: The Origin

  • The point where the X-axis and Y-axis intersect.
  • It's our central reference point, like 'home' on a map.
  • Its coordinates are always (0, 0).

🎙️ The Origin is the starting point for all our measurements. Any movement to the right or up from the origin is positive, and any movement to the left or down is negative. It's the anchor of our system.

⚪
The Origin (0,0)

X-Axis: Horizontal Street, Y-Axis: Vertical Street

  • The X-axis runs left-to-right, like a horizontal road.
  • Positive numbers are to the right of the origin, negative to the left.
  • The Y-axis runs up-and-down, like a vertical road.
  • Positive numbers are above the origin, negative below.

🎙️ Think of it like a city grid. The X-axis tells you how far east or west you are, and the Y-axis tells you how far north or south. Together, they give you a unique position.

Giving Addresses: Coordinates

  • Every point on the plane has a unique 'address'.
  • This address is called its coordinates, written as an ordered pair (x, y).
  • The first number is the x-coordinate, the second is the y-coordinate.

🎙️ Just like your house has a unique address, every point on our coordinate plane has a unique ordered pair (x, y). Remember, the order matters! (2, 3) is different from (3, 2).

🏠
Point's Address (x,y)

The X-coordinate: How Far Sideways?

  • The x-coordinate is also called the abscissa.
  • It tells us the horizontal distance of a point from the Y-axis.
  • Positive 'x' means moving right from the origin; negative 'x' means moving left.

🎙️ The x-coordinate is your first step from the origin. It dictates your position along the horizontal axis. If x is 5, you move 5 units to the right. If x is -3, you move 3 units to the left.

➡️
Horizontal movement
11 / 30

The Y-coordinate: How Far Up or Down?

  • The y-coordinate is also called the ordinate.
  • It tells us the vertical distance of a point from the X-axis.
  • Positive 'y' means moving up from the origin; negative 'y' means moving down.

🎙️ After moving horizontally, the y-coordinate tells you how far to move vertically. If y is 4, you move 4 units up. If y is -2, you move 2 units down. This completes your point's location.

⬆️
Vertical movement

Putting it Together: (x, y)

  • To locate a point like (3, 2):
  • First, move 3 units along the X-axis (to the right).
  • Then, from that position, move 2 units parallel to the Y-axis (upwards).
  • This marks your point!

🎙️ Remember the sequence: 'x' first, then 'y'. It's like walking down a street (x) and then finding the correct building number (y). This ordered pair is unique for every point.

Dividing the Plane: Quadrants

  • The X and Y axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • These regions are called quadrants.
  • They are numbered I, II, III, and IV in an anti-clockwise direction.

🎙️ Each quadrant has a specific combination of positive and negative x and y coordinates. Knowing which quadrant a point lies in can give us a quick idea of its position.

➗
Divided plane
15 / 30

Quadrant I: The 'Positive-Positive' Zone

  • Located in the upper-right section of the coordinate plane.
  • Both the x-coordinate and the y-coordinate are positive.
  • Example: (4, 7) or (1.5, 2.5)

🎙️ This is where both your horizontal and vertical movements from the origin are in the positive direction. Think of it as moving right and then up. It's the 'top-right' area.

➕➕
Positive X, Positive Y
16 / 30

Quadrant II: The 'Negative-Positive' Zone

  • Located in the upper-left section of the coordinate plane.
  • The x-coordinate is negative, and the y-coordinate is positive.
  • Example: (-3, 5) or (-0.5, 6)

🎙️ Here, you move left from the origin (negative x) and then up (positive y). This quadrant is in the 'top-left' area. Always remember the sign convention for x and y.

↖️
Quadrant II direction
17 / 30

Quadrant III: The 'Negative-Negative' Zone

  • Located in the lower-left section of the coordinate plane.
  • Both the x-coordinate and the y-coordinate are negative.
  • Example: (-6, -2) or (-10, -1)

🎙️ In this quadrant, both movements are in the negative direction: left from the origin (negative x) and then down (negative y). It's the 'bottom-left' area of the plane.

↙️
Quadrant III direction
18 / 30

Quadrant IV: The 'Positive-Negative' Zone

  • Located in the lower-right section of the coordinate plane.
  • The x-coordinate is positive, and the y-coordinate is negative.
  • Example: (7, -4) or (2.5, -9)

🎙️ Finally, in Quadrant IV, you move right from the origin (positive x) and then down (negative y). This is the 'bottom-right' area. Knowing these sign patterns is crucial for plotting.

Points ON the Axes

  • What if a point doesn't fall into any quadrant?
  • If a point lies exactly on the X-axis or Y-axis, it's not in a quadrant.
  • These points have a special characteristic for their coordinates.

🎙️ Points on the axes are like being exactly on a street, not inside a building on that street. They are the boundaries between the quadrants and have a zero for one of their coordinates.

Point on X-axis: Always (x, 0)

  • Any point lying on the X-axis has its y-coordinate as 0.
  • This is because it has no vertical distance from the X-axis.
  • Examples: (5, 0), (-2, 0), (7.5, 0).

🎙️ If you are standing directly on the X-axis, you haven't moved up or down at all. So, your vertical position (y-coordinate) is zero. You only have a horizontal position.

↔️
Point on X-axis

Point on Y-axis: Always (0, y)

  • Any point lying on the Y-axis has its x-coordinate as 0.
  • This is because it has no horizontal distance from the Y-axis.
  • Examples: (0, 3), (0, -4), (0, 10).

🎙️ Similarly, if you are directly on the Y-axis, you haven't moved left or right from the origin. Your horizontal position (x-coordinate) is zero. You only have a vertical position.

Plotting Points: Step-by-Step

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move horizontally (along X-axis) according to the x-coordinate.
  • From there, move vertically (parallel to Y-axis) according to the y-coordinate.
  • Mark the final position with a dot and label it.

🎙️ Plotting is like following a treasure map! You start at the designated spot, take your first instruction (x-movement), then your second instruction (y-movement), and you'll find your treasure (the point).

2Move along X-axis (x-coord)
3Move parallel to Y-axis (y-coord)
4Mark the point

Let's Plot: Point B (-2, -1)

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Swapping x and y: Always remember (x, y), not (y, x).
  • Misidentifying quadrants: Be careful with positive and negative signs.
  • Not starting from the origin: All movements begin at (0,0).
  • Incorrect scaling: Ensure equal spacing on your axes.

🎙️ These are the most frequent errors students make. Paying attention to the order of coordinates, the signs, and starting from the origin will help you avoid these pitfalls and plot accurately.
