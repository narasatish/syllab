Understanding Area: Space Inside a Shape

  • Area is the amount of surface a 2D shape covers.
  • It's measured in square units (e.g., square centimeters).
  • Think of it as counting how many unit squares fit inside.

🎙️ Area tells us how much flat space a shape takes up. Imagine you're painting a wall; the area tells you how much paint you need. It's always about the inside, not the boundary.

Units of Area: Standard Ways to Measure

  • Common units: square centimeters (cm²), square meters (m²).
  • Larger areas: square kilometers (km²) for land.
  • Smaller areas: square millimeters (mm²) for tiny objects.

🎙️ Just like we use meters for length, we use square units for area. A square centimeter is the area of a square with 1 cm sides. Always remember to write the unit with a 'square' or '²'!

Area of a Rectangle: Length × Width

  • A rectangle has four straight sides and four right angles.
  • Formula: Area = Length × Width (or Breadth).
  • Length (l) is the longer side, Width (w) is the shorter side.

🎙️ The rectangle is one of the simplest shapes. To find its area, you just multiply its length by its width. This formula is fundamental and used in many real-world calculations.

Worked Example: Finding Room Area

  • A room is 5 meters long and 4 meters wide.
  • Area = Length × Width
  • Area = 5 m × 4 m = 20 m²

🎙️ Let's calculate the area for a typical room. First, we identify the given dimensions. Then, we apply the formula for the area of a rectangle. Don't forget the square units in your final answer!

1Identify Length (5m)
2Identify Width (4m)
3Multiply: 5 × 4
4Result: 20 m²

Area of a Square: Side × Side

  • A square is a special rectangle where all four sides are equal.
  • Formula: Area = Side × Side (or Side²).
  • If one side is 's', then Area = s × s.

🎙️ Since all sides of a square are equal, finding its area is even simpler. You just multiply the length of one side by itself. This is why we often see it written as 'side squared'.

Area of a Triangle: Half × Base × Height

  • A triangle is a polygon with three edges and three vertices.
  • Formula: Area = ½ × Base × Height.
  • The 'height' must be perpendicular to the 'base'.

🎙️ Triangles are everywhere! To find their area, we need the base and the perpendicular height. Imagine a rectangle cut diagonally in half – that's why we use 'half' in the formula.

Worked Example: Calculating Sail Area

  • A triangular sail has a base of 4 meters and a height of 6 meters.
  • Area = ½ × Base × Height
  • Area = ½ × 4 m × 6 m = 12 m²

🎙️ Let's calculate the area of our example sail. We substitute the base and height into the formula. Remember to multiply by one-half at the end to get the correct area.

1Identify Base (4m)
2Identify Height (6m)
3Calculate: ½ × 4 × 6
4Result: 12 m²

Area of a Parallelogram: Base × Height

  • A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides.
  • Formula: Area = Base × Height.
  • The 'height' is the perpendicular distance between the parallel bases.

🎙️ A parallelogram looks like a 'slanted' rectangle. If you cut a triangle from one end and move it to the other, it forms a rectangle! That's why its area formula is similar to a rectangle's: base times height.

Area of a Rhombus: ½ × d1 × d2

  • A rhombus is a quadrilateral with all four sides equal in length.
  • Its diagonals bisect each other at right angles.
  • Formula: Area = ½ × (diagonal 1 × diagonal 2).

🎙️ A rhombus is like a 'squashed' square. Instead of base and height, we use its diagonals. The diagonals cut the rhombus into four right-angled triangles, which helps derive this formula.

💎

Worked Example: Area of a Kite

  • A kite has diagonals of 10 cm and 8 cm.
  • Area = ½ × d1 × d2
  • Area = ½ × 10 cm × 8 cm = 40 cm²

🎙️ Kites are often shaped like rhombuses. Let's calculate the area of a kite using its diagonal lengths. Remember, the order of the diagonals doesn't matter since multiplication is commutative.

1Identify d1 (10cm)
2Identify d2 (8cm)
3Calculate: ½ × 10 × 8
4Result: 40 cm²

Area of a Trapezium: ½ × (a+b) × h

  • A trapezium (or trapezoid) has at least one pair of parallel sides.
  • Formula: Area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height.
  • The parallel sides are 'a' and 'b', 'h' is the perpendicular height.

🎙️ A trapezium has a unique shape with one pair of parallel sides. The formula takes the average of the lengths of these parallel sides and multiplies it by the height. This is a very useful formula in engineering and architecture.

Worked Example: Calculating Field Area

  • A trapezoidal field has parallel sides of 12 m and 8 m, and a height of 5 m.
  • Area = ½ × (a + b) × h
  • Area = ½ × (12 + 8) × 5 = ½ × 20 × 5 = 50 m²

🎙️ Let's calculate the area of this field. First, add the lengths of the parallel sides, then multiply by the height, and finally, divide by two. This gives us the total area in square meters.

1Identify parallel sides (12m, 8m)
2Identify height (5m)
3Calculate: ½ × (12+8) × 5
4Result: 50 m²
