What is a Circle?

  • A circle is a collection of all points in a plane.
  • These points are at a fixed distance from a fixed point (the center).

🎙️ Imagine drawing a shape where every point on its boundary is exactly the same distance from one central point. That's precisely what a circle is! It's perfectly round and has no corners.

⭕
Perfectly Round

Center and Radius

  • The fixed point is called the Center (O).
  • The fixed distance is called the Radius (r).
  • Radius connects the center to any point on the circle.

🎙️ The very middle point of the circle is its 'center'. Think of it as the heart of the circle. The distance from this center to any point on the circle's edge is always the same, and we call this distance the 'radius'.

🎯
The Heart of the Circle

Diameter

  • A line segment passing through the center.
  • It connects two points on the circle.
  • Diameter (d) = 2 × Radius (r).

🎙️ If you draw a straight line right through the center of the circle, connecting two points on its boundary, that line is called the diameter. It's always twice as long as the radius.

Circles in Real Life: The Wheel

  • A bicycle wheel is a great example of a circle.
  • The hub is the center, spokes are radii, and the rim is the circle itself.

🎙️ Look at a bicycle wheel! The central part where all the spokes meet is like the center of our circle. Each spoke acts as a radius, and the outer rim that touches the ground is the circle's boundary. Wheels make travel possible because they are perfect circles.

Chord

  • A line segment connecting any two points on a circle.
  • A diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

🎙️ Imagine picking any two points on the circle's edge and drawing a straight line between them. That line is called a chord. The special chord that passes through the center is the diameter, and it's always the longest possible chord.

📏
Connecting Points

Arc

  • A continuous piece of a circle.
  • It's a part of the circumference.
  • Can be a minor arc (smaller) or major arc (larger).

🎙️ An arc is simply a curved part of the circle's boundary. Think of it like a segment of a rainbow! We can have small arcs, called minor arcs, or larger ones, called major arcs.

🌈
A Curved Path

Segment of a Circle

  • The region enclosed by a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • Can be a minor segment or a major segment.

🎙️ If you cut a pizza straight across, not necessarily through the center, the piece you get is like a segment. It's the area bounded by a chord and the curved arc it cuts off.

🍕
Slice without the Center

Sector of a Circle

  • The region enclosed by two radii and the arc between them.
  • Looks like a 'slice' of the circle.
  • Can be a minor sector or a major sector.

🎙️ A sector is what you get when you cut a piece of cake or pizza starting from the center. It's the area defined by two radii and the arc connecting their endpoints.

🎙️ Next time you enjoy a slice of pizza, remember you're holding a mathematical sector! The pointed end is the center, the two straight edges are radii, and the curved crust is the arc.

Circumference of a Circle

  • The distance around the circle.
  • It's the perimeter of the circle.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd (where π ≈ 3.14 or 22/7).

🎙️ The circumference is simply the total length of the boundary of the circle. If you were to 'unroll' the circle and measure its length, that would be the circumference. We use the special number 'pi' (π) in its calculation.

🌐
Around the World

Area of a Circle

  • The amount of surface enclosed by the circle.
  • It's the space inside the circle.
  • Formula: A = πr².

🎙️ While circumference measures the 'outside' of the circle, the area measures the 'inside' - how much flat space the circle covers. This is also calculated using pi, but this time, the radius is squared.

🏞️
Space Inside

Worked Example: Calculating C & A

  • Problem: A circular swimming pool has a radius of 7 meters.
  • Find its circumference and area (use π = 22/7).

🎙️ Let's apply our formulas! For a pool with a 7-meter radius, the circumference would be 2 * (22/7) * 7, which is 44 meters. And the area would be (22/7) * 7 * 7, which is 154 square meters.

Congruent Circles

  • Circles are congruent if they have the same radius.
  • They are identical in size and shape.

🎙️ Congruent means 'exactly the same'. So, if two circles have the exact same radius, they are congruent. You could place one perfectly on top of the other, and they would match.

Theorem: Perpendicular from Center

  • The perpendicular from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
  • It divides the chord into two equal parts.

🎙️ This is an important property! If you draw a line from the center of a circle that meets a chord at a 90-degree angle, that line will always cut the chord exactly in half. This helps us find lengths in circles.

📐
Dividing a Chord
19 / 25

Worked Example: Using Chord Theorem

  • Problem: A chord of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle with radius 10 cm.
  • Find the distance of the chord from the center.

🎙️ Here, we can form a right-angled triangle using the radius, half the chord length, and the distance from the center. Using Pythagoras theorem, we can find this distance. Half the chord is 8 cm, radius is 10 cm, so distance squared = 10^2 - 8^2 = 100 - 64 = 36. Distance = 6 cm.

➕
Solving with Pythagoras

Theorem: Equal Chords are Equidistant

  • Equal chords of a circle (or congruent circles) are equidistant from the center.
  • Conversely, chords equidistant from the center are equal.

🎙️ This theorem tells us that if two chords have the same length, they will be the same distance away from the center of the circle. And if they are the same distance from the center, they must have the same length.

⚖️
Balanced Chords

🎙️ When you connect the two ends of a chord to the center of the circle, you form an angle. This angle is called the angle subtended by the chord at the center. It's crucial for future theorems about angles in circles.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on the circumference of a circle.
  • The circle is said to circumscribe the quadrilateral.

🎙️ Imagine a four-sided shape, a quadrilateral. If all its four corners, or vertices, touch the boundary of a single circle, then it's called a cyclic quadrilateral. It has some very special properties.

Properties of a Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • The sum of opposite angles is 180 degrees.
  • Example: Angle A + Angle C = 180°, Angle B + Angle D = 180°.

🎙️ The most important property of a cyclic quadrilateral is that its opposite angles always add up to 180 degrees. This makes it very useful in solving geometry problems involving angles.
