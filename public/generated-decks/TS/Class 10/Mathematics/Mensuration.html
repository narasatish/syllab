Surface Area of Combined Solids: Exposed Surfaces

  • When solids are combined, some surfaces disappear (are hidden).
  • We calculate the surface area of only the *exposed* parts of each solid.

🎙️ This is crucial! Don't just add the individual surface areas. Identify which surfaces are touching and therefore hidden. Only the parts visible from the outside contribute to the total surface area.

Calculating Toy's Surface Area - Step-by-Step

  • Identify components: Cone and Hemisphere.
  • Lateral Surface Area of Cone (CSA): πrl
  • Curved Surface Area of Hemisphere (CSA): 2πr²
  • Total SA = CSA of Cone + CSA of Hemisphere

🎙️ First, find the curved surface area of the cone. Then, find the curved surface area of the hemisphere. Notice we don't include the base of the cone or the flat top of the hemisphere, as they are joined!

1CSA of Cone
2+ CSA of Hemisphere
3= Total SA

Volume of Combined Solids: Simply Add Them Up!

  • Unlike surface area, when calculating volume, the space occupied by each solid is independent.
  • Total Volume = Volume of Solid 1 + Volume of Solid 2 + ...

🎙️ Calculating volume for combined solids is generally simpler. Since volume is the space occupied, we simply add the volumes of the individual components, as no space is 'hidden' or 'disappears'.

Calculating Gulab Jamun's Volume - Step-by-Step

The Golden Rule: Volume Stays the Same!

  • When a solid is converted from one shape to another, its mass and volume remain constant.
  • The amount of material doesn't change, only its form does.

🎙️ This is a fundamental principle: the total amount of material does not change during conversion. Therefore, the volume of the original solid will always be equal to the total volume of the new solid(s) formed.

Frustum of a Cone: Key Formulae

  • Volume = (1/3)πh (R² + r² + Rr)
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = πl (R + r)
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = CSA + Area of top base + Area of bottom base

🎙️ The frustum has two circular bases of different radii (R and r) and a height (h). 'l' is the slant height. These formulas are derived from the cone formulas, so they might look familiar.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Forgetting to exclude hidden surfaces for SA of combined solids.
  • Mixing up Surface Area and Volume formulas.
  • Errors in unit conversion (e.g., cm to m).
  • Incorrectly calculating slant height for cones/frustums.

🎙️ Many students make these errors, so be extra careful! Always visualize the shape, double-check your formulas, and pay attention to units in your calculations.
