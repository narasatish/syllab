What are Similar Figures?

  • Same shape, different size.
  • One is an enlargement or reduction of the other.
  • Angles remain the same, sides change proportionally.

🎙️ Imagine looking at a photo on your phone and then zooming in or out. The picture remains the same, but its size changes. That's the core idea behind similar figures – they maintain their shape but can vary in size.

Congruent vs. Similar: What's the Difference?

  • Congruent: Same shape AND same size.
  • Similar: Same shape, but DIFFERENT size.

🎙️ This is a crucial distinction. Congruent figures are like identical twins – perfectly matching. Similar figures are like a family photo where everyone has the same pose but some are children and some are adults – same form, different scale.

Conditions for Similarity: Equal Angles (AAA)

  • All corresponding angles must be equal.
  • If ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ The first key condition for two triangles to be similar is that their corresponding angles must be exactly the same. Imagine rotating and resizing one triangle; its angles won't change, only its side lengths.

📏
Angle Match
6 / 25

Conditions for Similarity: Proportional Sides (SSS)

  • Corresponding sides must be in the same ratio.
  • AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD = k (constant ratio).

🎙️ The second condition involves the sides. If one triangle is a scaled version of another, then all its sides must have been scaled by the same factor. This means the ratio of corresponding sides will be constant.

↔️
Side Ratios

Defining Similar Triangles Formally

  • Two triangles are similar if:
  • 1. Their corresponding angles are equal.
  • 2. Their corresponding sides are in the same ratio.

🎙️ So, combining both conditions, we get the formal definition. Both angles matching AND sides being proportional must hold true. We use the symbol '~' to denote similarity, for example, ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

AAA Similarity Criterion (Angle-Angle-Angle)

  • If all three corresponding angles are equal, the triangles are similar.
  • Often simplified to AA Similarity (two angles imply the third).

🎙️ This theorem states that if the three angles of one triangle are respectively equal to the three angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar. It's a powerful shortcut, as knowing just two angles are equal is often enough, because the third angle will automatically be equal.

📐
Angle Rule
11 / 25

Worked Example: Applying AAA Similarity

  • Given ΔABC and ΔPQR:
  • ∠A=40°, ∠B=60°, ∠P=40°, ∠Q=60°.
  • Are they similar? Why?

🎙️ Let's solve this together. Since ∠A = ∠P and ∠B = ∠Q, by AA similarity, ΔABC is similar to ΔPQR. Remember, we don't even need to check the third angle, as the sum of angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees.

12 / 25

SSS Similarity Criterion (Side-Side-Side)

  • If corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar.
  • AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD implies ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ This criterion focuses purely on the sides. If you can show that all three pairs of corresponding sides have the same ratio, then the triangles are similar. This means one triangle is simply an enlargement or reduction of the other.

↔️
Side Ratio Rule
13 / 25

Worked Example: Applying SSS Similarity

  • ΔABC: AB=3, BC=4, CA=5.
  • ΔPQR: PQ=6, QR=8, RP=10.
  • Are they similar? Find the ratio.

🎙️ Let's check the ratios: 3/6 = 1/2, 4/8 = 1/2, 5/10 = 1/2. Since all corresponding side ratios are equal to 1/2, by SSS similarity, ΔABC ~ ΔPQR. The ratio of similarity is 1:2.

14 / 25

SAS Similarity Criterion (Side-Angle-Side)

  • One angle equal, and the sides including that angle proportional.
  • If ∠A = ∠D AND AB/DE = AC/DF, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ SAS similarity is a combination. You need one pair of equal corresponding angles, AND the two sides that 'include' or 'form' that angle must be proportional. This is a very common and useful criterion.

📐↔️
Angle-Side Rule
15 / 25

Worked Example: Applying SAS Similarity

  • ΔABC: AB=4, AC=6, ∠A=50°.
  • ΔPQR: PQ=8, PR=12, ∠P=50°.
  • Are they similar?

🎙️ Here, ∠A = ∠P = 50°. Now check the sides including these angles: AB/PQ = 4/8 = 1/2. AC/PR = 6/12 = 1/2. Since the included angles are equal and the corresponding sides are proportional, by SAS similarity, ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) / Thales Theorem

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides proportionally.
  • If DE || BC, then AD/DB = AE/EC.

🎙️ This is a fundamental theorem! It tells us that when a line inside a triangle is parallel to one of its sides, it creates a smaller similar triangle and divides the other two sides into segments that are in proportion. This is extremely useful for finding unknown lengths.

➖
Parallel Line Rule
19 / 25

Worked Example: Applying BPT

  • In ΔABC, DE || BC.
  • AD=3cm, DB=6cm, AE=2cm.
  • Find EC.

🎙️ Using the BPT, we know that AD/DB = AE/EC. Substitute the given values: 3/6 = 2/EC. Now, solve this proportion for EC. You'll find that EC is 4 cm. This demonstrates how BPT helps us find unknown lengths.

20 / 25

Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle proportionally, then the line is parallel to the third side.
  • If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE || BC.

🎙️ The converse of BPT is equally important. It allows us to prove that a line segment is parallel to another side if we can show that it divides the other two sides in the same ratio. It's like working backwards from the BPT.

⬆️
Proportionality Implies Parallel

Areas of Similar Triangles: What's the Relationship?

  • Ratio of areas = (ratio of corresponding sides)²
  • Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (CA/FD)²

🎙️ This is a very important property! When triangles are similar, the ratio of their areas is not just the ratio of their sides, but the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides. This makes sense because area is a two-dimensional measurement.

🏞️
Area Scaling
23 / 25

Worked Example: Calculating Area Ratio

  • ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.
  • AB/PQ = 2/3.
  • Find Area(ΔABC) / Area(ΔPQR).

🎙️ Using the theorem we just learned, the ratio of the areas will be the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides. So, (2/3) squared is 4/9. This means the area of ΔABC is 4/9ths the area of ΔPQR.
