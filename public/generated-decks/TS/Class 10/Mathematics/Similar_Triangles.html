What are Similar Figures?

  • Figures that have the same shape, but not necessarily the same size.

Congruent vs. Similar: Key Differences

  • Congruent: Same Shape, Same Size
  • Similar: Same Shape, Different Size (usually)
  • All congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent.

🎙️ This slide clarifies the core difference. For congruent figures, both shape and size match perfectly. For similar figures, only the shape matches. A good way to remember is: if you can make one figure perfectly overlap the other by only scaling (enlarging or shrinking) and moving it, they are similar.

Understanding Similar Polygons

  • Two polygons are similar if:
  • 1. Their corresponding angles are equal.
  • 2. Their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).

🎙️ When we talk about polygons, like squares, rectangles, or triangles, being similar, two conditions must be met. First, all their matching angles must be equal. Second, the ratio of their matching sides must be constant. This constant ratio is called the scale factor.

AAA Similarity: Angle-Angle-Angle

  • If corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar.
  • Meaning: ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F
  • Then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF (The '~' symbol means 'is similar to').

🎙️ The AAA criterion is very intuitive. If all three angles of one triangle are equal to the corresponding three angles of another triangle, then their shapes must be identical, making them similar. Their sides will automatically be proportional.

➕

AA Similarity: The Shortcut!

  • If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the triangles are similar.
  • Why? The third angle will automatically be equal (angle sum property).
  • So, AA is enough to prove similarity!

🎙️ This is a super useful shortcut! Since the sum of angles in any triangle is 180 degrees, if you know two pairs of corresponding angles are equal, the third pair *must* also be equal. So, you don't always need to check all three angles, just two!

SSS Similarity: Side-Side-Side

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.
  • Meaning: AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD = k (a constant ratio)
  • Then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ The SSS similarity criterion focuses on the sides. If the ratio of all three pairs of corresponding sides is the same (meaning they are proportional), then the triangles must be similar. This 'k' is our scale factor.

SAS Similarity: Side-Angle-Side

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other triangle, and the sides including these angles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.
  • Meaning: ∠A = ∠D AND AB/DE = AC/DF
  • Then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ SAS similarity is a combination. You need one pair of equal angles, AND the two sides that *form* that angle must be proportional. It's crucial that the angle is *between* the proportional sides, just like in SAS congruence.

BPT: The Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • Also known as Thales Theorem.
  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle intersecting the other two sides, then it divides the two sides proportionally.
  • Visual: Draw a triangle ABC, line DE parallel to BC.

🎙️ Now let's dive into a very important theorem: the Basic Proportionality Theorem, or BPT. It's about what happens when you draw a line inside a triangle that's parallel to one of its sides. This theorem is a cornerstone for many proofs and problems.

⏸️
Parallel Lines

Understanding BPT Visually

  • Draw ΔABC.
  • Draw line DE parallel to BC, where D is on AB and E is on AC.
  • BPT states: AD/DB = AE/EC.

🎙️ Let's visualize this. Imagine a triangle ABC. If you draw a line segment DE inside it, such that DE is parallel to BC, then the theorem says that this line divides the sides AB and AC in the same ratio. This means AD divided by DB will be equal to AE divided by EC.

Using BPT: A Solved Example

  • In ΔABC, DE || BC.
  • If AD = 3cm, DB = 6cm, AE = 2cm.
  • Find EC.

🎙️ Let's apply BPT to a problem. We have a triangle with a parallel line. We know three lengths and need to find the fourth. Using the BPT ratio AD/DB = AE/EC, we can set up an equation: 3/6 = 2/EC. This simplifies to 1/2 = 2/EC, so EC = 4cm.

Converse of BPT: The Reverse!

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line must be parallel to the third side.
  • This is the opposite of BPT.
  • Useful for proving lines are parallel.

🎙️ Just like many theorems have a converse, BPT does too. The converse says: if you find a line segment inside a triangle that divides the two sides in the same ratio, then you can conclude that this line segment *must* be parallel to the third side. It's a powerful tool for proving parallelism.

Area Relationship of Similar Triangles

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • If ΔABC ~ ΔDEF, then Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (CA/FD)².

🎙️ This theorem is very important! It tells us how the areas of similar triangles relate to their sides. It's not just the ratio of sides, but the *square* of the ratio of their corresponding sides. This makes sense because area is a 2-dimensional measurement.

Area Theorem: A Practical Use

  • If ΔABC ~ ΔDEF, and AB=2cm, DE=4cm.
  • If Area(ΔABC) = 9 cm².
  • Find Area(ΔDEF).

🎙️ Let's apply the area theorem. We have two similar triangles and know the ratio of their sides (2/4 = 1/2). We also know the area of the smaller triangle. Using the formula, Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)², we get 9/Area(ΔDEF) = (1/2)², which means 9/Area(ΔDEF) = 1/4. So, Area(ΔDEF) = 36 cm².

Revisiting Pythagoras Theorem

  • For a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse).
  • Important for problems involving right triangles and similarity.

🎙️ While not directly a similarity theorem, Pythagoras theorem is often used in problems involving right-angled triangles, especially when similar triangles are formed by altitudes. A quick recap helps us connect these concepts when needed.

Right Triangles and Similarity

  • If a perpendicular is drawn from the vertex of the right angle to the hypotenuse of a right triangle:
  • Then the triangles on both sides of the perpendicular are similar to the whole triangle and to each other.
  • Creates three similar triangles!

🎙️ This is a fascinating property! When you drop an altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse, it creates two smaller triangles. Each of these smaller triangles is similar to the original large triangle, and they are also similar to each other. This means you have three similar triangles to work with!

Don't Get Caught! Common Mistakes

  • Confusing congruence with similarity.
  • Incorrectly identifying corresponding angles/sides.
  • Not squaring the ratio for area problems.
  • Applying BPT when lines are NOT parallel.

🎙️ It's easy to make small errors, so let's be aware of them. Always double-check if you're dealing with congruence or similarity. Make sure you match the correct corresponding parts. Remember the square for area, and crucial: BPT *only* works if the lines are parallel!
