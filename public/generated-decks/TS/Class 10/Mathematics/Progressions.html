What are Progressions?

  • A list of numbers following a specific rule
  • Each number is called a 'term'
  • Helps us predict what comes next

🎙️ Think of progressions as number patterns. They are sequences where each term is related to the previous one by a fixed rule, making them predictable.

Sequences vs. Series

  • Sequence: An ordered list of numbers (e.g., 2, 4, 6)
  • Series: The sum of the terms in a sequence (e.g., 2 + 4 + 6)

🎙️ It's important to differentiate between a sequence, which is just a list, and a series, which is the sum of the terms in that list. We will work with both today.

Arithmetic Progression (AP)

  • Each term is obtained by adding a fixed number
  • This fixed number is called the 'common difference' (d)
  • Can be increasing or decreasing

🎙️ Our first type of progression is the Arithmetic Progression. Here, the difference between consecutive terms is always the same, which we call the common difference.

Identifying an AP: Common Difference

  • To find 'd', subtract any term from its succeeding term
  • d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = ...
  • If 'd' is constant, it's an AP

🎙️ To check if a given list of numbers is an AP, simply calculate the difference between consecutive terms. If this difference remains constant throughout, then it's an AP.

Real-life AP: Ladder Rungs

  • The lengths of ladder rungs often decrease uniformly
  • Example: 45 cm, 42 cm, 39 cm, 36 cm, ...
  • Here, the common difference 'd' is -3 cm

🎙️ Imagine a ladder where the rungs get shorter as you go up. This is a perfect example of an AP, where each rung is a fixed amount shorter than the one below it.

General Form of an AP

  • An AP can be written as: a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, ...
  • 'a' is the first term
  • 'd' is the common difference

🎙️ Every Arithmetic Progression follows this general structure. Knowing the first term 'a' and the common difference 'd' allows us to write out the entire sequence.

Finding the nth Term of an AP

  • Formula: a_n = a + (n-1)d
  • a_n is the nth term we want to find
  • n is the position of the term in the sequence

🎙️ What if you need to find the 50th term without writing all the terms? This formula is your shortcut. It helps us find any term in an AP directly.

9 / 25

Worked Example: Find the 10th term

  • Given AP: 2, 7, 12, ...
  • Identify 'a' and 'd'
  • Apply the formula a_n = a + (n-1)d

🎙️ Let's apply the nth term formula to a real example. Here, our first term is 2 and the common difference is 5. We need to find the 10th term.

1Identify a=2, d=7-2=5
2Use formula: a_10 = 2 + (10-1)5
3Calculate: 2 + 9*5 = 2 + 45 = 47
10 / 25

Worked Example: Which term is 78?

  • Given AP: 3, 8, 13, 18, ...
  • We know a_n = 78, find 'n'
  • Substitute values into the formula

🎙️ Sometimes, we are given a term's value and asked to find its position. In this example, we need to find which term in the sequence 3, 8, 13, 18, ... is equal to 78.

1Identify a=3, d=8-3=5, a_n=78
2Substitute: 78 = 3 + (n-1)5
3Solve for n: 75 = (n-1)5 => 15 = n-1 => n=16

Sum of first n terms of an AP

  • Formula: S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
  • S_n is the sum of the first 'n' terms
  • Alternative: S_n = n/2 [a + a_n] (if last term a_n is known)

🎙️ Beyond finding individual terms, we often need to find the sum of a certain number of terms in an AP. This formula helps us do that efficiently without adding each term manually.

Worked Example: Sum of first 20 terms

  • Given AP: 1, 3, 5, ...
  • Identify 'a', 'd', and 'n'
  • Apply the sum formula S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]

🎙️ Let's calculate the sum of the first 20 terms for this AP. We have the first term, the common difference, and the number of terms, so we can directly use the sum formula.

1Identify a=1, d=3-1=2, n=20
2Use formula: S_20 = 20/2 [2(1) + (20-1)2]
3Calculate: 10 [2 + 19*2] = 10 [2 + 38] = 10 * 40 = 400

Geometric Progression (GP)

  • Each term is obtained by multiplying by a fixed number
  • This fixed number is called the 'common ratio' (r)
  • Terms grow or shrink rapidly

🎙️ Now let's move on to our second type of progression: Geometric Progression. Instead of adding, terms here are generated by multiplying by a constant factor, called the common ratio.

✖️

Identifying a GP: Common Ratio

  • To find 'r', divide any term by its preceding term
  • r = a₂/a₁ = a₃/a₂ = ...
  • If 'r' is constant, it's a GP

🎙️ Similar to how we found the common difference for an AP, we find the common ratio for a GP by dividing consecutive terms. This ratio must be constant for it to be a GP.

Real-life GP: Bacterial Growth

  • Bacteria often double in number every fixed period
  • Example: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, ... (doubling every hour)
  • Here, the common ratio 'r' is 2

🎙️ Bacterial growth is a classic example of a Geometric Progression. The number of bacteria multiplies by a constant factor, leading to very rapid growth.

Finding the nth Term of a GP

  • Formula: a_n = ar^(n-1)
  • a_n is the nth term we want to find
  • n is the position of the term in the sequence

🎙️ To find any specific term in a Geometric Progression, we use this powerful formula. It involves the first term 'a', the common ratio 'r', and the term number 'n'.

Worked Example: Find the 5th term

  • Given GP: 2, 6, 18, ...
  • Identify 'a' and 'r'
  • Apply the formula a_n = ar^(n-1)

🎙️ Let's apply the nth term formula for a GP. In this example, the first term is 2 and the common ratio is 3. We need to find the fifth term.

1Identify a=2, r=6/2=3
2Use formula: a_5 = 2 * 3^(5-1)
3Calculate: 2 * 3^4 = 2 * 81 = 162

Sum of first n terms of a GP

  • Formula: S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r-1) (for r > 1)
  • Formula: S_n = a(1 - r^n)/(1-r) (for r < 1)
  • S_n is the sum of the first 'n' terms

🎙️ Just like with APs, we can also find the sum of the first 'n' terms in a GP. There are two forms of the formula, depending on whether the common ratio 'r' is greater or less than 1.

Worked Example: Sum of first 4 terms

  • Given GP: 3, 6, 12, ...
  • Identify 'a', 'r', and 'n'
  • Apply the sum formula S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r-1)

🎙️ Let's calculate the sum of the first four terms for this GP. Since our common ratio is 2, which is greater than 1, we use the first version of the sum formula.

1Identify a=3, r=6/3=2, n=4
2Use formula: S_4 = 3(2^4 - 1)/(2-1)
3Calculate: 3(16 - 1)/1 = 3 * 15 = 45

Recap: Arithmetic vs. Geometric Progressions

  • AP: Constant 'common difference' (d)
  • GP: Constant 'common ratio' (r)
  • Both are predictable number patterns with specific formulas
  • Used in various real-life scenarios

🎙️ We've covered two main types of progressions today: Arithmetic and Geometric. Remember that APs involve addition/subtraction, while GPs involve multiplication/division. Both are powerful tools for understanding patterns!
