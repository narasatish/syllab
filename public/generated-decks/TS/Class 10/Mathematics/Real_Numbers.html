Euclid's Division Lemma

  • Given positive integers 'a' and 'b', there exist unique integers 'q' and 'r'.
  • Such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.
  • 'a' is dividend, 'b' is divisor, 'q' is quotient, 'r' is remainder.

🎙️ Now, let's move to some important theorems. Euclid's Division Lemma is a fundamental concept. It states that for any two positive integers, 'a' and 'b', we can always find unique whole numbers 'q' (quotient) and 'r' (remainder) such that 'a' equals 'b' times 'q' plus 'r', and the remainder 'r' is always less than 'b'.

➗
Dividend = Divisor x Quotient + Remainder

Euclid's Division Algorithm for HCF

  • A technique to compute the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two positive integers.
  • Repeatedly apply Euclid's Division Lemma.
  • The last non-zero remainder is the HCF.

🎙️ Building on the Lemma, we have Euclid's Division Algorithm. This is a powerful method to find the HCF of two numbers. It involves applying the division lemma repeatedly until the remainder becomes zero. The divisor at that final step is our HCF.

1Step 1: Apply EDL
2Step 2: Check remainder
3Step 3: New divisor/dividend
4Step 4: Repeat until remainder is 0

Worked Example: HCF of 135 and 225

  • Step 1: 225 = 135 × 1 + 90
  • Step 2: 135 = 90 × 1 + 45
  • Step 3: 90 = 45 × 2 + 0
  • HCF is 45 (the divisor when remainder is 0).

🎙️ Let's work through an example. To find the HCF of 135 and 225, we start by dividing 225 by 135. The remainder is 90. Then, we use 135 as the new dividend and 90 as the new divisor. We continue this process until we get a remainder of zero. The divisor at that step, which is 45, is our HCF.

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

  • Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes.
  • This factorization is unique, apart from the order of its prime factors.
  • Example: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 2² × 3.

🎙️ Next, we have the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. This theorem is extremely important and forms the basis of many number theory concepts. It basically says that any composite number can be broken down into a unique set of prime numbers multiplied together. The order doesn't matter, but the prime factors themselves are unique.

Worked Example: HCF & LCM using FTA

  • Find HCF and LCM of 6 and 20.
  • Prime factors of 6 = 2 × 3
  • Prime factors of 20 = 2 × 2 × 5 = 2² × 5
  • HCF = common factors (lowest power) = 2¹ = 2
  • LCM = all factors (highest power) = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60

🎙️ The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic is super useful for finding HCF and LCM. For example, to find the HCF and LCM of 6 and 20, we first find their prime factorizations. The HCF is the product of the common prime factors raised to their lowest powers. The LCM is the product of all prime factors raised to their highest powers.

Terminating Decimal Expansions

  • A rational number x = p/q (q ≠ 0) has a terminating decimal expansion.
  • IF the prime factorization of 'q' is of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ, where n and m are non-negative integers.
  • Example: 3/8 = 3/(2³) = 0.375.

🎙️ Here's the key rule: A rational number, when written in its simplest form p/q, will have a terminating decimal expansion if and only if the prime factors of its denominator, 'q', are only 2s and/or 5s. If 'q' has any other prime factor, it won't terminate.

✅
Rule for Terminating

Worked Example: Is 13/3125 Terminating?

  • Number: x = 13/3125
  • Denominator q = 3125
  • Prime factorization of 3125 = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 5⁵
  • Since q is of the form 2⁰5⁵, the decimal expansion is terminating.
  • 13/3125 = 0.00416

🎙️ Let's test this rule. Consider the number 13/3125. First, we look at the denominator, 3125. We find its prime factorization, which turns out to be 5 to the power of 5. Since this is of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ (where n is 0), we can confidently say that its decimal expansion will be terminating.

Non-Terminating Repeating Decimals

  • A rational number x = p/q (q ≠ 0) has a non-terminating repeating decimal expansion.
  • IF the prime factorization of 'q' is NOT of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ.
  • Meaning 'q' has prime factors other than 2 or 5.

🎙️ Conversely, if the prime factorization of the denominator 'q' contains any prime factor other than 2 or 5, then the rational number will have a non-terminating but repeating decimal expansion. This is the other side of the coin for rational number decimal forms.
