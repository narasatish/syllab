Quick Recap: Trigonometric Ratios

  • Sine (sin) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Cosine (cos) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Tangent (tan) = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ Yes, SOH CAH TOA helps us remember them! Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine is Adjacent over Hypotenuse, and Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent. These ratios are our tools for solving problems in this chapter.

Understanding the Line of Sight

  • The 'Line of Sight' is an imaginary line.
  • It connects the eye of an observer to the object being viewed.
  • It's crucial for defining angles in trigonometry applications.

🎙️ The first important concept is the 'Line of Sight'. This is simply the straight line from your eye, as the observer, to whatever object you are looking at. It forms one side of the triangle we will use in our calculations.

Angle of Elevation: Looking Up!

  • When you look UP at an object, the angle formed is the Angle of Elevation.
  • It is the angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight.
  • The object is above the horizontal level of the observer's eye.

🎙️ Now, let's talk about the 'Angle of Elevation'. This angle is created when you lift your head to look at something that is higher than you. It's always measured upwards from your horizontal line of sight.

Angle of Depression: Looking Down!

  • When you look DOWN at an object, the angle formed is the Angle of Depression.
  • It is the angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight.
  • The object is below the horizontal level of the observer's eye.

🎙️ Conversely, the 'Angle of Depression' occurs when you look downwards. Imagine you are on a balcony, and you look at something on the street below. The angle formed between your horizontal eye level and your line of sight to the object is the angle of depression.

Elevation vs. Depression: A Quick Comparison

  • Both angles are measured with respect to the horizontal line.
  • Elevation is when the object is above the observer's horizontal level.
  • Depression is when the object is below the observer's horizontal level.
  • They are often alternate interior angles in problem diagrams.

🎙️ It's important to remember that both angles, elevation and depression, are always measured from a horizontal line. The key difference is whether you're looking up or down. Often, in problems, the angle of depression from one point is equal to the angle of elevation from another point due to parallel lines.

  • The angle of elevation to the top of the tower is 45°.
  • Find the height of the tower.

🎙️ Let's try our first example. Imagine you're standing a certain distance from a tower, and you look up at its top. We are given the distance and the angle of elevation. Our goal is to find the height of the tower. Think about which ratio relates opposite and adjacent sides.

Solution 1: Drawing and Identifying

  • Let AB be the tower and C be the observer's position.
  • BC = 15m (distance from tower).
  • ∠BCA = 45° (angle of elevation).
  • We need to find AB (height of the tower).

🎙️ First, draw a right-angled triangle ABC, where B is the base of the tower, A is the top, and C is where the observer stands. The angle at B is 90 degrees. We know the adjacent side BC and the angle C. We want to find the opposite side AB.

15 / 25

Solution 1: Applying the Right Ratio

  • We have the 'Adjacent' side (BC) and need the 'Opposite' side (AB).
  • The trigonometric ratio that relates Opposite and Adjacent is Tangent.
  • So, tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.

🎙️ Since we have the adjacent side and need to find the opposite side relative to the given angle, the tangent ratio is perfect here. Remember, tan(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent. Let's substitute our known values into this equation.

➗
Choosing the Ratio
16 / 25

Solution 1: Final Calculation

  • tan(45°) = AB / 15
  • We know tan(45°) = 1.
  • 1 = AB / 15
  • AB = 15 meters.

🎙️ Substituting tan 45 degrees, which is 1, we get 1 equals AB divided by 15. A simple cross-multiplication gives us the height of the tower. So, the tower is 15 meters tall. See how easy it is with trigonometry?

🎙️ Now, let's try an example involving the angle of depression. Imagine a lighthouse standing tall, and from its top, we spot a ship. We know the height of the lighthouse and the angle of depression. We need to find the horizontal distance to the ship.

Solution 2: Setting up the Problem

  • Let AB be the lighthouse and C be the ship's position.
  • AB = 75m (height of lighthouse).
  • Angle of depression from A to C is 30°.
  • Draw a horizontal line AX from A. Then ∠XAC = 30°.
  • Since AX || BC, ∠ACB = ∠XAC = 30° (Alternate Interior Angles).

🎙️ Draw a right-angled triangle ABC, where AB is the lighthouse and BC is the distance we need to find. The angle of depression is outside our triangle, but by using the property of alternate interior angles with the horizontal line, we find that the angle of elevation from the ship to the top of the lighthouse is also 30 degrees.

19 / 25

Solution 2: Using Trigonometric Ratios

  • In ΔABC, we have the 'Opposite' side (AB) and need the 'Adjacent' side (BC).
  • Again, the Tangent ratio is suitable here.
  • tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.

🎙️ With angle C now known as 30 degrees, we have the opposite side AB (height of the lighthouse) and we need to find the adjacent side BC (distance to the ship). The tangent ratio, once again, is the perfect choice for this relationship.

Solution 2: Finding the Distance

  • tan(30°) = AB / BC
  • tan(30°) = 1 / √3
  • 1 / √3 = 75 / BC
  • BC = 75√3 meters ≈ 129.9 meters.

🎙️ Substituting the values, tan 30 degrees is 1/√3. So, 1/√3 equals 75 divided by BC. Solving for BC, we get 75√3 meters. If we approximate √3 as 1.732, the distance is about 129.9 meters. This shows how trigonometry helps measure distances across water!
