What is the Line of Sight?

  • It's an imaginary line from your eye to the object you're looking at.
  • It forms the hypotenuse of our imaginary right-angled triangle.
  • Always straight, connecting observer to object.

🎙️ The Line of Sight is like a laser beam shooting from your eye directly to what you're observing. It's the path your gaze takes, and it's crucial for forming our triangles.

Angle of Elevation: Looking Up!

  • Angle formed between the Line of Sight and the horizontal level.
  • When the object is above the observer's horizontal level.
  • Always measured upwards from the horizontal.

🎙️ When you look at an object that is higher than you, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal line from your eye is the Angle of Elevation. Remember, it's always measured from the horizontal.

Angle of Depression: Looking Down!

  • Angle formed between the Line of Sight and the horizontal level.
  • When the object is below the observer's horizontal level.
  • Always measured downwards from the horizontal.

🎙️ When you look at an object that is lower than you, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal line from your eye is the Angle of Depression. It's crucial to remember this horizontal reference.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing Angle of Elevation with Angle of Depression.
  • Not drawing a clear diagram first.
  • Incorrectly applying SOH CAH TOA (e.g., using sin for adjacent/hypotenuse).
  • Not converting units or forgetting units in the answer.

🎙️ Many students make these small errors. Always take your time to draw the diagram correctly and carefully choose your trigonometric ratio. Double-check your calculations and units!
