What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of a triangle.
  • Specifically focuses on right-angled triangles.
  • The word comes from Greek: 'trigonon' (triangle) and 'metron' (measure).

🎙️ Simply put, trigonometry is all about measuring triangles. It helps us find unknown sides or angles when we know some others. This is incredibly useful in many real-world situations.

Right-Angled Triangle Basics

  • Has one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The longest side is the Hypotenuse (opposite 90°).
  • Other two sides are Opposite and Adjacent to a reference angle.

🎙️ Trigonometry mainly deals with right-angled triangles. Remember the Pythagorean theorem? That's your first step. Now, let's learn how to label the other two sides based on a chosen angle.

🔺

Identifying Sides in a Right Triangle

  • **Hypotenuse:** Always opposite the 90° angle.
  • **Opposite Side:** Directly across from the reference angle (θ).
  • **Adjacent Side:** Next to the reference angle (θ), not the hypotenuse.

🎙️ This is a critical step. Always start by finding the hypotenuse. Then, pick one of the acute angles as your reference angle, usually denoted by theta (θ). The side facing θ is opposite, and the side next to θ (but not the hypotenuse) is adjacent.

Trigonometric Ratios - The Big Idea

  • Ratios of the lengths of sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • These ratios relate an angle to the side lengths.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.
  • They are constant for a given angle, regardless of triangle size.

🎙️ Imagine you have two similar right-angled triangles. Even if they are different sizes, the ratio of their corresponding sides will be the same. Trigonometric ratios capture these constant relationships for specific angles.

📏
Side Relationships
7 / 25

Sine (sin) Ratio

  • **Sine of an angle (sin θ) = Opposite side / Hypotenuse.**
  • Often remembered as SOH (Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse).
  • Value always between 0 and 1 for acute angles.

🎙️ The sine ratio helps us connect the side opposite our reference angle with the hypotenuse. For example, if you know the angle of a ramp and its length (hypotenuse), you can find its vertical height (opposite side) using sine.

🌊
Opposite / Hypotenuse
8 / 25

Cosine (cos) Ratio

  • **Cosine of an angle (cos θ) = Adjacent side / Hypotenuse.**
  • Often remembered as CAH (Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse).
  • Value always between 0 and 1 for acute angles.

🎙️ Cosine relates the adjacent side to the hypotenuse. Think of finding the horizontal distance a ladder reaches from a wall if you know its length and the angle it makes with the ground. That's a job for cosine!

🧭
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
9 / 25

Tangent (tan) Ratio

  • **Tangent of an angle (tan θ) = Opposite side / Adjacent side.**
  • Often remembered as TOA (Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent).
  • Can also be written as sin θ / cos θ.

🎙️ Tangent is unique because it doesn't involve the hypotenuse directly. It's useful when you're dealing with the two legs of the right triangle. For instance, finding the height of a tree knowing your distance from it and the angle to its top.

⛰️
Opposite / Adjacent

Remembering the Ratios

  • **SOH**: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • **CAH**: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • **TOA**: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent
  • A simple way to memorize the first three ratios!

🎙️ This mnemonic, 'SOH CAH TOA', is your best friend for remembering these three fundamental ratios. Practice saying it out loud until it sticks in your mind. It will make solving problems much easier.

🧠
Memory Trick

Cosecant (csc) Ratio

  • **Cosecant of an angle (csc θ) = 1 / sin θ.**
  • It is the reciprocal of the sine ratio.
  • **csc θ = Hypotenuse / Opposite side.**

🎙️ Just as every number has a reciprocal, so do our trigonometric ratios. Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine. If you know sine, you can easily find cosecant by flipping the fraction.

🔄
Reciprocal of Sine
12 / 25

Secant (sec) Ratio

  • **Secant of an angle (sec θ) = 1 / cos θ.**
  • It is the reciprocal of the cosine ratio.
  • **sec θ = Hypotenuse / Adjacent side.**

🎙️ Similarly, secant is the reciprocal of cosine. Once you've calculated cosine, finding secant is just a matter of inverting the fraction. These reciprocal ratios are also very important in higher trigonometry.

↔️
Reciprocal of Cosine
13 / 25

Cotangent (cot) Ratio

  • **Cotangent of an angle (cot θ) = 1 / tan θ.**
  • It is the reciprocal of the tangent ratio.
  • **cot θ = Adjacent side / Opposite side.**
  • Also, cot θ = cos θ / sin θ.

🎙️ And finally, cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent. Knowing the first three ratios (sin, cos, tan) allows you to easily find the other three (csc, sec, cot) by simply taking their reciprocals.

🔁
Reciprocal of Tangent

Angles of Elevation & Depression

  • **Angle of Elevation:** Angle from horizontal *up* to an object.
  • **Angle of Depression:** Angle from horizontal *down* to an object.
  • Crucial for solving real-world height and distance problems.

🎙️ These two angles are very important in applications. Always remember they are measured from a *horizontal* line. If you're looking up at a bird, it's an angle of elevation. If you're looking down from a cliff, it's an angle of depression.

Special Angles: csc, sec, cot
