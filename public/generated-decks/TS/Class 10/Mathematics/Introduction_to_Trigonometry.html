Naming the Sides

  • Hypotenuse: The longest side, opposite the 90° angle.
  • Opposite Side: The side opposite to the reference angle.
  • Adjacent Side: The side next to the reference angle (not hypotenuse).

🎙️ Every right-angled triangle has three unique sides. The longest one, always opposite the 90-degree angle, is the Hypotenuse. The other two, 'Opposite' and 'Adjacent', depend on which acute angle we are looking at.

The Six Key Ratios

  • These are ratios of the sides of a right triangle.
  • They relate angles to side lengths.
  • Six main ratios: Sine, Cosine, Tangent, and their reciprocals.

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are simply fractions that compare the lengths of two sides of a right-angled triangle. These ratios help us connect the angles of a triangle with the lengths of its sides. There are six main ratios we'll learn.

➗➗➗➗➗➗
Six Ratios
10 / 30

Introducing Sine (sin)

  • Ratio of the length of the Opposite side to the Hypotenuse.
  • sin(angle) = Opposite / Hypotenuse.
  • Example: For angle A, sin A = BC / AC.

🎙️ The first ratio is Sine, often written as 'sin'. It's defined as the length of the side opposite to our reference angle divided by the length of the hypotenuse. Remember, 'sin' is always a ratio.

📏
Opposite / Hypotenuse

Introducing Cosine (cos)

  • Ratio of the length of the Adjacent side to the Hypotenuse.
  • cos(angle) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.
  • Example: For angle A, cos A = AB / AC.

🎙️ Next up is Cosine, written as 'cos'. This ratio compares the length of the side adjacent to our reference angle with the length of the hypotenuse. It gives us another way to relate the angle to the sides.

↔️
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
12 / 30

Introducing Tangent (tan)

  • Ratio of the length of the Opposite side to the Adjacent side.
  • tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • Example: For angle A, tan A = BC / AB.

🎙️ Finally, we have Tangent, or 'tan'. This ratio is different as it doesn't involve the hypotenuse directly. It's the length of the opposite side divided by the length of the adjacent side. An interesting fact is that tan A can also be written as sin A / cos A.

The Other Three Ratios

  • Each of the primary ratios has a reciprocal.
  • These are Cosecant, Secant, and Cotangent.
  • They are simply 1 divided by the primary ratio.

🎙️ Just like how 1/2 is the reciprocal of 2, our three main trigonometric ratios also have their reciprocals. These are cosecant, secant, and cotangent, and they are very straightforward to understand once you know sine, cosine, and tangent.

🔄🔄🔄
Flipping Ratios
15 / 30

Cosecant (csc) - Reciprocal of Sine

  • csc(angle) = 1 / sin(angle).
  • csc(angle) = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • Example: For angle A, csc A = AC / BC.

🎙️ Cosecant, abbreviated as 'csc', is the reciprocal of sine. So, if sin A is Opposite/Hypotenuse, then csc A is simply Hypotenuse/Opposite. It's like flipping the fraction!

Hypotenuse / Opposite

Secant (sec) - Reciprocal of Cosine

  • sec(angle) = 1 / cos(angle).
  • sec(angle) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.
  • Example: For angle A, sec A = AC / AB.

🎙️ Secant, or 'sec', is the reciprocal of cosine. So, it's defined as the hypotenuse divided by the adjacent side. Easy to remember once you know cosine!

↗️
Hypotenuse / Adjacent

Cotangent (cot) - Reciprocal of Tangent

  • cot(angle) = 1 / tan(angle).
  • cot(angle) = Adjacent / Opposite.
  • Example: For angle A, cot A = AB / BC.

🎙️ And finally, Cotangent, or 'cot', is the reciprocal of tangent. This means it's the adjacent side divided by the opposite side. With these six ratios, you can describe any angle-side relationship in a right triangle.

Understanding 0° and 90°

  • These are 'limiting' cases, visualized by shrinking/stretching triangles.
  • When angle approaches 0°, opposite side approaches 0, adjacent approaches hypotenuse.
  • When angle approaches 90°, opposite side approaches hypotenuse, adjacent approaches 0.

🎙️ The angles 0 degrees and 90 degrees are special because they represent extreme cases. We can think of them by imagining a right triangle where one of the acute angles becomes very, very small (close to 0) or very, very large (close to 90). The sides change dramatically in these scenarios.

Special Trigonometric Equations

  • Equations that are true for all values of the angle.
  • The most fundamental: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
  • These identities help simplify complex expressions.

🎙️ Trigonometric identities are like special equations that are always true, no matter what valid angle you put into them. The most famous and fundamental one is sin squared theta plus cos squared theta equals one. These identities are powerful tools for solving more complex problems in trigonometry.

Watch Out for These Pitfalls!

  • Confusing Opposite/Adjacent sides: Always refer to the angle.
  • Mixing up reciprocal ratios: sin is 1/csc, not 1/sec.
  • Calculation errors with specific angle values: Practice makes perfect.

🎙️ Even experienced students can make simple mistakes. Be very careful when identifying the opposite and adjacent sides, as they depend on your reference angle. Also, make sure you don't confuse the reciprocal ratios. And always double-check your calculations, especially with square roots!
