What is a Circle?

  • A set of points equidistant from a central point.
  • Has a radius (distance from center to edge) and a diameter (across the center).
  • We see circles everywhere around us!

🎙️ Before we talk about tangents and secants, let's quickly recap what a circle is. Remember, it's a perfectly round shape where every point on its boundary is the same distance from its center.

Line Intersecting at One Point: A Tangent

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • This unique point is called the 'point of contact'.
  • The distance from the center to the line is exactly equal to the radius.

🎙️ Now, for our main topic: a 'tangent'. A tangent is a line that touches the circle at only one single point. It doesn't cut through; it just grazes the edge. This single point is very important!

Tangent vs. Secant: A Quick Comparison

  • Tangent: Intersects at one point (point of contact).
  • Secant: Intersects at two distinct points.
  • Tangent: Touches the edge. Secant: Cuts through the interior.

🎙️ Let's quickly compare these two important lines. The key difference is the number of points of intersection with the circle: one for a tangent, and two for a secant.

Property 1: Tangent is Perpendicular to Radius

  • The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius.
  • This radius is drawn through the point of contact.
  • This means they form a 90-degree angle.

🎙️ This is the first and a very important property of tangents. Wherever a tangent touches a circle, if you draw a radius to that exact point of contact, the radius and the tangent will always meet at a perfect right angle.

Why is it Perpendicular? (Explanation)

  • The shortest distance from a point (center) to a line (tangent) is always the perpendicular distance.
  • The radius is the shortest distance from the center to the tangent line.
  • Any other line from the center to a point on the tangent would be longer than the radius.

🎙️ Why is this true? Because the radius drawn to the point of contact is the shortest possible distance from the center to the tangent line. And the shortest distance from a point to a line is always along the perpendicular.

1Draw radius to contact point
2Take any other point on tangent
3Connect center to that point
4This new line is longer than radius

Example 1: Using Property 1

  • A tangent AB at point A to a circle with center O has a radius of 5 cm.
  • If the distance from the center O to point B on the tangent is 13 cm,
  • Find the length of the tangent segment AB.

🎙️ Let's apply this property. We have a right-angled triangle OAB, where OA is the radius (5 cm) and OB is the hypotenuse (13 cm). We can use the Pythagorean theorem: OA² + AB² = OB² to find AB.

Tangents from a Point INSIDE the Circle

  • If a point P lies inside the circle,
  • NO tangent can be drawn from it to the circle.
  • Any line passing through an internal point will always be a secant.

Property 2: Equal Tangent Lengths

  • The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are EQUAL.
  • If PA and PB are tangents from point P, then PA = PB.
  • This is another very important property for solving problems!

🎙️ Yes, they are equal! This is our second major property: the lengths of the two tangent segments from an external point to a circle are always equal. This fact is extremely useful in many geometry problems.

⚖️
Perfect Balance
19 / 25

Why are Tangent Lengths Equal? (Explanation)

  • Draw radii OA and OB to the points of contact, and join OP.
  • This forms two right-angled triangles: ΔOAP and ΔOBP (from Property 1).
  • These two triangles are congruent by RHS (Right angle, Hypotenuse, Side) rule.
  • Therefore, their corresponding parts, PA and PB, must be equal.

🎙️ We can prove this using triangle congruence. By drawing radii to the points of contact and connecting the external point to the center, we form two right-angled triangles that can be proven congruent using the RHS rule. This directly shows that the tangent lengths are equal.

1Draw radii to contact points
2Connect external point to center
3Identify two right triangles
4Prove congruence (RHS rule)
5Conclude tangent lengths are equal

  • If the length of tangent PA is 8 cm,
  • What is the length of tangent PB?

🎙️ This is a straightforward application of Property 2. Since PA and PB are tangents from the same external point P, their lengths must be equal. So, if PA is 8 cm, then PB is also 8 cm.

Chapter Recap: Tangents and Secants

  • Secant: A line intersecting a circle at two distinct points.
  • Tangent: A line touching a circle at exactly one point (point of contact).
  • Property 1: The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact (forms 90°).
  • Property 2: The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
