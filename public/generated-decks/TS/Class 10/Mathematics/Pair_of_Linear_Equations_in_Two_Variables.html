Defining a Linear Equation in Two Variables

  • An equation that can be written in the form ax + by + c = 0.
  • Where a, b, c are real numbers, and a and b are not both zero.
  • x and y are the two variables.

🎙️ So, formally, a linear equation in two variables looks like this. Remember, 'linear' means the highest power of the variables is 1, and 'two variables' means we have two different unknowns, usually x and y.

What are the Solutions?

  • A pair of values (x, y) that satisfies the equation.
  • For 2x + 3y = 60, (15, 10) is a solution (2*15 + 3*10 = 30 + 30 = 60).
  • There are infinitely many solutions for a single linear equation in two variables.

🎙️ A solution isn't just one number anymore; it's a pair of numbers, one for x and one for y, that makes the equation true. And guess what? A single equation like this has endless possibilities for solutions!

What is a Pair of Linear Equations?

  • Two linear equations in the same two variables.
  • General form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0.
  • We look for a common solution (x, y) that satisfies *both* equations.

🎙️ So, a pair means we have two such equations. Our goal is to find values for x and y that work for *both* equations simultaneously. This common solution is what helps us solve real-world puzzles.

Case 1: Intersecting Lines

  • The two lines cross each other at exactly one point.
  • This point of intersection (x, y) is the *unique solution* to the pair of equations.
  • Condition: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂

🎙️ If the lines cross, they have one common point. This point is the only (x, y) pair that satisfies both equations, giving us a unique solution to our problem.

✖️
One Meeting Point
16 / 30

Case 2: Parallel Lines

  • The two lines never cross; they run side-by-side.
  • They have no common point, meaning *no solution* exists for the pair.
  • Condition: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂

🎙️ Just like railway tracks, parallel lines never meet. If your equations result in parallel lines, it means there's no (x, y) pair that can satisfy both conditions simultaneously.

🛤️
Never Meet
17 / 30

Case 3: Coincident Lines

  • One line lies exactly on top of the other line.
  • Every point on one line is also on the other line.
  • This means *infinitely many solutions* exist.
  • Condition: a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

🎙️ Coincident lines are essentially the same line. Since every point on one is also on the other, they share all their points, leading to an infinite number of common solutions.

Algebraic Method 1: Substitution

  • Step 1: Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation.
  • Step 2: Substitute this expression into the *other* equation.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting linear equation in one variable.
  • Step 4: Substitute the found value back into the expression from Step 1 to find the other variable.

🎙️ The substitution method is like replacing one unknown with its equivalent expression from another equation. It simplifies the problem into a single-variable equation, which you already know how to solve.

2Substitute

Example: Solving by Substitution

  • Solve: x + y = 14 and x - y = 4.
  • From (1), x = 14 - y.
  • Substitute into (2): (14 - y) - y = 4 => 14 - 2y = 4 => 2y = 10 => y = 5.
  • Substitute y=5 back: x = 14 - 5 => x = 9. Solution: (9, 5).

🎙️ Let's walk through an example. See how we isolate one variable, plug it into the second equation, solve for one, and then find the other. It's like a puzzle!

Algebraic Method 2: Elimination

  • Step 1: Multiply equations by constants to make coefficients of one variable equal.
  • Step 2: Add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting equation for the remaining variable.
  • Step 4: Substitute this value into either original equation to find the other variable.

🎙️ The elimination method is powerful. We strategically modify the equations so that when we add or subtract them, one of the variables simply vanishes, making it easy to solve for the other.

Example: Solving by Elimination

  • Solve: 2x + 3y = 13 and 5x - 2y = 4.
  • Multiply (1) by 2, (2) by 3: 4x + 6y = 26 and 15x - 6y = 12.
  • Add new equations: (4x + 15x) + (6y - 6y) = 26 + 12 => 19x = 38 => x = 2.
  • Substitute x=2 into (1): 2(2) + 3y = 13 => 4 + 3y = 13 => 3y = 9 => y = 3. Solution: (2, 3).

🎙️ Here, we aimed to eliminate 'y'. We multiplied to get +6y and -6y, so adding them cancelled 'y' out. This method is often preferred for its directness.

Algebraic Method 3: Cross-Multiplication

  • Step 1: Write equations in standard form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0, a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0.
  • Step 2: Write coefficients as shown in the cross-multiplication diagram.
  • Step 3: Apply the cross-multiplication formula to find x and y.
  • This method is useful for quick solutions, especially in exams.

🎙️ The cross-multiplication method is a formula-based approach that can be very fast once you understand the pattern. It's derived from the elimination method but offers a direct way to find x and y.

Example: Solving by Cross-Multiplication

  • Solve: x + 2y - 4 = 0 and 2x + y - 5 = 0.
  • a₁=1, b₁=2, c₁=-4; a₂=2, b₂=1, c₂=-5.
  • x / (b₁c₂ - b₂c₁) = y / (c₁a₂ - c₂a₁) = 1 / (a₁b₂ - a₂b₁).
  • x / (2(-5) - 1(-4)) = y / ((-4)(2) - (-5)(1)) = 1 / (1(1) - 2(2)).
  • x / (-10+4) = y / (-8+5) = 1 / (1-4) => x / -6 = y / -3 = 1 / -3.
  • x = -6/-3 = 2, y = -3/-3 = 1. Solution: (2, 1).

🎙️ This example shows the application of the cross-multiplication formula. It might look complex at first, but with practice, it becomes a powerful tool for solving these pairs of equations.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Sign Errors: Very common, especially in elimination and cross-multiplication.
  • Substitution Errors: Forgetting to substitute into the *other* equation.
  • Graphical Errors: Incorrectly plotting points or drawing lines.
  • Not checking solutions: Always substitute your answer back into *both* original equations.

🎙️ Even experienced students make these errors. Always double-check your signs, ensure you're substituting correctly, and take your time with plotting. A quick check of your solution can save many marks!
