What are Two Dimensions?

  • Length and Width/Height.
  • Flat surfaces, like paper or a screen.
  • Objects without depth.

🎙️ Imagine drawing on a piece of paper. You can move left-right and up-down, but you can't go 'into' the paper. That's a two-dimensional world. It has length and breadth, but no thickness or depth.

The Cartesian Coordinate System

  • Invented by René Descartes.
  • Uses two perpendicular lines: X-axis and Y-axis.
  • Locates any point in a plane using (x, y) coordinates.

🎙️ To precisely locate points in our 2D world, we use the Cartesian Coordinate System. It's like a grid system where the horizontal line is the X-axis and the vertical line is the Y-axis. They meet at a special point called the Origin.

Plotting Points (x, y)

  • First number (x) is horizontal distance from origin.
  • Second number (y) is vertical distance from origin.
  • Always move along X-axis first, then Y-axis.

🎙️ To plot a point like (3, 2), you start at the origin (0,0). Move 3 units along the X-axis (right if positive, left if negative), then 2 units along the Y-axis (up if positive, down if negative). This gives us the exact location of the point.

1Start at Origin (0,0)
2Move X units horizontally
3Move Y units vertically
4Mark the point

What Exactly is a Line?

  • A straight path of points extending infinitely in both directions.
  • Has no thickness or width.
  • Defined by at least two points.

🎙️ In geometry, a line isn't just a thick mark; it's an infinitely thin, perfectly straight path. It goes on forever in both directions, without any curves or breaks. You only need two distinct points to draw a unique straight line.

The Equation of a Line

  • A mathematical rule that all points on the line satisfy.
  • General form: Ax + By + C = 0.
  • A, B, C are real numbers, A and B are not both zero.

🎙️ Just like every person has a name, every line has an equation that describes it. This equation is a relationship between the x and y coordinates of every point that lies on that line. The general form helps us represent any straight line.

Understanding Slope (Gradient)

  • Measures the steepness of a line.
  • How much the line 'rises' for a given 'run'.
  • Denoted by 'm'.

🎙️ Slope, also called gradient, tells us how steep a line is. Is it a gentle hill or a very steep mountain? It's calculated by how much the vertical distance changes (rise) for every unit change in the horizontal distance (run).

⛰️
Steepness
9 / 25

Calculating Slope from Two Points

  • Given two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2).
  • Formula: m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1).
  • Order of points matters for consistency, but not for final slope value.

🎙️ To find the slope of a line passing through two points, we simply find the difference in their y-coordinates and divide it by the difference in their x-coordinates. Remember to subtract the coordinates in the same order for both numerator and denominator.

1Identify (x1, y1) and (x2, y2)
2Calculate change in Y (y2 - y1)
3Calculate change in X (x2 - x1)
4Divide change in Y by change in X

Different Types of Slopes

  • Positive Slope: Line goes up from left to right.
  • Negative Slope: Line goes down from left to right.
  • Zero Slope: Horizontal line.
  • Undefined Slope: Vertical line.

🎙️ Slopes can be positive, negative, zero, or undefined. A positive slope means the line is rising, like climbing a hill. A negative slope means it's falling. A horizontal line has zero slope, and a perfectly vertical line has an undefined slope because the 'run' (change in x) is zero.

Worked Example: Finding Slope

  • Find the slope of the line passing through (2, 3) and (6, 5).
  • x1=2, y1=3, x2=6, y2=5.
  • m = (5-3) / (6-2) = 2 / 4 = 1/2.

🎙️ Let's work through an example. If we have points (2, 3) and (6, 5), we apply the formula. (5-3) gives us 2, and (6-2) gives us 4. So, the slope is 2/4, which simplifies to 1/2. This means for every 2 units moved horizontally, the line rises 1 unit vertically.

X-intercept and Y-intercept

  • X-intercept: Where the line crosses the X-axis (y=0).
  • Y-intercept: Where the line crosses the Y-axis (x=0).
  • These points are useful for graphing a line.

🎙️ Intercepts are special points where the line cuts across the axes. The x-intercept is where the line meets the x-axis, meaning its y-coordinate is zero. Similarly, the y-intercept is where it meets the y-axis, meaning its x-coordinate is zero. They are very helpful for sketching lines.

The Slope-Intercept Form: y = mx + c

  • A very popular form of a linear equation.
  • 'm' is the slope of the line.
  • 'c' is the y-intercept of the line.

🎙️ The slope-intercept form, y = mx + c, is incredibly useful because it directly shows us two key properties of the line: its slope 'm' and its y-intercept 'c'. This makes it very easy to understand and graph a line just by looking at its equation.

📈
Slope and Intercept

Worked Example: Slope-Intercept Form

  • Convert 3x + 2y = 6 to slope-intercept form.
  • 2y = -3x + 6.
  • y = (-3/2)x + 3. Here m = -3/2, c = 3.

🎙️ Let's convert a general form equation into slope-intercept form. We need to isolate 'y'. First, move the 'x' term to the right side, then divide everything by the coefficient of 'y'. From y = (-3/2)x + 3, we can immediately see the slope is -3/2 and the y-intercept is 3.

Understanding Parallel Lines

  • Lines that are always the same distance apart.
  • They never intersect, no matter how far they extend.
  • Crucially, parallel lines have the EXACT SAME SLOPE.

🎙️ Think of railway tracks or the opposite edges of a ruler. These are examples of parallel lines. They run alongside each other forever without ever meeting. In terms of mathematics, this means they share the exact same steepness or slope.

Understanding Perpendicular Lines

  • Lines that intersect at a right angle (90 degrees).
  • The product of their slopes is -1 (m1 * m2 = -1).
  • If one line is horizontal, the perpendicular line is vertical.

🎙️ Perpendicular lines are like the corners of a square or the intersection of a crossroad. They meet at a perfect right angle. The mathematical rule for their slopes is very important: if you multiply the slope of one line by the slope of the other, you'll always get -1.

Worked Example: Parallel or Perpendicular?

  • Line 1: y = 2x + 1 (m1 = 2).
  • Line 2: y = -1/2x + 5 (m2 = -1/2).
  • m1 * m2 = 2 * (-1/2) = -1. So, they are perpendicular.

🎙️ Let's determine if two lines are parallel or perpendicular. We first find their slopes. Here, the slopes are 2 and -1/2. If we multiply them, 2 times -1/2 equals -1. This tells us that these two lines are perpendicular to each other.
