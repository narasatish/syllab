Law of Conservation of Mass

  • Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction
  • The total mass of reactants must equal the total mass of products
  • This means the number of atoms of each element must be conserved!

🎙️ This law, given by Antoine Lavoisier, is crucial. It means that atoms are not lost or gained during a reaction; they are only rearranged. Therefore, the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation.

Steps for Balancing Chemical Equations (Hit and Trial Method)

  • 1. Write the unbalanced equation.
  • 2. Count atoms of each element on both sides.
  • 3. Start balancing with the element present in the most complex compound (usually non-metals first, then O, then H).
  • 4. Use coefficients (numbers in front of formulas), NEVER change subscripts!
  • 5. Recheck all atoms until balanced.

🎙️ Balancing equations is like solving a puzzle. We use the 'hit and trial' method. The key is to only add coefficients in front of the formulas, never change the small numbers (subscripts) within a formula, as that changes the substance itself!

2Count Atoms
3Add Coefficients
4Recheck

Worked Example 1: Balancing H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

  • 1. O is unbalanced (2 vs 1). Place '2' in front of H₂O: H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
  • 2. Now H is unbalanced (2 vs 4). Place '2' in front of H₂: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
  • 3. Check: H (4 vs 4), O (2 vs 2). Balanced!

🎙️ Let's balance our water formation equation. We first fix oxygen by putting a '2' in front of water. This makes hydrogen unbalanced, so we then put a '2' in front of hydrogen gas. Now, all atoms are equal on both sides!

What Information Does a Balanced Equation Give?

  • Identities of reactants and products
  • Physical states (s, l, g, aq)
  • Relative number of moles/molecules involved
  • Stoichiometry (quantitative relationships)

🎙️ A well-written, balanced chemical equation is a treasure trove of information! It tells us not just what reacts and what forms, but also their physical states and the exact ratios in which they combine.

Limitations of Chemical Equations

  • Don't tell about reaction rate (how fast it occurs)
  • Don't show reaction conditions (temperature, pressure, catalyst)
  • Don't indicate energy changes (exothermic/endothermic)

🎙️ While powerful, chemical equations have some limitations. They don't inherently tell us if a reaction is fast or slow, if it needs heating or cooling, or if it releases or absorbs energy. We often add extra notation for that.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • NEVER change subscripts (e.g., changing H₂O to H₂O₂)
  • Forgetting to balance all elements, especially oxygen and hydrogen
  • Not counting atoms correctly on both sides
  • Incorrectly writing chemical formulas

🎙️ Many students make common errors when balancing. The biggest one is changing the small numbers (subscripts) – remember, that changes the chemical itself! Always double-check your atom counts for every element.
