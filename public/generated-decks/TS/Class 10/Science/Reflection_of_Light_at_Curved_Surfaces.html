Important Terms: Centre of Curvature (C)

  • Centre of the sphere the mirror is part of
  • Denoted by 'C'
  • Not on the mirror surface itself

🎙️ Imagine the spherical mirror as part of a larger hollow sphere. The center of that imaginary sphere is called the Centre of Curvature, 'C'. For a concave mirror, C is in front; for a convex mirror, it's behind.

🎙️ The distance between the Pole (P) and the Centre of Curvature (C) is known as the Radius of Curvature, 'R'. It's essentially the radius of the large imaginary sphere from which the mirror was cut.

Principal Focus (F) for Concave Mirrors

  • Point where parallel rays converge after reflection
  • Located between P and C
  • Denoted by 'F'

🎙️ For a concave mirror, when parallel rays of light strike its surface, they all converge to a single point on the principal axis after reflection. This point is called the Principal Focus, 'F'.

Principal Focus (F) for Convex Mirrors

  • Point from which parallel rays *appear* to diverge
  • Located behind the mirror
  • Denoted by 'F'

🎙️ For a convex mirror, parallel rays of light diverge after reflection. If you trace these reflected rays backwards, they appear to come from a single point behind the mirror. This point is its Principal Focus, 'F'.

Important Terms: Focal Length (f)

  • Distance from Pole (P) to Principal Focus (F)
  • Denoted by 'f'
  • Relation: f = R/2

🎙️ The distance between the Pole (P) and the Principal Focus (F) is called the Focal Length, 'f'. For spherical mirrors, the focal length is half of the radius of curvature, meaning f = R/2.

  • Essential for image formation

  • Ray directed towards C reflects back

Image Formation: Convex Mirrors

  • Always form virtual and erect images
  • Image is always diminished (smaller)
  • Image is always formed behind the mirror

🎙️ Convex mirrors are simpler in image formation. They always form virtual, erect, and diminished images, regardless of the object's position. The image is always located behind the mirror, between P and F.
