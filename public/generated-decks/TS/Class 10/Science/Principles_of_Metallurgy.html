Ores and Minerals

  • Minerals are naturally occurring solid substances found in the Earth's crust.
  • An ore is a mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably.
  • All ores are minerals, but not all minerals are ores.

🎙️ Imagine a treasure hunt for metals! Minerals are like all the rocks you find, but ores are the special rocks that have enough metal to be worth taking out. We only extract from ores because it's economical.

Common Ores and Their Metals

  • Bauxite is the main ore of Aluminium (Al).
  • Haematite is a common ore of Iron (Fe).
  • Cinnabar is an ore of Mercury (Hg).

🎙️ Different metals are found in different types of ores. For instance, if you want aluminium, you'd look for bauxite. Knowing the right ore is the first step to getting the metal.

What is Gangue?

  • Gangue refers to the unwanted rocky or earthy impurities present in an ore.
  • It includes sand, clay, and other non-metallic materials.
  • Removing gangue is crucial to get pure metal.

🎙️ When we dig up an ore, it's not just pure metal! It's mixed with a lot of dirt and rock, which we call gangue. We need to get rid of this gangue before we can extract the metal itself.

Step 1: Crushing and Grinding (Pulverization)

  • Large lumps of ore are crushed into smaller pieces.
  • These smaller pieces are then ground into a fine powder.
  • This increases the surface area for further processing.

🎙️ Imagine trying to cook a whole potato versus diced potato. Smaller pieces cook faster! Similarly, crushing and grinding ores make it easier for chemicals to react with them in later steps.

Step 2: Concentration of Ore

  • This process removes gangue from the powdered ore.
  • It increases the proportion of the valuable mineral.
  • Different methods are used based on the ore's properties.

🎙️ Once the ore is a fine powder, the next big challenge is to separate the valuable metal-containing particles from the unwanted gangue. This step is also called 'enrichment' of the ore.

🔬
Separating Impurities

Method 1: Hand Picking

  • Used when ore particles and gangue are visibly different in size or colour.
  • Labour-intensive but simple for specific ores.
  • Not suitable for fine powders.

🎙️ This is the simplest method, just like picking out different coloured candies from a bowl. If the ore and gangue are clearly distinct, workers can manually separate them. However, it's not practical for most ores.

🖐️
Manual Separation
11 / 25

Method 2: Gravity Separation (Hydraulic Washing)

  • Works when ore particles are heavier than gangue particles.
  • Powdered ore is washed with a stream of water.
  • Lighter gangue particles are washed away, heavier ore settles.

🎙️ Think of panning for gold! Gold is very heavy, so water washes away the lighter sand, leaving the gold behind. This method uses the difference in density to separate the ore from the gangue.

💧
Washing Away Gangue
12 / 25

Method 3: Froth Floatation

  • Mainly used for sulphide ores (e.g., galena, zinc blende).
  • Ore particles are wetted by oil, gangue by water.
  • Air is blown in, creating froth that carries ore particles to the surface.

🎙️ This method is quite clever! It uses the property that some ore particles prefer oil and float with air bubbles, while gangue particles prefer water and sink. It's very effective for sulphide ores.

🫧
Floating Ore
13 / 25

Method 4: Magnetic Separation

  • Used when either the ore or the gangue is magnetic.
  • Powdered ore passes over a magnetic roller.
  • Magnetic particles are attracted, non-magnetic fall separately.

🎙️ This method is perfect for ores like iron ore (haematite) which are magnetic. A strong magnet pulls the magnetic ore particles one way, while the non-magnetic gangue goes another, separating them.

🧲

Step 3: Extraction of Crude Metal

  • This step involves converting the concentrated ore into crude metal.
  • It often includes two main processes: Calcination/Roasting and Reduction.
  • The choice depends on the type of ore and metal reactivity.

🎙️ Now that we have concentrated ore, the next big step is to actually get the metal out of it. This usually involves heating the ore and then using a reducing agent to remove oxygen or other elements.

🔥
Heating the Ore
16 / 25

Calcination

  • Heating the ore strongly in the absence of air.
  • Used for carbonate ores (e.g., limestone) and hydroxide ores.
  • Removes volatile impurities like water or carbon dioxide.

🎙️ Imagine baking something in an oven without letting air in. Calcination does this for ores like calcium carbonate, driving off carbon dioxide and leaving behind metal oxide.

♨️
Heating without Air
17 / 25

Roasting

  • Heating the ore strongly in the presence of air.
  • Mainly used for sulphide ores (e.g., zinc blende, copper glance).
  • Converts sulphide ores into metal oxides.

🎙️ Unlike calcination, roasting requires air. It's like burning something to change its form. Sulphide ores react with oxygen in the air to form metal oxides and sulphur dioxide gas.

💨
Heating with Air

Reduction of Metal Oxide

  • The metal oxide obtained from calcination or roasting is reduced.
  • Reduction involves removing oxygen from the metal oxide.
  • Common reducing agents include carbon (coke), carbon monoxide, or active metals.

🎙️ After calcination or roasting, we have a metal oxide. Now, we need to remove that oxygen to get the pure metal. This is done by 'reduction,' often using substances that love oxygen more than the metal does.

⬇️
Removing Oxygen
19 / 25

Example: Iron Extraction in a Blast Furnace

  • Iron ore (haematite) is reduced using coke (carbon) and limestone.
  • Coke acts as the reducing agent, removing oxygen from iron oxide.
  • Molten iron is collected at the bottom.

🎙️ The blast furnace is a huge example of reduction in action. Here, carbon in the form of coke takes oxygen from iron oxide, leaving behind molten iron. Limestone helps remove other impurities.

🏭

Step 4: Refining of Crude Metal

  • The metal obtained after reduction is often impure (crude metal).
  • Refining removes remaining impurities to get pure metal.
  • Different methods like electrolytic refining, distillation, etc., are used.

🎙️ Even after all those steps, the metal isn't perfectly pure. It still has some tiny bits of impurities. Refining is the final step to make the metal as pure as possible for its intended use.

✨
Purifying Metals

Electrolytic Refining

  • Uses electricity to purify metals.
  • Impure metal acts as anode, pure metal as cathode.
  • Metal ions from anode dissolve and deposit as pure metal on cathode.

🎙️ This is a very common and effective method, especially for highly pure metals. Electricity drives the process, moving the pure metal from the impure block to a clean sheet, leaving impurities behind.

Example: Electrolytic Refining of Copper

  • Impure copper is made the anode.
  • A thin strip of pure copper is the cathode.
  • Copper sulphate solution acts as the electrolyte.

🎙️ In copper refining, when electricity flows, pure copper from the impure anode dissolves and deposits onto the pure copper cathode. The impurities either settle down as 'anode mud' or dissolve in the electrolyte.
