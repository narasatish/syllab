What are Acids?

  • Substances that are sour in taste.
  • Turn blue litmus paper red.
  • Release H+ ions in water.

🎙️ Acids are substances we encounter every day. Think of a lemon or tamarind; their sour taste comes from acids. A key property is their ability to change the color of certain indicators.

Properties of Acids

  • Corrosive nature (can burn skin).
  • React with metals to produce hydrogen gas.
  • React with bases to form salt and water.

🎙️ Acids are not just sour; they have other distinct properties. Strong acids can be very dangerous and corrosive, meaning they can damage materials and skin. They also have specific reactions with metals and bases.

What are Bases?

  • Substances that are bitter in taste.
  • Feel soapy or slippery to touch.
  • Turn red litmus paper blue.

🎙️ Now let's move to bases. Unlike acids, bases typically taste bitter and feel slippery. Think of soap – that slippery feel is a characteristic of bases. They also have the opposite effect on litmus paper compared to acids.

Properties of Bases

  • Caustic nature (can burn skin).
  • React with acids to form salt and water.
  • Good conductors of electricity in solution.

🎙️ Just like acids, strong bases can also be caustic and harmful. They are also excellent conductors of electricity when dissolved in water, and they readily react with acids in a special way we'll discuss later.

What are Indicators?

  • Substances that change colour in acids or bases.
  • Help us identify if a solution is acidic or basic.
  • Natural and synthetic indicators exist.

🎙️ Since we can't taste or touch all chemicals, we use indicators. These special substances change their color when they come into contact with an acid or a base, giving us a visual clue about the nature of the solution.

The pH Scale: Measuring Strength

  • A scale from 0 to 14 to measure acidity or basicity.
  • pH < 7 is acidic, pH > 7 is basic, pH = 7 is neutral.
  • Lower pH means stronger acid, higher pH means stronger base.

🎙️ The pH scale is a universal way to quantify how acidic or basic a substance is. It ranges from 0 to 14. A pH of 7 is perfectly neutral, like pure water. Values below 7 are acidic, and above 7 are basic.

pH and Tooth Decay

  • Bacteria in mouth produce acids after eating sugar.
  • Lowers mouth pH below 5.5, causing enamel erosion.
  • Toothpaste, being basic, neutralizes this acid.

🎙️ Tooth decay is a common problem linked to pH. When we eat sugary foods, bacteria in our mouth produce acids, lowering the pH. This acidic environment then starts to dissolve tooth enamel. Using basic toothpaste helps neutralize these acids.

What are Salts?

  • Formed by the reaction of an acid and a base.
  • Composed of a positive ion (cation) from a base and a negative ion (anion) from an acid.
  • Many are neutral, but can be acidic or basic.

🎙️ Salts are a fascinating class of compounds. They are the product of the reaction between an acid and a base. Chemically, they are ionic compounds formed from the positive part of a base and the negative part of an acid.

➕➖
Ionic Compound

🎙️ Table salt, or sodium chloride, is perhaps the most famous salt. But there are many others! Washing soda, used for cleaning, and Plaster of Paris, used in construction and casts, are also important examples of salts.

Types of Salts

  • Neutral salts: formed from strong acid + strong base (e.g., NaCl).
  • Acidic salts: formed from strong acid + weak base (e.g., NH4Cl).
  • Basic salts: formed from weak acid + strong base (e.g., Na2CO3).

🎙️ Salts aren't always neutral. Their nature depends on the strength of the acid and base that formed them. A strong acid and strong base give a neutral salt, while combinations involving a weak component lead to acidic or basic salts.

Neutralization Reaction

  • Reaction between an acid and a base.
  • Forms salt and water as products.
  • Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O.

🎙️ This is a very important reaction! Neutralization is when an acid reacts with a base to cancel out each other's properties, producing salt and water. It's a fundamental concept in chemistry.
