What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of a triangle.
  • Comes from Greek words 'Trigon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's all about understanding how the lengths of the sides of a triangle change as its angles change. This field helps us solve many real-world problems.

Identifying Sides for an Angle

  • Hypotenuse: Always opposite the 90° angle.
  • Opposite Side: Opposite to the reference angle.
  • Adjacent Side: Next to the reference angle (not the Hypotenuse).

🎙️ This is crucial! The 'Opposite' and 'Adjacent' sides depend on which acute angle you are focusing on. The Hypotenuse remains the same, but the other two sides swap roles depending on your reference angle.

Introducing Trigonometric Ratios

  • Ratios of sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • These ratios depend on the acute angle.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.

🎙️ Just like how we have ratios in daily life, trigonometry uses ratios of the lengths of the sides of a right triangle. These special ratios help us link angles to side lengths. Let's explore the first three.

➗➗➗➗
Side Ratios
7 / 25

Sine (sin) Ratio

  • Ratio of the length of the Opposite side to the Hypotenuse.
  • sin(angle) = Opposite / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ The Sine ratio tells us how tall the 'opposite' side is compared to the 'hypotenuse' for a given angle. Think of it as a measure of the angle's 'vertical reach' relative to the longest side.

⬆️
Opposite / Hypotenuse
8 / 25

Cosine (cos) Ratio

  • Ratio of the length of the Adjacent side to the Hypotenuse.
  • cos(angle) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ The Cosine ratio, on the other hand, tells us about the 'horizontal reach' of the angle, comparing the 'adjacent' side to the 'hypotenuse'. It's about how 'wide' the angle is.

➡️
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
9 / 25

Tangent (tan) Ratio

  • Ratio of the length of the Opposite side to the Adjacent side.
  • tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.

🎙️ The Tangent ratio is unique as it doesn't involve the hypotenuse directly. It compares the 'opposite' side to the 'adjacent' side. Interestingly, it can also be expressed as sin(angle) / cos(angle).

↗️
Opposite / Adjacent
10 / 25

Remembering the Ratios: SOH CAH TOA!

  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ This is a super helpful trick to remember the first three ratios! Just say 'SOH CAH TOA' to yourself. It's an easy way to recall which sides go with which ratio. Practice it a few times!

Reciprocal Ratios: Cosecant (csc)

  • Reciprocal of Sine.
  • csc(angle) = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • csc(angle) = 1 / sin(angle).

🎙️ Just like we have reciprocals for numbers, trigonometric ratios also have reciprocals. Cosecant is simply the hypotenuse divided by the opposite side, which is the inverse of the sine ratio.

🔄
Inverse of Sine
14 / 25

Reciprocal Ratios: Secant (sec)

  • Reciprocal of Cosine.
  • sec(angle) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.
  • sec(angle) = 1 / cos(angle).

🎙️ Secant is the reciprocal of the cosine ratio. It's the hypotenuse divided by the adjacent side. Remember these reciprocal relationships, as they are very useful in solving problems.

↩️
Inverse of Cosine
15 / 25

Reciprocal Ratios: Cotangent (cot)

  • Reciprocal of Tangent.
  • cot(angle) = Adjacent / Opposite.
  • cot(angle) = 1 / tan(angle).

🎙️ Finally, Cotangent is the reciprocal of the tangent ratio. It's the adjacent side divided by the opposite side. Knowing these six ratios forms the complete foundation of basic trigonometry.

➿
Inverse of Tangent

Angle of Elevation

  • Angle formed between the horizontal line and your line of sight.
  • When you look UP at an object.

🎙️ The angle of elevation is a common term in trigonometry. When you look upwards from a horizontal line to an object above, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal is the angle of elevation.

⬆️
Looking Up
18 / 25

Angle of Depression

  • Angle formed between the horizontal line and your line of sight.
  • When you look DOWN at an object.

🎙️ Conversely, the angle of depression is formed when you look downwards from a horizontal line to an object below. It's important to remember that both angles of elevation and depression are measured from a horizontal line.

Key Takeaways from Introduction to Trigonometry

  • Trigonometry relates sides and angles of right triangles.
  • Six ratios: sin, cos, tan, csc, sec, cot.
  • SOH CAH TOA is a memory aid.
  • Used to find unknown heights and distances.

🎙️ We've covered a lot today! Remember the core idea: trigonometry helps us measure triangles indirectly. The six ratios are your tools, and SOH CAH TOA is your guide. These concepts are fundamental.
