What is Probability?

  • It quantifies uncertainty.
  • Expressed as a number between 0 and 1.

🎙️ Probability, in simple terms, is how likely something is to happen. It gives us a numerical value for chance. Think of it like a scale, where 0 means it will definitely not happen, and 1 means it will definitely happen.

Key Terms: Experiment & Outcome

  • Experiment: An action or process with well-defined results.
  • Outcome: A possible result of an experiment.
  • Example: Tossing a coin is an experiment, Heads or Tails are outcomes.

🎙️ Before we dive deeper, let's understand some basic terms. An experiment is any action we perform that has a clear set of results. Each of these results is called an outcome. For instance, rolling a dice is an experiment, and getting a 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 is an outcome.

Key Terms: Event

  • Event: A collection of one or more outcomes of an experiment.
  • It's a subset of all possible outcomes.
  • Example: Getting an even number when rolling a die (2, 4, 6) is an event.

🎙️ An event is simply a specific outcome or a group of outcomes that we are interested in. If we roll a die, 'getting an even number' is an event, as it includes the outcomes 2, 4, and 6. 'Getting a 3' is also an event.

The Probability Formula

  • P(E) = (Number of Favorable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Possible Outcomes)
  • P(E) represents the probability of Event E.
  • This formula is fundamental to all probability calculations.

🎙️ This is the most important formula you will learn today! To calculate the probability of any event 'E', you simply divide the number of ways that event can happen by the total number of possible results. Remember this formula well!

Example: Tossing a Coin

  • Experiment: Tossing a fair coin.
  • Total Possible Outcomes: {Head, Tail} (2 outcomes).
  • Event E: Getting a Head.
  • Favorable Outcomes for E: {Head} (1 outcome).

🎙️ Let's apply our formula to a simple coin toss. When you toss a coin, there are only two possible outcomes: Head or Tail. If we want to find the probability of getting a Head, how many favorable outcomes do we have? Just one!

Worked Example: Coin Toss

  • Total Outcomes = 2
  • Favorable Outcomes (Head) = 1
  • P(Head) = 1 / 2

🎙️ So, using our formula, the probability of getting a Head is 1 divided by 2, which is 0.5. This means there's a 50% chance of getting a Head. It's quite straightforward, isn't it?

Probability always between 0 and 1

  • The value of probability for any event E is always between 0 and 1, inclusive.
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  • A probability of 0 means the event is impossible. A probability of 1 means the event is certain.

🎙️ It's crucial to remember that a probability value can never be less than 0 or greater than 1. If your calculation gives you a negative number or a number like 1.5, you know you've made a mistake! The range 0 to 1 covers all possibilities.

The Impossible Event

  • An event that cannot happen has a probability of 0.
  • There are no favorable outcomes for such an event.
  • Example: Getting an 8 when rolling a standard six-sided die.

🎙️ Some events simply cannot occur. For example, if you roll a standard die, you can never get an 8. In such cases, the number of favorable outcomes is zero, making the probability 0 divided by the total outcomes, which is 0. This is an impossible event.

The Sure Event (Certain Event)

  • An event that is certain to happen has a probability of 1.
  • All outcomes are favorable for such an event.
  • Example: Getting a number less than 7 when rolling a standard six-sided die.

🎙️ On the other hand, some events are guaranteed to happen. If you roll a standard die, you are absolutely certain to get a number less than 7 (since all numbers from 1 to 6 are less than 7). Here, all possible outcomes are favorable outcomes, so the probability is 1.

Complementary Events

  • If E is an event, then 'not E' (denoted as Ē or E') is its complementary event.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • Example: If P(rain) = 0.3, then P(no rain) = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7.

🎙️ Complementary events are two events where one happening means the other cannot, and together they cover all possibilities. The sum of the probability of an event and its complement is always 1. This is a very useful property for solving problems.

Probability with Playing Cards

  • A standard deck has 52 cards.
  • It has 4 suits: Spades (♠️), Hearts (❤️), Diamonds (♦️), Clubs (♣️).
  • Each suit has 13 cards: A, 2, 3, ..., 10, J, Q, K.
  • Hearts and Diamonds are red; Spades and Clubs are black.

🎙️ Many probability problems involve a deck of playing cards. It's essential to understand the composition of a standard deck. Knowing the number of suits, colors, and specific cards will make these problems much easier to solve.

Worked Example: Drawing a King

  • Total Outcomes = 52
  • Favorable Outcomes (King) = 4
  • P(King) = 4 / 52 = 1 / 13

🎙️ So, the probability of drawing a King is 4 out of 52, which simplifies to 1/13. This means that, on average, if you draw a card 13 times, you would expect to draw a King once. It's a fairly low chance.

Key Concepts - A Quick Review

  • Probability measures likelihood (0 to 1).
  • P(E) = Favorable / Total Outcomes.
  • Impossible Event: P(E) = 0.
  • Sure Event: P(E) = 1.
  • Complementary Events: P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

🎙️ Let's quickly recap the main ideas we've covered today. Probability helps us quantify chance, and the core formula is essential. Remember the range of probability and the concepts of impossible, sure, and complementary events.
