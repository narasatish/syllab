  • Values that remain fixed.
  • They do not change.
  • Numbers like 5, -7, 1/2 are constants.

What are Variables?

  • Values that can change.
  • Represented by letters (x, y, a, b).
  • Their value can vary in different situations.

🎙️ In contrast to constants, variables are symbols, usually letters, that represent quantities whose values can change. For example, 'x' can be 2 in one problem and 5 in another.

Algebraic Expressions

  • Combination of constants and variables.
  • Connected by arithmetic operations (+, -, *, /).
  • Examples: 2x + 5, 3y - 7.

🎙️ When we combine constants and variables using operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division, we form what are called algebraic expressions. They are like mathematical sentences.

Building Blocks: Terms

  • Parts of an expression separated by + or -.
  • Each term can be a constant, a variable, or a product.

Understanding Polynomials

  • An algebraic expression.
  • Variables have only non-negative integer exponents.
  • No division by variables, no fractional powers.

🎙️ A polynomial is a special type of algebraic expression. The key rule for a polynomial is that the powers of the variables must always be whole numbers – no fractions, no negatives. This makes them well-behaved expressions.

Components of a Polynomial: Terms

  • Individual parts separated by + or -.
  • Each term is a product of a constant and variables.
  • Example: In 4x² + 3x - 5, terms are 4x², 3x, -5.

🎙️ Just like any expression, polynomials are made of terms. Each term in a polynomial is a product of a number and one or more variables raised to non-negative integer powers.

🧩
Building Blocks

Components of a Polynomial: Coefficients

  • The numerical factor of a term.
  • It multiplies the variable part.
  • Example: In 4x² + 3x - 5, 4 is coefficient of x², 3 of x.

🎙️ The coefficient is the number that stands in front of the variable part of a term. It tells us how many times the variable part is being multiplied. If there's no number, the coefficient is usually 1.

✖️
Numerical Factor

Components of a Polynomial: Exponents

  • The power to which a variable is raised.
  • Must be non-negative integers (0, 1, 2, 3...).
  • Example: In 4x², the exponent of x is 2.

🎙️ The exponent, or power, indicates how many times the base number is multiplied by itself. For a polynomial, these exponents must always be whole numbers, never fractions or negative values.

⬆️
Raised To

The Degree of a Polynomial

  • Highest exponent of the variable in the polynomial.
  • For polynomials with one variable.
  • It helps classify polynomials.

🎙️ The degree of a polynomial is a very important characteristic. It's simply the largest exponent of the variable present in any of its terms. This 'highest power' helps us categorize different types of polynomials.

Types by Terms: Monomial

  • A polynomial with exactly one term.
  • Examples: 5x, -3y², 7.
  • Mono means 'one'.

🎙️ We can classify polynomials based on how many terms they have. A monomial is the simplest type, consisting of just a single term. It's like having just one item in a list.

Types by Terms: Binomial

  • A polynomial with exactly two terms.
  • Examples: x + 2, 3y - 5x, a² + b.
  • Bi means 'two'.

🎙️ Next, we have binomials. As the prefix 'bi' suggests, these polynomials are made up of exactly two terms, separated by a plus or minus sign. Think of a bicycle with two wheels.

Types by Terms: Trinomial

🎙️ And finally, trinomials. These polynomials contain exactly three terms. It's like a tricycle with three wheels! This classification helps us describe polynomials quickly.

Types by Degree: Linear Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 1.
  • General form: ax + b (a ≠ 0).
  • Examples: 2x + 3, y - 7.

🎙️ Now let's classify polynomials based on their degree. A linear polynomial is one where the highest power of the variable is 1. When graphed, it forms a straight line, hence 'linear'.

Types by Degree: Quadratic Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 2.
  • General form: ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0).
  • Examples: x² + 5x + 6, 3y² - 4.

🎙️ A quadratic polynomial has a degree of 2. This means the highest power of the variable is 2. These are very common in mathematics and describe many real-world phenomena, like the path of a thrown ball.

Types by Degree: Cubic Polynomial

  • A polynomial with degree 3.
  • General form: ax³ + bx² + cx + d (a ≠ 0).

🎙️ Moving on, a cubic polynomial has a degree of 3. The highest power of the variable in such a polynomial is 3. These polynomials also appear in various scientific and engineering applications.

Zeroes of a Polynomial

  • A value of the variable that makes the polynomial equal to zero.
  • Also called 'roots' of the polynomial.
  • Finding zeroes is crucial in solving equations.

🎙️ The 'zero' of a polynomial is a very important concept. It's the specific value you can substitute for the variable that makes the entire polynomial expression evaluate to zero. Think of it as the solution to the polynomial equation P(x) = 0.

Finding Zeroes: Example

  • Polynomial: P(x) = x - 5
  • To find zero, set P(x) = 0.
  • x - 5 = 0 => x = 5. So, 5 is the zero.

🎙️ Let's try a simple example. If we have the polynomial P(x) = x - 5, to find its zero, we simply set the expression equal to zero and solve for x. This gives us the value that makes the polynomial 'zero out'.
