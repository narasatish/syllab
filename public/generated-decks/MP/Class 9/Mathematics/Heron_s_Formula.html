Basic Area of a Triangle

  • Requires knowing the base and the perpendicular height.
  • Works well for right-angled or easily measurable triangles.

Heron's Formula: The Concept

  • Finds area using only the lengths of the three sides.
  • No need to calculate or measure the height.
  • Works for all types of triangles: scalene, isosceles, equilateral.

🎙️ The core idea behind Heron's Formula is its simplicity. You don't need any angles or perpendicular heights. Just the three side lengths, and you're good to go!

➕

Step 1: Calculate the Semi-Perimeter (s)

  • Perimeter is the sum of all sides (a + b + c).
  • Semi-perimeter (s) is half of the perimeter.
  • Formula: s = (a + b + c) / 2

🎙️ The first step is to calculate something called the 'semi-perimeter'. 'Semi' means half, so it's simply half the total length of all the triangle's sides added together. This 's' value is crucial for the formula.

📏
Half the Boundary
9 / 25

Step 2: Apply Heron's Formula

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Where 'a', 'b', 'c' are the side lengths.
  • And 's' is the semi-perimeter.

🎙️ Once you have 's', you can plug all the values into this formula. It might look a bit complex with the square root, but it's just multiplication and subtraction inside. Remember to calculate the parts inside the brackets first!

➗
The Magic Formula

How to Use Heron's Formula (Steps)

  • 1. Identify side lengths (a, b, c).
  • 2. Calculate semi-perimeter (s = (a+b+c)/2).
  • 3. Calculate (s-a), (s-b), (s-c).
  • 4. Multiply s * (s-a) * (s-b) * (s-c).
  • 5. Find the square root of the result.

🎙️ Let's break down the process into clear steps. If you follow these five steps carefully, you'll be able to solve any problem using Heron's Formula. Practice makes perfect!

Worked Example 1: Triangle with Sides 3, 4, 5

  • Sides: a=3, b=4, c=5
  • s = (3+4+5)/2 = 12/2 = 6
  • Area = √[6(6-3)(6-4)(6-5)] = √[6*3*2*1] = √36 = 6 sq units.

🎙️ This is a classic right-angled triangle, so we know the area is 6. Let's see if Heron's formula gives us the same answer. And yes, it does! This proves the formula works even for simple cases.

Example: Equilateral Triangle

  • Sides: a=6cm, b=6cm, c=6cm

Example: Isosceles Triangle

  • Sides: a=5cm, b=5cm, c=8cm
  • s = (5+5+8)/2 = 18/2 = 9cm

Beyond Triangles: Area of Quadrilaterals

  • Any quadrilateral can be divided into two triangles.
  • Find the length of the diagonal.
  • Calculate the area of each triangle using Heron's Formula, then add them up.

🎙️ Heron's Formula isn't just for triangles! You can use it to find the area of any four-sided shape (quadrilateral) by simply dividing it into two triangles along one of its diagonals. Then, apply Heron's to each triangle.

Worked Example 3: Quadrilateral Area

  • Quadrilateral ABCD with sides AB=9, BC=12, CD=5, DA=8.
  • Diagonal AC=13.
  • Triangle ABC: s=17, Area=54. Triangle ADC: s=13, Area=30.
  • Total Area = 54 + 30 = 84 sq units.

🎙️ Here's how it works. We have a quadrilateral. We draw a diagonal, say AC. Now we have two triangles, ABC and ADC. We calculate the area of each using Heron's, and then sum them up for the total area of the quadrilateral.

Summary & Key Takeaways

  • Area is the space a shape covers.
  • Basic Area = ½ × Base × Height.
  • Heron's Formula: Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)].
  • Semi-perimeter (s) = (a+b+c)/2.
  • Useful when height is not known.
