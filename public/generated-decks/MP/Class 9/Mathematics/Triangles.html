What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides.
  • Three vertices and three angles.
  • It's the simplest polygon.

🎙️ A triangle, as its name suggests, is a 'three-angle' shape. It's a closed figure made up of three line segments. These segments meet at three distinct points called vertices, forming three interior angles.

📐
Three-sided shape

Elements of a Triangle

  • Sides: AB, BC, CA
  • Vertices: A, B, C
  • Angles: ∠A, ∠B, ∠C

🎙️ Every triangle has three main elements: its sides, its vertices, and its angles. The sides are the line segments forming the triangle, vertices are the corners where sides meet, and angles are formed at these vertices.

Equilateral Triangle

  • All three sides are equal in length.
  • All three angles are also equal (60° each).
  • Highly symmetrical.

🎙️ An equilateral triangle is a special type where all its three sides are exactly the same length. Because of this, all its interior angles are also equal, each measuring 60 degrees. Think of it as perfectly balanced!

📏
All sides equal
6 / 25

Isosceles Triangle

  • At least two sides are equal in length.
  • Angles opposite to equal sides are also equal.
  • Common in many designs.

🎙️ In an isosceles triangle, at least two of its sides have the same length. A unique property of these triangles is that the angles opposite to these equal sides are also equal. You often see isosceles triangles in designs or logos.

↔️
Two sides equal
7 / 25

Scalene Triangle

  • All three sides have different lengths.
  • All three angles are also different.
  • No symmetry from sides/angles.

🎙️ A scalene triangle is the most 'general' type when classified by sides. Here, all three sides have completely different lengths. Consequently, all its three interior angles will also be different from each other.

📉
All sides different

Acute-angled Triangle

  • All three angles are acute angles.
  • Each angle is less than 90°.
  • Looks 'sharp' at all corners.

🎙️ Now, let's classify triangles based on their angles. An acute-angled triangle is one where all three of its interior angles are acute, meaning each angle measures less than 90 degrees. It looks 'pointy' at all its corners.

➕
All angles < 90°
10 / 25

Right-angled Triangle

  • Exactly one angle is a right angle (90°).
  • The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.
  • Crucial for Pythagoras theorem.

🎙️ A right-angled triangle is very important in geometry. It has exactly one angle that measures 90 degrees, forming a perfect 'L' shape. The side opposite this 90-degree angle is always the longest and is called the hypotenuse.

📐
One angle = 90°
11 / 25

Obtuse-angled Triangle

  • Exactly one angle is an obtuse angle.
  • One angle is greater than 90°.
  • The other two angles must be acute.

🎙️ An obtuse-angled triangle has one angle that is obtuse, meaning it measures greater than 90 degrees. It can only have one such angle, because if it had two, the sum of angles would exceed 180 degrees, which is impossible for a triangle.

➖
One angle > 90°

Angle Sum Property

  • The sum of all interior angles in any triangle is always 180°.
  • This is a fundamental property.
  • Applies to all types of triangles.

🎙️ This is one of the most important properties of triangles. No matter what kind of triangle you have – equilateral, isosceles, scalene, acute, right, or obtuse – the sum of its three interior angles will always add up to exactly 180 degrees. This property is universally true.

➕
Angles add up

Visualizing Angle Sum Property

  • Draw a line parallel to one side through the opposite vertex.
  • Observe alternate interior angles and angles on a straight line.
  • They form a straight angle (180°).

🎙️ Imagine drawing a line parallel to one side of a triangle, passing through the opposite vertex. You'll see that the three angles of the triangle can be rearranged to form a perfect straight line, demonstrating why their sum is always 180 degrees.

Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given angles 50° and 70°.
  • Let the third angle be x.
  • 50° + 70° + x = 180° => x = 60°.

🎙️ Let's apply the Angle Sum Property. If you have a triangle with two angles measuring 50 and 70 degrees, how do you find the third angle? You simply subtract the sum of the known angles from 180 degrees to find the missing one.

Exterior Angle Property

  • An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two interior opposite angles.
  • Exterior angle + adjacent interior angle = 180° (linear pair).
  • Useful for finding unknown angles.

🎙️ When you extend one side of a triangle, an exterior angle is formed. This exterior angle has a special relationship: it's equal to the sum of the two interior angles that are not adjacent to it. Also, an exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle form a linear pair, summing to 180 degrees.

➡️
Outside angle rule

Using Exterior Angle Property

  • Interior opposite angles: 60° and 80°.
  • Exterior angle = 60° + 80° = 140°.
  • Check: Adjacent interior angle = 180° - 140° = 40° (60+80+40=180).

🎙️ Consider a triangle where two interior opposite angles are 60 and 80 degrees. According to the Exterior Angle Property, the exterior angle will be the sum of these two, which is 140 degrees. This property can simplify finding angles quickly.

Congruence of Triangles

  • Two geometric figures are congruent if they have the same shape and same size.
  • For triangles, it means they can be perfectly superimposed on each other.
  • Symbol: ≅

🎙️ Congruence is a very important concept. When two triangles are congruent, it means they are exact copies of each other – same shape, same size. If you place one on top of the other, they would perfectly match. We use the symbol '≅' to denote congruence.

👯
Identical twins

SSS Congruence Rule (Side-Side-Side)

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to three sides of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • No need to check angles if sides match.

🎙️ The SSS rule is simple: if you know that all three sides of one triangle are exactly the same length as the corresponding three sides of another triangle, then those two triangles must be congruent. You don't even need to measure their angles!

📏📏📏
All sides match

SAS Congruence Rule (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If two sides and the *included* angle of one triangle are equal to two sides and the *included* angle of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • The angle *must* be between the two sides.

🎙️ The SAS rule says that if two sides and the angle *between* them (the included angle) in one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle in another triangle, then the triangles are congruent. The position of the angle is crucial here.

📏📐📏
Side, Angle, Side
21 / 25

ASA Congruence Rule (Angle-Side-Angle)

  • If two angles and the *included* side of one triangle are equal to two angles and the *included* side of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • The side *must* be between the two angles.

🎙️ Similar to SAS, the ASA rule requires two angles and the side *between* them (the included side) to be equal in both triangles for them to be congruent. This rule is often used when we have information about the angles and the connecting side.

📐📏📐
Angle, Side, Angle

RHS Congruence Rule (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side)

  • Applies only to right-angled triangles.
  • If the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one side of another right-angled triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.

🎙️ The RHS rule is specifically for right-angled triangles. If the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) and one other side of one right triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one other side of another right triangle, then they are congruent.

📐🦵
