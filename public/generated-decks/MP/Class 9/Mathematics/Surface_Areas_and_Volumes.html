What is Surface Area?

  • The total area of all the faces of a 3D object
  • Imagine 'unfolding' the shape flat
  • Measured in square units (cm², m²)

🎙️ Surface area is simply the total area of the 'skin' of a 3D object. If you could flatten out all its sides, the total area of those flattened pieces would be the surface area. It tells us how much material is needed to cover the object.

Lateral Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • Area of the four 'walls' of the cuboid
  • Excludes the top and bottom faces
  • Useful for painting walls or covering sides

🎙️ Sometimes, we only need to consider the area of the sides, not the top or bottom. This is called the Lateral Surface Area. Think about painting only the walls of a room, not the ceiling or floor.

Total Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • l = length, b = breadth, h = height
  • Sum of areas of all 6 faces

🎙️ To find the Total Surface Area, we add up the areas of all six faces. We have two faces of length x breadth, two of breadth x height, and two of length x height. This gives us the formula 2(lb + bh + hl).

1Area of Front/Back: 2(l×h)
2Area of Top/Bottom: 2(l×b)
3Area of Sides: 2(b×h)
4Total: 2(lh+lb+bh)

Surface Area of a Cube

  • Each face is a square with side 'a'
  • Area of one face = a²
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a²

🎙️ Since a cube has 6 identical square faces, and the area of one square face is side × side (a²), the total surface area is simply 6 times a². This formula is very handy for cubic objects.

Curved Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • The area of the curved part only
  • Imagine unrolling the curved surface into a rectangle
  • CSA = 2πrh

🎙️ If you cut open a cylinder's curved surface and flatten it, it forms a rectangle. The length of this rectangle is the circumference of the base (2πr), and its width is the height (h). So, the area is 2πrh.

🔄
Unrolling
16 / 25

Total Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • TSA = Curved Surface Area + Area of two circular bases
  • Area of one circular base = πr²
  • TSA = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r)

🎙️ To get the total surface area, we simply add the area of the two circular bases to the curved surface area. Each base is a circle, so its area is πr². Combining these gives us 2πrh + 2πr², which can also be written as 2πr(h+r).

1Curved Area: 2πrh
2Top Circle: πr²
3Bottom Circle: πr²
4Total: 2πrh + 2πr²

What is Volume?

  • The amount of space occupied by a 3D object
  • Describes the 'capacity' of a container
  • Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³, litres)

🎙️ Now, let's shift our focus to Volume. While surface area is about the 'skin', volume is about the 'stuff' inside. It tells us how much liquid, air, or any substance a 3D object can hold. Think of filling a glass with water.

Volume of a Cuboid

  • Volume = Length × Breadth × Height
  • V = l × b × h
  • Units: cubic units (e.g., cm³, m³)

🎙️ For a cuboid, finding the volume is straightforward. You simply multiply its three dimensions: length, breadth, and height. This gives you the total space it occupies or can contain.

Volume of a Cube

  • Since all sides are equal (a)
  • Volume = side × side × side
  • V = a³

🎙️ Just like with surface area, the volume of a cube is a special case of a cuboid. Since all its sides are equal, the volume is simply the side length multiplied by itself three times, or a-cubed.

Volume of a Cylinder

  • Volume = Area of base × Height
  • Area of circular base = πr²
  • V = πr²h

🎙️ For a cylinder, imagine stacking many thin circular discs. The volume is the area of its circular base (πr²) multiplied by its height (h). This formula is crucial for understanding the capacity of cylindrical containers.
