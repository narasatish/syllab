What is Coordinate Geometry?

  • Branch of mathematics.
  • Uses numbers to describe positions.
  • Connects algebra and geometry.

🎙️ Coordinate Geometry is a fantastic tool that helps us describe the exact location of any point in a plane using numbers. It's like giving an address to every single point, making it easy to find them.

The Cartesian System

  • Developed by René Descartes.
  • Uses two perpendicular lines.
  • Forms a coordinate plane.

🎙️ The foundation of Coordinate Geometry is the Cartesian System, named after the French mathematician René Descartes. He came up with the brilliant idea of using two number lines that cross each other to pinpoint locations.

The X-axis

  • The horizontal number line.
  • Represents left-right movement.
  • Positive to the right, negative to the left.

🎙️ The X-axis is our horizontal number line. Think of it like walking left and right. Moving right from the center gives positive numbers, and moving left gives negative numbers.

The Y-axis

  • The vertical number line.
  • Represents up-down movement.
  • Positive upwards, negative downwards.

🎙️ Similarly, the Y-axis is our vertical number line. This is like moving up and down. Moving up from the center gives positive numbers, and moving down gives negative numbers.

The Origin (0,0)

  • Point where X-axis and Y-axis intersect.
  • Always represented by (0, 0).
  • Our starting point for all measurements.

🎙️ The point where the X-axis and Y-axis cross each other is called the Origin. It's like the 'home base' or 'zero point' on our map, always having coordinates (0, 0).

Quadrants

  • The plane is divided into four regions.
  • Called Quadrant I, II, III, IV.
  • Numbered counter-clockwise.

🎙️ When the X and Y axes cross, they divide the entire plane into four parts, which we call quadrants. We number them I, II, III, and IV, starting from the top-right and moving counter-clockwise.

Coordinates of a Point (x, y)

  • A pair of numbers (x, y).
  • Describes a unique position.
  • Always written as (x-coordinate, y-coordinate).

🎙️ Every point in the coordinate plane has a unique 'address' called its coordinates. This address is always written as a pair of numbers, (x, y), where 'x' tells us the horizontal position and 'y' tells us the vertical position.

The Address

The Abscissa (X-coordinate)

  • The x-coordinate of a point.
  • Its distance from the Y-axis.
  • Indicates horizontal position.

🎙️ The first number in the coordinate pair (x, y) is called the abscissa. It tells us how far left or right the point is from the Y-axis. It's the horizontal component of the point's location.

↔️
Horizontal distance
11 / 25

The Ordinate (Y-coordinate)

  • The y-coordinate of a point.
  • Its distance from the X-axis.
  • Indicates vertical position.

🎙️ The second number in the coordinate pair (x, y) is called the ordinate. It tells us how far up or down the point is from the X-axis. It's the vertical component of the point's location.

Worked Example: Plot P(3, 2)

  • Move 3 units right (positive x).
  • Move 2 units up (positive y).

Points on the X-axis

  • Any point on the X-axis has its y-coordinate as 0.
  • Its form is (x, 0).
  • Examples: (5, 0), (-3, 0).

🎙️ If a point lies directly on the X-axis, it means it hasn't moved up or down from the X-axis. So, its y-coordinate will always be zero. For example, the point (5, 0) is on the X-axis.

↔️
No vertical movement
18 / 25

Points on the Y-axis

  • Any point on the Y-axis has its x-coordinate as 0.
  • Its form is (0, y).
  • Examples: (0, 7), (0, -2).

🎙️ Similarly, if a point lies directly on the Y-axis, it means it hasn't moved left or right from the Y-axis. Therefore, its x-coordinate will always be zero. For example, the point (0, 7) is on the Y-axis.
