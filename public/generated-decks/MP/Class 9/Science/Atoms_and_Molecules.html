What are Atoms and Molecules?

  • Atoms: The smallest particles of an element.
  • Molecules: Formed when two or more atoms join together.
  • They are the basic units of all matter.

🎙️ Imagine building a house. The smallest bricks are like atoms. When you put many bricks together to form a wall or a room, that's like forming a molecule. Everything in the universe is made of these tiny building blocks.

  • Help us understand how elements combine.

🎙️ For atoms to combine and form molecules, they follow some fixed rules, just like traffic rules for cars. These rules are called the Laws of Chemical Combination, and they were discovered by brilliant scientists.

Law of Conservation of Mass

  • Mass can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products.
  • Applies to all physical and chemical changes.

🎙️ This law means that in any chemical reaction, the total amount of 'stuff' you start with will be the same amount you end up with. For example, if you burn a candle, the wax and oxygen turn into smoke and ash, but the total mass remains constant.

Law of Constant Proportions

  • Pure chemical compounds always contain elements in fixed ratios.
  • Ratio is by mass, regardless of source.
  • Example: Water (H₂O) always has Hydrogen and Oxygen in 1:8 mass ratio.

🎙️ No matter where you get water from – a tap, a river, or even another planet – it will always be made of hydrogen and oxygen in the exact same proportion by mass, which is 1:8. This law is very important for understanding how compounds are formed.

Dalton's Atomic Theory

  • John Dalton proposed his theory in 1808.
  • Based on laws of chemical combination.
  • Explained the nature of matter and reactions.

🎙️ John Dalton was a brilliant scientist who put forward a comprehensive theory about atoms. His ideas helped explain why the laws of chemical combination work the way they do, laying the foundation for modern chemistry.

👨‍🔬
John Dalton

Postulates of Dalton's Atomic Theory

  • All matter is made of tiny, indivisible particles called atoms.
  • Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties.
  • Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.

🎙️ Dalton's theory had several key points. He said atoms are like tiny, solid balls that cannot be broken down further. He also stated that atoms of the same element are exactly alike, but different from atoms of other elements.

What is an Atom?

  • The smallest particle of an element.
  • May or may not exist independently.
  • Retains the chemical properties of that element.

🎙️ An atom is incredibly tiny, so small that you can't see it even with the most powerful optical microscope. Think of it as the fundamental unit that defines an element, like a single letter in an alphabet.

How Big are Atoms?

  • Extremely small; sizes are measured in nanometres (nm).
  • 1 nanometre = 10⁻⁹ metres (one billionth of a metre).
  • Example: Radius of a hydrogen atom is 0.037 nm.

🎙️ Atoms are unimaginably small! To give you an idea, if you stacked a million hydrogen atoms, they would barely be as thick as a single sheet of paper. We use a special unit called the nanometre to measure their size.

Symbols of Elements: Dalton's Way

  • Dalton used unique symbols for elements.
  • These were pictorial representations.
  • Helped in representing elements in his theory.

🎙️ Before modern symbols, Dalton had his own creative way of representing elements. He drew circles with different patterns inside them, like a dot for hydrogen or a cross for sulphur. These were like little pictures for each element.

Modern Symbols of Elements

  • IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry) approves symbols.
  • Symbols are usually one or two letters.
  • First letter is always capital, second is small (if present).

🎙️ Today, we use a much simpler and standardized system for element symbols. For example, 'H' for Hydrogen, 'O' for Oxygen, and 'Na' for Sodium. This makes it easy for scientists all over the world to understand each other.

What is a Molecule?

  • A group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together.
  • Can be atoms of the same element or different elements.
  • Exists independently and shows properties of the substance.

🎙️ When atoms get together and form a strong bond, they create a molecule. Think of it like friends holding hands. A molecule is the smallest unit of a substance that can exist on its own and still have all the properties of that substance.

Molecules of Elements

  • Composed of two or more identical atoms.
  • Examples: O₂ (Oxygen), N₂ (Nitrogen), O₃ (Ozone).
  • Some elements like Helium (He) exist as single atoms.

🎙️ In molecules of elements, all the atoms are the same. For instance, the oxygen we breathe is actually O₂, meaning two oxygen atoms are joined together. Some elements, like noble gases, prefer to stay alone as single atoms.

Molecules of Compounds

  • Composed of atoms of two or more different elements.
  • Atoms are joined in a fixed ratio by mass.
  • Examples: H₂O (Water), CO₂ (Carbon dioxide), NH₃ (Ammonia).

🎙️ When different types of atoms combine, they form molecules of compounds. Water, for example, is a compound made of hydrogen and oxygen atoms. The properties of a compound are totally different from the properties of its individual elements.

What is an Ion?

  • A charged particle.
  • Formed when an atom gains or loses electrons.
  • Cations are positive ions (lost electrons), Anions are negative (gained electrons).

🎙️ Sometimes, atoms can gain or lose tiny particles called electrons. When this happens, they become electrically charged and are called ions. Think of them as atoms with a 'plus' or 'minus' sign, like little batteries!

Writing Chemical Formulae

  • A chemical formula represents the composition of a compound.
  • Shows the elements present and their number of atoms.
  • Uses element symbols and subscripts.

🎙️ Chemical formulae are like the secret code of chemistry. They give us a quick way to write down what a molecule is made of. For example, H₂O instantly tells us it's water, with two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

Rules for Writing Chemical Formulae

  • Write the symbols of the elements.
  • Write their valencies (combining power) below each symbol.
  • Criss-cross the valencies to get subscripts.

🎙️ There's a simple method to write chemical formulae correctly. It involves understanding the 'valency' of each element, which is its capacity to combine with other elements. We'll practice this together!

Worked Example: Formula for Water

  • Elements: Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O).
  • Valencies: H (1), O (2).
  • Criss-cross valencies: H₂O₁.
  • Final Formula: H₂O.

🎙️ Let's apply the rules to water. Hydrogen has a valency of 1, and Oxygen has a valency of 2. When we criss-cross these, we get H₂O. The '1' is usually not written, so it's simply H₂O.

Worked Example: Formula for Sodium Chloride

  • Elements: Sodium (Na), Chlorine (Cl).
