2D Shapes vs. 3D Shapes

  • 2D shapes: Flat, have only length and breadth (e.g., square, circle).
  • 3D shapes: Solid, have length, breadth, and height (e.g., cube, cylinder).
  • 2D shapes have 'area', 3D shapes have 'surface area' and 'volume'.

🎙️ Before we dive deep, let's quickly recall the difference between 2D and 3D shapes. Think of a drawing on paper as 2D, while a real-life box is 3D. This distinction is crucial for our chapter.

What is Surface Area?

  • The total area of all the faces (surfaces) of a 3D object.
  • It's like the 'skin' or 'covering' of the object.
  • Measured in square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ Surface area tells us how much material would be needed to cover the entire outside of a 3D object. Imagine wrapping a gift box – the amount of wrapping paper needed is its surface area.

What is Volume?

  • It's the 'capacity' or 'inside space' of the object.
  • Measured in cubic units (e.g., cm³, m³).

🎙️ Volume, on the other hand, tells us how much space a 3D object takes up, or how much it can hold. Think of filling a container with water – the amount of water it holds is its volume.

Units for Area and Volume

  • Area is a 2D measurement, so its units are 'square' (e.g., cm², m²).
  • Volume is a 3D measurement, so its units are 'cubic' (e.g., cm³, m³).
  • Always remember to write the correct units with your answers!

🎙️ It's very important to use the correct units. Area is measured in square units because it involves two dimensions, while volume is measured in cubic units because it involves three dimensions. Don't mix them up!

The Cuboid: A Rectangular Box

  • A 3D shape with 6 rectangular faces.
  • It has 12 edges and 8 vertices.
  • Examples: Matchbox, brick, book.

🎙️ Our first shape is the cuboid, which is like a rectangular box. Imagine unfolding a matchbox – that's its net. It has three distinct dimensions: length, breadth, and height.

📦

Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • A cuboid has 3 pairs of identical faces.
  • Area of front/back faces: l × h
  • Area of top/bottom faces: l × b
  • Area of side faces: b × h
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl)

🎙️ To find the total surface area of a cuboid, we sum the areas of all its six faces. Since opposite faces are identical, we can use the formula 2 times (length x breadth + breadth x height + height x length).

📐
Faces of a cuboid

Example: Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • Given: length (l) = 5 cm, breadth (b) = 3 cm, height (h) = 2 cm.
  • Formula: TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • Calculation: 2((5×3) + (3×2) + (2×5)) = 2(15 + 6 + 10) = 2(31) = 62 cm².

The Cube: A Special Cuboid

  • A cuboid where all its faces are identical squares.
  • All 6 faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices are present.
  • All sides (length, breadth, height) are equal, let's call it 'a'.

🎙️ A cube is a special type of cuboid where all its sides are equal. Think of a dice or an ice cube. This makes its calculations a bit simpler, as all faces are identical squares.

🎲

Surface Area of a Cube

  • Each face of a cube is a square with side 'a'.
  • Area of one face = a × a = a².
  • Since there are 6 identical faces, Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a².

🎙️ Because a cube has six identical square faces, its total surface area is simply 6 times the area of one face. If the side length is 'a', the area of one face is a-squared, so the total is 6a-squared.

🧱
Six square faces

  • Calculation: 6 × (4 cm)² = 6 × 16 cm² = 96 cm².

Curved Surface Area (CSA) of a Cylinder

  • Area of only the curved part, excluding the circular bases.
  • Imagine unrolling the curved surface into a rectangle.
  • The length of this rectangle is 2πr (circumference), and width is h.
  • CSA = 2πrh.

🎙️ The curved surface area is just the area of the side of the cylinder. If you cut open a can and flatten it, you get a rectangle. The length of this rectangle is the circumference of the base, and its width is the height of the cylinder.

🌀
Unrolled surface
18 / 25

Total Surface Area (TSA) of a Cylinder

  • Includes the curved surface area AND the area of both circular bases.
  • Area of one circular base = πr².
  • Area of two circular bases = 2πr².
  • TSA = CSA + 2 × (Area of base) = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r).

🎙️ For the total surface area, we simply add the area of the two circular bases to the curved surface area. Remember, the area of a circle is pi-r-squared. This gives us the complete formula for TSA.

➕
Curved + Bases

Example: Total Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • Given: radius (r) = 7 cm, height (h) = 10 cm. (Use π = 22/7)
  • Formula: TSA = 2πr(h+r)
  • Calculation: 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (10+7) = 2 × 22 × 17 = 44 × 17 = 748 cm².

🎙️ Let's apply the TSA formula for a cylinder. Substitute the given radius and height into the formula. It's often helpful to use pi as 22/7 when the radius is a multiple of 7 for easier calculation.

Volume of a Cuboid

  • The space occupied by a rectangular box.
  • It's the product of its length, breadth, and height.
  • Formula: Volume (V) = l × b × h.

🎙️ Calculating the volume of a cuboid is straightforward. You simply multiply its three dimensions: length, breadth, and height. This gives you the total space it fills.

📏
Length × Breadth × Height
21 / 25

Volume of a Cube

  • The space occupied by a cube.
  • Since all sides are equal (a), the formula simplifies.
  • Formula: Volume (V) = a × a × a = a³.

🎙️ For a cube, because all its sides are equal, the volume is simply the side length multiplied by itself three times, or 'a cubed'. This is a direct application of the cuboid volume formula.

🧊
Side cubed

Volume of a Cylinder

  • The space occupied by a cylinder.
  • It's the area of its circular base multiplied by its height.
  • Formula: Volume (V) = Area of base × height = πr²h.

🎙️ To find the volume of a cylinder, imagine stacking many thin circles on top of each other. The volume is the area of one circular base multiplied by the total height of the stack.

🧪
Base area × Height
