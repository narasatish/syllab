What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • It has four vertices and four angles.
  • The word 'quad' means four, and 'lateral' means sides.

🎙️ Simply put, any closed figure with four straight sides is a quadrilateral. Think of a simple drawing with four corners and four edges. That's a quadrilateral!

The Trapezium

  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.

🎙️ Imagine a table with slanted legs but parallel top and bottom edges. That's a trapezium. Only one set of its opposite sides runs parallel to each other.

The Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral where both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  • Opposite sides are equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal.

🎙️ A parallelogram is like a 'tilted' rectangle. Both sets of opposite sides are parallel, and this gives it some interesting properties regarding its sides and angles.

Properties of a Parallelogram

  • Diagonals bisect each other.
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add up to 180°).
  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.

🎙️ These properties are very important for solving problems related to parallelograms. Remember that 'bisect' means to cut into two equal halves. So the diagonals cut each other exactly in the middle.

📐
Key features

Worked Example: Parallelogram Angles

  • In a parallelogram ABCD, if angle A = 70°.
  • Find the measures of angles B, C, and D.

🎙️ Since opposite angles are equal, angle C will also be 70°. Consecutive angles are supplementary, so angle B = 180° - 70° = 110°. And angle D will also be 110°.

The Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.
  • Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).
  • Diagonals bisect the angles of the rhombus.

🎙️ Think of a diamond shape. A rhombus is essentially a 'tilted' square. All its sides are equal, which gives its diagonals a special property of meeting at 90 degrees.

The Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.
  • Diagonals are equal in length and bisect each other.

🎙️ This is a very familiar shape! Your classroom door, a book, or a blackboard are all rectangles. Its defining feature is having all four corners perfectly square, or 90 degrees.

The Square

🎙️ The square is a very special quadrilateral! It has the properties of both a rectangle (all 90-degree angles) and a rhombus (all equal sides). It's a perfect, symmetrical shape.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral is 360°.
  • This is a fundamental property for all quadrilaterals.

🎙️ Just like a triangle's angles add up to 180°, a quadrilateral's interior angles always sum up to 360 degrees. This is a very useful property for finding missing angles.

➕
Angles add up to 360°

Worked Example: Finding a Missing Angle

  • In a quadrilateral ABCD, angles A, B, and C are 80°, 110°, and 70° respectively.
  • Find the measure of angle D.

🎙️ We know the sum of all angles is 360°. So, add the given angles (80+110+70 = 260°). Then subtract this sum from 360° to find angle D (360 - 260 = 100°). So, angle D is 100°.
