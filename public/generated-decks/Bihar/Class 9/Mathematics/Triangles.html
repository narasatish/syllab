What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides.
  • It has three vertices and three angles.
  • Simplest polygon in geometry.

🎙️ A triangle is a closed figure made up of three line segments. These segments meet at three points called vertices, forming three angles inside. It's the most basic shape with straight sides.

🔺
A basic triangle

Elements of a Triangle

  • Vertices: The three corner points (A, B, C).
  • Sides: The three line segments (AB, BC, CA).
  • Angles: The three interior angles (∠A, ∠B, ∠C).

🎙️ Every triangle has three main components: its corners are called vertices, the lines connecting them are its sides, and the space between the sides at each vertex forms an angle. Understanding these parts is crucial.

Types of Triangles (by Sides): Equilateral

  • All three sides are equal in length.
  • All three angles are also equal (60° each).
  • A perfectly balanced triangle.

🎙️ An equilateral triangle is special because all its sides are exactly the same length. This also means all its interior angles are equal, each measuring 60 degrees. It's a very symmetrical shape.

📏
Equal sides
6 / 25

Types of Triangles (by Sides): Isosceles

  • Exactly two sides are equal in length.
  • Angles opposite to equal sides are also equal.
  • Common in many designs.

🎙️ An isosceles triangle has two sides of equal length. An interesting property is that the angles opposite these two equal sides are also equal. Think of a roof gable, it often forms an isosceles triangle.

📏
Two equal sides
7 / 25

Types of Triangles (by Sides): Scalene

  • All three sides are of different lengths.
  • All three angles are also different.
  • No equal sides or angles.

🎙️ In a scalene triangle, every side has a different length. Consequently, all three interior angles will also be different from each other. It's the most 'uneven' type of triangle.

📏
All sides different

Types of Triangles (by Angles): Acute-angled

  • All three angles are acute (less than 90°).
  • No angle is 90° or greater.
  • Looks 'sharp' at all corners.

🎙️ An acute-angled triangle is one where all its interior angles are acute, meaning each angle measures less than 90 degrees. It looks like a 'sharp' triangle from all sides.

➕
All angles < 90°
10 / 25

Types of Triangles (by Angles): Right-angled

  • Exactly one angle is a right angle (90°).
  • The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.
  • Very important in trigonometry.

🎙️ A right-angled triangle is easily identified by having one angle that is exactly 90 degrees. This specific type of triangle is extremely important in many areas of mathematics, especially trigonometry, which you will learn more about later.

📐
One angle = 90°

Types of Triangles (by Angles): Obtuse-angled

  • Exactly one angle is obtuse (greater than 90°).
  • The other two angles must be acute.
  • Looks 'wide' at one corner.

🎙️ An obtuse-angled triangle has one angle that is greater than 90 degrees. Since the sum of all angles must be 180 degrees, the other two angles must necessarily be acute. It appears 'stretched' or 'wide' at one vertex.

➖
One angle > 90°

Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

  • The sum of all three interior angles is always 180°.
  • This is a fundamental property.
  • Useful for finding missing angles.

🎙️ No matter what type of triangle it is, if you add up the measures of its three interior angles, the total will always be exactly 180 degrees. This property is incredibly useful for solving problems involving unknown angles.

Worked Example 1: Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given angles: 60° and 70°.
  • Let the third angle be 'x'.
  • 60° + 70° + x = 180°.

🎙️ If two angles of a triangle are 60° and 70°, we can find the third angle using the angle sum property. We set up the equation 60 + 70 + x = 180, which simplifies to 130 + x = 180. Solving for x gives us 50 degrees.

Exterior Angle Property

  • An exterior angle is formed by extending one side.
  • It equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • Another powerful tool for angle calculations.

🎙️ When you extend one side of a triangle, the angle formed outside is called an exterior angle. This exterior angle is always equal to the sum of the two interior angles that are not adjacent to it. This provides a shortcut for certain problems.

↔️
Exterior angle

Worked Example 2: Using Exterior Angle Property

  • Interior opposite angles: 50° and 60°.
  • Exterior angle = 50° + 60°.
  • So, exterior angle = 110°.

🎙️ Imagine a triangle with two interior angles of 50 and 60 degrees. The exterior angle formed by extending a side, opposite to these two, will simply be their sum. So, 50 plus 60 gives us 110 degrees for the exterior angle.

Congruence of Triangles: What does it mean?

  • Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and size.
  • They can perfectly overlap each other.
  • For triangles, all corresponding sides and angles must be equal.

🎙️ Congruence is a very important concept in geometry. When two triangles are congruent, it means they are exact copies of each other. If you were to place one on top of the other, they would perfectly match up, side for side and angle for angle.

🤝
Perfect match

Congruence Criteria: SSS (Side-Side-Side)

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to three sides of another.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • Knowing all sides ensures congruence.

🎙️ The SSS criterion states that if all three sides of one triangle are equal in length to the corresponding three sides of another triangle, then those two triangles must be congruent. No need to check angles if all sides match.

📏📏📏
All sides match
18 / 25

Congruence Criteria: SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If two sides and the *included* angle of one triangle are equal.
  • To two sides and the *included* angle of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ SAS means if two sides and the angle *between* them (the included angle) in one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and included angle in another, then the triangles are congruent. The order of letters is important here.

📏📐📏
Side, Angle, Side
19 / 25

Congruence Criteria: ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)

  • If two angles and the *included* side of one triangle are equal.
  • To two angles and the *included* side of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ The ASA criterion states that if two angles and the side *between* them (the included side) in one triangle are equal to the corresponding parts in another, then the triangles are congruent. This is similar to SAS but with angles and the connecting side.

📐📏📐
Angle, Side, Angle
20 / 25

Congruence Criteria: AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)

  • If two angles and a *non-included* side of one triangle are equal.
  • To two angles and a *non-included* side of another triangle.
  • Then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ AAS is similar to ASA, but here the side is *not* between the two given angles. If two angles and any one corresponding side (not necessarily included) are equal, the triangles are congruent. This is often a direct consequence of ASA and the angle sum property.

📐📐📏
Angle, Angle, Side

Congruence Criteria: RHS (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side)

  • Specific to right-angled triangles.
  • If the hypotenuse and one side of a right triangle are equal.
  • To the hypotenuse and one side of another right triangle, they are congruent.

🎙️ RHS is a special congruence rule only for right-angled triangles. If the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) and one other side of two right triangles are equal, then the triangles are congruent. It's a very powerful rule for right triangles.
