Heron's Formula: The Big Idea

  • Finds area using only side lengths
  • No need for perpendicular height
  • Works for ALL types of triangles

🎙️ The amazing thing about Heron's Formula is that it allows us to calculate the area of any triangle, whether it's equilateral, isosceles, or scalene, simply by knowing the lengths of its three sides.

Key Term 1: Perimeter of a Triangle

  • Sum of all three sides
  • Let sides be a, b, c
  • Perimeter = a + b + c

🎙️ Before we dive into the formula, let's quickly review some terms. The perimeter of any shape is the total length of its boundary. For a triangle, it's just the sum of its three sides.

Key Term 2: Semi-Perimeter (s)

  • Half of the perimeter
  • Denoted by 's'
  • s = (a + b + c) / 2

🎙️ Heron's Formula uses a special term called the 'semi-perimeter'. 'Semi' means half, so the semi-perimeter is simply half of the triangle's total perimeter. We use the letter 's' to represent it.

➗
Half the Sum

Heron's Formula: The Magic Equation

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Where a, b, c are side lengths
  • And s is the semi-perimeter

🎙️ And here it is! Heron's Formula. It might look a bit complex at first, but it's very logical. You multiply the semi-perimeter by the differences of the semi-perimeter and each side, then take the square root.

📝
The Formula Itself

Steps to Apply Heron's Formula

  • 1. Find the perimeter (a+b+c)
  • 2. Calculate the semi-perimeter (s)
  • 3. Substitute values into the formula
  • 4. Calculate the final area

🎙️ Using Heron's Formula is a step-by-step process. First, find the total length of the sides. Then, calculate half of that. After that, plug these values into the formula and solve for the area.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Semi-Perimeter

  • Sides: a=50m, b=80m, c=120m
  • Perimeter = 50 + 80 + 120 = 250m
  • Semi-perimeter (s) = 250 / 2 = 125m

🎙️ First, let's find the perimeter by adding all the side lengths. 50 plus 80 plus 120 gives us 250 meters. Then, we divide this by two to get the semi-perimeter, which is 125 meters.

Worked Example 1: Applying Heron's Formula

  • s = 125m, a=50m, b=80m, c=120m
  • Area = √[125(125-50)(125-80)(125-120)]
  • Area = √[125 × 75 × 45 × 5]
  • Area ≈ 1452.37 sq m

🎙️ Now, we substitute these values into Heron's Formula. After performing the subtractions and multiplications under the square root, we find the area of the field to be approximately 1452.37 square meters.

Worked Example 2: Applying Heron's Formula

  • s = 42m, a=26m, b=28m, c=30m
  • Area = √[42(42-26)(42-28)(42-30)]
  • Area = √[42 × 16 × 14 × 12]
  • Area = 336 sq m

🎙️ Now, substitute these values into Heron's Formula: s is 42, and then we subtract each side. Multiply all the terms under the square root and find the square root. The area comes out to exactly 336 square meters.
