What is Coordinate Geometry?

  • Describes position using numbers.
  • Uses a system of coordinates.
  • Helps locate points in a plane.

🎙️ Imagine trying to tell someone exactly where something is. Coordinate Geometry gives us a powerful tool to do just that! It's like having a unique address for every single point in a flat surface, using just numbers.

The Cartesian System

  • Developed by René Descartes.
  • Uses two perpendicular lines.
  • Foundation of Coordinate Geometry.

🎙️ This brilliant system was invented by the French mathematician and philosopher, René Descartes. He imagined using two intersecting lines to describe any point. That's why it's named after him!

The Number Line Revisited

  • A line with equally spaced numbers.
  • Positive numbers to the right of zero.
  • Negative numbers to the left of zero.

🎙️ You are already familiar with the number line. It helps us locate numbers in one dimension. Coordinate Geometry extends this idea to two dimensions, using two number lines.

Introducing the Coordinate Plane

  • Two perpendicular number lines.
  • Intersect at a point called the origin.
  • Forms a 2D surface.

🎙️ Now, imagine two number lines, one horizontal and one vertical, crossing each other at their zero points. This creates a flat surface called the Coordinate Plane, or sometimes the Cartesian Plane.

The Axes and the Origin

  • Horizontal line is X-axis (x'Ox).
  • Vertical line is Y-axis (y'Oy).
  • Intersection point is Origin (O).

🎙️ The horizontal line is traditionally called the X-axis, and the vertical line is the Y-axis. The point where they cross each other is super important; it's called the Origin, and it's our starting point for everything.

🎯
Center Point

Dividing the Plane into Quadrants

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • Each region is called a Quadrant.
  • Numbered counter-clockwise from top-right.

🎙️ When the X and Y axes cross, they cut the entire plane into four distinct parts. We call these parts 'Quadrants'. They are numbered counter-clockwise, starting from the top-right section.

Locating a Point in the Plane

  • Requires two numbers.
  • First number is distance along X-axis.
  • Second number is distance along Y-axis.

🎙️ To find any point on this plane, we need two pieces of information, not just one. It's like giving directions: 'go 3 steps right, then 2 steps up'. These two numbers are the point's 'coordinates'.

📌
Pinpointing Location
10 / 25

Coordinates of a Point (x, y)

  • Written as an ordered pair (x, y).
  • x is the abscissa (X-coordinate).
  • y is the ordinate (Y-coordinate).

🎙️ We write the coordinates of a point as an ordered pair, always (x, y). The 'x' value tells us how far left or right from the origin, and the 'y' value tells us how far up or down. Remember, 'x' always comes first!

Worked Example: Plotting A(3, 2)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 3 units right (positive X).
  • Move 2 units up (positive Y).

🎙️ Let's plot our first point! To plot A(3, 2), we begin at the origin. The '3' tells us to move 3 units to the right along the X-axis. Then, the '2' tells us to move 2 units upwards, parallel to the Y-axis. That's point A!

Worked Example: Plotting B(-2, 4)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 2 units left (negative X).
  • Move 4 units up (positive Y).

🎙️ Now let's try a point with a negative X-coordinate. For B(-2, 4), we again start at the origin. The '-2' means we move 2 units to the *left* along the X-axis. Then, the '4' means we move 4 units *up*. This point will be in the second quadrant.

1Start at (0,0)
2Move 2 left
3Move 4 up

Worked Example: Plotting C(4, -3)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 4 units right (positive X).
  • Move 3 units down (negative Y).

🎙️ What if the Y-coordinate is negative? For C(4, -3), we move 4 units to the *right* from the origin. Then, the '-3' tells us to move 3 units *downwards*. This point will be in the fourth quadrant.

1Start at (0,0)
2Move 4 right

Worked Example: Plotting D(-3, -1)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 3 units left (negative X).
  • Move 1 unit down (negative Y).

🎙️ Finally, let's plot a point where both coordinates are negative. For D(-3, -1), we move 3 units to the *left* from the origin, and then 1 unit *downwards*. This point will be in the third quadrant.

Points on the X-axis

  • Any point on the X-axis has its Y-coordinate as 0.
  • The form is (x, 0).
  • Example: (5, 0), (-2, 0).

🎙️ If a point lies directly on the X-axis, it means it hasn't moved up or down from the X-axis. Therefore, its Y-coordinate will always be zero. Remember this special property for points on the axes.

➡️

Points on the Y-axis

  • Any point on the Y-axis has its X-coordinate as 0.
  • The form is (0, y).
  • Example: (0, 3), (0, -4).

🎙️ Similarly, if a point lies directly on the Y-axis, it means it hasn't moved left or right from the Y-axis. So, its X-coordinate will always be zero. These are important observations to remember.

⬆️

Coordinates of the Origin

  • The point where X-axis and Y-axis intersect.
  • Its coordinates are (0, 0).
  • It is the reference point for all other points.

🎙️ The Origin is the central point of our coordinate system. Since it's the 'zero' point for both the X and Y axes, its coordinates are always (0, 0). All other points are measured relative to the origin.
