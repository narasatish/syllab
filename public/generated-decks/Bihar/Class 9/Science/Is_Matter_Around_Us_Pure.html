What are Pure Substances?

  • Consist of a single type of particle.
  • Have a uniform composition throughout.
  • Cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by physical processes.

🎙️ A pure substance is like a perfect recipe with only one ingredient. Every part of it is exactly the same, and you can't easily break it down into simpler things using simple methods.

  • Cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions.
  • Made up of only one type of atom.
  • Examples: Gold, Oxygen, Iron.

Compounds: Pure Substances from Elements

  • Formed when two or more elements chemically combine.
  • Combined in a fixed ratio by mass.
  • Properties are different from their constituent elements.

🎙️ Compounds are like new dishes created by combining different ingredients in a specific way. Once combined, the new substance has entirely different properties from its original elements, like water from hydrogen and oxygen.

Chemical Combination

Examples of Compounds

  • Water (H₂O): Hydrogen and Oxygen combined.
  • Common Salt (NaCl): Sodium and Chlorine combined.
  • Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): Carbon and Oxygen combined.

🎙️ Water is a compound we all know well. It's formed when hydrogen and oxygen atoms chemically bond. Similarly, the salt we use daily is a compound of sodium and chlorine, having very different properties than sodium metal or chlorine gas.

What are Mixtures?

  • Contain two or more pure substances physically mixed together.
  • Components retain their individual properties.
  • Can be separated by physical methods.

🎙️ Unlike compounds, mixtures are like a fruit salad where each fruit keeps its own taste and identity. The ingredients are just put together, not chemically bonded, so they can be separated fairly easily.

Homogeneous Mixtures: Uniform Blend

  • Have a uniform composition throughout.
  • Components are indistinguishable from each other.
  • Examples: Salt solution, Air.

🎙️ In a homogeneous mixture, everything is so well mixed that you can't tell the different parts apart. Imagine dissolving sugar in water; the sweetness is uniform throughout, and you can't see the sugar particles anymore.

Heterogeneous Mixtures: Non-Uniform Blend

  • Have a non-uniform composition.
  • Components can be seen as distinct parts.
  • Examples: Sand and water, Oil and water.

🎙️ In contrast, heterogeneous mixtures have visible boundaries between their components. If you mix sand and water, you can clearly see the sand settling at the bottom. The mixture isn't the same throughout.

Solutions: A Special Homogeneous Mixture

  • A homogeneous mixture of two or more substances.
  • The particles are very small and uniformly distributed.
  • Consists of a solute (substance dissolved) and a solvent (substance doing the dissolving).

🎙️ A solution is the most common type of homogeneous mixture. It's when one substance completely disappears into another, like sugar in water. The substance that dissolves is the solute, and the one that dissolves it is the solvent.

💧
Perfectly Dissolved
18 / 25

Properties of a Solution

  • Particles are smaller than 1 nm in diameter.
  • Cannot be seen by the naked eye.
  • Does not scatter a beam of light (Tyndall effect absent).
  • Stable: Solute particles do not settle down.

🎙️ Solutions have unique characteristics due to their tiny particles. They are transparent, meaning light passes straight through, and the dissolved particles will never separate or settle at the bottom, making them very stable.

Suspensions: Heterogeneous & Settling

  • A heterogeneous mixture.
  • Solid particles are dispersed in a liquid.
  • Particles are large enough to be seen and settle over time.

🎙️ Suspensions are like a temporary mix, where solid particles are floating around in a liquid. If you leave it undisturbed, gravity will eventually pull the particles down, and they will settle at the bottom.

🌪️
Particles in Motion
21 / 25

Properties of a Suspension

  • Particles are large (greater than 100 nm).
  • Can be seen with the naked eye.
  • Scatters a beam of light (Tyndall effect).
  • Unstable: Particles settle down on their own.

🎙️ The large particle size in suspensions gives them distinct properties. They appear cloudy, scatter light, and are unstable, meaning the particles will eventually separate and settle if left alone.

Colloids: The In-Between Mixture

  • A heterogeneous mixture, but appears homogeneous.
  • Particle size is intermediate (1-100 nm).
  • Particles are uniformly spread throughout the solution.

🎙️ Colloids are fascinating because they trick your eyes! They look uniform, but they are actually heterogeneous. Their particle size is bigger than solutions but smaller than suspensions, giving them unique properties.

🥛

24 / 25

Properties of a Colloid

  • Particles are intermediate in size (1-100 nm).
  • Cannot be seen by the naked eye.
  • Scatters a beam of light (Tyndall effect present).
  • Stable: Particles do not settle down.

🎙️ Colloids are stable and don't settle, similar to solutions, but unlike solutions, they scatter light, a phenomenon called the Tyndall effect. This means you can see the path of a light beam through them.

🎙️ Milk is a perfect example of a colloid. It looks uniform and white, but it's actually tiny fat globules and protein particles suspended in water. If you shine a flashlight through it, you can observe the Tyndall effect.
