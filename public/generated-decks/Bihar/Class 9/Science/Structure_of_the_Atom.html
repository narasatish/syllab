What Exactly is an Atom?

  • Smallest particle of an element.
  • Retains the chemical properties of that element.
  • Too small to be seen even with powerful microscopes.

🎙️ An atom is the smallest particle of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element. Think of them as the tiny LEGO bricks that build everything we see and touch.

Early Ideas: Unseen Particles

  • Ancient Indian philosophers (Maharishi Kanad) proposed 'Parmanu'.
  • Ancient Greek philosophers (Democritus) proposed 'atomos'.
  • Both suggested matter is made of indivisible particles.

🎙️ Long ago, ancient Indian and Greek philosophers wondered what everything was made of. They proposed that matter could be broken down into tiny, indivisible particles, which they called 'Parmanu' or 'atomos'.

Dalton's Atomic Theory: First Scientific Model

  • John Dalton proposed his theory in 1808.
  • Atoms are indivisible, indestructible particles.
  • Atoms of the same element are identical.

🎙️ John Dalton, an English scientist, gave us the first scientific theory of atoms in the early 1800s. He proposed that atoms are indivisible spheres, and all atoms of a given element are exactly alike.

Beyond Dalton: Atoms are Divisible!

  • Dalton's theory had limitations.
  • Later experiments showed atoms are not simple spheres.
  • Atoms are made of even smaller, subatomic particles.

🎙️ Later experiments by other scientists revealed that Dalton's idea of indivisible atoms was incorrect! It was found that atoms are actually composed of even smaller particles, challenging the initial atomic theory.

Discovery of Electrons: The Negatively Charged Particle

  • Discovered by J.J. Thomson in 1897.
  • Using cathode ray tube experiments.
  • Electrons carry a negative charge and have very little mass.

🎙️ J.J. Thomson, through his cathode ray experiments, discovered the electron. This was the first subatomic particle found, proving that atoms are indeed divisible and contain these tiny, negatively charged particles.

Thomson's Model: The 'Plum Pudding' Atom

  • Proposed by J.J. Thomson.
  • Atom is a sphere of uniform positive charge.
  • Electrons (plums) are embedded within it.

🎙️ Thomson imagined the atom as a sphere of positive charge with negatively charged electrons embedded in it, much like plums in a pudding or seeds in a watermelon. This was the 'Plum Pudding' model.

Real Example: Static Electricity & Electrons

  • Rubbing a balloon on hair causes attraction.
  • Electrons are transferred from hair to balloon.
  • Shows electrons can move and create charge.

🎙️ When you rub a balloon on your hair, you're actually transferring electrons! This simple act of creating static electricity demonstrates that electrons are mobile and can move from one object to another.

Discovery of Protons: The Positively Charged Particle

  • Discovered by E. Goldstein (canal rays) and Rutherford.
  • Protons carry a positive charge.
  • Located in the atom's nucleus, much heavier than electrons.

🎙️ E. Goldstein's canal ray experiments, and later Rutherford's work, led to the discovery of protons. These particles carry a positive charge and are much heavier than electrons, residing in the atom's core.

Rutherford's Experiment: Probing the Atom

  • Ernest Rutherford's famous alpha-particle scattering experiment.
  • Alpha particles (positive charge) fired at a thin gold foil.
  • Aims to understand the internal structure of the atom.

🎙️ Rutherford designed a famous experiment where he bombarded a thin gold foil with fast-moving alpha particles. He wanted to see how these particles interacted with the atoms in the foil and reveal their structure.

🎯
Alpha Particles
11 / 25

Rutherford's Observations: Surprising Results!

  • Most alpha particles passed straight through the foil.
  • A few were deflected by small angles.
  • A very few (1 in 12,000) bounced back completely.

🎙️ Rutherford's observations were surprising! Most alpha particles passed straight through, indicating a lot of empty space. Some were deflected, and astonishingly, a very few bounced back completely, implying a dense center.

Rutherford's Model: The Nuclear Atom

  • Atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus at its center.
  • Electrons revolve around the nucleus in well-defined orbits.
  • Most of the atom's mass is concentrated in the nucleus.

🎙️ Based on his observations, Rutherford proposed that the atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus at its center, with electrons orbiting around it, much like planets around the sun. This is the nuclear model.

🪐
Planetary Model

Challenges to Rutherford: Why Atoms Don't Collapse

  • Classical physics predicts orbiting electrons would lose energy.
  • Electrons should spiral into the nucleus, making atoms unstable.
  • Rutherford's model could not explain the stability of an atom.

🎙️ A major problem with Rutherford's model was its inability to explain atomic stability. According to classical physics, orbiting electrons should continuously lose energy and eventually fall into the nucleus, causing the atom to collapse.

❓
Stability Question
14 / 25

Bohr's Model: Fixed Orbits & Energy Shells

  • Niels Bohr proposed fixed energy levels or 'shells'.
  • Electrons do not radiate energy while in these discrete orbits.
  • Electron jumps between shells involve energy absorption/emission.

🎙️ Niels Bohr improved Rutherford's model by proposing that electrons orbit in specific, stable energy shells or discrete orbits, without radiating energy. This explained the stability of atoms and their characteristic spectra.

Bohr's model of the atom proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy levels

  • Electricity excites electrons in neon gas.
  • Electrons jump to higher energy levels.
  • They fall back, releasing energy as colored light.

🎙️ The vibrant colors of neon lights are a real-world example of Bohr's model. When electricity is passed through neon gas, electrons jump to higher energy levels and then fall back, emitting light of specific colors.

Discovery of Neutrons: The Neutral Partner

  • Discovered by James Chadwick in 1932.
  • Neutrons have no electrical charge (neutral).
  • Located in the nucleus, mass similar to protons.

🎙️ James Chadwick discovered the neutron, a subatomic particle with no charge, residing in the nucleus along with protons. It has a mass similar to that of a proton and plays a crucial role in nuclear stability.

Summary: The Atom's Three Main Parts

  • Protons: Positive charge, in nucleus.
  • Neutrons: No charge, in nucleus.
  • Electrons: Negative charge, orbit nucleus.

🎙️ So, the atom is made of three fundamental particles: protons (positive) and neutrons (neutral) forming the dense nucleus, and electrons (negative) orbiting around it. This is our current understanding of the atomic structure.

Atomic Number (Z): The Identity Card

  • Number of protons in an atom's nucleus.
  • Unique for each element (e.g., all Carbon atoms have 6 protons).
  • For a neutral atom, Atomic Number = Number of Electrons.

🎙️ The atomic number (Z) is the number of protons in an atom's nucleus. It uniquely identifies an element; no two elements have the same atomic number. For a neutral atom, it also equals the number of electrons.

🆔
Unique Identifier
19 / 25

Mass Number (A): The Weight of the Nucleus

  • Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
  • Represents the approximate mass of the atom.
  • Electrons contribute negligible mass.

🎙️ The mass number (A) is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom's nucleus. Since electrons have very little mass, the mass number essentially tells us the 'weight' of the atom's nucleus.

Isotopes: Same Element, Different Neutrons

  • Atoms of the same element (same atomic number).
  • Have different numbers of neutrons.
  • Result in different mass numbers.

🎙️ Isotopes are atoms of the same element, meaning they have the same number of protons and thus the same atomic number. However, they differ in the number of neutrons, leading to different mass numbers.

Variations

Real Example: Isotopes in Medical Imaging

  • Radioactive isotopes used as 'tracers' in the body.
  • Help diagnose diseases (e.g., PET scans).
  • Also used in cancer therapy (e.g., Cobalt-60).

🎙️ Radioactive isotopes like Carbon-14 or Iodine-131 are invaluable in medicine. They are used as tracers in diagnostic imaging like PET scans to detect diseases or in radiation therapy to treat cancer.
