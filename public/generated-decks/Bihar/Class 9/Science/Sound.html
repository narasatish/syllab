What is Sound?

  • Sound is a form of energy that produces the sensation of hearing.
  • It travels as waves through a medium.
  • We experience sound constantly in our daily lives.

🎙️ Sound is a fundamental part of our world. It's not just noise; it's a type of energy that our ears can detect, allowing us to perceive the world around us. Think of it like ripples in water, but in the air.

Sound is Produced by Vibrations

  • Sound is generated when objects vibrate.
  • Vibration means a rapid back-and-forth movement.
  • These vibrations disturb the surrounding medium.

🎙️ The key to producing sound is vibration. Whenever you hear something, an object somewhere is vibrating. For example, when you speak, your vocal cords vibrate to produce your voice.

🎙️ Let's look at some common examples. When a guitarist plucks a string, you can often see it blurring as it vibrates. Similarly, a ringing bell or a drum skin vibrates visibly when struck.

How Sound Travels: Propagation

  • Sound needs a medium to travel from one place to another.
  • It travels as a series of compressions and rarefactions.
  • These disturbances move through the medium, not the particles themselves.

🎙️ Once sound is produced, it needs to travel to reach our ears. This journey is called propagation. Imagine a line of dominoes falling; each domino pushes the next, but the dominoes themselves don't travel far, only the 'fall' does.

Sound Needs a Medium

  • Sound can travel through solids, liquids, and gases.
  • It cannot travel through a vacuum.
  • The speed of sound varies depending on the medium.

🎙️ Unlike light, sound cannot travel in empty space. It requires a material medium – like air, water, or even a table – to transmit its vibrations. This is why you can't hear anything in outer space.

The Bell Jar Experiment

  • An electric bell rings inside an airtight jar.
  • As air is pumped out, the sound becomes fainter.
  • In a vacuum, no sound is heard, though the hammer still strikes.

🎙️ This famous experiment clearly demonstrates that sound needs a medium. Even though the bell is ringing, if there's no air to carry the vibrations, we hear nothing. This proves the necessity of a medium.

How Fast Does Sound Travel?

  • Speed of sound is the distance sound travels per unit time.
  • It is much slower than the speed of light.
  • Affected by properties of the medium like temperature and density.

🎙️ Have you ever seen lightning before hearing thunder? This is because light travels much faster than sound. The speed of sound changes with the material it's traveling through, and even with temperature.

Comparing Sound Speed

  • Sound travels fastest in solids.
  • It travels slower in liquids than in solids.
  • Sound travels slowest in gases (like air).

🎙️ This is a very important concept. Think about why you might hear a train approaching by putting your ear to the tracks before you hear it through the air. Steel is a solid, and sound travels much faster through it.

Wavelength (λ)

  • The distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions.
  • Represented by the Greek letter lambda (λ).
  • Measured in metres (m).

🎙️ Imagine a Slinky spring. If you push one end, you create a compression. The distance between one compressed region and the next similar compressed region is the wavelength. It tells us how 'spread out' the wave is.

📏
Distance of a Wave

Frequency (ν)

  • The number of oscillations (vibrations) per unit time.
  • Determines the pitch of the sound.
  • Measured in Hertz (Hz).

🎙️ Frequency tells us how many times a particle of the medium vibrates back and forth in one second. A higher frequency means more vibrations per second, resulting in a higher-pitched sound, like a whistle.

Amplitude (A)

  • The maximum displacement of particles from their mean position.
  • Determines the loudness or intensity of the sound.
  • Measured in units of the medium's displacement (e.g., metres).

🎙️ Amplitude is like the 'height' of the sound wave. A larger amplitude means the particles are displaced more forcefully, which we perceive as a louder sound. Think of hitting a drum gently versus hitting it hard.

Pitch and Frequency

  • Pitch is how high or low a sound appears to be.
  • Higher frequency leads to higher pitch.
  • Lower frequency leads to lower pitch.

🎙️ Pitch is a very important characteristic of sound. A child's voice typically has a higher pitch than an adult male's voice because their vocal cords vibrate at a higher frequency. Musical instruments use pitch to create melodies.

Loudness and Amplitude

  • Loudness is the intensity of sound as perceived by the ear.
  • It is proportional to the square of the amplitude.
  • Measured in Decibels (dB).

🎙️ Loudness is how strong or faint a sound feels. When you turn up the volume on a speaker, you are increasing the amplitude of the sound waves, making them louder. Be careful with very loud sounds as they can damage your hearing.

Sound Can Reflect

  • Sound waves bounce off surfaces, similar to light reflection.
  • Hard, smooth surfaces are good reflectors of sound.
  • The laws of reflection apply to sound waves too.

🎙️ Just like a ball bounces off a wall, sound waves can bounce off surfaces. This phenomenon is called reflection. This is why rooms with bare walls can sound very echoey, as the sound keeps reflecting.

What is an Echo?

  • An echo is the repetition of sound due to reflection.
  • Requires a minimum distance between source and reflector.
  • Our brain needs about 0.1 seconds to distinguish two sounds.

🎙️ You've probably experienced an echo in a large, empty hall or a valley. For us to hear a distinct echo, the reflected sound must reach our ears at least 0.1 seconds after the original sound. This requires the reflecting surface to be far enough away.

Reverberation

  • Multiple reflections of sound in an enclosed space.
  • Causes persistence of sound after the source stops.
  • Excessive reverberation can make speech unclear.

🎙️ Reverberation is like many echoes happening very quickly, overlapping with each other. In large auditoriums, if there's too much reverberation, the sound becomes muddled and difficult to understand. Architects design concert halls to control reverberation.

Our Hearing Range

  • Humans can hear sounds between 20 Hz and 20,000 Hz.
  • Sounds below 20 Hz are Infrasound.
  • Sounds above 20,000 Hz are Ultrasound.

🎙️ Our ears are amazing, but they have limits. We can't hear everything. Animals like dogs can hear much higher frequencies (ultrasound), and elephants use very low frequencies (infrasound) to communicate over long distances.

Uses of Ultrasound

  • Used in medical imaging (Sonography) to see inside the body.
  • Used for detecting flaws in metal blocks.
  • SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging) uses ultrasound to measure ocean depth.

🎙️ Ultrasound has many practical and life-saving applications. Doctors use it to check on babies during pregnancy, and engineers use it to find tiny cracks in bridges or airplane parts that are not visible to the eye.
