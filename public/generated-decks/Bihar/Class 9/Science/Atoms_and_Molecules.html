What are Atoms?

  • Smallest particle of an element.
  • Cannot be seen with naked eye.
  • Retains chemical identity.

🎙️ Imagine breaking down a piece of gold into smaller and smaller pieces. Eventually, you'd reach a point where you have the smallest possible piece that is still gold. That's an atom!

Are Atoms Divisible?

  • Dalton believed atoms were indivisible.
  • Modern science shows atoms have subatomic particles.

🎙️ Early scientists like John Dalton thought atoms were the ultimate, unbreakable units. However, we now know that atoms themselves are made of even smaller particles: protons, neutrons, and electrons.

What are Molecules?

  • Two or more atoms chemically bonded.
  • Can be same type of atoms (O₂).
  • Can be different types (H₂O).
  • Exist independently.

🎙️ When two or more atoms decide to join hands and form a stable group, they create a molecule. For example, two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom combine to form a water molecule.

Atoms vs. Molecules

  • Atom: Smallest unit of an element.
  • Molecule: Smallest unit of a compound or element that can exist independently.
  • Atoms are building blocks, molecules are structures.

🎙️ Think of it like this: an atom is like a single LEGO brick. A molecule is like two or more LEGO bricks joined together to make a small structure. Both are important, but serve different roles.

Law of Conservation of Mass

  • Mass is neither created nor destroyed.
  • During a chemical reaction.
  • Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products.

🎙️ This fundamental law, given by Antoine Lavoisier, states that in any chemical reaction, the total mass of the substances you start with will always be equal to the total mass of the substances you end up with. Nothing is lost, nothing is gained.

⚖️
Balance of mass

Example: Conservation of Mass

  • Burning wood: Appears to lose mass.
  • Ash is lighter, but gases are produced.
  • Total mass of wood + oxygen = ash + smoke (gases).

🎙️ When you burn wood, it seems like the mass decreases because the ash is much lighter. But remember, smoke and gases are also produced. If you could collect all the products, including the gases, their total mass would equal the original wood and the oxygen used.

2Burning
3Ash + Smoke

Law of Constant Proportions

  • Also known as Law of Definite Proportions.
  • A chemical compound always contains exactly the same proportion of elements by mass.
  • Regardless of source or method of preparation.

🎙️ This law, given by Joseph Proust, means that pure water, whether it's from a river in Bihar or a lab in America, will always have hydrogen and oxygen combined in the same fixed ratio by mass.

🧪
Fixed ratios

Example: Constant Proportions (Water)

  • Water (H₂O) always has Hydrogen and Oxygen.
  • Mass ratio of H:O is always 1:8.
  • 1 gram of Hydrogen combines with 8 grams of Oxygen.

🎙️ Let's take water as an example. No matter where you get it from, pure water will always consist of hydrogen and oxygen in a fixed mass ratio of 1:8. This means for every 1 gram of hydrogen, there are 8 grams of oxygen.

Dalton's Atomic Theory: Postulates 1-3

  • 1. All matter is made of tiny particles called atoms.
  • 2. Atoms are indivisible particles.
  • 3. Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties.

🎙️ John Dalton proposed his atomic theory in 1808. His first three postulates laid the groundwork, stating that all matter is made of atoms, which are indivisible, and that all atoms of a particular element are exactly alike.

1Matter = Atoms
2Atoms Indivisible
3Same Element = Same Atoms
12 / 25

Dalton's Atomic Theory: Postulates 4-6

  • 4. Atoms of different elements have different masses and properties.
  • 5. Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
  • 6. Atoms are neither created nor destroyed in chemical reactions.

🎙️ The remaining postulates explain how different elements have different atoms, how atoms combine to form compounds in simple ratios, and reinforce the law of conservation of mass by stating atoms are neither created nor destroyed.

1Different Elements = Different Atoms
2Atoms Combine in Ratios
3Atoms Conserved in Reactions

Atomic Mass

  • Mass of a single atom.
  • Too small to measure directly.
  • Expressed in Atomic Mass Units (amu or u).

🎙️ Since atoms are incredibly small, their individual masses are also tiny. We can't weigh them on a regular balance. So, we use a special unit called the atomic mass unit to express their masses.

⚖️
Mass of an atom
15 / 25

How to find Atomic Mass?

  • Relative atomic mass is used.
  • Compared to a standard reference atom.
  • Carbon-12 isotope is the standard (12 u).

🎙️ We determine the mass of an atom by comparing it to a standard. For a long time, hydrogen was used, but now Carbon-12 is the universally accepted standard. One atomic mass unit is defined as exactly one-twelfth the mass of one atom of carbon-12.

Carbon-12 (12u)
Hydrogen (1u)
Oxygen (16u)

What is a Mole?

  • A unit to measure amount of substance.
  • Like a 'dozen' for eggs, but much larger.
  • Contains a specific number of particles.

🎙️ Just as we use 'dozen' to count 12 eggs, chemists use 'mole' to count a huge number of atoms or molecules. It's a convenient way to deal with the extremely large number of particles in even a small amount of substance.

📦
A 'package' of particles

Avogadro's Number

  • The number of particles in one mole.
  • Value: 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
  • Can be atoms, molecules, or ions.

🎙️ This incredibly large number, 6.022 × 10²³, is known as Avogadro's number. It's the number of atoms in 12 grams of Carbon-12, and it represents one mole of any substance.

✨✨✨✨✨✨
6.022 x 10²³ particles

Molar Mass

  • Mass of one mole of a substance.
  • Numerically equal to atomic/molecular mass.
  • Expressed in grams per mole (g/mol).

🎙️ Molar mass is simply the mass of one mole of a substance. If the atomic mass of an element is 'X' atomic mass units, then its molar mass is 'X' grams per mole. It connects the microscopic world of atoms to the macroscopic world we can measure.

⚖️
Mass of one mole

Worked Example: Molar Mass of Water (H₂O)

  • Atomic mass of H = 1 u
  • Atomic mass of O = 16 u
  • Molecular mass of H₂O = (2 × 1 u) + (1 × 16 u) = 18 u
  • Molar mass of H₂O = 18 g/mol

🎙️ Let's calculate the molar mass of water. We know hydrogen has an atomic mass of 1 u and oxygen has 16 u. So, a water molecule (H₂O) has a molecular mass of 18 u. Therefore, one mole of water weighs 18 grams.

1H: 1u, O: 16u
2H₂O = (2x1) + 16
3Molecular Mass = 18u
4Molar Mass = 18 g/mol

Ions

  • Atoms or groups of atoms with an electric charge.
  • Formed by gaining or losing electrons.
  • Cations: Positively charged (lose electrons).
  • Anions: Negatively charged (gain electrons).

🎙️ Sometimes, atoms don't remain neutral. They can either lose electrons to become positively charged ions, called cations, or gain electrons to become negatively charged ions, called anions. These charged particles are crucial for forming many compounds.

⚡
Charged particles

Writing Chemical Formulae: Simple Ions

  • Identify ions and their charges (e.g., Na⁺, Cl⁻).
  • Criss-cross the numerical values of charges.
  • Write symbols with subscript numbers.

🎙️ To write a chemical formula, we first identify the ions and their charges. For example, sodium is Na⁺ and chloride is Cl⁻. We then criss-cross the numerical values of the charges to get the subscripts, resulting in NaCl for sodium chloride.

1Na⁺, Cl⁻
2Charges: 1, 1
3Criss-cross (simplify)
4Formula: NaCl
23 / 25

Writing Chemical Formulae: Polyatomic Ions

  • Polyatomic ions are groups of atoms with a charge (e.g., SO₄²⁻, NH₄⁺).
  • Treat polyatomic ion as a single unit.
  • Use parentheses if more than one polyatomic ion is needed.

🎙️ Polyatomic ions are groups of atoms that carry a charge, like the sulfate ion (SO₄²⁻). When writing formulas with them, treat them as a single unit. If you need more than one, put the polyatomic ion in parentheses before adding the subscript.

1Mg²⁺, SO₄²⁻
2Charges: 2, 2
3Simplify (1,1)
4Formula: MgSO₄
