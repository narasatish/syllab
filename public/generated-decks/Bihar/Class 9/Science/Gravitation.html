What is Gravitation?

  • It's a natural force of attraction.
  • Every object in the universe attracts every other object.
  • This attraction is called the Gravitational Force.

🎙️ Imagine an apple falling from a tree. It always falls towards the Earth. This simple observation led to the discovery of gravitation. It's a fundamental force that acts between any two objects with mass.

Universal Law of Gravitation

  • Proposed by Sir Isaac Newton.
  • States that every object attracts every other object.
  • The force depends on their masses and distance between them.

🎙️ Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation is a cornerstone of physics. It explains not just apples falling, but also the motion of planets around the Sun and the Moon around the Earth. It's truly universal.

Formula for Gravitational Force

  • F = G * (m1 * m2) / r²
  • F is the gravitational force.
  • m1, m2 are masses of the objects, r is distance between them.

🎙️ This formula helps us calculate the exact strength of the gravitational force. Notice that the force is directly proportional to the product of masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance. This means if objects are heavier or closer, the force is stronger.

Gravitational Constant (G)

  • G is the Universal Gravitational Constant.
  • Its value is approximately 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg².
  • It's a constant for all pairs of objects in the universe.

🎙️ The gravitational constant 'G' is a tiny but very important number. It ensures that the formula gives us the correct magnitude of the force. Because G is so small, gravitational force is only noticeable when at least one of the objects has a very large mass, like a planet.

Free Fall

  • When an object falls only under the influence of gravity.
  • No air resistance or other forces acting on it.
  • All objects in free fall accelerate at the same rate.

🎙️ Imagine dropping a stone and a feather in a vacuum. They would both hit the ground at the same time! This is because, in the absence of air resistance, gravity accelerates all objects equally, regardless of their mass. This phenomenon is called free fall.

Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

  • The acceleration produced by Earth's gravity.
  • Denoted by 'g'.
  • Its average value on Earth's surface is 9.8 m/s².

🎙️ When an object is in free fall, its velocity increases every second. This rate of increase in velocity is called acceleration due to gravity. The value of 'g' is slightly different at various places on Earth, but 9.8 m/s² is a good average to remember.

Variation of 'g'

  • Value of 'g' decreases with altitude (height).
  • Value of 'g' also decreases with depth (below surface).
  • It's maximum at the Earth's surface.

🎙️ The Earth is not a perfect sphere, and its mass distribution isn't uniform. This causes 'g' to vary. As you go higher, you're farther from Earth's center, so 'g' decreases. As you go deeper, some mass of the Earth is above you, reducing the net pull, so 'g' decreases there too.

Mass vs. Weight

  • Mass: Amount of matter in an object (constant).
  • Weight: Force with which gravity pulls an object (varies).
  • Weight = Mass × g (W = m × g).

🎙️ This is a common point of confusion. Your mass is the same whether you are on Earth or the Moon. However, your weight changes because the gravitational pull (g) is different. Mass is a scalar quantity, while weight is a vector quantity.

Weight on Earth vs. Moon

  • Acceleration due to gravity on Moon is about 1/6th of Earth's.
  • So, your weight on the Moon would be 1/6th of your weight on Earth.
  • Your mass, however, remains the same.

🎙️ This is why astronauts can jump much higher on the Moon! If you weigh 600 N on Earth, you would weigh approximately 100 N on the Moon. This difference is solely due to the Moon's weaker gravitational pull.

Thrust and Pressure

  • Thrust: The force acting perpendicular to a surface.
  • Pressure: Thrust per unit area (P = Thrust / Area).
  • Smaller area means greater pressure for the same thrust.

🎙️ These concepts are closely related to how forces are distributed. Think about pushing a nail with a hammer: the force is concentrated on the small tip, creating high pressure. This is why sharp objects can cut or pierce easily.

Buoyancy

  • The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.
  • This force makes objects feel lighter in water.
  • It acts opposite to the direction of gravity.

🎙️ Have you ever noticed how easy it is to lift a heavy bucket of water when it's submerged, but it becomes heavy once you pull it out? That's due to buoyancy. The water pushes the bucket upwards, making it seem lighter.

Archimedes' Principle

  • When an object is immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force.
  • This force is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.
  • This principle explains why objects float or sink.

🎙️ Archimedes' Principle is a very important concept. It tells us exactly how much buoyant force an object will experience. If the buoyant force is greater than the object's weight, it floats; if less, it sinks.

Applications of Archimedes' Principle

Worked Example 1: Calculating 'g'

  • Mass of Earth (M) = 6 × 10²⁴ kg
  • Radius of Earth (R) = 6.4 × 10⁶ m
  • g = GM/R² ≈ 9.8 m/s²

🎙️ Let's put the formula to use! By substituting the values for the Universal Gravitational Constant (G), the mass of the Earth, and the radius of the Earth, we can actually derive the value of 'g' that we use in our calculations. This shows the connection between G and g.

Worked Example 2: Calculating Weight

  • A person has a mass of 50 kg.
  • What is their weight on Earth? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
  • Weight = mass × g = 50 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 490 N.

🎙️ Here's a simple calculation for weight. Remember, weight is a force and is measured in Newtons (N). This example clearly shows how your mass and the acceleration due to gravity combine to determine your weight.
