What is a Circle?

  • A collection of all points in a plane.
  • Equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a dot on a paper. Now, if you mark all the points that are exactly 5 cm away from that dot, what shape would you get? Yes, a circle! The dot is its center.

Key Terms: Center and Radius

  • Center: The fixed point inside the circle.
  • Radius: The distance from the center to any point on the circle.
  • All radii of a circle are equal in length.

🎙️ The center is like the heart of the circle. The radius is a line segment connecting the center to the boundary of the circle. Think of it as the 'reach' from the center.

🎯
Center and a line to the edge

Key Term: Diameter

  • A line segment passing through the center.
  • Connects two points on the circle.
  • It is twice the length of the radius (D = 2r).

🎙️ The diameter is the widest part of a circle. If you extend a radius straight through the center to the other side of the circle, you get a diameter. It's always twice the radius.

📏
Longest chord through center

  • Bangles and rings are circular.
  • Coins and clocks often have circular faces.

🎙️ Look around you! You'll find circles everywhere. The wheels of a bicycle, a car, or even a Ferris wheel are perfect examples of circles in motion. They help us move and function.

Key Term: Chord

  • A line segment joining any two points on a circle.
  • Does not necessarily pass through the center.
  • The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

🎙️ Imagine cutting a straight line across a pizza slice, without necessarily going through the middle. That line segment inside the circle is a chord. It connects two points on the boundary.

🧵
A line segment inside

Key Term: Arc

  • A continuous piece of a circle.
  • Formed by two points on the circle.
  • Can be a 'minor arc' (smaller) or 'major arc' (larger).

🎙️ An arc is just a part of the circle's boundary. If you take two points on a circle, the path along the circle between them is an arc. Like a rainbow, it's a curved segment.

🌈
Part of the circle's boundary

Key Term: Segment

  • The region bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • A chord divides a circle into two segments.
  • Can be 'minor segment' or 'major segment'.

🎙️ If you cut a pizza with a straight line (a chord), the pieces you get are segments. The area enclosed by the chord and the arc it cuts off is called a segment. It's a region, not just a line.

🍕
Area cut by a chord

Key Term: Sector

  • The region bounded by two radii and an arc.
  • Like a slice of pizza or cake.
  • Can be 'minor sector' or 'major sector'.

🎙️ A sector is what you get when you cut a slice of cake or pizza from the center. It's the area enclosed by two radii and the arc between their endpoints. It's a very common shape!

Tangent to a Circle

  • A line that intersects the circle at exactly ONE point.
  • This point is called the 'point of contact'.
  • It 'touches' the circle without crossing inside.

🎙️ Imagine a line just grazing the edge of a circle. That's a tangent! It touches the circle at only one point, like a car's tire touching the road at a single instant.

👆
Touching at one point

Properties of a Tangent

  • The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius.
  • The radius passes through the point of contact.
  • This forms a 90-degree angle.

🎙️ This is a very important property! If you draw a radius to the point where the tangent touches the circle, they will always meet at a perfect right angle. This is useful for many problems.

📐
Right angle at contact

Real-life Example: Tangent

  • A wheel rolling on a flat road.
  • The road is tangent to the wheel at the point of contact.
  • The path of a ball thrown in a curved trajectory.

🎙️ Think about a bicycle wheel rolling on the ground. At any moment, the ground touches the wheel at just one point. That's a real-life tangent! The road is tangent to the wheel.

Tangents from a Point: Case 1

  • If the point is INSIDE the circle.
  • NO tangent can be drawn from this point.
  • Any line through it will be a secant.

🎙️ If you have a point inside the circle, like the center itself, can you draw a line that just touches the circle once? No! Any line you draw from an inside point will always cross the circle at two points.

🚫
No tangent possible
15 / 25

Tangents from a Point: Case 2

  • If the point is ON the circle.
  • Exactly ONE tangent can be drawn from this point.
  • This tangent is perpendicular to the radius at that point.

🎙️ If the point is directly on the boundary of the circle, you can draw exactly one line that touches the circle at that point and no other. This is the definition of a tangent itself.

☝️
One tangent possible
16 / 25

Tangents from a Point: Case 3

  • If the point is OUTSIDE the circle.
  • Exactly TWO tangents can be drawn from this point.
  • These tangents will have equal lengths.

🎙️ Now, if you have a point outside the circle, you can draw two distinct lines that touch the circle at exactly one point each. Think of it like a 'V' shape pointing towards the circle.

✌️
Two tangents possible

Worked Example: Tangent Length

  • Radius = 5 cm, distance from center to external point = 13 cm.
  • Find the length of the tangent segment.
  • Use Pythagoras Theorem (r² + t² = d²).

🎙️ Remember the property that the radius is perpendicular to the tangent? This forms a right-angled triangle! We can use Pythagoras theorem: radius squared plus tangent length squared equals distance from center to external point squared. So, 13² - 5² = 169 - 25 = 144, square root of 144 is 12 cm.

Length of Tangents from External Point

  • Theorem: The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
  • If P is an external point, and PA, PB are tangents.
  • Then PA = PB.

🎙️ This is a very useful theorem! If you draw two tangents from the same point outside a circle, the segments from that external point to their points of contact on the circle will always be exactly the same length.

↔️
Tangents are equal

🎙️ Let's review what we learned about tangents. Zero tangents from an inside point. A 90-degree angle between radius and tangent. Yes, the lengths of tangents from an external point are equal.

Secant to a Circle

  • A line that intersects the circle at TWO distinct points.
  • It 'cuts through' the circle.
  • Contains a chord of the circle.

🎙️ Unlike a tangent that just touches, a secant cuts right through the circle. It enters the circle at one point and exits at another, creating a chord inside the circle.

✂️
Cutting through the circle

Tangent vs. Secant

  • Tangent: Intersects at one point.
  • Secant: Intersects at two points.
  • Tangent: Only touches the boundary.
  • Secant: Passes through the interior.

🎙️ The key difference is the number of intersection points. A tangent has one point of contact, while a secant has two. Think of a tangent as a 'touching' line and a secant as a 'cutting' line.

Quick Check 3: Tangent or Secant?

  • A line that intersects a circle at two points is a ______.
  • A line that intersects a circle at one point is a ______.
  • Can a tangent pass through the center of a circle?

🎙️ Let's quickly test your understanding. A line intersecting at two points is a secant. A line intersecting at one point is a tangent. No, a tangent cannot pass through the center, as it would then intersect at two points.

Recap: Key Concepts of Circles

  • Circle: Set of points equidistant from a center.
  • Radius, Diameter, Chord, Arc, Segment, Sector.
  • Tangent: Touches at one point, perpendicular to radius.
  • Secant: Cuts through at two points.
