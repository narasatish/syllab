Euclid's Division Lemma: The Remainder Rule

  • For any positive integers 'a' and 'b', there exist unique integers 'q' and 'r'.
  • Such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.
  • 'a' is dividend, 'b' is divisor, 'q' is quotient, 'r' is remainder.

🎙️ Euclid's Division Lemma is a fundamental rule about division. It simply states that when you divide one positive integer by another, you'll always get a unique quotient and a unique remainder that's smaller than the divisor. It's the basis for finding HCF.

Finding HCF using EDL: Example 1 (Step 1)

  • Find the HCF of 135 and 225.
  • Apply EDL: a = bq + r.
  • Since 225 > 135, let a = 225, b = 135.

🎙️ Let's find the HCF of two numbers using EDL. First, identify the larger number as 'a' and the smaller as 'b'. Then, apply the division lemma to them, finding the quotient and remainder.

1Identify a & b
2Apply a=bq+r
14 / 25

Finding HCF using EDL: Example 1 (Step 2)

  • 225 = 135 × 1 + 90.
  • Since remainder 90 ≠ 0, make 135 the new 'a' and 90 the new 'b'.
  • 135 = 90 × 1 + 45. Remainder 45 ≠ 0, make 90 the new 'a' and 45 the new 'b'.
  • 90 = 45 × 2 + 0. Remainder is 0, so the divisor (45) is the HCF.

🎙️ We continue the process. If the remainder isn't zero, the old divisor becomes the new dividend, and the remainder becomes the new divisor. We repeat this until the remainder is zero. The divisor at that step is our HCF.

1If r ≠ 0, new a=b, new b=r
2Repeat until r = 0
3HCF is divisor when r=0

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: Prime Factorization

  • Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes.
  • This factorization is unique, apart from the order of its prime factors.

🎙️ The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic is a powerful concept! It says that any number bigger than 1 that isn't prime can be broken down into a unique set of prime numbers multiplied together. Think of it like a number's unique DNA!

18 / 25

HCF and LCM using FTA: Example 1 (Step 2)

  • HCF: Product of smallest power of common prime factors. Common prime factor is 2, smallest power is 2¹. HCF(6, 20) = 2.
  • LCM: Product of greatest power of all prime factors. All factors are 2, 3, 5. Greatest powers are 2², 3¹, 5¹.
  • LCM(6, 20) = 2² × 3 × 5 = 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

🎙️ For HCF, we look for common prime factors and take the smallest power. For LCM, we take all prime factors involved, using their highest powers. This method guarantees we find the correct HCF and LCM efficiently.

1Identify Common Primes (HCF)
2Identify All Primes (LCM)
3Calculate HCF & LCM

Rational Numbers: Non-terminating Repeating Decimals

  • A rational number p/q (in simplest form) has a non-terminating repeating decimal expansion if the prime factorization of q is not of the form 2ⁿ5ᵐ.

🎙️ On the other hand, if the denominator of a simplified fraction has any other prime factors besides 2 or 5, its decimal representation will go on forever, but it will always repeat a pattern. This is a non-terminating repeating decimal.

Proving Irrationality: By Contradiction

  • Method often used to prove numbers like √2 are irrational.
  • Assume the number is rational (p/q form).
  • Show this assumption leads to a contradiction.
  • Conclude the initial assumption was false, hence the number is irrational.

🎙️ Proving a number is irrational can be tricky. We often use a clever method called 'proof by contradiction'. We assume the opposite (that it's rational) and then show that this assumption leads to a logical impossibility, proving our original statement true.
