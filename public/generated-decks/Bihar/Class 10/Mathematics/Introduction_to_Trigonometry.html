What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of a triangle.
  • Especially for right-angled triangles.
  • 'Trigon' means triangle, 'metron' means measure.

🎙️ Trigonometry, simply put, is the study of how the sides and angles of a triangle are connected. It's like a special tool we use to understand triangles better, especially right-angled ones. The word itself comes from Greek: 'trigon' for triangle and 'metron' for measure.

Right-Angled Triangle: A Quick Look

  • Has one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The longest side is called the Hypotenuse.
  • The other two sides are called Legs.

🎙️ Before we dive deeper, let's quickly recall what a right-angled triangle is. It's a triangle with one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees. Remember the side opposite to the right angle? That's the longest side, called the Hypotenuse. The other two sides are often called legs.

Sides Relative to an Angle (θ)

  • Hypotenuse: Always opposite the 90° angle.
  • Opposite Side: Opposite to the angle θ.
  • Adjacent Side: Next to the angle θ (not the hypotenuse).

🎙️ Now, this is important! For any other acute angle (let's call it theta, θ) in the right triangle, the names of the other two sides change. The 'Opposite' side is directly across from angle θ. The 'Adjacent' side is next to angle θ, but it's not the hypotenuse. The Hypotenuse always remains the same.

What are Trigonometric Ratios?

  • Ratios of the sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • These ratios depend on the acute angle (θ).
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are simply fractions that relate the lengths of the sides of a right-angled triangle to its acute angles. For a specific angle, these ratios always stay the same, no matter the size of the triangle. We'll learn about six such important ratios.

📏
Side Ratios

Sine (sin θ): The Opposite-Hypotenuse Ratio

  • Defined as the ratio of the Opposite side to the Hypotenuse.
  • Formula: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ Our first ratio is Sine, often written as 'sin θ'. It's defined as the length of the side opposite to the angle θ, divided by the length of the hypotenuse. Think of 'S' for Sine, 'O' for Opposite, 'H' for Hypotenuse – SOH!

🌊

Cosine (cos θ): The Adjacent-Hypotenuse Ratio

  • Defined as the ratio of the Adjacent side to the Hypotenuse.
  • Formula: cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ Next up is Cosine, written as 'cos θ'. This ratio is the length of the side adjacent to angle θ, divided by the length of the hypotenuse. Remember 'C' for Cosine, 'A' for Adjacent, 'H' for Hypotenuse – CAH!

🌙
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
9 / 25

Tangent (tan θ): The Opposite-Adjacent Ratio

  • Defined as the ratio of the Opposite side to the Adjacent side.
  • Formula: tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • Also, tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.

🎙️ Our third main ratio is Tangent, or 'tan θ'. It's the ratio of the side opposite to angle θ, divided by the side adjacent to angle θ. Think 'T' for Tangent, 'O' for Opposite, 'A' for Adjacent – TOA! An interesting fact is that tan θ can also be found by dividing sin θ by cos θ.

Remembering the Ratios: SOH CAH TOA

  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ Here's a super helpful trick to remember these three ratios: SOH CAH TOA! Say it out loud a few times. 'SOH' helps you recall Sine, Opposite, Hypotenuse. 'CAH' reminds you of Cosine, Adjacent, Hypotenuse. And 'TOA' stands for Tangent, Opposite, Adjacent. Practice this, and you'll never forget!

Worked Example: Finding Basic Ratios

  • Given a right triangle with sides: Opposite = 3, Adjacent = 4, Hypotenuse = 5.
  • Find sin A, cos A, tan A.

🎙️ Let's try an example. Imagine a right-angled triangle where for angle A, the opposite side is 3 units, the adjacent side is 4 units, and the hypotenuse is 5 units. Can you calculate sin A, cos A, and tan A using our SOH CAH TOA mnemonic? (Pause for students to think) sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4.

The Reciprocal Trigonometric Ratios

  • Cosecant (cosec θ): Reciprocal of sin θ (H/O).
  • Secant (sec θ): Reciprocal of cos θ (H/A).
  • Cotangent (cot θ): Reciprocal of tan θ (A/O).

🎙️ Besides the main three, there are three more ratios, which are simply the reciprocals of sin, cos, and tan. Cosecant, or cosec θ, is 1/sin θ. Secant, or sec θ, is 1/cos θ. And Cotangent, or cot θ, is 1/tan θ. These are just the original ratios flipped upside down!

Worked Example: Using Reciprocal Ratios

  • If sin A = 3/5, find cosec A.
  • If cos B = 4/5, find sec B.

🎙️ Let's apply our knowledge of reciprocal ratios. If we know that sin A is 3/5, what will be cosec A? That's right, it's 5/3! Similarly, if cos B is 4/5, then sec B will be 5/4. It's quite straightforward once you know the relationship.

Worked Example: Using Specific Angle Values

  • Find the value of sin 30° + cos 60°.
  • Find the value of tan 45° × sin 90°.

🎙️ Let's use our table of specific angle values. What is sin 30°? It's 1/2. What is cos 60°? Also 1/2. So, sin 30° + cos 60° = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. For the second one, tan 45° is 1, and sin 90° is 1. So, 1 × 1 = 1. Simple, right?

Ratios of Complementary Angles

  • Two angles are complementary if their sum is 90°.
  • sin (90° - A) = cos A; cos (90° - A) = sin A.
  • tan (90° - A) = cot A.

🎙️ Remember complementary angles? They are two angles that add up to 90 degrees. In trigonometry, there's a special relationship between the ratios of complementary angles. For example, the sine of an angle is equal to the cosine of its complementary angle. This helps simplify many expressions.

Worked Example: Complementary Angle Ratios

  • Evaluate: sin 60° / cos 30°.
  • Simplify: tan 20° cot 70°.

🎙️ Let's try an example. To evaluate sin 60° / cos 30°, we know that cos 30° is the same as sin (90° - 30°), which is sin 60°. So, sin 60° / cos 30° becomes sin 60° / sin 60°, which simplifies to 1. For the second one, cot 70° is the same as tan (90° - 70°), which is tan 20°. So, tan 20° cot 70° becomes tan 20° × (1/tan 20°), which is 1.

Real-Life Problem: Finding Height

  • You stand 50m from a tower.
  • The angle of elevation to the top is 30°.
  • How tall is the tower? (Use tan 30° = 1/√3).

🎙️ Here's a practical problem: Imagine you're standing 50 meters away from a tall tower. You look up at the top, and the angle from your eye level to the top (called the angle of elevation) is 30 degrees. How can you find the height of the tower? We can use the tangent ratio here: tan 30° = Height / Distance. So, Height = 50 * tan 30° = 50 / √3 meters.

What are Trigonometric Identities?

  • Equations that are true for all values of the angle θ.
  • Similar to algebraic identities (e.g., (a+b)²).
  • Very useful for simplifying expressions and proving equations.

🎙️ Just like in algebra where we have identities that are true for all values of variables, in trigonometry, we have 'Trigonometric Identities'. These are equations involving trigonometric ratios that hold true for every possible value of the angle. They are super helpful for simplifying complex trigonometric expressions.

The First Fundamental Identity

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
  • This is derived directly from the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Very frequently used.

🎙️ Our first and arguably most important identity is sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. This identity comes directly from the Pythagorean theorem applied to a right-angled triangle. It means that if you square the sine of an angle and add it to the square of its cosine, you will always get 1. Remember this one well!

🥇

The Second Fundamental Identity

  • 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ.
  • This identity is derived from the first identity by dividing by cos²θ.

🎙️ The second key identity is 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ. This identity can actually be derived from our first identity (sin²θ + cos²θ = 1) by dividing all terms by cos²θ. It shows a powerful relationship between the tangent and secant ratios. Keep an eye out for it in problems!

The Third Fundamental Identity

  • 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.
  • This identity is derived from the first identity by dividing by sin²θ.

🎙️ And finally, our third fundamental identity is 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ. Similar to the previous one, this identity can be derived from sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by dividing all terms by sin²θ. These three identities are the building blocks for solving many advanced trigonometry problems.
