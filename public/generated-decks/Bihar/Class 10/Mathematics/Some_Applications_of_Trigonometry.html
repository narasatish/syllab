Why Apply Trigonometry?

  • Measure heights and distances indirectly.
  • Used in engineering, navigation, astronomy.
  • Solves problems we can't measure directly.

Angle of Elevation

  • Angle formed when you look UP at an object.
  • Between the horizontal line and the line of sight.
  • Always measured from the observer's eye level.

🎙️ When you look up at something, like a kite in the sky or the top of a tree, the angle your eye makes with the ground is called the Angle of Elevation. It's like your head is 'elevating' to see.

Angle of Depression

  • Angle formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • Between the horizontal line and the line of sight.
  • Always measured from the observer's eye level.

🎙️ Conversely, when you look down from a height, like from a balcony at a car on the street, the angle formed is the Angle of Depression. Your gaze is 'depressing' or going down.

Important Terms

  • Line of Sight: Line from observer's eye to object.
  • Horizontal Level: Line parallel to the ground from observer's eye.
  • These form the sides of our right triangle.

🎙️ It's crucial to understand these two lines. The line of sight connects you to what you're seeing, and the horizontal level is your eye-level line, which is parallel to the ground.

How to Solve Problems

  • 1. Draw a clear diagram.
  • 2. Label knowns and unknowns.
  • 3. Identify the right-angled triangle.

🎙️ Solving these problems is like following a recipe. First, visualize and draw, then label carefully. Once you have your right triangle, picking the right ratio will lead you to the answer.

Worked Example 1: Elevation

  • A tower is 30m high.
  • Angle of elevation of its top from a point on the ground 30m away from its foot.
  • Find the angle of elevation.

🎙️ Let's try our first example. Imagine a tower. You are standing some distance away and looking at its very top. We need to find that angle of elevation.

🏗️
Tower Problem
11 / 25

Solution 1 (Part 1)

  • Let AB be the tower, C be the point on the ground.
  • Height AB = 30m, Distance BC = 30m.
  • We need to find angle ACB.

🎙️ First, we draw a right-angled triangle ABC, with the right angle at B. AB is the height of the tower, and BC is the distance from the foot of the tower to the observer.

📝
Setting up
12 / 25

Solution 1 (Part 2)

  • In ΔABC, tan C = AB/BC.
  • tan C = 30/30 = 1.
  • Since tan 45° = 1, Angle C = 45°.
  • The angle of elevation is 45°.

🎙️ Here, we know the opposite side (height) and the adjacent side (distance). So, the tangent ratio is perfect. Calculating it, we find the angle to be 45 degrees.

✅
Solved!

  • Find the distance of the ship from the lighthouse.

🎙️ Now let's tackle an angle of depression problem. We're at the top of a lighthouse, looking down at a ship. We know the height and the angle, and we need to find the distance.

🚢
Ship Problem
14 / 25

Solution 2 (Part 1)

  • Let AB be the lighthouse, C be the ship.
  • Height AB = 75m.
  • Angle of depression from A to C is 30°.
  • Horizontal line AX parallel to BC. Angle XAC = Angle ACB = 30° (Alternate interior angles).

🎙️ Draw your lighthouse and the ship. Remember, the angle of depression is formed with the horizontal line from the observer's eye. Using alternate interior angles, we can place this angle inside our right triangle.

📐
Diagram Setup
15 / 25

Solution 2 (Part 2)

  • In right ΔABC, tan(ACB) = AB/BC.
  • tan 30° = 75/BC.
  • 1/√3 = 75/BC.
  • BC = 75√3 meters.
  • The ship is 75√3 meters from the lighthouse.

🎙️ Again, we have the opposite side (height) and need the adjacent side (distance), so tangent is our friend. Substitute the value of tan 30° and solve for BC to get the distance.

💡
Distance Found

Worked Example 3: Both Angles

  • From the top of a 10m building, angle of elevation of top of cable tower is 60°.
  • Angle of depression of its foot is 45°.
  • Find the height of the tower.

🎙️ This problem involves looking up at a tower and down at its base from a building. We'll need to use both elevation and depression to find the total height of the cable tower.

🏢
Building & Tower
18 / 25

Solution 3 (Part 1)

  • Let AB be building (10m), CD be tower.
  • Draw AX parallel to BD (ground).
  • Angle XAC = 60°, Angle XAD = 45°.
  • In ΔABD, ∠ADB = 45° (alt. int. angles).
  • tan 45° = AB/BD => 1 = 10/BD => BD = 10m.

🎙️ Draw your building and the tower. From the top of the building, draw a horizontal line. This creates two triangles. First, use the angle of depression to find the distance between the building and the tower.

🪜
First steps
19 / 25

Solution 3 (Part 2)

  • Since AX || BD, AX = BD = 10m.
  • In ΔCEX, tan 60° = CE/AX.
  • √3 = CE/10 => CE = 10√3m.
  • Total height of tower CD = CE + ED = CE + AB.
  • CD = 10√3 + 10 = 10(√3 + 1)m.

🎙️ Now, use the angle of elevation in the upper triangle. You found the base distance in the previous step. Calculate the part of the tower above the building, then add the building's height to get the total.
