Revisiting the Circle

  • A set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point.
  • That fixed point is called the 'center' of the circle.
  • The constant distance is known as the 'radius' (r).

🎙️ Before we dive into areas, let's quickly recap what a circle is. Remember, it's defined by its center and its radius. Think of drawing a circle with a compass – the point where the compass stays is the center, and the distance to the pencil is the radius.

Important Terms of a Circle

  • Radius (r): Distance from the center to any point on the boundary.
  • Diameter (d): A line segment passing through the center, connecting two points on the boundary (d = 2r).
  • Circumference (C): The total distance around the circle.

🎙️ These three terms are crucial for understanding circles. The radius is fundamental, the diameter is simply twice the radius, and the circumference is like the perimeter of a circle – how long its boundary is.

Calculating Circumference

  • The formula for the circumference (C) of a circle is C = 2πr.
  • Alternatively, since d = 2r, C = πd.
  • π (Pi) is a mathematical constant, approximately 22/7 or 3.14.

🎙️ Now, let's learn how to calculate the circumference. The constant 'Pi' is very important here. Remember, you can use either the radius or the diameter in your calculations, depending on what information is given.

Example: Finding Circumference

  • Question: Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 7 cm.
  • Solution: Using C = 2πr, substitute r = 7 cm and π = 22/7.
  • C = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm.

Understanding Area

  • Area is the amount of surface covered by a flat shape.
  • It is measured in square units, such as cm² or m².
  • Area tells us 'how much space' is inside the boundary of a shape.

🎙️ While circumference is the 'distance around', area is the 'space inside'. Imagine painting a circular wall; the amount of paint needed depends on the area. Always remember to use square units for area.

Area of a Circle: The Formula

  • The formula for the Area (A) of a circle is A = πr².
  • Here, 'r' stands for the radius of the circle.
  • Again, π (Pi) is approximately 22/7 or 3.14.

🎙️ This is a very important formula to remember: Area equals Pi times radius squared. Make sure you square the radius first before multiplying by Pi. This formula helps us find the space occupied by any circular object.

Introducing a Sector

  • A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • It looks like a slice of pizza or a piece of a pie.
  • The size of a sector is defined by its 'central angle' (θ).

🎙️ Now we move to parts of a circle. A sector is essentially a 'slice' of the circle. Imagine cutting a cake from the center outwards – that piece is a sector. The angle at the center determines how big the slice is.

🍕

Area of a Sector Formula

  • The area of a sector is a fraction of the total area of the circle.
  • Area of Sector = (θ/360°) × πr².
  • Here, θ (theta) is the central angle of the sector in degrees.

🎙️ Since a sector is just a part of the whole circle, its area is a fraction of the total circle's area. This fraction is determined by the central angle, θ, divided by 360 degrees. This formula is very logical and easy to apply.

🎙️ A clock face is an excellent visual for sectors. When the minute hand moves from 12 to 3, it covers exactly a quarter of the circle, which corresponds to a 90-degree angle. This clearly shows how a central angle defines a sector.

Understanding a Segment

  • A segment is the region bounded by a chord and an arc of a circle.
  • It's like cutting off a piece of a circular disc with a straight line.
  • There are two types: minor segment (smaller) and major segment (larger).

🎙️ A segment is different from a sector. While a sector uses two radii, a segment uses a chord – a straight line connecting two points on the circle. Imagine a straight cut across a circular pizza; the part cut off is a segment.

Area of a Segment Formula

  • Area of Minor Segment = Area of the Sector - Area of the Triangle.
  • The triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord.
  • Calculating the triangle's area may require trigonometry (e.g., ½ r² sinθ).

🎙️ To find the area of a segment, we subtract the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord from the area of the corresponding sector. This usually involves using trigonometric formulas like ½ab sinC for the triangle's area.

Pizza Slice vs. Segment

  • A whole pizza slice is a sector (arc + two radii).
  • If you imagine eating only the crust and topping, leaving the triangular base, the remaining part is a segment.
  • It helps differentiate between a 'slice' (sector) and a 'cut-off piece' (segment).

🎙️ Let's use our pizza analogy again. The entire slice, from the center to the crust, is a sector. If you were to cut off the triangular part of the slice with a straight line, the remaining curved part with a straight base would be a segment. This helps clarify the difference.

Area of a Circular Ring (Annulus)

  • A circular ring, or annulus, is the region between two concentric circles.
  • It has an outer radius (R) and an inner radius (r).
  • Area of Ring = Area of Outer Circle - Area of Inner Circle = πR² - πr² = π(R² - r²).

🎙️ Sometimes, we need to find the area of a region that's like a hollow circle, such as a washer. This is called a circular ring or annulus. The formula is simply the area of the larger circle minus the area of the smaller circle.

Example: Ring Area

  • Solution: Area = π(R² - r²) = π(10² - 5²).
  • Area = π(100 - 25) = 75π cm².
