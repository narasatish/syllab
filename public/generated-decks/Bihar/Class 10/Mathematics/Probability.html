What is Probability?

  • It's the measure of how likely an event is to occur.
  • Expressed as a number between 0 and 1.
  • Helps us quantify uncertainty.

🎙️ Simply put, probability tells us how often we expect something to happen out of all possible outcomes. It gives us a numerical value for likelihood, helping us make sense of uncertain situations.

Key Terms: Experiment & Outcome

  • An Experiment is an activity with an observable result.
  • Examples: Tossing a coin, rolling a die.
  • An Outcome is a possible result of an experiment.

🎙️ Before we calculate probabilities, we need to understand some basic terms. An experiment is any action where the result isn't predetermined. Each possible result of that experiment is called an outcome.

Key Terms: Sample Space

  • The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.
  • Represented by 'S'.
  • Example: Tossing a coin, S = {Head, Tail}.

🎙️ The sample space is like a complete list of everything that *could* happen. When you toss a coin, the only things that can happen are getting a Head or a Tail. So, {Head, Tail} is the sample space.

Key Terms: Event

  • One or more outcomes of an experiment.
  • A subset of the sample space.
  • Example: Getting a 'Head' when tossing a coin.

🎙️ An event is what we are interested in. It could be a single outcome, like rolling a '6' on a die, or a group of outcomes, like rolling an 'even number' (2, 4, or 6).

The Probability Formula

  • P(E) = (Number of Favorable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Outcomes)
  • P(E) is the probability of an Event E.
  • Favorable outcomes are outcomes that satisfy the event.

🎙️ This is the core formula! To find the probability of an event, you count how many ways your desired outcome can happen, and then divide that by the total number of all possible outcomes.

The Probability Scale

  • Probability values always range from 0 to 1.
  • 0 means an event is impossible.
  • 1 means an event is certain.

🎙️ Think of probability as a scale. If something absolutely cannot happen, its probability is 0. If it's guaranteed to happen, its probability is 1. All other events fall somewhere in between.

Complementary Events

  • Events E and 'not E' are complementary.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • Example: P(Rain) + P(No Rain) = 1.

🎙️ Complementary events are two events that are the only two possible outcomes and are mutually exclusive. If one happens, the other cannot. The sum of their probabilities is always 1.

↔️
Opposite Events
18 / 25

Worked Example: P(not Head)

  • We know P(Head) = 1/2.
  • P(not Head) = 1 - P(Head).
  • P(not Head) = 1 - 1/2 = 1/2.

🎙️ Using the coin toss example, if the probability of getting a Head is 1/2, then the probability of *not* getting a Head (which means getting a Tail) must also be 1 - 1/2, which is 1/2.
