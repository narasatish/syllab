What is Heredity?

  • Transfer of traits from parents to offspring
  • Like eye colour, hair type, height
  • Also known as inheritance

🎙️ Heredity is simply the process where you get characteristics from your parents. Think about how you might have your mother's eyes or your father's smile. This transfer happens through genetic material.

Understanding Variations

  • Differences among individuals of a species
  • No two individuals are exactly alike
  • Can be subtle or significant

🎙️ Even though you inherit traits, you are not an exact copy of your parents. These differences, big or small, are called variations. They make each one of us unique, even siblings often have variations.

Causes of Variations

  • Errors during DNA copying
  • Sexual reproduction mixes genes
  • Environmental factors can also play a role

🎙️ Variations arise mainly due to two reasons. First, DNA copying is not always perfect, leading to small changes. Second, in sexual reproduction, genes from two parents combine in new ways, increasing diversity.

Accumulation of Variations

  • Variations build up over generations
  • Important for survival of species
  • Drives evolution and adaptation

🎙️ Small variations, generation after generation, can add up. This accumulation is crucial because it helps a species adapt to changing environments. It's the engine that drives the process of evolution.

Gregor Mendel: Father of Genetics

  • Austrian monk, experimented with pea plants
  • Discovered basic laws of heredity
  • His work was foundational for genetics

🎙️ Our understanding of heredity largely began with Gregor Mendel. He carefully observed how traits were passed in pea plants and, through his meticulous experiments, uncovered the fundamental principles of inheritance.

Key Terms in Genetics

  • Gene: Unit of heredity
  • Allele: Different forms of a gene
  • Dominant: Trait expressed even with one copy
  • Recessive: Trait expressed only with two copies

🎙️ Before we go further, let's understand some important terms. A gene is like a tiny instruction for a trait. Alleles are different versions of that instruction, and they can be dominant or recessive in how they show up.

Genotype vs. Phenotype

  • Genotype: Genetic makeup of an organism
  • Phenotype: Observable traits of an organism
  • Genotype determines phenotype

🎙️ Genotype is the internal genetic code, like the instruction manual inside. Phenotype is what you actually see, like the finished product. For example, a plant's genotype might carry genes for tallness, and its phenotype would be its actual tall height.

Monohybrid Cross: One Trait

  • Study of inheritance of a single trait
  • Mendel's first experiments
  • Example: Plant height (Tall vs. Dwarf)

🎙️ A monohybrid cross is a simple experiment where we focus on how just one specific characteristic is inherited. Mendel used this method to understand how traits like plant height or flower color were passed down.

Example: Pea Plant Height

  • Pure Tall (TT) crossed with Pure Dwarf (tt)
  • F1 generation: All Tall (Tt)
  • F2 generation: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf ratio

🎙️ Mendel crossed pure tall pea plants with pure dwarf ones. In the first generation (F1), all offspring were tall. But when F1 plants were self-pollinated, the second generation (F2) showed both tall and dwarf plants in a predictable ratio.

Mendel's Law of Dominance

  • In a pair of contrasting traits, one is dominant
  • The dominant trait masks the recessive one
  • Example: Tallness is dominant over dwarfness in peas

🎙️ This law states that when two different alleles are present, only one, the dominant allele, will be expressed. The recessive allele is present but hidden. This explains why all F1 pea plants were tall, as tallness is dominant.

Mendel's Law of Segregation

  • Alleles separate during gamete formation
  • Each gamete receives only one allele
  • Ensures genetic variation in offspring

🎙️ The Law of Segregation explains that during the formation of reproductive cells (gametes), the two alleles for a trait separate from each other. So, each gamete carries only one allele, ensuring that offspring get a mix of genetic information.

Dihybrid Cross: Two Traits

  • Study of inheritance of two traits simultaneously
  • Example: Seed shape (Round/Wrinkled) and color (Yellow/Green)
  • More complex than monohybrid crosses

🎙️ A dihybrid cross involves studying how two different traits are inherited at the same time. This helps us understand if the inheritance of one trait affects the inheritance of another, or if they are independent.

Example: Pea Seed Traits

  • Pure Round Yellow (RRYY) x Pure Wrinkled Green (rryy)
  • F1 generation: All Round Yellow (RrYy)
  • F2 generation: 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio

🎙️ Mendel crossed plants with round yellow seeds with plants having wrinkled green seeds. The F1 generation all had round yellow seeds. When F1 plants were self-pollinated, the F2 generation showed a specific ratio of four different phenotypes.

Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment

  • Alleles for different traits assort independently
  • Inheritance of one trait doesn't affect another
  • Applies to genes on different chromosomes

🎙️ This law states that the alleles for different traits are sorted into gametes independently of one another. For example, the gene for seed color doesn't influence the gene for seed shape when they are passed on to offspring.

How Traits are Expressed

  • Genes contain instructions for making proteins
  • Proteins carry out functions in the body
  • These functions lead to observable traits

🎙️ Genes don't directly create a trait; they provide the code. This code is used to make proteins, which are the workhorses of our cells. These proteins then perform specific jobs that result in the characteristics we observe.

Human Sex Determination

  • Females have two X chromosomes (XX)
  • Males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY)
  • Sperm determines the sex of the child

🎙️ Human females have two X chromosomes, while males have one X and one Y. A mother always passes an X chromosome. The father determines the sex because he can pass either an X (for a girl) or a Y (for a boy) chromosome.

Acquired vs. Inherited Traits

  • Inherited: Passed from parents (e.g., eye color)
  • Acquired: Developed during lifetime (e.g., learning to read)
  • Only inherited traits are passed to offspring

🎙️ It's important to distinguish between traits we are born with and those we develop. Inherited traits are in our genes. Acquired traits, like a scar or a skill, are not encoded in our DNA and cannot be passed on.

Heredity and Evolution

  • Variations are the raw material for evolution
  • Natural selection acts on these variations
  • Leads to adaptation and new species over time

🎙️ The variations we discussed are not just random differences; they are critical for evolution. Natural selection favors individuals with beneficial variations, allowing them to survive and reproduce better, slowly changing a species over generations.
