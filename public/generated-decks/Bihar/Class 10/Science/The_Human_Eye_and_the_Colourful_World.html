The Cornea and Iris: Gatekeepers of Light

  • Cornea: Transparent outer layer, refracts light.
  • Iris: Coloured part, controls pupil size.
  • Example: A camera's aperture.

🎙️ The cornea is the clear front window of your eye, bending light as it enters. Behind it is the iris, the coloured part that gives your eyes their unique shade, and it acts like a camera's aperture, adjusting the amount of light entering.

Pupil and Lens: Focusing the Image

  • Pupil: Opening in the center of the iris.
  • Lens: Crystalline, adjustable focus.
  • Example: Magnifying glass.

🎙️ The pupil is the black opening in the center of your iris, which changes size to let more or less light in. The eye lens, located behind the pupil, is a flexible, transparent structure that fine-tunes the focus of light onto the retina, much like a magnifying glass.

🎙️ The retina is the crucial inner lining at the back of the eye, where light is converted into electrical signals. These signals are then transmitted through the optic nerve directly to your brain, which interprets them as the images you see.

Myopia: Seeing Near, Blurring Far

  • Distant objects appear blurred.
  • Image forms in front of the retina.
  • Caused by elongated eyeball or thick lens.

🎙️ Myopia, or nearsightedness, is a common defect where a person can see nearby objects clearly, but distant objects appear blurry. This happens because the light rays from distant objects converge too soon, forming the image in front of the retina.

🛣️
Distant Blur
11 / 25

Correcting Myopia: Bringing Focus Back

  • Use of a concave lens.
  • Diverges light rays before entering eye.
  • Shifts image onto the retina.

🎙️ Myopia can be corrected by using spectacles or contact lenses with a concave lens. This lens diverges the incoming light rays slightly before they enter the eye, ensuring that the image focuses perfectly on the retina.

Hypermetropia: Seeing Far, Blurring Near

  • Nearby objects appear blurred.
  • Image forms behind the retina.

🎙️ Hypermetropia, or farsightedness, is the opposite of myopia. Here, a person can see distant objects clearly, but nearby objects appear blurry. This occurs because the light rays from near objects converge too late, forming the image behind the retina.

Advance Sunrise & Delayed Sunset: Longer Days

  • Sun appears 2 minutes early at sunrise.
  • Sun appears 2 minutes late at sunset.
  • Caused by atmospheric refraction bending light.

🎙️ Atmospheric refraction also makes the sun visible to us about two minutes before it actually rises above the horizon and for about two minutes after it has set. This is because the light from the sun bends over the horizon, making it appear higher than its actual position.

Scattering of Light: Spreading the Colours

  • Light rays deviate from straight path.
  • Caused by interaction with particles in medium.
  • Blue light scatters more than red light.

🎙️ Scattering of light occurs when light rays strike tiny particles in a medium and get redirected in various directions. The amount of scattering depends on the wavelength of light and the size of the particles. Blue light, with its shorter wavelength, scatters more effectively.
