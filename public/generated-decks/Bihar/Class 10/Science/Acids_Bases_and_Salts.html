What are Acids?

  • Sour in taste.
  • Turn blue litmus red.
  • Corrosive in nature.
  • Release H⁺ ions in water (Arrhenius concept).

🎙️ Acids are substances that have a distinct sour taste, like lemon juice. They are known for their ability to change the color of certain indicators, for example, turning blue litmus paper red. Many acids are corrosive, meaning they can cause damage, and chemically, they release hydrogen ions when dissolved in water.

Strong vs. Weak Acids

  • Strong acids dissociate completely in water.
  • Weak acids dissociate partially in water.
  • Strong acids are highly corrosive.
  • Weak acids are less corrosive, often found in food.

🎙️ Acids can be classified as strong or weak based on how much they ionize in water. Strong acids, like hydrochloric acid, completely break apart into ions, making them very reactive. Weak acids, such as acetic acid in vinegar, only partially ionize, which is why we can consume them in moderation.

What are Bases?

  • Bitter in taste.
  • Soapy to touch.
  • Turn red litmus blue.
  • Release OH⁻ ions in water (Arrhenius concept).

🎙️ Now let's talk about bases. Bases have a bitter taste, like bitter gourd, and feel slippery or soapy to the touch. They are the opposite of acids in terms of litmus paper, turning red litmus blue. Chemically, bases release hydroxide ions when dissolved in water.

Strong vs. Weak Bases

  • Strong bases dissociate completely in water.
  • Weak bases dissociate partially in water.
  • Strong bases are corrosive.
  • Weak bases are less corrosive, used in antacids.

🎙️ Similar to acids, bases can also be strong or weak. Strong bases, like sodium hydroxide, fully ionize in water and are very reactive. Weak bases, such as ammonium hydroxide, only partially ionize and are found in common household products like cleaning agents and some antacids.

What are Indicators?

  • Substances that show different colors in acidic and basic mediums.
  • Used to test whether a substance is an acid or a base.
  • Help us avoid tasting or touching chemicals.
  • Can be natural or synthetic.

🎙️ Since we cannot taste or touch acids and bases due to their corrosive nature, we use special substances called indicators. These indicators change color when they come into contact with an acid or a base, providing a safe way to identify them. We have both natural and man-made indicators.

The pH Scale: Measuring Acidity & Basicity

  • A scale to measure the strength of acids and bases.
  • Ranges from 0 to 14.
  • pH < 7 indicates acidic solution.
  • pH > 7 indicates basic solution.
  • pH = 7 indicates neutral solution.

🎙️ The pH scale is a crucial tool in chemistry that quantifies how acidic or basic a solution is. It ranges from 0 to 14. A pH value below 7 indicates an acidic solution, while a value above 7 signifies a basic solution. A pH of exactly 7 represents a perfectly neutral solution, like pure water.

Understanding pH Values

  • Lower pH (0-6) means stronger acid.
  • Higher pH (8-14) means stronger base.
  • pH 7 is neutral (e.g., pure water).
  • Universal indicator shows a range of colors for different pH values.

🎙️ Let's visualize the pH scale. Solutions with pH values close to 0 are very strong acids, like battery acid. As the pH increases towards 7, acidity decreases. Similarly, as pH moves from 7 towards 14, the basicity increases, with values near 14 representing strong bases like drain cleaner. Pure water is right in the middle at pH 7.

Neutralization Reaction

  • Acid + Base → Salt + Water
  • Process where an acid and a base react to form a salt and water.
  • Heat is usually released (exothermic reaction).
  • Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

🎙️ One of the most important reactions involving acids and bases is neutralization. In this reaction, an acid and a base combine to form a salt and water. This reaction typically releases heat, making it an exothermic process. A classic example is hydrochloric acid reacting with sodium hydroxide to produce common salt and water.

What are Salts?

  • Ionic compounds formed from the reaction of an acid and a base.
  • Consist of a cation (from base) and an anion (from acid).
  • Often crystalline solids at room temperature.
  • Many are soluble in water.

🎙️ After discussing acids and bases, let's understand salts. Salts are ionic compounds formed when an acid reacts with a base during neutralization. They are composed of a positively charged ion from the base and a negatively charged ion from the acid. Most salts are crystalline solids and dissolve well in water.

Families of Salts

  • Salts can be acidic, basic, or neutral.
  • Neutral salts: formed from strong acid + strong base (e.g., NaCl).
  • Acidic salts: formed from strong acid + weak base (e.g., NH₄Cl).
  • Basic salts: formed from weak acid + strong base (e.g., Na₂CO₃).

🎙️ Just like acids and bases, salts themselves can exhibit acidic, basic, or neutral properties depending on the strength of the acid and base they are derived from. For instance, common salt, NaCl, is neutral because it comes from a strong acid and a strong base. Ammonium chloride is acidic, and sodium carbonate is basic.

Common Salt: Sodium Chloride (NaCl)

  • Chemically known as Sodium Chloride.
  • Obtained from seawater evaporation and rock salt mines.
  • Essential component of our diet.
  • Raw material for many important chemicals.

🎙️ Common salt, or sodium chloride, is perhaps the most familiar salt. We obtain it from evaporating seawater or by mining it as rock salt. It's not just a seasoning for our food; it's also vital for our body's functions. More importantly, it serves as the starting material for producing a wide range of other useful chemicals.

Chemicals from Common Salt: Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)

  • Also known as Caustic Soda.
  • Produced by chlor-alkali process.
  • Electrolysis of aqueous NaCl solution.
  • Uses: soaps, detergents, paper making, artificial fibers.

🎙️ One crucial chemical derived from common salt is sodium hydroxide, or caustic soda. It's produced through a process called chlor-alkali, where an electric current is passed through a salt solution. This yields not only NaOH but also chlorine gas and hydrogen gas, all of which have numerous industrial applications, like making soap and paper.

Chemicals from Common Salt: Bleaching Powder (CaOCl₂)

  • Chemical name: Calcium Oxychloride.
  • Produced by passing chlorine gas over dry slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂).

🎙️ Another important chemical is bleaching powder. It's made by reacting chlorine gas, which is obtained from the chlor-alkali process, with dry slaked lime. Bleaching powder is widely used in textile industries for bleaching clothes, as a disinfectant for water, and as an oxidizing agent in many chemical reactions.
