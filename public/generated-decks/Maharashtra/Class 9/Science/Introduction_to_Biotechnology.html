What is Biotechnology?

  • Bio: Refers to Biology, meaning living organisms or their components.
  • Technology: Means the application of scientific knowledge for practical purposes.
  • Biotechnology is using living organisms or their products to develop new technologies and products.

🎙️ Let's break down the word. 'Bio' comes from biology, meaning life. 'Technology' is about applying science. So, biotechnology is simply applying biological knowledge to create new things or solve problems.

Curd: Our Everyday Biotechnology!

  • When milk turns into curd, it's a simple form of biotechnology!
  • Tiny bacteria, called Lactobacillus, are the 'workers' here.
  • They convert the sugar (lactose) in milk into lactic acid.
  • This process is called fermentation, an ancient biotech technique.

🎙️ This is a perfect example of biotechnology happening right in our homes! The bacteria are living organisms that perform a specific function – changing milk into curd – which is beneficial to us.

Biotechnology: Not So New!

  • People have been using living organisms for thousands of years.
  • Making bread using yeast, brewing beer, or fermenting wine.
  • These traditional methods are the early forms of biotechnology.
  • They laid the foundation for modern advancements.

🎙️ While the term 'biotechnology' might sound modern, its practices are very old. Our ancestors were unknowingly using biotechnology when they made fermented foods and drinks.

The Modern Revolution!

  • In the 20th century, our understanding of DNA grew rapidly.
  • This knowledge led to advanced techniques like genetic engineering.
  • Now, we can precisely modify organisms at a molecular level.
  • This marked the beginning of modern biotechnology.

🎙️ The discovery of DNA's structure and how it works was a game-changer. It allowed scientists to go beyond traditional fermentation and start 'editing' the very blueprint of life.

What is Genetic Engineering?

  • It's the direct manipulation of an organism's genes.
  • This is done using biotechnology methods.
  • The goal is to change the characteristics of the organism.
  • Or to make it produce new substances, like medicines.

🎙️ Genetic engineering is about making precise changes to an organism's DNA. It's like a molecular surgeon carefully operating on the genetic material to achieve a desired outcome.

✂️🧬

Insulin: A Lifesaver!

  • People with diabetes need insulin to control blood sugar.
  • Earlier, insulin was extracted from animals (pigs or cows).
  • Now, scientists insert the human insulin gene into bacteria.
  • These engineered bacteria then produce human insulin efficiently.

🎙️ This is a brilliant example of genetic engineering saving lives. Instead of relying on animal sources, we can now produce pure human insulin in large quantities, making it safer and more accessible.

Growing Plants in a Lab!

  • Imagine taking a tiny piece – a cell or a small tissue – from a plant.
  • And then growing a whole new, identical plant from it!
  • This happens in a sterile lab environment, not in soil.
  • It's like a special plant nursery inside a test tube.

🎙️ This technique is fascinating because it allows us to multiply plants very quickly and efficiently, even rare or difficult-to-grow species, all from a tiny starting piece.

A plant cell is a eukaryotic cell with a rigid cell wall, large vacuole, and chloroplasts.
14 / 30

What is Tissue Culture?

  • It's the technique of growing cells, tissues, or organs.
  • Outside the original organism, in an artificial environment.
  • It uses a special nutrient medium and sterile conditions.
  • Also known as micropropagation for plants.

🎙️ Tissue culture is a powerful method. By providing the right nutrients and conditions, we can make plant cells grow and develop into full plants, or even grow human cells for research and medicine.

🔬
Petri Dish Culture

Stronger, Healthier Plants!

  • Tissue culture helps produce many identical plants rapidly.
  • All from a tiny piece of a healthy parent plant.
  • This ensures the new plants are disease-free and vigorous.
  • Great for crops like bananas, orchids, and sugarcane.

🎙️ Imagine you have one special plant that is resistant to diseases. With tissue culture, you can create hundreds or thousands of exact copies of that plant, ensuring a healthy and abundant harvest.

Recap: Key Techniques

  • Genetic Engineering: Modifying an organism's DNA to change its traits or products.
  • Tissue Culture: Growing cells, tissues, or organs in a controlled lab environment.
  • Fermentation: Using microorganisms to produce substances, like in curd or bread making.

🎙️ Remember these three main techniques. Genetic engineering is about changing the blueprint, tissue culture is about growing things in a lab, and fermentation is about using microbes for chemical changes.
