What is an Ecosystem?

  • A community of living organisms.
  • Interacting with their non-living environment.
  • Example: A pond, a forest, a desert.

🎙️ An ecosystem is like a big neighbourhood where all living things, from tiny bacteria to giant trees and animals, live and interact with each other. They also interact with non-living things like sunlight, water, and soil. Every interaction is important!

Consumers: The Eaters

  • Organisms that cannot make their own food.
  • They obtain energy by eating other organisms.
  • Can be herbivores, carnivores, or omnivores.

🎙️ Consumers are the organisms that rely on others for their food. They can't perform photosynthesis, so they have to eat producers or other consumers to get the energy they need. Think of a cow eating grass, or a tiger hunting a deer.

Types of Consumers

  • Herbivores: Eat only plants (e.g., cow, deer).
  • Carnivores: Eat only animals (e.g., lion, tiger).
  • Omnivores: Eat both plants and animals (e.g., human, bear).

🎙️ Consumers are categorised based on their diet. Herbivores are primary consumers, feeding directly on plants. Carnivores are secondary or tertiary consumers, eating other animals. Omnivores have a mixed diet, making them very adaptable.

What is a Food Chain?

  • Shows how energy moves from one organism to another.
  • Starts with producers, ends with top consumers.
  • Example: Grass → Deer → Tiger.

🎙️ A food chain illustrates a direct sequence of who eats whom. It always begins with producers, which convert sunlight into food. Then, primary consumers eat producers, followed by secondary consumers eating primary consumers, and so on.

Trophic Levels: Level 1 - Producers

🎙️ The first trophic level is always occupied by producers. They are the entry point of energy into the ecosystem, converting solar energy into chemical energy stored in their bodies. Without them, no other life forms could exist!

The 10% Law: A Rule of Thumb

  • Imagine you have ₹100.
  • You can only give ₹10 to your friend.
  • The rest ₹90 is used by you or lost.

🎙️ The 10% Law is a simple but powerful concept. It states that, on average, only about 10% of the energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next. The remaining 90% is used by the organism for its life processes or lost as heat to the environment.

Understanding the 10% Law

  • Producers have 100% available energy.
  • Primary consumers get only 10% of that.
  • Secondary consumers get 1% (10% of 10%).
  • Tertiary consumers get 0.1% (10% of 1%).

🎙️ This means that if producers have 10,000 units of energy, primary consumers will get only 1,000 units. Secondary consumers will get only 100 units, and tertiary consumers just 10 units. This explains why there are fewer organisms at higher trophic levels.

Energy Pyramid: Visualizing Energy Loss

  • A graphical representation of energy at each level.
  • Always upright, showing decreasing energy upwards.
  • Broad base (producers), narrow top (top consumers).

🎙️ The energy pyramid visually demonstrates the 10% law. The largest block at the bottom represents the vast energy available to producers. Each successive block upwards gets smaller, illustrating the significant loss of energy at each transfer, explaining why top predators are fewer in number.

1Tertiary Consumers (0.1%)
2Secondary Consumers (1%)
3Primary Consumers (10%)
4Producers (100%)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing food chain with food web.
  • Forgetting the 10% energy loss rule.
  • Not including decomposers in the cycle.
  • Assuming energy is recycled (it flows, not recycles).

🎙️ Students often mix up food chains and food webs. Remember, a food chain is a single path, while a food web is many interconnected paths. Also, energy flows in one direction and is lost as heat; it's not recycled like nutrients. Decomposers are vital for nutrient recycling, but energy flows through and out.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Ecosystems have biotic and abiotic components.
  • Energy flows from producers to consumers to decomposers.
  • Food chains are simple paths, food webs are complex networks.
  • Only ~10% energy transfers to the next trophic level (10% Law).

🎙️ Today, we've explored the fascinating journey of energy in an ecosystem. From the sun's initial energy captured by producers, through various consumers, and finally recycled by decomposers, energy flow is fundamental to life. Remember the 10% rule and the difference between food chains and webs.
