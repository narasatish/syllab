What is Microbiology?

  • Includes bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and algae.

🎙️ Microbiology is a branch of science dedicated to studying these tiny living things, called microbes. It explores their structure, function, their role in nature, and how they affect us.

Bacteria: Single-Celled Life

  • Simplest and most abundant microbes.
  • Single-celled prokaryotes.
  • Found almost everywhere.

🎙️ Bacteria are truly amazing! They are single-celled organisms, meaning their entire body is just one cell. They are incredibly diverse and can be found in almost every environment on Earth.

Structure of a Bacterium

  • Cell wall for protection.
  • Cell membrane controls entry/exit.
  • Cytoplasm contains genetic material.
  • Flagella for movement (some types).

🎙️ Though tiny, bacteria have a well-defined structure. Key parts include a strong cell wall, a cell membrane, and cytoplasm where their genetic material is located. Some even have tails called flagella to swim!

Different Shapes of Bacteria

  • Cocci: Spherical (e.g., Streptococcus).
  • Bacilli: Rod-shaped (e.g., Lactobacillus).
  • Spirilla: Spiral-shaped (e.g., Spirillum).

🎙️ Bacteria come in various interesting shapes! Some are round like tiny balls, called cocci. Others are rod-like, known as bacilli, and some even have a spiral shape, called spirilla.

Real Example: Bacteria in Curd

  • Lactobacillus bacteria turn milk into curd.
  • They convert lactose sugar into lactic acid.
  • This process is called fermentation.

🎙️ Have you ever wondered how milk transforms into delicious curd? It's all thanks to beneficial bacteria like Lactobacillus! They ferment the milk, making it thick and tangy.

Viruses: Tiny Non-Living Particles

  • Much smaller than bacteria.
  • Require a host cell to reproduce.
  • Considered on the border of living and non-living.

🎙️ Viruses are fascinating and a bit mysterious. They are so small that they can only be seen with powerful electron microscopes. They can't live or reproduce on their own; they need to invade a living cell to multiply.

Invading Viruses

Structure of a Virus

  • Genetic material (DNA or RNA).
  • Protein coat (capsid) surrounds it.
  • Some have an outer envelope.

🎙️ A virus is essentially genetic material, either DNA or RNA, encased in a protective protein shell called a capsid. Some viruses have an additional outer layer called an envelope.

  • Spreads through respiratory droplets.
  • Symptoms include fever, cough, body aches.

Fungi: Decomposers of Nature

  • Include yeasts, molds, and mushrooms.
  • Heterotrophic: obtain food from others.
  • Many are decomposers, recycling nutrients.

🎙️ Fungi are a diverse group that plays a crucial role in our ecosystem. Unlike plants, they don't make their own food; instead, they absorb nutrients from their surroundings, often breaking down dead matter.

🍄
Mushroom

Types of Fungi

  • Yeasts: Single-celled (e.g., for baking).
  • Molds: Multicellular, filamentous (e.g., on food).
  • Mushrooms: Larger, visible fruiting bodies.

🎙️ Fungi come in various forms. Yeasts are tiny single-celled fungi, while molds are made of many thread-like structures. Mushrooms are the largest and most visible type of fungi.

Protozoa: Animal-like Microbes

  • Single-celled eukaryotic organisms.
  • Often motile (can move).
  • Found in water and soil.

🎙️ Protozoa are fascinating single-celled organisms that share characteristics with animals. Many of them can move independently and are commonly found in aquatic environments like ponds and lakes.

Real Example: Amoeba

  • Amoeba proteus is a well-known protozoan.
  • Moves using pseudopods ('false feet').
  • Engulfs food particles using phagocytosis.

🎙️ The Amoeba is a classic example of a protozoan. It's famous for its ability to change shape and move using temporary extensions called pseudopods, which it also uses to capture food.

Algae: Plant-like Microbes

  • Perform photosynthesis like plants.
  • Produce oxygen and are food source.
  • Can be single-celled or multicellular.

🎙️ Algae are unique among microbes because they can produce their own food using sunlight, just like plants! They are vital for producing oxygen and form the base of many aquatic food chains.

Microbes: The Trouble Makers

  • Disease-causing (pathogens): Bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa.
