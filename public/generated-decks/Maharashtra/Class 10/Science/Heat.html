Heat vs. Temperature: Key Differences

  • Heat is energy; Temperature is a measure of energy.

  • Heat units: Joule/Calorie; Temperature units: °C/°F/K.

🎙️ Heat is the total internal energy, while temperature is the average kinetic energy of particles. A large bathtub of lukewarm water might have more heat energy than a small cup of boiling water, but the boiling water has a higher temperature.

Specific Heat Capacity: Definition

  • Amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C or 1 K.
  • Symbol: 'c' or 's'.
  • Formula: Q = mcΔT (where Q is heat, m is mass, ΔT is change in temperature).
  • SI Unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

🎙️ Specific heat capacity is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C or 1 K. Its SI unit is J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. The formula is Q = mcΔT.

Heat Capacity

Land and Sea Breezes Explained

  • Water has a high specific heat capacity compared to land.
  • Land heats up and cools down faster than the sea.

🎙️ Water has a very high specific heat capacity compared to land. During the day, land heats up faster than the sea, causing land breeze. At night, land cools faster, leading to sea breeze, moderating coastal climates.

Latent Heat: Hidden Heat of Change

  • When ice melts, its temperature doesn't change, but it absorbs heat.
  • This 'hidden' heat is used to change the state of matter.
  • It doesn't cause a temperature rise.

🎙️ When ice melts into water, its temperature doesn't change even though it's absorbing heat. Where does this heat go? It's used to change the state, and we call it latent heat.

Latent Heat of Fusion: Melting Ice

  • Heat required to change 1 kg of solid to liquid at its melting point.
  • No change in temperature during this process.
  • For ice, it's about 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg.

🎙️ Latent heat of fusion is the heat required to change 1 kg of a solid into a liquid at its melting point without a change in temperature. For ice, it's about 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg.

Latent Heat of Vaporization: Boiling Water

  • Heat required to change 1 kg of liquid to gas at its boiling point.
  • Again, no temperature change during the phase transition.
  • Steam at 100°C has more energy than water at 100°C.

🎙️ Latent heat of vaporization is the heat required to change 1 kg of a liquid into a gas at its boiling point without a change in temperature. Steam at 100°C causes more severe burns than boiling water at 100°C due to this extra latent heat.

Conduction: Heat Through Contact

  • Transfer of heat through direct contact between particles.
  • No actual movement of the material itself.
  • Most common in solids, especially metals (good conductors).

🎙️ Conduction is the transfer of heat through direct contact between particles, without any actual movement of the material itself. It's most common in solids, especially metals, where particles vibrate and pass energy along.

🎙️ Convection is the transfer of heat in fluids (liquids and gases) due to the actual movement of the fluid particles. Hotter, less dense fluid rises, and colder, denser fluid sinks, creating convection currents, like boiling water or room heaters.

Thermal Expansion: Getting Bigger with Heat

  • Most materials expand when heated and contract when cooled.
  • This phenomenon is called thermal expansion.
  • Caused by increased kinetic energy of particles, making them vibrate more and spread out.

🎙️ What happens to most materials when they get hot? They expand! This phenomenon is called thermal expansion, and it's crucial in many engineering applications, like railway tracks.

Types of Thermal Expansion

  • Linear Expansion: Change in length (e.g., metal rod).
  • Superficial (Area) Expansion: Change in surface area (e.g., metal sheet).
  • Volumetric (Cubical) Expansion: Change in volume (solids, liquids, gases).

🎙️ Solids can undergo linear (change in length), superficial (change in area), or volumetric (change in volume) expansion. Liquids and gases primarily show volumetric expansion.

Water's Special Trick: Anomalous Expansion

  • Water behaves unusually between 0°C and 4°C.
  • It contracts when heated from 0°C to 4°C (most liquids expand).
  • Reaches maximum density at 4°C, then expands normally above 4°C.

🎙️ Unlike most liquids, water contracts when heated from 0°C to 4°C, reaching its maximum density at 4°C. Above 4°C, it expands normally. This anomalous behavior is vital for aquatic life in cold regions.

Calorimetry: Measuring Heat Exchange

  • Calorimetry is the measurement of heat changes.
  • Principle of Heat Exchange: Heat lost = Heat gained.
  • Assumes no heat loss to the surroundings (isolated system).

🎙️ Calorimetry is the measurement of heat changes. The principle of heat exchange states that when hot and cold objects are in thermal contact, heat lost by the hot object equals the heat gained by the cold object, assuming no heat loss to surroundings.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing Heat and Temperature.
  • Forgetting Latent Heat during phase changes.
  • Incorrect units in numerical problems.

🎙️ Students often confuse heat and temperature, or forget the latent heat during phase changes. Remember, temperature measures average kinetic energy, while heat is the total energy transferred, and always pay attention to units.
