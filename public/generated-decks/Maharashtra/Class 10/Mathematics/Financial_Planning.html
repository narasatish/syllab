Simple Interest: Basic Earnings on Your Money

  • Interest earned only on the original principal amount.
  • Calculated using P x R x T / 100.
  • Principal (P), Rate (R), Time (T).

🎙️ Simple interest is the easiest way to calculate interest. Imagine you lend ₹100 to a friend, and they agree to pay you ₹10 extra every year. That extra ₹10 is the simple interest, always calculated on the original ₹100.

Simple Interest Example

  • Rohan deposits ₹1000 at 5% simple interest for 2 years.
  • P = ₹1000, R = 5%, T = 2 years.
  • Interest = (1000 * 5 * 2) / 100 = ₹100.

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. If Rohan puts ₹1000 in a bank that gives 5% simple interest for 2 years, he earns ₹50 each year. So, after two years, he gets ₹100 in interest. His total amount will be ₹1100.

Compound Interest: The Power of 'Interest on Interest'

  • Interest earned on principal AND accumulated interest.
  • Money grows faster over time.
  • Often called the '8th wonder of the world'.

🎙️ Compound interest is truly magical! It's like a snowball rolling down a hill, gathering more snow and getting bigger and bigger. Here, your interest also starts earning interest, making your money grow much faster than simple interest.

❄️
Snowball Effect
13 / 30

Compound Interest Example

  • Rohan deposits ₹1000 at 5% compound interest for 2 years.
  • Year 1 Interest: (1000 * 5 * 1) / 100 = ₹50.
  • Year 2 Principal: ₹1000 + ₹50 = ₹1050.
  • Year 2 Interest: (1050 * 5 * 1) / 100 = ₹52.50.
  • Total Interest = ₹50 + ₹52.50 = ₹102.50.

🎙️ Using Rohan's example: In year 1, he earns ₹50. Now, his principal becomes ₹1050. In year 2, he earns interest on ₹1050, which is ₹52.50. See, he earned more interest than with simple interest because the previous year's interest was added!

Goods and Services Tax (GST): Understanding Our Taxes

  • A single indirect tax for the entire nation.
  • Replaced many previous taxes (VAT, Service Tax, etc.).
  • Applied to goods and services.

🎙️ Now, let's shift gears to something we encounter daily: taxes! GST is like a common umbrella tax that covers almost all goods and services in India. It simplified our tax system and made buying and selling easier across states.

🧾
Unified Tax

Types of GST: CGST, SGST, IGST

  • CGST: Central GST (goes to Central Govt.).
  • SGST: State GST (goes to State Govt.).
  • IGST: Integrated GST (for inter-state transactions).

🎙️ When you buy something within your state, the GST is split between the Central Government (CGST) and your State Government (SGST). If you buy something from another state, it's IGST, which is collected by the Centre and then distributed.

💸💸💸
Tax Distribution

Calculating GST: An Example

  • Item price: ₹500, GST rate: 18%.
  • GST Amount = 18% of ₹500 = ₹90.
  • Total Bill = ₹500 + ₹90 = ₹590.

🎙️ Let's say you buy a book for ₹500 and the GST rate is 18%. You simply calculate 18% of ₹500, which is ₹90. So, your total bill would be ₹590. This is how GST is added to the price of goods and services.
