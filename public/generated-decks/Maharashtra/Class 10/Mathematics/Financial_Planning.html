Understanding Income and Expenses

  • Income: Money you receive (e.g., salary, pocket money).
  • Expenses: Money you spend (e.g., food, transport, entertainment).

🎙️ These are the two fundamental pillars of financial planning. Income is what comes in, and expenses are what goes out. To have good financial health, ideally, your income should be more than your expenses.

Understanding GST (Goods and Services Tax)

  • GST is a tax applied to most goods and services in India.
  • It replaced many other indirect taxes.
  • It's included in the price you pay for many items.

🎙️ You see GST on almost every bill you receive, from a restaurant to a clothing store. It's a single, unified tax system in India, making the tax structure simpler and more transparent. It's an important part of our economy.

🧾
Tax Bill

How Does GST Work?

  • It's a consumption tax, paid by the end consumer.
  • Collected at each stage of sale, but the final burden is on the consumer.
  • Different items have different GST rates (e.g., 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%).

🎙️ Think of it as a chain. At each step of the supply chain, a small portion of GST is collected, but the system ensures that only the final consumer bears the entire tax. This prevents 'tax on tax' effect.

1Manufacturer sells to Wholesaler
2Wholesaler sells to Retailer
3Retailer sells to Consumer
4Consumer pays final GST

Let's Calculate GST!

  • GST Amount = (Original Price) × (GST Rate / 100)
  • Final Price = Original Price + GST Amount

🎙️ The calculation of GST is quite straightforward. You simply find the percentage of the original price and add it to get the final amount. Let's try an example to make it clearer.

Worked Example: Mobile Phone Purchase

  • Original price of a mobile phone = ₹10,000.
  • GST rate = 18%.
  • GST Amount = ₹10,000 × (18/100) = ₹1,800.
  • Final Price = ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 = ₹11,800.

🎙️ So, when you buy a phone for ₹11,800, ₹1,800 of that money goes to the government as GST. This is how the government collects revenue to fund public services.

Budgeting: Your Financial Roadmap

🎙️ Budgeting is the practical application of financial planning. It's a detailed plan that tells you how much money you have, how much you need for expenses, and how much you can save or invest. It's your personal money manager!

Steps to Create Your Own Budget

  • List all your sources of income.
  • List all your regular expenses.
  • Track your spending for a month.
  • Adjust your spending to save more if needed.

🎙️ Start with your pocket money or any allowance you receive. Write down everything you spend money on. At the end of the month, see if you spent more than you earned, or if you managed to save. Then, make changes for the next month.
