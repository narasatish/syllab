What is an Arithmetic Progression (AP)?

  • A sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant
  • This constant difference is called the 'common difference' (d)

🎙️ Now, let's define an Arithmetic Progression, or AP. It's a special type of sequence where the difference between any two consecutive terms is always the same. We call this constant difference the 'common difference', denoted by 'd'.

How to Identify an Arithmetic Progression

  • Subtract a term from its succeeding term
  • Do this for several pairs
  • If the difference is always the same, it's an AP!

🎙️ To check if a given sequence is an AP, simply pick any term and subtract the term just before it. Do this for at least two pairs of consecutive terms. If you get the same difference every time, then congratulations, you've found an AP!

2Subtract previous term
3Repeat for next pair
4Check if same difference

Understanding the Common Difference (d)

  • The constant difference between any two consecutive terms
  • Calculated as: d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = ...
  • Can be positive, negative, or zero

🎙️ The common difference, 'd', is the heart of an AP. It's the fixed number that you add to any term to get the next term. Remember, 'd' can be positive if the terms are increasing, negative if they are decreasing, or even zero if all terms are the same.

Worked Example: Find the Common Difference

  • Sequence: 5, 8, 11, 14, ...
  • Step 1: Subtract 8 - 5 = 3
  • Step 2: Subtract 11 - 8 = 3
  • Step 3: Subtract 14 - 11 = 3

🎙️ Let's try an example. For the sequence 5, 8, 11, 14, ... we calculate the difference between consecutive terms. Since the difference is consistently 3, we can confirm it's an AP, and our common difference 'd' is 3.

2a3 - a2 = 11 - 8 = 3

The General Form of an AP

  • If 'a' is the first term and 'd' is the common difference, then the AP is:
  • a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, ...
  • Each term is found by adding 'd' to the previous term

🎙️ We can express any Arithmetic Progression using a general form. If 'a' represents the very first term and 'd' is our common difference, then the sequence will look like: a, then a plus d, then a plus two d, and so on. This shows how each term is built.

Finding the nth Term (t_n)

  • What if we want the 100th term without listing them all?
  • There's a formula to find any term directly!
  • This term is called the 'nth term' or t_n

🎙️ Imagine needing to find the 50th or 100th term of an AP. It would be tedious to list them all out! Thankfully, mathematics provides us with a powerful formula that allows us to calculate any term directly, which we call the 'nth term' or t_n.

Formula for the nth Term (t_n)

  • t_n = a + (n-1)d
  • t_n = the nth term
  • a = the first term
  • n = the term number (e.g., 5th, 10th)
  • d = the common difference

🎙️ Here it is, the key formula for finding any term in an AP: t_n equals 'a' plus 'n minus one' times 'd'. Make sure you understand what each variable stands for: t_n is the term you want, 'a' is the start, 'n' is its position, and 'd' is the step size.

Worked Example: Find the 10th Term

  • AP: 2, 5, 8, 11, ...
  • Find a, d, n: a=2, d=3, n=10
  • Apply formula: t_10 = 2 + (10-1)3
  • t_10 = 2 + 9 * 3 = 2 + 27 = 29

🎙️ Let's use our formula. For the AP 2, 5, 8, 11, ... we want the 10th term. Here, a is 2, d is 3, and n is 10. Plugging these values into the formula, we get 2 plus 9 times 3, which simplifies to 29. So, the 10th term is 29.

Sum of the First n Terms (S_n)

  • Sometimes we need to add up all terms up to a certain point
  • For example, total savings after 12 months
  • The sum of the first 'n' terms is denoted by S_n

🎙️ Beyond finding individual terms, we often need to calculate the sum of a certain number of terms in an AP. For instance, if you're saving money each month in an AP, you'd want to know your total savings after a year. This total is called the 'sum of the first n terms', or S_n.

Formula for Sum of n Terms (S_n)

  • S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
  • S_n = sum of the first n terms
  • n = number of terms
  • a = first term
  • d = common difference

🎙️ Here is the main formula for the sum of the first 'n' terms of an AP. It states that S_n equals 'n divided by 2' multiplied by 'two times a plus n minus one times d'. This formula is incredibly useful for quickly summing many terms.

Alternative Sum Formula (S_n)

  • If the last term (l) is known: S_n = n/2 [a + l]
  • l = t_n = a + (n-1)d
  • This formula is simpler if you already have the last term

🎙️ There's also a simpler version of the sum formula if you already know the last term of the AP, denoted as 'l'. In this case, S_n is simply 'n divided by 2' multiplied by 'a plus l'. Remember, 'l' is just another way of writing t_n.

Worked Example: Sum of First 20 Terms

  • AP: 1, 3, 5, 7, ...
  • Find a, d, n: a=1, d=2, n=20
  • Apply formula: S_20 = 20/2 [2(1) + (20-1)2]
  • S_20 = 10 [2 + 19 * 2] = 10 [2 + 38] = 10 * 40 = 400

🎙️ Let's calculate the sum of the first 20 terms for the AP 1, 3, 5, 7, ... Here, a=1, d=2, and n=20. Plugging these values into the sum formula gives us 10 times '2 plus 19 times 2', which simplifies to 10 times 40, resulting in a sum of 400.

Worked Example: How Many Terms for a Given Sum?

  • AP: 9, 17, 25, ... Sum = 636
  • Find a, d: a=9, d=8
  • Substitute into S_n formula: 636 = n/2 [2(9) + (n-1)8]
  • Simplify to quadratic: 4n² + 5n - 636 = 0
  • Solve for n (e.g., quadratic formula), n=12 (n must be positive)

Recap: Key Concepts of AP

  • Sequence: Ordered list of numbers

  • t_n = a + (n-1)d (nth term formula)
