What is Mensuration?

  • Branch of mathematics.
  • Deals with measurement of geometric figures.
  • Calculates length, area, volume.

🎙️ Mensuration is like our mathematical toolbox for measuring things. It helps us find out how much space a shape covers, how long its boundaries are, or how much it can hold.

Cuboid: A Familiar Shape

  • Six rectangular faces.
  • Opposite faces are identical.
  • Example: A brick, a book.

🎙️ A cuboid is a very common 3D shape, like a shoebox or a brick. It has six flat surfaces, and each one is a rectangle. Opposite faces are always the same size.

🧱
Brick
6 / 25

Cuboid: Surface Area

  • Total Surface Area (TSA): Sum of areas of all 6 faces.
  • TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • Lateral Surface Area (LSA): Area of 4 side faces (excluding top/bottom).

🎙️ The surface area of a cuboid is the total area of all its outer surfaces. Imagine painting the cuboid; the area you paint is its surface area. TSA includes all faces, while LSA is just the sides.

📐
Area Calculation

Cuboid: Volume

  • Space occupied by the cuboid.
  • Volume = length × breadth × height
  • Units are cubic units (e.g., cm³).

🎙️ Volume tells us how much space a cuboid can hold, or how much material it's made of. Think of a water tank; its volume tells us how much water it can store. It's calculated by multiplying its three dimensions.

💧
Capacity
8 / 25

Worked Example: Cuboid

  • Q: Find TSA and Volume of a cuboid with l=5cm, b=3cm, h=2cm.
  • TSA = 2(5x3 + 3x2 + 2x5) = 2(15+6+10) = 2(31) = 62 cm²
  • Volume = 5x3x2 = 30 cm³

🎙️ Let's apply the formulas we just learned. For a cuboid with these dimensions, we simply substitute the values into the TSA and Volume formulas to get our answers. Remember the correct units!

Cube: A Special Cuboid

  • All six faces are identical squares.
  • All edges are of equal length (side 'a').
  • Example: Dice, Rubik's Cube.

🎙️ A cube is a special type of cuboid where all its sides are equal in length. This means all six faces are perfect squares. Think of a dice or a Rubik's Cube.

🎲
Dice
10 / 25

Cube: Surface Area & Volume

  • TSA = 6a² (6 faces, each area a²)
  • LSA = 4a² (4 side faces)
  • Volume = a³ (side x side x side)

🎙️ Since all sides are equal, the formulas for a cube simplify nicely. Total surface area is just 6 times the area of one square face. Volume is simply the side length cubed.

📏
Cube Formulas

Worked Example: Cube

  • Q: Find TSA and Volume of a cube with side 'a'=4cm.
  • TSA = 6(4)² = 6(16) = 96 cm²
  • Volume = (4)³ = 64 cm³

🎙️ Here's an example for a cube. If the side length is 4 cm, we can quickly calculate its total surface area and volume using our specific cube formulas. Notice how straightforward it is.

Cylinder: Round and Smooth

  • Two circular bases (top and bottom).
  • One curved surface.
  • Example: A tin can, water pipe.

🎙️ A cylinder is a familiar shape with two flat circular ends and a smooth, curved side. Think of a tin can of food or a water pipe. It has a uniform circular cross-section.

🥫
Tin Can
13 / 25

Cylinder: Surface Area

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = CSA + 2(Area of base)
  • TSA = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r)

🎙️ For a cylinder, the curved surface area is like the label on a can. The total surface area includes this curved part plus the areas of the top and bottom circular bases. Remember 'r' is radius and 'h' is height.

🌀
Curved Surface
14 / 25

Cylinder: Volume

  • Volume = Area of base × height
  • Volume = πr²h
  • Units are cubic units (e.g., m³).

🎙️ The volume of a cylinder is found by multiplying the area of its circular base by its height. Imagine stacking many thin circular discs; their combined space would be the cylinder's volume.

🥛
Glass of Milk
15 / 25

Worked Example: Cylinder

  • Q: Find CSA and Volume of a cylinder with r=7cm, h=10cm (Use π=22/7).
  • CSA = 2(22/7)(7)(10) = 440 cm²
  • Volume = (22/7)(7)²(10) = 22/7 * 49 * 10 = 22 * 7 * 10 = 1540 cm³

🎙️ Let's work through an example for a cylinder. With the given radius and height, we can calculate both its curved surface area and its volume. Pay attention to the value of pi given in the problem.

Cone: Pointed at the Top

  • One circular base.
  • One curved surface tapering to a point (vertex).
  • Example: Ice cream cone, party hat.

🎙️ A cone is a shape with a circular base and a curved surface that narrows to a single point at the top, called the vertex. Think of an ice cream cone or a party hat.

🍦
Ice Cream Cone

Cone: Surface Area

  • Slant height (l): Distance from vertex to any point on base circle.
  • CSA = πrl
  • TSA = CSA + Area of base = πrl + πr² = πr(l+r)

🎙️ For a cone, we introduce 'slant height' (l), which is different from its perpendicular height (h). The curved surface area involves 'r' and 'l'. Total surface area adds the base area to this.

🔺
Cone Dimensions
18 / 25

Cone: Volume

  • Volume = (1/3) × Area of base × height
  • Volume = (1/3)πr²h
  • A cone's volume is 1/3 of a cylinder with same base & height.

🎙️ Interestingly, the volume of a cone is exactly one-third the volume of a cylinder that has the same base radius and the same perpendicular height. This is a key relationship to remember.

⚖️

Worked Example: Cone

  • Q: Find Volume of a cone with r=3cm, h=4cm. (First find l if needed for SA)
  • Volume = (1/3)π(3)²(4) = (1/3)π(9)(4) = 12π cm³
  • (l = √(r²+h²) = √(3²+4²) = √25 = 5cm)

🎙️ Here's an example for calculating the volume of a cone. We use the given radius and height directly in the volume formula. Remember, for surface area, you'd first need to calculate the slant height 'l'.

Sphere: Perfectly Round

  • A perfectly round 3D object.
  • Every point on its surface is equidistant from its center.
  • Example: Football, globe.

🎙️ A sphere is a perfectly round solid shape, like a football or a globe. Every single point on its surface is exactly the same distance from its center, which is called the radius.

⚽
Football
21 / 25

Sphere: Surface Area & Volume

  • Surface Area = 4πr²
  • Volume = (4/3)πr³
  • Only one dimension: radius (r).

🎙️ For a sphere, we only need its radius to calculate both its surface area and its volume. The formulas are quite elegant and involve pi and the radius raised to a power.

🌍

Hemisphere: Half a Sphere

  • Exactly half of a sphere.
  • One curved surface and one flat circular base.
  • Example: A bowl, half an orange.

🎙️ A hemisphere is simply half of a sphere. It has a curved surface and also a flat, circular base. Think of a bowl or cutting an orange exactly in half.

🥣
Bowl
23 / 25

Hemisphere: Surface Area & Volume

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πr² (half of sphere's SA)
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr² + πr² = 3πr²
  • Volume = (2/3)πr³ (half of sphere's Volume)

🎙️ For a hemisphere, the curved surface area is half that of a full sphere. But for total surface area, we must add the area of its flat circular base. The volume is, of course, half the sphere's volume.
