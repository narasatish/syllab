n(S): Number of Sample Points

  • The total count of distinct outcomes in the sample space.
  • Represented as n(S).

🎙️ For a single coin toss, S = {H, T}, so n(S) = 2. For rolling a die, S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}, so n(S) = 6. It's simply the size of your sample space.

Defining Probability: The Formula!

  • Probability of an Event P(E) = n(E) / n(S)
  • It's the ratio of favorable outcomes to total outcomes.

🎙️ This is the most important formula in probability! It tells us how likely an event is by comparing how many ways it can happen to how many total possibilities there are.

➗
Favorable / Total

Properties of Probability

  • The probability of any event E is always between 0 and 1 (inclusive).
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.

🎙️ A probability of 0 means the event is impossible, and 1 means it's certain. You'll never get a probability like -0.5 or 1.2. Always check your answers!

Complementary Events

  • If E is an event, then 'not E' (denoted as E' or Eᶜ) is its complementary event.
  • P(E) + P(E') = 1, or P(E') = 1 - P(E).

🎙️ If the probability of rain is 0.3, then the probability of 'not rain' is 1 - 0.3 = 0.7. These two events cover all possibilities and sum up to 1.

Common Mistakes

  • Incorrectly identifying Sample Space (S) or Event (E).
  • Calculation errors (e.g., simplifying fractions wrong).
  • Stating probability values outside the range [0, 1].

🎙️ Always double-check your list of all possible outcomes and your desired outcomes. Ensure your final answer is a fraction or decimal between 0 and 1.

Recap: Key Takeaways!

  • Probability measures the likelihood of an event.
