Naming the Sides: Hypotenuse, Opposite, Adjacent

  • Hypotenuse: Longest side, opposite the 90° angle
  • Opposite Side: Side directly across from the *angle of interest*
  • Adjacent Side: Side next to the *angle of interest* (not the hypotenuse)

🎙️ Every right-angled triangle has three special sides. The longest side, always opposite the 90-degree angle, is the Hypotenuse. For any *other* angle we are focusing on, the side directly across from it is the Opposite side, and the side next to it (that isn't the hypotenuse) is the Adjacent side.

The First Ratio: Sine (sin)

  • Sine of an angle (θ) is the ratio of the Opposite side to the Hypotenuse
  • Formula: sin(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Remember it as SOH (Sine = Opposite/Hypotenuse)

🎙️ Our first trigonometric ratio is Sine, often written as 'sin'. For any acute angle in a right triangle, its sine value is found by dividing the length of the side opposite to that angle by the length of the hypotenuse. A famous mnemonic to remember this is SOH: Sine = Opposite over Hypotenuse.

Example 1: Calculating Sine

  • Consider a right triangle ABC, Angle B = 90°
  • For angle A: Opposite side = BC = 3
  • Hypotenuse = AC = 5
  • So, sin(A) = 3/5

🎙️ Let's try an example. Imagine a right triangle where the side opposite to angle A is 3 units long, and the hypotenuse is 5 units long. To find sin(A), we simply divide 3 by 5, giving us 0.6. It's that simple!

The Second Ratio: Cosine (cos)

  • Cosine of an angle (θ) is the ratio of the Adjacent side to the Hypotenuse
  • Formula: cos(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Remember it as CAH (Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse)

🎙️ Next up is Cosine, or 'cos'. For the same angle, its cosine value is found by dividing the length of the side adjacent to that angle by the length of the hypotenuse. Our mnemonic for this is CAH: Cosine = Adjacent over Hypotenuse.

The Third Ratio: Tangent (tan)

  • Tangent of an angle (θ) is the ratio of the Opposite side to the Adjacent side
  • Formula: tan(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent
  • Remember it as TOA (Tangent = Opposite/Adjacent)

🎙️ Finally, we have Tangent, or 'tan'. This ratio compares the opposite side to the adjacent side, without involving the hypotenuse. The mnemonic here is TOA: Tangent = Opposite over Adjacent. It's also equal to sin(θ) / cos(θ).

The Reciprocal Ratios: Cosecant (cosec)

  • Cosecant (cosec or csc) is the reciprocal of Sine
  • Formula: cosec(θ) = 1 / sin(θ) = Hypotenuse / Opposite
  • It's like flipping the sin ratio upside down!

🎙️ Just like numbers have reciprocals (like 2 and 1/2), trigonometric ratios also have reciprocals. The reciprocal of sine is Cosecant, often written as 'cosec' or 'csc'. It's simply the hypotenuse divided by the opposite side.

The Reciprocal Ratios: Secant (sec)

  • Secant (sec) is the reciprocal of Cosine
  • Formula: sec(θ) = 1 / cos(θ) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent
  • It's the flipped version of the cos ratio.

🎙️ Similarly, the reciprocal of cosine is Secant, written as 'sec'. So, if you know the cosine of an angle, just flip the fraction to get its secant! It's Hypotenuse divided by Adjacent.

The Reciprocal Ratios: Cotangent (cot)

🎙️ And finally, the reciprocal of tangent is Cotangent, written as 'cot'. It's the adjacent side divided by the opposite side. Knowing these three reciprocal ratios gives you a complete set of six trigonometric ratios!

Deriving Ratios for 45°

  • Consider an isosceles right-angled triangle (45°-45°-90°)
  • If equal sides are 'a', hypotenuse is 'a√2'
  • sin(45°) = 1/√2, cos(45°) = 1/√2, tan(45°) = 1

🎙️ Let's see how we get these values. For 45 degrees, imagine a square cut diagonally. You get an isosceles right triangle. If the equal sides are 'a', by Pythagoras, the hypotenuse is 'a√2'. From this, we can easily find sin, cos, and tan of 45 degrees.

Deriving Ratios for 30° and 60°

  • Consider an equilateral triangle cut in half (30°-60°-90°)
  • Sides are in ratio 1:√3:2
  • sin(30°) = 1/2, cos(30°) = √3/2, tan(30°) = 1/√3
  • sin(60°) = √3/2, cos(60°) = 1/2, tan(60°) = √3

🎙️ For 30 and 60 degrees, we use an equilateral triangle. If you cut an equilateral triangle in half, you get a 30-60-90 triangle. The sides will be in the ratio 1:√3:2. Using these side lengths, we can derive all the ratios for both 30 and 60 degrees.

These are equations involving trigonometric ratios that hold true for all valid angles. They are like your superpowers for simplifying problems!

The Fundamental Identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

  • Derived directly from the Pythagorean theorem
  • States that the square of sine plus the square of cosine of an angle is always 1
  • Extremely useful for solving many problems

🎙️ This is the most important and fundamental trigonometric identity. It comes directly from the Pythagorean theorem! It tells us that for any angle θ, if you square its sine value and add it to the square of its cosine value, the result will always be 1. Remember this one well!

More Powerful Identities

  • 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
  • 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
  • These are derived from the first identity by dividing by cos²θ and sin²θ respectively
  • Use them to simplify expressions or prove other identities

🎙️ Beyond the first identity, we have two more crucial ones. The first, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, is obtained by dividing sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ. The second, 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ, is obtained by dividing by sin²θ. These identities are invaluable for simplifying complex trigonometric expressions.

Beware! Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Opposite and Adjacent sides (depends on the angle!)
  • Incorrectly recalling special angle values
  • Calculation errors (especially with square roots)
  • Not writing units or proper steps in proofs

🎙️ Trigonometry can be tricky, and students often make similar mistakes. Always double-check which side is opposite and which is adjacent *for the specific angle you're working with*. Memorizing the special angle values correctly is key, and be careful with your calculations, especially with terms involving square roots.
