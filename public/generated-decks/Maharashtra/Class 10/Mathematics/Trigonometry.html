What is Trigonometry?

  • Derived from Greek words: 'trigonon' (triangle) and 'metron' (measure)
  • Studies relationships between sides and angles of triangles
  • Primarily focuses on right-angled triangles

🎙️ Trigonometry is essentially the science of measuring triangles. It helps us understand how the lengths of sides relate to the angles inside a triangle.

The Right-Angled Triangle: Our Foundation

  • Has one angle exactly 90 degrees
  • The longest side is the Hypotenuse
  • The other two angles are acute (less than 90 degrees)

🎙️ Our entire study of trigonometric ratios revolves around the right-angled triangle. Identifying its key features is the first step.

Identifying Sides of a Right Triangle

  • Always relative to a specific acute angle (θ)
  • Crucial for setting up trigonometric ratios

🎙️ To correctly apply trigonometry, you must identify these three sides based on the angle you are considering. The hypotenuse is constant, but opposite and adjacent switch based on the reference angle.

1Hypotenuse: Longest side, opposite the 90° angle.
2Opposite: Side directly facing the reference angle (θ).
3Adjacent: Side next to the reference angle (θ), not the hypotenuse.

Introducing Trigonometric Ratios

  • Ratios of lengths of sides of a right-angled triangle
  • There are six main ratios
  • These ratios depend only on the angle, not the size of the triangle

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are just fractions comparing two sides of a right triangle. For a given angle, these ratios will always be the same, regardless of how big or small the triangle is.

Remember SOH CAH TOA!

  • A mnemonic for the three primary ratios
  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ This simple phrase is your best friend for remembering the definitions of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent. Practice saying it until it's second nature!

The Sine Ratio (sin θ)

  • sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Represents the 'height' component relative to the hypotenuse
  • Example: Finding the height of a kite based on string length and angle

🎙️ The sine ratio tells us how much the opposite side is compared to the hypotenuse. Imagine looking up at something; the height you see is related to the sine of your viewing angle.

📏
Vertical Measurement

The Cosine Ratio (cos θ)

  • cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Represents the 'base' or 'horizontal' component relative to the hypotenuse
  • Example: Calculating the distance to a tree based on its height and angle

🎙️ Cosine gives us the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse. Think of it as the 'reach' or 'shadow length' from a point, based on an angle.

↔️
Horizontal Measurement

The Tangent Ratio (tan θ)

  • tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent
  • Relates the two 'legs' of the right triangle
  • Example: Finding the angle of a ramp given its height and length along the ground

🎙️ Tangent is very useful when you want to relate the height and base without involving the hypotenuse. It's often used to determine slopes or angles of elevation.

Introducing Reciprocal Ratios

  • Each primary ratio has a reciprocal
  • Cosecant (cosec), Secant (sec), Cotangent (cot)
  • Simply the inverse of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

🎙️ Just like how 2 and 1/2 are reciprocals, trigonometric ratios also have their inverses. These reciprocal ratios are equally important in solving problems.

🔄
Flipping Ratios
12 / 25

Cosecant (cosec θ)

  • Reciprocal of Sine
  • cosec θ = Hypotenuse / Opposite
  • Also written as csc θ

🎙️ If you know the sine of an angle, finding its cosecant is as simple as flipping the fraction. For example, if sin θ = 1/2, then cosec θ = 2.

⬇️
Inverse Sine
13 / 25

Secant (sec θ)

  • Reciprocal of Cosine
  • sec θ = Hypotenuse / Adjacent
  • Often used in advanced calculations

🎙️ Similarly, the secant ratio is the reciprocal of the cosine ratio. If cos θ = 3/5, then sec θ = 5/3. Keep these relationships in mind.

➡️
Inverse Cosine
14 / 25

Cotangent (cot θ)

  • Reciprocal of Tangent
  • cot θ = Adjacent / Opposite
  • Can also be expressed as cos θ / sin θ

🎙️ The cotangent ratio is the reciprocal of the tangent ratio. If tan θ = 4/3, then cot θ = 3/4. This ratio is also very useful in various problems.

Worked Example: Calculate Ratios

  • Given a right triangle with Opposite = 3, Adjacent = 4, Hypotenuse = 5.
  • sin θ = Opposite/Hypotenuse = 3/5
  • cos θ = Adjacent/Hypotenuse = 4/5
  • tan θ = Opposite/Adjacent = 3/4

The First Pythagorean Identity

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • Derived directly from the Pythagorean theorem
  • A cornerstone identity in trigonometry

🎙️ This identity is incredibly important and frequently used. It states that the square of the sine of an angle plus the square of its cosine is always equal to one. Remember, sin²θ means (sin θ)².
