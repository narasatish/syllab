What is Similarity?

  • Shapes that look the same, but can be different sizes.
  • One shape is an enlarged or reduced version of the other.
  • Like zooming in or out on your phone camera!

🎙️ Imagine you take a photo on your phone and then zoom in or out. The picture still shows the same objects, just bigger or smaller. That's the core idea of similarity!

Similar vs. Congruent Shapes

  • Similar: Same shape, different size possible.
  • Congruent: Same shape AND same size.
  • Congruent shapes are always similar, but similar shapes are not always congruent.

🎙️ This is a key distinction. Think of two identical twins – they are congruent. Now think of a parent and child – they are similar, sharing features but different sizes. Congruent means exact match in every way.

Ratio of Areas of Two Triangles - Introduction

  • How do we compare the space two triangles cover?
  • It depends on their base and height.
  • Let's find a formula to compare them!

🎙️ When we talk about the area of a triangle, we know it's half times base times height. What happens if we compare the areas of two different triangles? How do their bases and heights play a role?

Formula for Ratio of Areas

  • The ratio of areas of two triangles is equal to the ratio of the products of their bases and corresponding heights.
  • Area₁/Area₂ = (base₁ × height₁)/(base₂ × height₂)
  • Remember: Area = (1/2) × base × height, the (1/2) cancels out!

🎙️ This formula is fundamental. It tells us that if one triangle has a larger base or a greater height, its area will be proportionally larger. The 1/2 cancels out in the ratio, simplifying things for us.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) - Introduction

  • Also known as Thales Theorem.
  • A powerful tool to find unknown lengths in triangles.
  • It's all about parallel lines inside a triangle!

🎙️ Now, let's move to a very important theorem in similarity: the Basic Proportionality Theorem, or BPT. This theorem helps us understand how a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides.

📏

BPT: The Statement

  • If a line parallel to one side of a triangle intersects the other two sides at distinct points, then the line divides the two sides in the same proportion.
  • In ΔABC, if DE || BC, then AD/DB = AE/EC.

🎙️ This is the formal statement of BPT. Imagine a triangle ABC, and a line DE drawn inside it, parallel to BC. The theorem states that this line DE cuts the other two sides, AB and AC, into segments that are proportional to each other.

Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.
  • It's the reverse of BPT!
  • Useful for proving lines are parallel.

🎙️ Just like many theorems have a converse, BPT does too! The converse of BPT is equally useful. If you can show that a line divides two sides proportionally, then you can conclude that the line must be parallel to the third side.

How to Prove Triangles are Similar?

  • We don't always need to check ALL sides and ALL angles.
  • Just like congruence, there are specific 'tests' for similarity.
  • Let's explore the three main tests!

🎙️ Imagine you have two triangles and you want to know if they are similar. Do you have to measure every single side and angle? Thankfully, no! We have shortcut rules, called tests, that help us prove similarity efficiently.

✔️
Checking Similarity
18 / 30

Test 1: AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) Similarity

  • If the corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the two triangles are similar.
  • If ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, and ∠C = ∠R, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.
  • Often shortened to AA test, as third angle is automatically equal.

🎙️ The AAA test is one of the most intuitive. If all three corresponding angles of two triangles are the same, then the triangles must be similar. They'll have the same shape, just potentially different sizes. Remember, if two angles are equal, the third one automatically is too!

Test 2: SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Similarity

  • If two sides of one triangle are proportional to two sides of another triangle, and their included angles are equal, then the triangles are similar.
  • If AB/PQ = AC/PR and ∠A = ∠P, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.

🎙️ The SAS test is another powerful one. Here, we look for proportionality in two pairs of sides, and equality in the angle between those two sides. If these conditions are met, the triangles are similar.

📏
Proportional Sides & Angle
21 / 30

Test 3: SSS (Side-Side-Side) Similarity

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles are in proportion, then the two triangles are similar.
  • If AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR, then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.
  • All sides are scaled by the same factor.

🎙️ Finally, the SSS test. If all three pairs of corresponding sides have the same ratio (meaning they are proportional), then the triangles are similar. This makes sense: if all dimensions are scaled consistently, the shape remains the same.

Properties of Similar Triangles: Sides & Perimeters

  • The ratio of corresponding sides is constant (the scale factor).
  • The ratio of their perimeters is equal to the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR = Perimeter(ABC)/Perimeter(PQR).

🎙️ Once we know two triangles are similar, we unlock many useful properties. The ratio of their corresponding sides is a constant value, often called the scale factor. Interestingly, their perimeters also share this same ratio!

Properties of Similar Triangles: Areas

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then Area(ABC)/Area(PQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (AC/PR)².

🎙️ This property is crucial. While side lengths and perimeters scale linearly, areas scale by the square of the scale factor. If sides are doubled, the area becomes four times larger! This is a common exam question.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing congruence with similarity.
  • Incorrectly identifying corresponding sides/angles.
  • Forgetting to square the ratio for areas of similar triangles.
  • Applying BPT when lines are not parallel.

🎙️ Students often make these errors. Always double-check if you're dealing with congruence or similarity. Ensure you match the correct corresponding parts and remember the 'square' for area ratios. And BPT only works with parallel lines!
