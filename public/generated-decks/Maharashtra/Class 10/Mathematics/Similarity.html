What is Similarity?

  • Same shape, different size
  • One is an enlargement or reduction of the other
  • Angles are equal, sides are proportional

🎙️ Similarity means that two objects have the exact same shape, but they can be of different sizes. Think of a small photo and its enlarged print. The angles remain the same, but the side lengths change proportionally.

Similarity vs. Congruence

  • Similar: Same shape, different size possible
  • Congruent: Same shape AND same size
  • Congruent figures are always similar, but not vice-versa

🎙️ It's important to distinguish similarity from congruence. Congruent figures are identical in both shape and size, like two identical coins. Similar figures only require the same shape, allowing for size differences.

Ratio of Areas of Two Triangles

  • Area of a triangle = (1/2) × base × height
  • Comparing areas of two triangles
  • Depends on their bases and heights

🎙️ Let's start with a foundational concept: how to compare the areas of two triangles. We know the basic formula for the area of a triangle. The ratio of their areas will be key to many problems in similarity.

Property 1: Ratio of Areas (General)

  • Ratio of areas = (b1 × h1) / (b2 × h2)
  • Product of base and height for each
  • Example: Triangles ABC and PQR

🎙️ The ratio of the areas of two triangles is equal to the ratio of the products of their bases and corresponding heights. This is the most general property and forms the basis for the other two specific cases.

📐📐

Property 2: Triangles with Common Height

  • If heights are equal (h1=h2)
  • Ratio of areas = ratio of bases
  • Area(T1) / Area(T2) = b1 / b2

🎙️ When two triangles share the same height, their area ratio simplifies significantly. It becomes directly proportional to the ratio of their bases. Imagine two triangles standing on the same line, reaching the same peak.

📏
Equal Height

Property 3: Triangles with Common Base

  • If bases are equal (b1=b2)
  • Ratio of areas = ratio of heights
  • Area(T1) / Area(T2) = h1 / h2

🎙️ Similarly, if two triangles share the same base, their area ratio is directly proportional to the ratio of their heights. Think of two triangles sharing the same bottom edge, but one is taller than the other.

🪜

Example 1: Applying Ratio of Areas

  • Triangle ABC: base BC=9, height AD=5
  • Triangle PQR: base QR=10, height PS=6
  • Find Area(ABC) / Area(PQR)

🎙️ Let's work through an example. We have two triangles with given bases and heights. By applying the general property of ratio of areas, we can easily find their ratio. Notice how the 0.5 cancels out.

1Area(ABC) = 0.5*9*5 = 22.5
2Area(PQR) = 0.5*10*6 = 30
3Ratio = 22.5/30 = 3/4

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

  • Also known as Thales Theorem
  • Line parallel to one side of a triangle
  • Divides the other two sides proportionally

🎙️ The Basic Proportionality Theorem, or BPT, is a cornerstone of similarity. It describes what happens when a line parallel to one side of a triangle intersects the other two sides. It states that this line divides the other two sides in the same ratio.

↔️
Parallel Line
10 / 25

Proof of BPT (Simplified)

  • Consider triangle ABC, line DE || BC
  • Connect BE and CD
  • Use ratio of areas of triangles with common vertex/base

🎙️ While a full formal proof involves more steps, the core idea is to use the concept of ratio of areas. By comparing triangles ADE with BDE, and ADE with CDE, and noting that triangles BDE and CDE have equal areas because they share a base and are between parallel lines, we arrive at the proportional relationship.

1Area(ADE)/Area(BDE) = AD/DB
2Area(ADE)/Area(CDE) = AE/EC
3Area(BDE) = Area(CDE) (same base DE, parallel lines)
4Therefore, AD/DB = AE/EC
11 / 25

Converse of BPT

  • If a line divides two sides proportionally
  • Then the line is parallel to the third side
  • The reverse statement of BPT

🎙️ Just as important as BPT is its converse. If you observe that a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, you can conclude that this line must be parallel to the third side. This is very useful for proving lines are parallel.

🔁
Reverse

Example 2: Applying BPT

  • In triangle PQR, ST || QR
  • PS = 3 cm, SQ = 6 cm, PT = 2 cm
  • Find TR

🎙️ Let's apply BPT to solve a problem. If we know the lengths of three segments created by a parallel line, we can easily find the fourth. Remember, the ratio must be consistent on both sides.

1PS/SQ = PT/TR (by BPT)
23/6 = 2/TR
3TR = (6 * 2) / 3
4TR = 4 cm

Tests of Similarity for Triangles

  • How to prove two triangles are similar?
  • Three main tests based on angles and sides
  • Just like congruence tests, but for similarity

🎙️ Just as we have tests for congruence, we also have specific tests to determine if two triangles are similar. These tests provide shortcuts, so we don't have to check all angles and all sides every time.

AA Test of Similarity (Angle-Angle)

  • If two angles of one triangle are equal
  • To two corresponding angles of another triangle
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ The AA test is the simplest. If you can show that two pairs of corresponding angles in two triangles are equal, then the third pair of angles must also be equal, and the triangles are automatically similar. This is a very powerful test.

∠∠
Two Equal Angles
15 / 25

SAS Test of Similarity (Side-Angle-Side)

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to an angle of another triangle
  • And the sides including these angles are proportional
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ The SAS test requires one pair of equal corresponding angles, and the ratio of the sides that include these angles must be equal. It's crucial that the angle is *between* the proportional sides.

📐
Angle Between Sides
16 / 25

SSS Test of Similarity (Side-Side-Side)

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles
  • Are in proportion
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ The SSS test states that if all three pairs of corresponding sides of two triangles are in the same ratio (proportional), then the triangles are similar. You don't need to check any angles if the sides are proportional.

↔️↔️↔️
Three Proportional Sides

Example 3: Identifying Similar Triangles

  • Triangle ABC: AB=3, BC=4, CA=5, Angle B=90°
  • Triangle PQR: PQ=6, QR=8, RP=10, Angle Q=90°
  • Are they similar? Which test?

🎙️ Let's check these two right-angled triangles. We can see that the ratio of corresponding sides is constant (1/2). Therefore, by the SSS test, these triangles are similar. We could also use SAS if we used the right angle.

1AB/PQ = 3/6 = 1/2
2BC/QR = 4/8 = 1/2
3CA/RP = 5/10 = 1/2
4All sides proportional, so SSS Test. Yes, similar.

Properties of Similar Triangles

  • Reflexivity: Every triangle is similar to itself
  • Symmetry: If A ~ B, then B ~ A
  • Transitivity: If A ~ B and B ~ C, then A ~ C

🎙️ Similarity, like congruence, is an equivalence relation. This means it satisfies three fundamental properties: reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity. These properties help us understand how similarity works in a broader context.

Theorem of Areas of Similar Triangles

  • Ratio of areas of two similar triangles
  • Is equal to the ratio of the squares of their corresponding sides
  • Area(T1)/Area(T2) = (s1/s2)^2

🎙️ This is a very important theorem. It connects the areas of similar triangles to the squares of their corresponding sides. If sides are in a ratio of 'k', then their areas are in a ratio of 'k squared'. This makes sense because area is a 2D measurement.

²
Squared Ratio

Example 4: Areas of Similar Triangles

  • Triangle ABC ~ Triangle PQR
  • AB = 2 cm, PQ = 4 cm
  • Area(ABC) = 9 sq cm. Find Area(PQR)

🎙️ Here, we use the theorem of areas of similar triangles. Since the side ratio is 1:2, the area ratio will be 1 squared to 2 squared, which is 1:4. So, if the smaller triangle has an area of 9, the larger one will have an area of 36.

1Area(ABC)/Area(PQR) = (AB/PQ)^2
29/Area(PQR) = (2/4)^2 = (1/2)^2 = 1/4
3Area(PQR) = 9 * 4
4Area(PQR) = 36 sq cm
