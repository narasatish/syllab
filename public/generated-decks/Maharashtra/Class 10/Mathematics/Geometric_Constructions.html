Constructing an Angle Bisector

  • Divides an angle into two equal angles.
  • Each new angle has half the measure of the original.
  • A fundamental construction for symmetry.

🎙️ An angle bisector is a ray that cuts a given angle into two perfectly equal halves. For example, if you bisect a 60-degree angle, you'll get two 30-degree angles. This is a crucial construction for creating symmetrical figures.

Worked Example: Bisecting a 60° Angle

  • Draw an angle ABC of 60°.
  • With B as center, draw an arc intersecting BA and BC.
  • From these intersection points, draw two more arcs meeting at D.
  • Draw ray BD. Ray BD is the angle bisector.

🎙️ Let's put this into practice. First, draw a 60-degree angle using your protractor. Then, follow the steps: use your compass to draw the initial arc, then two more arcs from the intersection points, and finally, draw the ray that bisects the angle.

Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

  • A line that cuts a segment at its midpoint.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle with the segment.
  • Every point on the bisector is equidistant from the segment's endpoints.

🎙️ A perpendicular bisector is a powerful construction that does two things: it cuts a line segment exactly in the middle, and it crosses it at a perfect right angle, or 90 degrees. This line is special because any point on it is equally far from both ends of the segment.

2Connect intersections
3Verify 90°

Worked Example: Perpendicular Bisector of a Line Segment

  • Draw a line segment AB of length 8 cm.
  • With A as center, draw arcs above and below AB (radius > half AB).
  • With B as center, draw arcs intersecting the previous ones.
  • Draw the line connecting the intersection points. This is the perpendicular bisector.

🎙️ Let's construct a perpendicular bisector for an 8 cm line segment. Remember to open your compass to a radius greater than half the segment's length. Draw arcs from both endpoints, then connect where they cross to form the bisector.

Worked Example: Perpendicular from External Point

  • Draw a line 'l' and a point P not on 'l'.
  • With P as center, draw an arc intersecting 'l' at two points A and B.
  • With A and B as centers, draw arcs intersecting below 'l' at Q.
  • Draw line PQ. PQ is perpendicular to 'l'.

🎙️ Let's practice drawing a perpendicular from a point outside the line. First, mark your line and an external point. Then, use your compass from the external point to create two intersection points on the line. These two points are key for the next step.

Worked Example: Copying a 45° Angle

  • Draw a 45° angle ABC.
  • Draw a ray PQ.
  • With B as center, draw an arc intersecting BA and BC.
  • With P as center and same radius, draw an arc intersecting PQ at R.
  • Measure distance between arc intersections on original angle.
  • With R as center and this distance, draw an arc intersecting the previous arc at S.
  • Draw ray PS. Angle SPQ is congruent to ABC.

🎙️ Let's try copying a 45-degree angle. The crucial step is accurately transferring the arc length from the original angle to your new working ray. This ensures that the 'opening' of your new angle is exactly the same as the original.

Dividing a Line Segment in a Given Ratio

  • Splitting a line segment into parts with a specific proportion.
  • Uses the concept of similar triangles or parallel lines.
  • Very useful in scale drawings and geometry problems.

🎙️ This construction allows us to divide a line segment into parts that have a specific ratio, for example, 2:3 or 1:4. It cleverly uses the properties of parallel lines to ensure the segments are divided proportionally. This is like scaling a map or blueprint!

1Draw ray at angle
2Mark equal divisions
3Connect last mark to end
4Draw parallel lines

Worked Example: Divide 7cm in Ratio 2:3

  • Draw line segment AB = 7 cm.
  • Draw a ray AX making an acute angle with AB.
  • Mark 2+3=5 equal points A1, A2, A3, A4, A5 on AX.
  • Join B to A5.
  • Draw a line through A2 parallel to BA5, intersecting AB at P.
  • Point P divides AB in the ratio 2:3.

🎙️ Let's divide a 7 cm line segment in the ratio 2:3. This means we need a total of 5 equal parts along our helper ray AX. Remember, the key is accurately drawing the parallel line from the correct division point to ensure the main segment is divided proportionally.

Constructing a Triangle Similar to a Given Triangle

  • Creating a triangle with the same shape but different size.
  • Corresponding angles are equal, corresponding sides are proportional.
  • Uses a specific scale factor (m/n).

🎙️ Similar triangles have the exact same shape but can be different sizes – one is an enlargement or reduction of the other. This construction allows you to create a new triangle that maintains all the angles of the original but has sides scaled by a specific factor.

1Draw given triangle
2Draw ray for divisions
3Mark divisions (m+n)
4Connect and draw parallel
