The Famous Pythagoras Theorem

  • Applies ONLY to right-angled triangles.
  • Statement: 'The square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides'.
  • Formula: a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse).

🎙️ Here it is, the core of our lesson! The Pythagoras Theorem. It's crucial to remember that this theorem is exclusive to right-angled triangles. The formula a² + b² = c² is your key to solving many problems involving these triangles.

How to Apply the Theorem

  • Step 1: Identify the hypotenuse (c) and the legs (a, b).
  • Step 2: Write down the formula: a² + b² = c².
  • Step 3: Substitute the known values into the formula.
  • Step 4: Solve the equation to find the unknown side.

🎙️ Applying the theorem is straightforward if you follow these steps. Always start by correctly identifying which side is the hypotenuse and which are the legs. This prevents common errors in setting up the equation.

2Write formula
3Substitute values
4Solve for unknown

Example 1: Finding the Hypotenuse

  • Given legs: a = 3 units, b = 4 units.
  • We need to find the hypotenuse 'c'.
  • Using a² + b² = c², we get 3² + 4² = c².
  • Calculation: 9 + 16 = c² => 25 = c² => c = 5 units.

🎙️ Let's work through a classic example. If the two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 3 and 4 units, what is the length of its hypotenuse? By substituting these values into our formula, we quickly find the answer to be 5 units.

1a=3, b=4
23²+4²=c²
39+16=c²
425=c², c=5

Example 2: Finding a Missing Leg

  • Given hypotenuse: c = 13 units, one leg: a = 5 units.
  • We need to find the other leg 'b'.
  • Using a² + b² = c², we get 5² + b² = 13².
  • Calculation: 25 + b² = 169 => b² = 144 => b = 12 units.

🎙️ Sometimes, you might know the hypotenuse and one leg, and need to find the other leg. The process is similar: plug in the known values and solve for the unknown. Here, with a hypotenuse of 13 and one leg of 5, the other leg is 12 units.

1a=5, c=13
25²+b²=13²
325+b²=169
4b²=144, b=12

What are Pythagorean Triplets?

  • A set of three positive integers (a, b, c).
  • They satisfy the Pythagoras Theorem: a² + b² = c².
  • These integers represent the sides of a right-angled triangle.

🎙️ Pythagorean Triplets are special sets of whole numbers that perfectly fit the theorem. They are like 'pre-calculated' sides of right-angled triangles, making some problems quicker to solve. The most famous one is (3, 4, 5).

🔢
(a, b, c)

Exploring Common Triplets

  • The most basic triplet: (3, 4, 5).
  • Other common triplets: (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17), (7, 24, 25).
  • Any multiple of a Pythagorean triplet is also a triplet (e.g., (6, 8, 10)).

🎙️ It's good to recognize some common Pythagorean Triplets. Knowing them can save you time in calculations. Also, remember that if you multiply each number in a triplet by the same integer, you get another valid Pythagorean Triplet!

Example 3: Is it a Triplet?

  • Given numbers: 6, 8, 10.
  • Assume the longest side (10) is 'c'.
  • Check if 6² + 8² = 10².
  • 36 + 64 = 100. Since 100 = 100, YES, it's a triplet!

🎙️ Let's test if a set of numbers forms a Pythagorean Triplet. Always square the two smaller numbers and add them. Then, square the largest number. If these results are equal, then congratulations, you've found a triplet!

The Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

  • It's the reverse logic of the original theorem.
  • Statement: 'If in a triangle, the square of one side is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.'
  • Helps us identify if a triangle is right-angled.

🎙️ The Converse of Pythagoras Theorem is equally important. While the original theorem tells us a property of right-angled triangles, the converse tells us how to identify if a triangle *is* right-angled, just by knowing its side lengths.

Using the Converse to Identify Right Angles

  • Step 1: Identify the longest side of the triangle (potential hypotenuse).
  • Step 2: Square all three side lengths.
  • Step 3: Check if the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • Step 4: If they are equal, the angle opposite the longest side is 90 degrees.

🎙️ This is how you apply the converse. It's a powerful tool for verifying if a given triangle is a right-angled triangle, without needing to measure any angles directly. Just compare the squares of its sides!

1Identify longest side
2Square all sides
3Check if a²+b²=c²
4If yes, it's right-angled

Example 4: Is this a Right Triangle?

  • Given sides: 7 cm, 24 cm, 25 cm.
  • Longest side is 25 cm (potential hypotenuse).
  • Check: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625.
  • Compare with 25² = 625. Since 625 = 625, YES, it's a right triangle!

🎙️ Let's apply the converse. If a triangle has sides 7, 24, and 25 cm, is it a right-angled triangle? By performing the calculations, we find that the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides equals the square of the longest side, confirming it is indeed a right triangle.

1Sides: 7, 24, 25
27²+24² = 49+576 = 625
325² = 625
4Since 625=625, Yes!

Practical Use: Checking for Square Corners

  • Carpenters and builders need perfectly square (90°) corners.
  • They use the 3-4-5 rule (a Pythagorean Triplet).
  • Measure 3 units along one side, 4 units along the other.
  • If the diagonal distance between these points is 5 units, the corner is square.

🎙️ This is a fantastic real-world application! When carpenters build, they need to make sure walls meet at a perfect 90-degree angle. They use the 3-4-5 rule, which is directly derived from the converse of Pythagoras, to quickly and accurately check if their corners are 'square'.
