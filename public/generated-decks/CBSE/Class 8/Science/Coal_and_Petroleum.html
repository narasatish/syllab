  • Used without much modification.
  • Examples: air, water, soil, minerals.

  • Present in unlimited quantity.
  • Not affected by human activities.
  • Examples: sunlight, air.

🎙️ Some natural resources are so abundant that they won't run out, even if we use them a lot. The sun provides energy every day, and we have plenty of air around us.

  • Limited quantity on Earth.

The Story of Fossil Fuels

  • Formed from dead organisms.
  • Buried deep underground for millions of years.
  • Coal, petroleum, and natural gas are fossil fuels.

🎙️ Fossil fuels are fascinating! They are essentially the remains of ancient plants and animals, transformed over millions of years by heat and pressure deep within the Earth. They are our primary energy source today.

Ancient Remains

How Coal is Formed: Carbonization

  • Dense forests buried under soil.
  • High pressure and temperature.
  • Slow conversion of dead vegetation into coal.

🎙️ Imagine huge ancient forests. When these plants died, they sank into swamps and were covered by layers of soil. Over millions of years, under immense heat and pressure, they slowly turned into coal. This process is called carbonization.

Coal: A Black Gold

  • Hard, black combustible mineral.
  • Main use is electricity generation.
  • Also used as fuel in industries and homes.

🎙️ Coal is a hard, black substance, often called 'black gold' due to its value. Its primary use is in thermal power plants to produce electricity. Many industries also rely on coal as a fuel source.

Coke: A Pure Form of Carbon

  • Tough, porous, black substance.
  • Almost pure form of carbon.
  • Used in steel manufacturing.

🎙️ When coal is heated strongly in the absence of air, we get coke. It's a very important product, mostly pure carbon, and essential for making steel and extracting many metals.

Coal Tar: A Black, Thick Liquid

  • Thick, black liquid with unpleasant smell.
  • Mixture of about 200 substances.
  • Used for making dyes, drugs, plastics, paints, explosives.

🎙️ Coal tar is a fascinating byproduct. It's a black, gooey liquid with a strong smell, but it's a treasure trove of chemicals. From this one substance, we get materials to make everything from medicines to paints and plastics.

Coal Gas: A Gaseous Fuel

  • Obtained during processing of coal to get coke.
  • Used as fuel in many industries.
  • First used for street lighting in London.

🎙️ Coal gas is released during the production of coke. It's a valuable gaseous fuel used in many factories. Historically, it was even used to light up street lamps, like in London back in the day.

Petroleum: Formed from Sea Organisms

  • Formed from dead marine organisms.
  • Sank to the bottom of the sea.
  • Covered by layers of sand and clay.

🎙️ Unlike coal, petroleum was formed from tiny marine plants and animals. When they died, their bodies settled at the bottom of the ocean and were covered by sediments. Over millions of years, under immense heat and pressure, they transformed into crude oil.

Where is Petroleum Found?

  • Trapped between layers of non-porous rocks.
  • Mixed with natural gas.
  • Drilled out from oil wells.

🎙️ Petroleum, also known as crude oil, is found deep beneath the Earth's surface, often under the seabed. It's usually trapped in reservoirs beneath layers of rock, often alongside natural gas. We extract it by drilling oil wells.

🛢️
Oil Well

Refining Petroleum: The Separation Process

  • Crude petroleum is a dark, oily liquid.
  • Separated into different components.
  • Process called refining.

🎙️ Crude petroleum, as it comes out of the ground, is not directly usable. It's a complex mixture. We separate it into its useful components like petrol, diesel, and kerosene through a process called refining, usually in an oil refinery.

⚗️
Distillation

  • Cleaner burning fuel.

🎙️ LPG, or Liquefied Petroleum Gas, is a common sight in our kitchens. It's stored under pressure in cylinders and is an efficient and relatively clean-burning fuel for cooking and heating our homes.

  • Used for power generation, vehicles.
  • Less polluting than petrol/diesel.

🎙️ Natural gas is often found with petroleum. It's a much cleaner fuel, and when compressed, it's called CNG, or Compressed Natural Gas. CNG is increasingly used in vehicles and for electricity generation due to its lower pollution levels.

Why Conserve Fossil Fuels?

  • Limited supply, exhaustible resources.
  • Formation takes millions of years.
  • Burning causes air pollution.

🎙️ Despite their importance, we must remember that fossil fuels are exhaustible. Their formation takes millions of years, far longer than we can replace them. Also, burning them releases pollutants into the atmosphere, causing environmental problems.

♻️
Save Resources

How Can We Conserve?

  • Use public transport or cycle.
  • Turn off lights/fans when not needed.
  • Use energy-efficient appliances.
  • Use renewable energy sources.

🎙️ There are many ways we can contribute to conservation. Simple actions like switching off lights, using public transport, or opting for energy-efficient devices can make a big difference. Promoting renewable energy is also key.

Chapter Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Natural resources are exhaustible or inexhaustible.
  • Fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) are exhaustible.
  • Formed over millions of years from dead organisms.
  • Essential for energy but need conservation.

🎙️ So, we've learned that resources are either endless or limited. Fossil fuels are our main energy source, but they're limited and take ages to form. It's crucial that we use them wisely and explore cleaner alternatives.
