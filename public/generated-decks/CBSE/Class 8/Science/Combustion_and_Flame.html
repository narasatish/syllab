What is Combustion?

  • A chemical process where a substance reacts with oxygen.
  • It produces heat and often light.
  • We call the burning substance a 'combustible' substance.

🎙️ Formally, combustion is a chemical reaction. It's when a substance rapidly combines with oxygen from the air, releasing energy primarily as heat and sometimes light. The material that burns is known as a combustible substance.

🎙️ You can hold a piece of paper or wood without it catching fire, even though oxygen is present. This is because they haven't reached their 'ignition temperature'. Think of it as the minimum heat required to start the burning process.

Ignition Temperature Defined

  • The lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire.
  • Different substances have different ignition temperatures.
  • A matchstick needs friction to reach its ignition temperature.

🎙️ So, ignition temperature is the crucial minimum temperature a combustible substance must reach before it can begin to burn. Until this temperature is met, even with fuel and oxygen, combustion will not occur.

Minimum Temp to Burn

🎙️ The outermost part of the flame is where the burning substance gets the most oxygen. This allows for complete combustion, making it the hottest zone, and it typically glows with a faint blue colour.

Middle Zone: Luminous and Yellow

  • The middle part of the flame, where partial combustion occurs.
  • Contains unburnt carbon particles that glow, making it luminous.
  • This is the bright yellow, moderately hot part.

🎙️ Just inside the outer zone is the middle zone. Here, there isn't enough oxygen for complete combustion, so some carbon particles remain unburnt. These particles glow intensely, giving the flame its characteristic bright yellow, luminous appearance.

Characteristics of a Good Fuel

  • High calorific value (produces lots of heat).
  • Moderate ignition temperature (easy to ignite, safe).
  • Burns without leaving much ash or harmful gases.
  • Cheap, readily available, and easy to store/transport.

🎙️ An ideal fuel should have several qualities: it should provide a lot of energy, be easy to start burning but not too quickly, burn cleanly, and be affordable and accessible for everyone.

Calorific Value: Fuel's Energy Power

  • The amount of heat energy produced on complete combustion of 1 kg of a fuel.
  • Measured in kilojoules per kilogram (kJ/kg).
  • Higher calorific value means more energy from less fuel.

🎙️ Calorific value is a measure of a fuel's efficiency. It tells us how much heat energy we can get from burning a specific amount of that fuel. Fuels with higher calorific values are generally preferred because they provide more energy.

Controlling Fire: The Fire Triangle in Reverse

  • To extinguish a fire, we must remove one or more conditions of the fire triangle.
  • 1. Remove the fuel. 2. Cut off the air supply. 3. Lower the temperature.
  • Different fires require different methods.

🎙️ Knowing the conditions for combustion helps us control fires. By removing the fuel, cutting off oxygen, or cooling the burning material below its ignition temperature, we can effectively put out a fire. Each method targets one side of the fire triangle.

2Cut Off Air

Fire Extinguishers: Fighting Flames

  • Water is common for wood/paper fires (cools, cuts oxygen).
  • CO2 extinguishers are best for electrical or oil fires (smothers, doesn't conduct electricity).
  • Sand can also cut off oxygen for small fires.

🎙️ Fire extinguishers are designed to quickly put out fires. Water works well for ordinary combustible materials by cooling them and creating steam to block oxygen. For electrical or oil fires, carbon dioxide extinguishers are used as CO2 is heavier than air and non-conductive.

Common Mistakes in Combustion and Flame

  • Confusing ignition temperature with melting point.
  • Assuming all burning produces a visible flame.
  • Thinking water is suitable for ALL types of fires.

🎙️ Students often confuse ignition temperature with melting point; remember, ignition is about starting to burn, not melting. Also, not all combustion produces a flame, like charcoal burning. And critically, water should never be used on electrical or oil fires due to safety hazards.
