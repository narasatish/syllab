What are Microorganisms?

  • Tiny living organisms, invisible to the naked eye.
  • Can only be seen with a microscope.
  • Found almost everywhere on Earth.

🎙️ Microorganisms, also known as microbes, are incredibly small creatures. We need a special tool called a microscope to observe them, as they are too tiny to be seen with just our eyes.

Types of Microorganisms: Bacteria

  • Single-celled organisms with a simple structure.
  • Can be rod-shaped, spherical, or spiral.
  • Example: Lactobacillus (found in curd).

🎙️ Bacteria are among the most common types of microbes. They are single-celled and come in various shapes. Lactobacillus, for instance, is a bacterium essential for making curd from milk.

Types of Microorganisms: Fungi

  • Mostly multicellular organisms, but some are unicellular (like yeast).
  • Grow on dead and decaying matter.
  • Example: Yeast (used in bread making), Penicillium (source of antibiotic).

🎙️ Fungi are another diverse group. While many are multicellular like mushrooms, yeast is a single-celled fungus crucial for baking. They play a vital role in decomposition.

🍄
Fungi

Types of Microorganisms: Protozoa

  • Single-celled animals, often found in water.
  • Some can move using pseudopods, flagella, or cilia.
  • Example: Amoeba, Plasmodium (causes malaria).

🎙️ Protozoa are fascinating single-celled organisms that resemble tiny animals. Many live in water and can move around. Plasmodium, a protozoan, is unfortunately responsible for causing malaria.

🌊
Water Dwellers

Types of Microorganisms: Algae

  • Plant-like organisms, mostly aquatic.
  • Can be unicellular or multicellular.
  • Perform photosynthesis, producing oxygen.
  • Example: Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas.

🎙️ Algae are unique microbes because, like plants, they can perform photosynthesis. They are mostly found in water and are crucial for producing oxygen in our environment. Think of the green scum you sometimes see in ponds.

🌿

Types of Microorganisms: Viruses

  • Are different from other microorganisms.
  • Reproduce only inside the host organism's cells.
  • Cause diseases like common cold, flu, polio, COVID-19.

🎙️ Viruses are a special case; they are considered to be on the border of living and non-living. They can only multiply by invading the cells of other living organisms. Many common diseases, including the flu, are caused by viruses.

Friendly Microorganisms: Curd & Bread Making

  • Lactobacillus bacteria converts milk into curd.
  • Yeast ferments sugar in dough, producing CO2.
  • CO2 bubbles make bread fluffy and soft.

🎙️ Microbes are incredibly useful in our kitchens! Lactobacillus bacteria are the unsung heroes behind delicious curd, while yeast makes our bread light and airy by releasing carbon dioxide gas during fermentation.

Friendly Microorganisms: Alcohol Production

  • Yeast is used for commercial production of alcohol and wine.
  • It converts natural sugars in grains/fruits into alcohol and CO2.
  • This process is called fermentation.

🎙️ Beyond baking, yeast also plays a crucial role in the production of alcoholic beverages. Louis Pasteur discovered this process, called fermentation, where yeast converts sugars into alcohol. This is a big industry!

Fermentation

Friendly Microorganisms: Medicinal Use - Antibiotics

  • Medicines that stop or kill the growth of disease-causing microbes.
  • Produced from specific bacteria and fungi.
  • Example: Penicillin, discovered by Alexander Fleming from a fungus.

🎙️ Microbes are lifesavers! Antibiotics are powerful medicines derived from certain bacteria and fungi. They target harmful microbes in our bodies without damaging our own cells, helping us recover from many bacterial infections.

💊
Medicine
13 / 25

Friendly Microorganisms: Medicinal Use - Vaccines

  • Weakened or dead disease-causing microbes are introduced into the body.
  • The body produces antibodies to fight the disease.
  • These antibodies remain, providing lifelong protection.

🎙️ Vaccines are another incredible medical advancement involving microbes. By introducing a harmless form of a pathogen, vaccines train our immune system to recognize and fight off future infections, keeping us safe from many diseases.

💉
Protection

Friendly Microorganisms: Environmental Clean-up

  • Decompose organic waste and dead plants/animals.
  • Break down complex substances into simpler ones.
  • Help keep the environment clean and recycle nutrients.

🎙️ Microbes are nature's clean-up crew! They act as decomposers, breaking down dead organic matter and waste. This process returns essential nutrients to the soil, making it fertile and keeping our environment healthy.

Friendly Microorganisms: Nitrogen Fixation

  • Certain bacteria (e.g., Rhizobium) convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable forms.
  • Essential for plant growth and soil fertility.
  • Found in the root nodules of leguminous plants.

🎙️ Nitrogen is vital for plants, but they can't use it directly from the air. This is where friendly bacteria like Rhizobium come in! They 'fix' atmospheric nitrogen into a form plants can absorb, enriching the soil.

Harmful Microorganisms: Disease-causing (Pathogens)

  • Microbes that cause diseases are called pathogens.
  • Can enter our body through air, water, food, or contact.
  • Disease can spread from an infected person to a healthy one (communicable diseases).

🎙️ Unfortunately, not all microorganisms are our friends. Some are harmful and cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants. These disease-causing microbes are known as pathogens, and they can spread easily.

🤒

Harmful Microorganisms: Human Diseases

  • Bacterial: Tuberculosis (TB), Cholera, Typhoid.
  • Viral: Common Cold, Flu, Polio, Measles.
  • Protozoal: Malaria, Dysentery.
  • Fungal: Ringworm, Athlete's foot.

🎙️ Many common and serious human diseases are caused by different types of pathogens. It's important to understand which type of microbe causes which disease, as this helps in choosing the right treatment.

Harmful Microorganisms: Plant Diseases

Food Preservation: Why is it important?

  • Microorganisms can spoil food, making it unfit for consumption.
  • Spoilage causes bad smell, bad taste, and discoloration.
  • Preservation prevents microbial growth, extending food shelf life.

🎙️ Have you ever noticed food going bad? That's the work of microorganisms! Food preservation is vital to stop these microbes from spoiling our food, ensuring it stays safe and edible for longer.

Food Preservation Methods

  • Chemical Method: Using salt or sugar (e.g., pickles, jams).
  • Oil and Vinegar: Prevents bacterial growth (e.g., pickles).
  • Heat and Cold Treatments: Pasteurization, refrigeration.
  • Storage: Airtight containers to prevent microbial access.

🎙️ There are many ways we can preserve food at home and commercially. From adding salt to making pickles to pasteurizing milk, these methods are designed to inhibit or kill the microbes that cause spoilage.

Microorganisms: Friend or Foe?

  • Friends: Curd, bread, alcohol, antibiotics, vaccines, decomposition, nitrogen fixation.
  • Foes: Cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants, spoil food.
  • Understanding them helps us harness benefits and prevent harm.

🎙️ So, are microorganisms friends or foes? The answer is both! They are indispensable for many processes vital to life and industry, yet some can cause severe harm. It's about recognizing their roles.
