How to Test Liquid Conductivity

  • Build a simple circuit with a battery, bulb, and wires.
  • Dip the free ends of the wires into the liquid.

🎙️ How do we find out if a liquid conducts electricity? We can set up a simple tester. Connect a battery, a bulb, and two wires. If the bulb lights up when the wires are in the liquid, it means the liquid conducts electricity.

Common Liquid Insulators

  • Distilled Water: Pure water, no ions.
  • Cooking Oil: Organic compounds, no free ions.
  • Sugar Solution: Sugar dissolves but doesn't form ions.

🎙️ On the other hand, some liquids do not conduct electricity. Distilled water is a classic example as it lacks impurities. Cooking oil and sugar solutions also do not conduct because they don't have charged particles that can move freely.

How Liquids Conduct Electricity

  • Liquids conduct due to the movement of ions.
  • Ions are charged particles (atoms or molecules).
  • These ions carry the electric current through the liquid.

🎙️ Unlike solids where electrons carry current, in liquids, it's the ions that do the job. When salts or acids dissolve in water, they break into positively and negatively charged ions. These ions then move towards the opposite electrodes, carrying the charge.

Introducing Electrolysis

  • The process of chemical decomposition of an electrolyte.
  • Caused by passing electric current through it.
  • Results in new substances being formed.

🎙️ When electric current causes a chemical change in a liquid, especially leading to its breakdown, we call this process electrolysis. It literally means 'breaking down with electricity'. It's a fundamental concept for many industrial applications.

⚡
Electric Breakdown

The Role of Electrodes

  • Metallic rods or plates dipped into the liquid.
  • Anode: Positive electrode, connected to battery's positive terminal.
  • Cathode: Negative electrode, connected to battery's negative terminal.

🎙️ For electrolysis to happen, we need electrodes. These are usually metal rods or plates that conduct electricity into and out of the liquid. The anode is the positive electrode, and the cathode is the negative electrode, attracting oppositely charged ions.

Anode (+)
Cathode (-)

Understanding the Electrolyte

  • The conducting liquid undergoing electrolysis.
  • Contains free ions that can move.
  • Examples: Acidified water, molten salts, salt solutions.

🎙️ The liquid itself, which conducts electricity and undergoes chemical change, is called the electrolyte. It must contain free-moving ions. Without an electrolyte, no chemical effect of electric current can occur.

💧
Conducting Liquid

What Happens at the Electrodes?

  • Positive ions move towards the cathode (negative electrode).
  • Negative ions move towards the anode (positive electrode).
  • Chemical reactions occur at the surface of the electrodes.

🎙️ At the electrodes, the ions lose or gain electrons, leading to new substances forming. For example, positive ions might gain electrons at the cathode, forming neutral atoms, while negative ions might lose electrons at the anode.

Electrolysis of Water

  • Acidified water (electrolyte).
  • Produces Hydrogen gas at the cathode.
  • Produces Oxygen gas at the anode.

🎙️ A classic example is the electrolysis of water. By adding a little acid, we make water conductive. When current passes, water breaks down into hydrogen gas at the negative electrode and oxygen gas at the positive electrode, showing a clear chemical change.

Electroplating - A Useful Effect

  • Depositing a thin layer of one metal onto another.
  • Uses the chemical effects of electric current.
  • Enhances appearance, prevents corrosion.

🎙️ One of the most important applications of chemical effects is electroplating. This process allows us to coat one metal with a thin layer of another metal, which is incredibly useful for protection, decoration, and improving durability.

The Electroplating Process

  • Object to be plated acts as the cathode.
  • Metal to be deposited acts as the anode.
  • Electrolyte contains ions of the coating metal.

🎙️ In electroplating, the object you want to coat is made the cathode. The metal you want to deposit forms the anode, and the electrolyte contains ions of that coating metal. When current flows, the metal ions from the electrolyte deposit onto the cathode.

Why Electroplate?

  • Corrosion Protection: Prevents rusting (e.g., zinc plating).
  • Enhanced Appearance: Makes objects shiny and attractive.
  • Improved Hardness: Increases surface durability.

🎙️ Electroplating offers several advantages. It protects metals from corrosion, making them last longer. It also makes objects look more appealing, and in some cases, it can even make the surface harder and more resistant to wear and tear.

Metal Purification

  • Crude metals can be purified using electrolysis.
  • Impure metal acts as anode, pure metal as cathode.
  • Example: Refining of copper.

🎙️ Electrolysis is also crucial for purifying metals. For example, in copper refining, impure copper is made the anode, and a thin strip of pure copper is the cathode. When current passes, pure copper deposits on the cathode, leaving impurities behind.

Producing Chemicals

  • Many industrial chemicals are produced via electrolysis.
  • Example: Caustic soda (NaOH) from brine (saltwater).
  • Chlorine gas and Hydrogen gas are also by-products.

🎙️ Beyond metals, electrolysis is vital for producing many important chemical compounds. For instance, caustic soda, a key chemical in soap and paper industries, is made by electrolyzing saltwater. This process also yields chlorine and hydrogen gas.
