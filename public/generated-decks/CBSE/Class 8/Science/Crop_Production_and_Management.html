What are Crops?

  • Plants grown on a large scale.
  • For food, fiber, or other products.
  • Requires specific conditions.

🎙️ When we talk about crops, we mean plants of the same kind that are cultivated in a big area. For example, a field full of wheat plants is a wheat crop. Humans grow these for various uses, primarily for food.

🌱
Plants for Purpose
4 / 25

Types of Crops: Kharif Crops

  • Sown in the rainy season (June-July).
  • Harvested in September-October.
  • Require plenty of water.

🎙️ Kharif crops are like our monsoon friends. They are sown when the rains begin and are harvested after the rainy season. Examples include paddy (rice), maize, soybean, groundnut, and cotton. They thrive with good rainfall.

Rainy Season Crops

Types of Crops: Rabi Crops

  • Sown in winter season (Oct-Nov).
  • Harvested in March-April.
  • Require less water, cool climate.

🎙️ Rabi crops are grown during the colder months. They are sown in autumn and harvested in spring. Wheat, gram, pea, mustard, and linseed are common examples. They don't need as much water as Kharif crops.

1. Preparation of Soil

  • First step before sowing.
  • Loosens and turns the soil.
  • Allows roots to penetrate deeply.

🎙️ Preparing the soil is crucial. It involves loosening and turning the soil, which helps in aeration and allows the roots to breathe easily. This process also brings nutrient-rich soil to the top.

🚜
Tractor Ploughing
8 / 25

Real-life Example: Ploughing

  • Process of loosening and turning soil.
  • Done using a plough.
  • Helps mix manure, remove weeds.

🎙️ Ploughing, or tilling, is commonly done with a plough, traditionally pulled by bullocks or now by tractors. This action creates small spaces in the soil for air and water, essential for seed germination and root growth. It also helps in uprooting weeds.

2. Sowing

  • Placing seeds in the soil.
  • Crucial step for crop yield.
  • Requires good quality seeds.

🎙️ Once the soil is prepared, the next step is sowing the seeds. It's essential to use healthy, good quality seeds to ensure a healthy crop. Damaged or diseased seeds will not germinate properly.

🌰
Planting Seeds
10 / 25

Traditional vs. Modern Sowing Methods

  • Traditional: Funnel-shaped tool.
  • Modern: Seed drill.
  • Seed drill saves time and labour.

🎙️ Traditionally, seeds were sown manually or using a simple funnel-shaped tool. Modern agriculture uses seed drills, which are attached to tractors. These sow seeds uniformly at proper distances and depths, ensuring better germination and saving a lot of time and effort.

3. Adding Manure and Fertilizers

  • Substances added to soil.
  • To replenish soil nutrients.
  • Essential for healthy plant growth.

🎙️ Just like we need vitamins, plants need nutrients. Manure and fertilizers are added to the soil to enhance its fertility and provide essential nutrients for the plants to grow strong and healthy. They act as food for the crops.

🧪
Nutrient Boost
12 / 25

Manure vs. Fertilizers

  • Manure: Natural, organic.
  • Fertilizers: Man-made chemicals.
  • Fertilizers are nutrient-specific.

🎙️ Manure is derived from decomposed plant and animal waste, making it organic and enriching the soil with humus. Fertilizers are chemical compounds, rich in specific nutrients like nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. While fertilizers give quick results, manure improves soil texture in the long run.

4. Irrigation

  • Supplying water to crops.
  • At regular intervals.
  • Crucial for proper growth.

🎙️ Water is essential for all living beings, and crops are no exception. Irrigation is the process of providing water to crops at various stages of their growth. Without adequate water, crops cannot absorb nutrients from the soil or perform photosynthesis.

💧
Water for Life
15 / 25

Traditional Methods of Irrigation

  • Moat (pulley system).
  • Chain pump.
  • Dhekli, Rahat (lever system).

🎙️ For centuries, farmers have used simple, traditional methods to irrigate their fields. These often involve manual labour or animal power to lift water from wells or canals. Examples include the moat, chain pump, dhekli, and rahat, which are still used in some regions.

🛶
Old Ways
16 / 25

Modern Methods of Irrigation

  • Sprinkler system.
  • Drip system.
  • Conserve water, efficient.

🎙️ Modern irrigation methods are designed to conserve water and ensure efficient distribution. The sprinkler system is great for uneven land, while the drip system delivers water directly to the plant roots, minimizing waste. These methods are a boon in areas with water scarcity.

🚿
Smart Watering

5. Protection from Weeds

  • Unwanted plants in a field.
  • Compete with crops for resources.
  • Reduce crop yield significantly.

🎙️ Imagine trying to grow your favorite plant, but other wild plants keep popping up and stealing its food and water. These are weeds! They compete with the main crop for sunlight, water, nutrients, and space, harming the crop's growth.

🌿
Uninvited Guests

Weeding Methods

  • Manual removal (khurpi).
  • Using weedicides (chemicals).
  • Tilling before sowing helps.

🎙️ Removing weeds, called weeding, is essential. It can be done manually by uprooting them or by using a khurpi. Farmers also use chemical substances called weedicides, which kill the weeds without harming the crop. Tilling the soil before sowing also helps in uprooting weeds.

6. Harvesting

  • Cutting of mature crops.
  • Done manually (sickle) or by machines.
  • Marks the end of crop growth.

🎙️ Harvesting is one of the most satisfying stages for a farmer – it's when the crop is finally mature and ready to be cut and gathered. This can be done with a sickle by hand or with large machines called harvesters, especially for large fields.

🌾

Threshing and Winnowing

  • Threshing: Separating grain from chaff.
  • Winnowing: Separating grain from lighter chaff.
  • Often done by combine harvesters.

🎙️ After harvesting, the grain still needs to be separated from the straw (chaff). Threshing is the process of beating out the grain from the harvested crop. Winnowing then uses wind to separate the lighter chaff from the heavier grain. Modern combine harvesters do both simultaneously.

Separating the Good

7. Storage

  • Protecting harvested grain.
  • From moisture, pests, microorganisms.
  • Ensures food availability year-round.

🎙️ Proper storage is critical. If harvested grains are not stored correctly, they can be spoiled by moisture, insects, rats, or microorganisms. Farmers store grains in jute bags or metallic bins, while large-scale storage happens in silos and granaries.

📦
Safe Keeping

Food from Animals (Animal Husbandry)

  • Provides milk, meat, eggs, honey.
  • Rearing animals on a large scale.
  • Proper food, shelter, care needed.

🎙️ While crops give us plant-based food, many people also rely on animal products. Animal husbandry is the practice of rearing and caring for farm animals to obtain food products like milk, eggs, and meat. It requires providing proper food, shelter, and health care to the animals.
