How Do We See Things?

  • Light from an object enters our eyes.
  • This light could be emitted by the object itself.
  • Or, reflected by the object from a light source.

🎙️ We don't 'see' objects directly with our eyes. Instead, light from the object enters our eyes, and our brain processes this information. It's like a messenger carrying information about the object.

Key Terms in Reflection

  • Incident Ray: Light ray falling on the surface.
  • Reflected Ray: Light ray bouncing back.
  • Normal: Perpendicular line to the surface at the point of incidence.

🎙️ To understand reflection better, we use specific terms. The light coming towards the mirror is the incident ray, and the light leaving is the reflected ray. The normal is an imaginary line that helps us measure angles.

Laws of Reflection: Angle Equality

  • The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection.
  • Angle i = Angle r.

🎙️ This is a very important law! Imagine the normal line; the angle between the incident ray and the normal is the angle of incidence. The angle between the reflected ray and the normal is the angle of reflection. These two angles are always equal.

Laws of Reflection: Plane of Incidence

  • All lie in the same plane.

Types of Reflection: Regular

  • Occurs from smooth, polished surfaces.
  • Parallel incident rays reflect as parallel rays.
  • Forms clear images (e.g., mirrors, still water).

🎙️ When light hits a perfectly smooth surface, all the reflected rays go in one direction, staying parallel. This is why you see a clear, sharp image in a mirror or in very still water.

✨
Smooth Surface
12 / 25

Types of Reflection: Diffused

  • Occurs from rough, irregular surfaces.
  • Parallel incident rays reflect in different directions.
  • No clear image formed (e.g., walls, paper).

🎙️ Most objects around us have rough surfaces. When light hits these, it scatters in many directions. This diffused reflection is actually why we can see non-luminous objects from all angles, but it doesn't form a clear image.

Image Formation by a Plane Mirror

  • Forms an image behind the mirror.
  • Image appears to be as far behind as the object is in front.

🎙️ When you look into a plane mirror, you see an image of yourself. This image isn't really 'there' on the mirror surface; it appears to be located behind the mirror.

Characteristics of Image by Plane Mirror

  • Virtual: Cannot be obtained on a screen.
  • Erect: Upright, not inverted.
  • Same size as the object.
  • Laterally inverted.

🎙️ The image formed by a plane mirror has several unique characteristics. It's virtual because light rays only *appear* to come from it, and it's always upright. It also has the same size as the object.

🎙️ This is a fun one! When you raise your right hand in front of a mirror, your image appears to raise its left hand. This swapping of left and right is called lateral inversion.

Dispersion of Light

  • The splitting of white light into its constituent colours.
  • Seven colours: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (VIBGYOR).
  • Different colours bend at different angles.

🎙️ White light, like sunlight, isn't just one colour. It's a mixture of seven colours! When white light passes through a prism or water droplets, it splits into these individual colours. This is dispersion.

The Human Eye: Lens, Retina & Working

  • Lens: Focuses light onto the retina.
  • Retina: Light-sensitive screen at the back of the eye.
  • Optic Nerve: Carries signals to the brain.

🎙️ After the pupil, the lens focuses light to form an image on the retina, which is like a screen. The retina has light-sensitive cells that send signals to the brain via the optic nerve, allowing us to 'see'.

Caring for Your Eyes

  • Avoid reading in very dim or very bright light.
  • Do not rub your eyes if something enters them.
  • Eat a balanced diet rich in Vitamin A.

🎙️ Our eyes are precious, so we must take good care of them. Always read in adequate light, and if something gets in your eye, wash it gently with clean water, don't rub it. A healthy diet also helps!
