Recap: What is a Fraction?

  • A part of a whole.
  • Represented as Numerator/Denominator.
  • Denominator shows total parts, Numerator shows chosen parts.

🎙️ Remember, a fraction tells us how much of something we have. If you have a pizza cut into 8 slices and you eat 4, you've eaten 4/8 of the pizza.

Recap: Types of Fractions

  • Proper: Numerator < Denominator (e.g., 1/2)
  • Improper: Numerator ≥ Denominator (e.g., 5/3)
  • Mixed: Whole number + Proper fraction (e.g., 1 2/3)

🎙️ We classify fractions based on the relationship between their numerator and denominator. Proper fractions are less than one, while improper and mixed fractions are greater than or equal to one.

Decimals as Fractions

  • Decimals are fractions with denominators of 10, 100, 1000, etc.
  • The place value determines the denominator.
  • Example: 0.1 is 1/10, 0.25 is 25/100.

🎙️ Every decimal number can be written as a fraction. The number of digits after the decimal point tells you how many zeros should be in your denominator.

Example: Converting Decimal to Fraction

  • Step 1: Write decimal as numerator without decimal point.
  • Step 2: Denominator is 1 followed by as many zeros as decimal places.
  • Step 3: Simplify the fraction.

🎙️ Let's take 0.75. The numerator is 75. Since there are two decimal places, the denominator is 100. So, 75/100, which simplifies to 3/4.

Percentages as Fractions

  • Percent means 'per hundred' or 'out of 100'.
  • The symbol '%' represents 'divided by 100'.
  • Example: 50% means 50/100.

🎙️ Percentages are another common way to express a part of a whole, always in relation to 100. If you score 80% on a test, it means you got 80 marks out of 100.

  • Step 1: Remove the '%' sign.
  • Step 2: Write the number as a numerator with 100 as the denominator.

🎙️ Converting percentages is straightforward. For 25%, we write it as 25 over 100. Then we simplify this fraction to its lowest terms, which is 1/4.

Ratios as Fractions

  • A ratio compares two or more quantities.
  • It can be written as a:b or a/b.
  • The ratio a:b can be expressed as the fraction a/b.

🎙️ Ratios are used to show the relationship between different amounts. When we say a ratio of 2:3, it means for every 2 parts of one thing, there are 3 parts of another. This can also be seen as a fraction.

⚖️
Comparing quantities

Example: Converting Ratio to Fraction

  • Ratio 2:3 means 2 parts of one quantity to 3 parts of another.
  • The fraction for the first quantity is 2/(2+3) = 2/5.
  • The fraction for the second quantity is 3/(2+3) = 3/5.

🎙️ If a ratio is 2:3, the total number of parts is 2+3=5. So, the first quantity is 2/5 of the total, and the second quantity is 3/5 of the total. Think of mixing colours!

Unitary Method and Fractions

  • Finding the value of a single unit first.
  • Then using this unit value to find the value of any desired quantity.
  • Often involves fractional calculations.

🎙️ The unitary method is a powerful problem-solving technique. It helps us break down complex problems into simpler steps by first figuring out the value of a single item or unit.

📦
Finding the value of one

Example: Unitary Method with Fractions

  • Problem: If 5 pens cost ₹50, what is the cost of 3 pens?
  • Step 1: Cost of 1 pen = ₹50 / 5 = ₹10.
  • Step 2: Cost of 3 pens = ₹10 × 3 = ₹30.

🎙️ Here, we first found the cost of one pen by dividing the total cost by the number of pens. Then, we multiplied that unit cost by the desired number of pens to find our answer.

1Cost of 5 pens = ₹50
2Cost of 1 pen = ₹50/5 = ₹10
3Cost of 3 pens = ₹10 × 3 = ₹30

Worked Example: Buying Groceries

  • You have ₹500. You spend 2/5 of your money on vegetables.
  • How much money did you spend?
  • Amount spent = (2/5) × ₹500 = ₹200.

🎙️ To find a fractional part of a whole amount, we multiply the fraction by the total. Here, 2/5 of ₹500 is ₹200. This means you have ₹300 left.

Worked Example: Sharing a Cake

  • A cake is divided into 8 equal slices.
  • You eat 1/4 of the cake, and your friend eats 3/8.
  • How many slices are left? (1/4 = 2/8)

🎙️ First, make sure all fractions have a common denominator. 1/4 is 2/8. So you ate 2 slices, and your friend ate 3 slices. Total eaten: 2+3=5 slices. Left: 8-5=3 slices.

Algebraic Expressions with Fractions

  • Finding common denominators is essential.

🎙️ Just like with numbers, we can have fractions that contain variables. The rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing these algebraic fractions are the same as for numerical fractions.

Example: Simplifying Algebraic Fractions

  • Simplify: (x/2) + (x/3)
  • Step 1: Find LCM of denominators (2 and 3) = 6.
  • Step 2: Rewrite fractions: (3x/6) + (2x/6).
  • Step 3: Add numerators: (3x + 2x)/6 = 5x/6.

🎙️ To add or subtract algebraic fractions, we must first find a common denominator, usually the Least Common Multiple (LCM). Then, we adjust the numerators accordingly and perform the operation.

Reciprocals and Division of Fractions

  • The reciprocal of a fraction (a/b) is (b/a).
  • To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.
  • Example: (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = (1/2) × (4/1).

🎙️ Reciprocals are important for division. When you divide by a fraction, you're essentially asking 'how many times does this smaller fraction fit into the larger one?' Multiplying by the reciprocal gives you that answer.

Flipping the fraction

Example: Dividing Fractions

  • Divide: (3/4) ÷ (1/8)
  • Step 1: Find the reciprocal of the divisor (1/8) which is (8/1).
  • Step 2: Change division to multiplication: (3/4) × (8/1).
  • Step 3: Multiply: (3 × 8) / (4 × 1) = 24/4 = 6.

🎙️ Imagine you have 3/4 of a meter and you want to cut it into pieces of 1/8 meter each. How many pieces would you get? The calculation shows you'd get 6 pieces.
