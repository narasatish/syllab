What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • It has four vertices and four angles.
  • The word 'quad' means four, 'lateral' means side.
  • Simple, closed figure.

🎙️ A quadrilateral is essentially any polygon that has exactly four sides. Because it has four sides, it also has four corners, which we call vertices, and four interior angles. Remember, it must be a closed figure, meaning all sides are connected.

📐
Four-sided figure

Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Sides: The four line segments forming the boundary.
  • Vertices: The four points where sides meet.
  • Angles: The four angles formed at the vertices.
  • Diagonals: Line segments joining opposite vertices.

🎙️ Every quadrilateral has these key elements. The sides are the boundaries, the vertices are the corners, and the angles are formed inside at these corners. Diagonals are important lines that connect opposite vertices, dividing the quadrilateral into triangles.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral is 360°.
  • Just like a triangle's angles sum to 180°.
  • This property holds true for ALL quadrilaterals.

🎙️ This is a fundamental property! No matter what kind of quadrilateral it is, if you add up all four of its interior angles, the total will always be 360 degrees. This property is very useful for finding unknown angles when others are given.

➕
Angles add up to 360°

Worked Example 1: Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given three angles: 80°, 100°, 95°.
  • Let the fourth angle be 'x'.
  • Use the Angle Sum Property.

🎙️ Here's how we apply the angle sum property. First, we state the property. Then, we substitute the given angles into the equation. Next, we sum the known angles and finally, we subtract that sum from 360 degrees to find the value of the unknown angle. Simple, isn't it?

1Sum of angles = 360°
280° + 100° + 95° + x = 360°
3275° + x = 360°
4x = 360° - 275° = 85°

The Trapezium (or Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with AT LEAST one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.
  • An isosceles trapezium has non-parallel sides of equal length.

🎙️ The trapezium is one of the most basic types of quadrilaterals. Its defining feature is having at least one pair of parallel sides. Think of the letter 'A' without the crossbar, or a road sign indicating a narrow bridge ahead. The other two sides can be of any length and angle.

The Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral where BOTH pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
  • Opposite sides are equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add up to 180°).

🎙️ A parallelogram is a special type of quadrilateral where not just one, but both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. This parallelism leads to some very useful properties: opposite sides are always equal, and opposite angles are also equal. Also, any two consecutive angles will add up to 180 degrees.

Opposite sides parallel and equal

Properties of Parallelogram: Diagonals

  • The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
  • This means they cut each other into two equal parts.
  • The point of intersection is the midpoint of both diagonals.

🎙️ Another crucial property of parallelograms involves their diagonals. When you draw both diagonals, they will always intersect exactly at their midpoints. This means each diagonal is divided into two equal segments by the other diagonal. This is a powerful property for proofs and calculations.

✖️
Diagonals bisect

Worked Example 2: Parallelogram Properties

  • Given parallelogram ABCD, with diagonal AC and BD intersecting at O.
  • If AO = 5 cm, find OC.
  • If angle ABC = 110°, find angle ADC.

🎙️ Let's apply what we just learned. Since the diagonals bisect each other, if one part of a diagonal is 5 cm, the other part must also be 5 cm. Similarly, because opposite angles in a parallelogram are equal, if one angle is 110 degrees, its opposite angle will also be 110 degrees. These properties simplify many problems.

1Diagonals bisect each other.
2So, AO = OC = 5 cm.
3Opposite angles are equal.
4So, angle ADC = angle ABC = 110°.

The Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.
  • Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).
  • Diagonals bisect the angles at the vertices.

🎙️ A rhombus is a special type of parallelogram. It has all the properties of a parallelogram, but with an added condition: all its four sides are equal. This extra condition gives it unique diagonal properties: they not only bisect each other but do so at a perfect 90-degree angle, and they also cut the vertex angles in half.

💎
All sides equal, diagonals perpendicular

The Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90° (right angles).
  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.
  • Diagonals are equal in length and bisect each other.

🎙️ The rectangle is another special parallelogram. While all its sides are not necessarily equal, all its angles are precisely 90 degrees. This property makes it very common in construction and design. Its diagonals are also equal in length, which is a key distinguishing feature from a general parallelogram or rhombus.

All angles 90°

The Square

  • A rectangle with all four sides equal.
  • Also a rhombus with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • All sides equal, all angles 90°.
  • Diagonals are equal, bisect each other at 90°.

🎙️ The square is the 'superhero' of quadrilaterals, combining the best features of both rectangles and rhombuses. It has all four sides equal (like a rhombus) AND all four angles equal to 90 degrees (like a rectangle). This means its diagonals are equal and bisect each other at right angles.

📦
All sides equal, all angles 90°

The Kite

  • A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of equal adjacent sides.
  • One pair of opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals are perpendicular to each other.
  • One diagonal bisects the other diagonal.

🎙️ The kite is a unique quadrilateral. Unlike a rhombus where all sides are equal, a kite has two pairs of equal sides, but these sides must be adjacent to each other. Its diagonals are always perpendicular, and one diagonal bisects the other, giving it a characteristic symmetrical shape.
