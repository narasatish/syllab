Introducing Quadrilaterals

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • Has four vertices and four angles.
  • A fundamental shape in geometry.

🎙️ Now, specifically, a quadrilateral is a type of polygon that has exactly four sides. It also has four corners, or vertices, and four angles. It's one of the most common shapes you'll encounter in daily life.

Key Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Four Sides (e.g., AB, BC, CD, DA)
  • Four Vertices (e.g., A, B, C, D)
  • Four Angles (e.g., ∠A, ∠B, ∠C, ∠D)
  • Two Diagonals (e.g., AC, BD)

🎙️ Every quadrilateral has specific parts. It has four sides that form its boundary, four points where the sides meet called vertices, and four angles formed at these vertices. We can also draw two diagonals by connecting opposite vertices.

Angle Sum Property of a Quadrilateral

  • The sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral is 360°.
  • Similar to a triangle's 180° sum.
  • This property holds true for all quadrilaterals.

🎙️ Just like a triangle's angles add up to 180 degrees, the angles inside any quadrilateral always add up to a specific value. Can you guess? It's 360 degrees! This is a very important property to remember.

➕
Angles Add Up
7 / 25

Proof: Angle Sum Property

  • Draw a diagonal to divide the quadrilateral into two triangles.
  • Each triangle's angles sum to 180°.
  • Total sum = 180° + 180° = 360°.

🎙️ We can easily prove this property. If you draw one diagonal inside any quadrilateral, it splits the quadrilateral into two triangles. Since each triangle's angles sum to 180 degrees, the total sum for the quadrilateral becomes 180 plus 180, which is 360 degrees.

Worked Example: Finding a Missing Angle

  • A quadrilateral has angles 70°, 90°, and 110°.
  • Find the measure of the fourth angle.
  • Solution: 360° - (70° + 90° + 110°) = 90°.

🎙️ Let's apply the angle sum property. If you know three angles of a quadrilateral, you can always find the fourth one. Just add the known angles and subtract the sum from 360 degrees. Here, the missing angle is 90 degrees.

Understanding the Trapezium

  • A quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.

🎙️ Our first special type is the trapezium. What makes it unique is that it has exactly one pair of parallel sides. Think of a road that narrows or widens – the parallel edges would be the bases, and the sloping edges would be the legs.

Introducing the Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
  • It is a special type of trapezium.
  • Opposite sides are also equal in length.

🎙️ Next, we have the parallelogram. This is a very important type of quadrilateral where *both* pairs of opposite sides are parallel to each other. Because of this, its opposite sides are also equal in length, and it has some other interesting properties.

Property 1: Opposite Sides are Equal

  • In a parallelogram, opposite sides have the same length.
  • If AB || DC and AD || BC, then AB = DC and AD = BC.
  • This is a defining characteristic.

🎙️ The first key property of a parallelogram is that its opposite sides are always equal in length. So, if you measure one pair of opposite sides, they will be identical, and the same applies to the other pair.

Property 2: Opposite Angles are Equal

  • The angles opposite to each other are equal in measure.
  • ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D.
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add up to 180°).

🎙️ Another important property is that the opposite angles within a parallelogram are equal. So, angle A will be equal to angle C, and angle B will be equal to angle D. Also, any two consecutive angles, like A and B, will add up to 180 degrees.

Equal Angles

Property 3: Diagonals Bisect Each Other

  • The diagonals of a parallelogram cut each other into two equal halves.
  • They intersect at their midpoint.
  • This means the intersection point divides each diagonal into two equal segments.

🎙️ Finally, let's talk about the diagonals. If you draw both diagonals in a parallelogram, they will always intersect exactly at their midpoints. This means each diagonal is divided into two equal parts by the other diagonal.

Worked Example: Using Parallelogram Properties

  • In parallelogram ABCD, if AB = 5 cm and ∠A = 70°.
  • What is the length of DC and the measure of ∠C and ∠B?
  • Solution: DC = 5 cm, ∠C = 70°, ∠B = 110°.

🎙️ Let's solve a problem using what we just learned. If we know one side and one angle of a parallelogram, we can find many other measures. Remember, opposite sides are equal, and opposite angles are equal, while consecutive angles are supplementary.

The Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal in length.
  • Its diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).
  • The diagonals also bisect the angles of the rhombus.

🎙️ A rhombus is a parallelogram where all four sides are equal. Imagine a tilted square. Its diagonals not only bisect each other but also intersect at a perfect right angle. Plus, these diagonals actually cut the corner angles exactly in half.

💎
Equal Sides, Angled Diagonals

The Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • Its opposite sides are equal.
  • The diagonals are equal in length and bisect each other.

🎙️ A rectangle is another special parallelogram, but this time, all its angles are 90 degrees. Think of your textbook or a door. Its opposite sides are equal, and an extra property is that its diagonals are also equal in length, though they don't necessarily meet at 90 degrees.

📏
Right Angles

The Square

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal AND all four angles equal to 90°.
  • It combines properties of both a rhombus and a rectangle.
  • The most 'regular' quadrilateral.

🎙️ Finally, the square! This is the most special of them all. A square is both a rhombus and a rectangle. It has all four sides equal AND all four angles are 90 degrees. It's truly a perfectly balanced quadrilateral.
