What is a Ratio?

  • A comparison of two quantities of the same unit.
  • Expressed as a:b or a/b.
  • Shows how much of one quantity there is compared to another.

🎙️ A ratio is like a comparison. Imagine you have two different groups of things, and you want to describe their relationship. For example, if you have 3 apples and 2 oranges, the ratio of apples to oranges is 3:2. Both quantities must be in the same unit for a valid ratio.

Understanding Proportion

  • An equality between two ratios.
  • If a:b = c:d, then a, b, c, d are in proportion.
  • The product of extremes equals the product of means (ad = bc).

🎙️ Now, what happens when two ratios are equal? That's when we talk about proportion! It means that the relationship between the first pair of quantities is the same as the relationship between the second pair. Think of it as a balance where both sides are perfectly equal.

Proportion Example

  • Ratio 1: 2 apples cost ₹20.
  • Ratio 2: 4 apples cost ₹40.
  • Are these in proportion? Yes, 2/20 = 4/40 (both simplify to 1/10).

🎙️ Let's see an example. If 2 apples cost ₹20, what about 4 apples? They would cost ₹40. Here, the ratio of apples to cost is 2:20 and 4:40. Since 2/20 simplifies to 1/10 and 4/40 also simplifies to 1/10, these two ratios are equal, meaning they are in proportion.

Direct Proportion

  • Two quantities increase or decrease together.
  • If one quantity doubles, the other quantity also doubles.
  • Ratio of quantities remains constant: x/y = k (constant).

🎙️ Direct proportion is a very common type of relationship. Imagine you're buying more of something; naturally, the total cost will also increase. If you buy less, the cost decreases. They move in the same direction, and their ratio stays constant.

Worked Example: Direct Proportion

  • If 5 pens cost ₹60, what is the cost of 8 pens?
  • Set up the proportion: 5/60 = 8/x.
  • Solve for x: 5x = 60 * 8.

🎙️ Let's solve a problem. We know the cost of 5 pens. We want to find the cost of 8 pens. Since it's a direct proportion (more pens, more cost), we can set up equal ratios. Cross-multiplication helps us find the unknown value. The cost of 8 pens would be ₹96.

Direct Proportion Problem

  • A car travels 150 km in 3 hours. How far will it travel in 5 hours?
  • Distance/Time = constant: 150/3 = x/5.
  • Solve for x: 3x = 150 * 5.

🎙️ Here's another one. If a car covers 150 km in 3 hours, and we want to know the distance for 5 hours, we again use direct proportion. More time means more distance. By setting up the ratio of distance to time, we find the car travels 250 km in 5 hours.

Inverse Proportion

  • Two quantities change in opposite directions.
  • If one quantity increases, the other decreases.
  • Product of quantities remains constant: x * y = k (constant).

🎙️ Now let's look at inverse proportion. This is when quantities move in opposite ways. If one goes up, the other comes down. For example, if you have more workers, a job gets done faster, meaning less time is needed. Their product remains constant.

Worked Example: Inverse Proportion

  • 6 workers can complete a task in 10 days. How many days will 12 workers take?

Inverse Proportion Problem

  • A car traveling at 60 km/h takes 2 hours. How long will it take at 80 km/h?
  • Speed * Time = constant: 60 * 2 = 80 * x.
  • Solve for x: 120 = 80x.

🎙️ Here's another one. If a car travels faster, it takes less time to cover the same distance. This is an inverse relationship. So, 60 km/h for 2 hours means the total distance is 120 km. At 80 km/h, it would take 1.5 hours to cover that same distance.

Direct or Inverse? How to Tell.

  • Ask: Do both quantities increase/decrease together? (Direct)
  • Ask: Does one increase while the other decreases? (Inverse)
  • Look for keywords: 'more...more', 'less...less' (Direct) vs. 'more...less', 'less...more' (Inverse).

🎙️ It's crucial to correctly identify whether a problem involves direct or inverse proportion. The easiest way is to ask yourself: 'If I increase one quantity, what happens to the other?' If they move in the same direction, it's direct. If they move in opposite directions, it's inverse.

The Unitary Method

  • Find the value of a single unit first.
  • Then, multiply or divide to find the required value.
  • Applicable for both direct and inverse proportion.

🎙️ The unitary method is a powerful technique for solving proportion problems. The core idea is to first figure out the value or quantity for 'one unit' of something. Once you know that, you can easily scale it up or down to find the answer for any number of units.

Finding the Unit

Unitary Method (Direct)

  • If 4 apples cost ₹48, what do 7 apples cost?
  • Step 1: Cost of 1 apple = ₹48 / 4 = ₹12.
  • Step 2: Cost of 7 apples = ₹12 * 7 = ₹84.

🎙️ Let's use the unitary method for a direct proportion problem. If 4 apples cost ₹48, we first find the cost of one apple by dividing. Then, to find the cost of 7 apples, we simply multiply the unit cost by 7. This is often an intuitive way to solve such problems.

2Multiply for total

Unitary Method (Inverse)

  • 5 workers build a wall in 12 days. How long for 1 worker?
  • Step 1: Time for 1 worker = 5 workers * 12 days = 60 days.
  • Step 2: If 10 workers, time = 60 days / 10 = 6 days.

🎙️ For inverse proportion with the unitary method, we find the 'total work' or 'total amount' that one unit would handle over a period. If 5 workers take 12 days, then one worker would take 5 times longer, which is 60 days. Then, if we have 10 workers, they will divide that 60 days among themselves.

1Find unit value (total work)
2Divide by new quantity

  • 3. Determine if it's direct or inverse proportion.
  • 4. Set up the equation (ratios or product).
