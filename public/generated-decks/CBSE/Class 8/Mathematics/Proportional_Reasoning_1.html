What is a Ratio?

  • Compares two quantities of the same kind.
  • Expressed as a fraction or using a colon (:).
  • Units must be the same for comparison.

🎙️ Think of a ratio as a way to compare two things. For example, if you have 3 red apples and 2 green apples, the ratio of red to green apples is 3:2. Remember, both quantities must be of the same type for a valid ratio!

Understanding Proportion

  • When two ratios are equal, they are in proportion.
  • Denoted by '::' or '='.
  • Example: 1:2 :: 3:6 (which means 1/2 = 3/6)

🎙️ Now, what if you have two different ratios, and they both represent the same relationship? That's what proportion is all about! If you double the ingredients in a recipe, the new ratio of ingredients will still be the same as the original.

Direct Proportion: The Idea

  • If one quantity increases, the other quantity also increases.
  • If one quantity decreases, the other quantity also decreases.
  • They move in the same direction.

🎙️ Think about buying sweets. If you buy more sweets, you pay more money, right? That's direct proportion! When two things change in the same way – both go up or both go down – they are directly proportional.

⬆️⬆️
Same Direction Change

Direct Proportion: Formal Definition

  • Two quantities x and y are in direct proportion if x/y = k (a constant).
  • As x increases, y increases proportionally.
  • As x decreases, y decreases proportionally.

🎙️ Mathematically, we say that if x and y are directly proportional, their ratio x/y always remains constant. This constant 'k' is called the constant of proportionality. This means their relationship is always predictable.

📈
Constant Ratio

Worked Example 3: Cost of Pens

  • If 5 pens cost ₹50, what is the cost of 8 pens?
  • More pens = more cost. This is direct proportion.

🎙️ Let's solve a problem. If 5 pens cost 50 rupees, how much would 8 pens cost? Since buying more pens means paying more money, we know this is a case of direct proportion. We can use a simple method to find the answer.

🖊️🖊️🖊️🖊️🖊️
5 pens
12 / 30

Method 1: Unitary Method for Direct Proportion

  • Step 1: Find the value of one unit.
  • Step 2: Multiply by the desired number of units.

🎙️ The unitary method is very straightforward. First, we find the cost of a single pen. If 5 pens cost ₹50, then one pen costs ₹10. Then, to find the cost of 8 pens, we simply multiply the cost of one pen by 8.

1Cost of 5 pens = ₹50
2Cost of 1 pen = ₹50 ÷ 5 = ₹10
3Cost of 8 pens = 8 × ₹10 = ₹80
13 / 30

Method 2: Cross-Multiplication for Direct Proportion

  • Set up the proportion: x1/y1 = x2/y2
  • Cross-multiply to solve for the unknown value.

🎙️ Another powerful method is cross-multiplication. We set up the two ratios as equal. If 5 pens cost ₹50 and 8 pens cost 'x', then 5/50 equals 8/x. By cross-multiplying, we can easily solve for 'x'.

1Pens: 5 → 8
2Cost: ₹50 → x
35/50 = 8/x
45x = 50 × 8 => 5x = 400
5x = 400 ÷ 5 = ₹80

Inverse Proportion: The Idea

  • If one quantity increases, the other quantity decreases.
  • If one quantity decreases, the other quantity increases.
  • They move in opposite directions.

🎙️ Now, let's look at a different kind of relationship. Imagine you're building a wall. If you have more workers, will it take more or less time to finish? Less time, right? This is inverse proportion – when one goes up, the other goes down.

⬆️⬇️
Opposite Direction Change

Inverse Proportion: Formal Definition

  • Two quantities x and y are in inverse proportion if x × y = k (a constant).
  • As x increases, y decreases proportionally.
  • As x decreases, y increases proportionally.

🎙️ Mathematically, if x and y are inversely proportional, their product (x multiplied by y) remains constant. Unlike direct proportion where the ratio is constant, here it's the product that stays the same. This is key to solving inverse proportion problems.

Worked Example 6: Workers and Time

  • If 4 workers can paint a house in 12 days, how many days will 6 workers take?
  • More workers = less time. This is inverse proportion.

🎙️ Let's apply this to a real problem. Four workers can paint a house in 12 days. If we add two more workers, so 6 workers in total, how many days will it take? Remember, more workers usually means less time to complete the job.

👷👷👷👷
4 Workers
18 / 30

Method 1: Unitary Method for Inverse Proportion

  • Step 1: Find the total 'work' units (product of initial quantities).
  • Step 2: Divide the total 'work' by the new quantity.

🎙️ For inverse proportion, the unitary method works a bit differently. We find the total 'work' unit, which is the product of workers and days. If it takes 48 worker-days to paint the house, then 6 workers will divide that work, taking 8 days.

14 workers take 12 days.
2Total 'work' = 4 workers × 12 days = 48 worker-days.
36 workers will take 48 ÷ 6 = 8 days.
19 / 30

Method 2: Product Method for Inverse Proportion

  • Set up the product: x1 × y1 = x2 × y2
  • Solve for the unknown value.

🎙️ The product method directly uses the definition of inverse proportion. We know that the product of the two quantities remains constant. So, 4 workers multiplied by 12 days equals 6 workers multiplied by 'x' days. This makes solving for 'x' very straightforward.

1Workers: 4 → 6
2Days: 12 → x
34 × 12 = 6 × x
448 = 6x
5x = 48 ÷ 6 = 8 days

Direct vs. Inverse: How to Identify?

  • Ask: Do both quantities increase/decrease together? (Direct)
  • Ask: Does one increase while the other decreases? (Inverse)
  • Look for keywords: 'more...more', 'less...less' (Direct); 'more...less', 'less...more' (Inverse).

🎙️ This is a crucial step! Before solving, always determine if the relationship is direct or inverse. Simply ask yourself: if one quantity goes up, does the other go up (direct) or down (inverse)? This will guide you to choose the correct formula.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing Direct and Inverse Proportion.
  • Incorrectly setting up the ratios/products.
  • Calculation errors (especially with fractions/decimals).
  • Not checking units before comparing quantities.

🎙️ Many students make these common errors. The biggest one is mixing up direct and inverse proportion, leading to using the wrong formula. Always double-check your initial identification and your calculations to avoid losing marks!

Exam Tips for Proportional Reasoning

  • Clearly state if it's Direct or Inverse Proportion.
  • Show all steps for unitary or cross-multiplication method.

🎙️ To score well, always begin by stating the type of proportion. Then, clearly show your working steps – this helps in getting partial marks even if the final answer is wrong. Don't forget to include units in your answer, like 'days' or 'rupees'!
