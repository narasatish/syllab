The Coordinate System: Our Map for Points

  • A grid-like system to locate any point in a plane.
  • Uses two perpendicular lines as references.
  • Forms the foundation for plotting all types of graphs.

🎙️ Think of the coordinate system as a special map. Just like you use street names and house numbers to find a place, we use coordinates to pinpoint exact locations on our graph.

X-axis and Y-axis: The Foundation Lines

  • The X-axis is the horizontal number line.
  • The Y-axis is the vertical number line.
  • They intersect at a special point called the Origin.

🎙️ These two lines are the backbone of our coordinate system. The X-axis usually represents independent variables like time, while the Y-axis represents dependent variables like temperature or distance.

Intersecting Axes

The Origin (0,0): Starting Point of Every Story

  • The point where the X-axis and Y-axis meet.
  • Its coordinates are always (0,0).
  • It's the reference point from which all other points are measured.

🎙️ Just like a journey starts from a specific place, every point on our graph is located relative to the Origin. It's our anchor, our zero point for both horizontal and vertical movements.

📍
The Central Point

Plotting Points: Finding Locations on the Map

  • Each point is defined by an (x, y) coordinate pair.
  • The first number (x) tells us horizontal movement.
  • The second number (y) tells us vertical movement.

🎙️ To plot a point, we always start at the origin. First, move left or right according to the x-coordinate, then move up or down according to the y-coordinate. It's like following directions!

1Start at Origin (0,0)
2Move x units along X-axis
3Move y units along Y-axis
4Mark the point
8 / 25

Example: Plotting the Point (3, 2)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 3 units to the right along the X-axis.
  • From there, move 2 units up parallel to the Y-axis.
  • Mark the point where you land!

🎙️ This simple example shows us the step-by-step process. The x-coordinate (3) tells us how far right to go, and the y-coordinate (2) tells us how far up. Practice makes perfect!

What is a Linear Graph? Straight Line Stories

  • A special type of line graph where all points lie on a single straight line.
  • Indicates a constant rate of change between the two quantities.
  • Represented by equations like y = mx + c.

🎙️ If the relationship between two things is steady and predictable, like the distance covered by a car moving at a constant speed, its graph will be a straight line – that's a linear graph!

📏

Non-Linear Graphs: Curved Paths and Varying Changes

  • Graphs where the plotted points do not form a straight line.
  • Shows that the rate of change is not constant.
  • Many real-world situations are best represented by non-linear graphs.

🎙️ Not everything in life follows a straight path! For example, the growth of a plant or the spread of a rumour doesn't happen at a constant rate, leading to curved or zig-zagged graphs.
