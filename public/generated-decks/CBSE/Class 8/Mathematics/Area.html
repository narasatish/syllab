What is Area?

  • The amount of surface inside a 2D shape.
  • Measured in square units.
  • It tells us how much space a flat object covers.

🎙️ Area is simply the space a flat shape takes up. Think of it like covering a floor with tiles – the number of tiles needed tells you the area of the floor.

Units of Area

  • Always expressed in 'square units'.
  • Common units: square centimetres (cm²), square metres (m²), square kilometres (km²).
  • Think of a tiny square, 1 cm by 1 cm. That's 1 cm².

🎙️ Since we're measuring a 2D surface, our units need to reflect that. We use 'square' units, like square centimetres or square metres, to show we're talking about a surface, not just a length.

Area of a Square

  • A square has four equal sides.
  • Formula: Side × Side (or Side²).
  • All angles are 90 degrees.

🎙️ A square is a very basic shape. To find its area, you just multiply the length of one side by itself. It's like counting how many small squares fit perfectly inside it.

⬛
Side x Side
6 / 25

Example: Area of a Square

  • Problem: Find the area of a square park with side 15 m.
  • Formula: Area = Side × Side
  • Calculation: 15 m × 15 m = 225 m².
  • Answer: The area of the square park is 225 square metres.

🎙️ Let's apply the formula! If a square park has a side of 15 metres, its area would be 15 multiplied by 15, giving us 225 square metres. Remember to always include the square units.

Area of a Rectangle

  • A rectangle has opposite sides equal.
  • Formula: Length × Breadth (or Width).
  • All angles are 90 degrees.

🎙️ Similar to a square, but with potentially different lengths for its sides. For a rectangle, we multiply its length by its breadth to find the area. Imagine tiling a rectangular floor.

▭
Length x Breadth
8 / 25

Example: Area of a Rectangle

  • Problem: A rectangular field is 20 m long and 10 m wide. Find its area.
  • Formula: Area = Length × Breadth
  • Calculation: 20 m × 10 m = 200 m².
  • Answer: The area of the rectangular field is 200 square metres.

🎙️ Here's another example. If a field is 20 metres long and 10 metres wide, we multiply these two dimensions to get 200 square metres. This tells us the total surface area of the field.

Area of a Triangle

  • A triangle has three sides.
  • Formula: (1/2) × Base × Height.
  • The height must be perpendicular to the base.

🎙️ Now let's move to triangles. The area of a triangle is half the product of its base and its height. Remember, the height is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex.

🔺

Base and Height in a Triangle

  • The 'base' can be any side of the triangle.
  • The 'height' is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to that base.
  • It's crucial to identify the correct base-height pair.

🎙️ Identifying the base and height correctly is key. The height is always at a 90-degree angle to the base. Sometimes, the height might fall outside the triangle, but the principle remains the same.

Perpendicular Height

Example: Area of a Triangle

  • Problem: Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 8 cm.
  • Formula: Area = (1/2) × Base × Height
  • Calculation: (1/2) × 10 cm × 8 cm = 40 cm².
  • Answer: The area of the triangle is 40 square centimetres.

🎙️ Let's calculate a triangle's area. If the base is 10 cm and the height is 8 cm, half of 10 times 8 gives us 40 square centimetres. This is how much surface the triangle covers.

Area of a Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides.
  • Formula: Base × Height.
  • Opposite sides are equal in length.

🎙️ A parallelogram looks like a 'slanted' rectangle. Its area is simply the product of its base and its perpendicular height. Imagine cutting off a triangular piece from one end and moving it to the other to form a rectangle.

▱
Base x Height

Base and Height in a Parallelogram

  • The 'base' is one of the parallel sides.
  • The 'height' is the perpendicular distance between the base and its opposite parallel side.
  • It's not the slanted side length.

🎙️ Just like with triangles, the height for a parallelogram must be the perpendicular distance. It's the straight up-and-down measurement between the parallel bases, not the length of the slanted side.

Example: Area of a Parallelogram

  • Problem: A parallelogram has a base of 12 cm and a height of 7 cm. Find its area.
  • Formula: Area = Base × Height
  • Calculation: 12 cm × 7 cm = 84 cm².
  • Answer: The area of the parallelogram is 84 square centimetres.

🎙️ Applying the formula, if a parallelogram has a base of 12 cm and a height of 7 cm, its area is 12 multiplied by 7, resulting in 84 square centimetres. Simple, isn't it?

Area of a Rhombus

  • A rhombus is a quadrilateral with all four sides equal in length.
  • Its diagonals bisect each other at right angles.
  • Formula: (1/2) × d1 × d2 (where d1 and d2 are the lengths of the diagonals).

🎙️ A rhombus is a special type of parallelogram where all sides are equal. Its area can be found using the lengths of its diagonals. Multiply the diagonals and then halve the result.

🔶
Half product of diagonals
18 / 25

Example: Area of a Rhombus

  • Problem: Find the area of a rhombus whose diagonals are 8 cm and 6 cm.
  • Formula: Area = (1/2) × d1 × d2
  • Calculation: (1/2) × 8 cm × 6 cm = 24 cm².
  • Answer: The area of the rhombus is 24 square centimetres.

🎙️ Let's work through this. If the diagonals are 8 cm and 6 cm, their product is 48 cm². Half of that is 24 cm². This formula is unique to shapes where diagonals are perpendicular bisectors.

Area of a Trapezium

  • A trapezium (or trapezoid) is a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides.
  • Formula: (1/2) × (sum of parallel sides) × height.
  • The height is the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides.

🎙️ A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. To find its area, we average the lengths of the parallel sides and then multiply by the perpendicular height between them. Think of it as an average length times height.

⏢
Half (a+b) x height
20 / 25

Example: Area of a Trapezium

  • Problem: Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides 10 cm and 14 cm, and height 5 cm.
  • Formula: Area = (1/2) × (a + b) × h
  • Calculation: (1/2) × (10 + 14) × 5 = (1/2) × 24 × 5 = 60 cm².
  • Answer: The area of the trapezium is 60 square centimetres.

🎙️ For this trapezium, first add the parallel sides (10+14=24). Then multiply by the height (24*5=120). Finally, halve it to get 60 square centimetres. This covers the surface within the trapezium.

Real-Life Application: Flooring

  • Problem: A room is 5 m long and 4 m wide. How many square tiles of side 50 cm are needed to cover the floor?
  • Step 1: Area of room = 5m × 4m = 20 m².
  • Step 2: Area of one tile = 0.5m × 0.5m = 0.25 m².
  • Step 3: Number of tiles = 20 / 0.25 = 80 tiles.

🎙️ This is a classic real-world problem. You first find the area of the entire floor, then the area of a single tile, and finally divide the total floor area by the tile area to find out how many tiles you need. Remember to keep units consistent!

Recap of Area Formulas

  • Square: Side × Side
  • Rectangle: Length × Breadth

  • Trapezium: (1/2) × (a + b) × Height
