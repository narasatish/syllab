Area: The Space Within

  • Area is the measure of the surface enclosed by a closed figure.
  • It's always measured in square units.
  • e.g., square centimetres (cm²), square metres (m²).

🎙️ Formally, Area quantifies the extent of a two-dimensional surface. It's crucial to remember that Area is always expressed in square units, such as square centimeters (cm²) or square meters (m²), because we are measuring a two-dimensional space.

Square's Area: Side x Side

  • Formula: Area = side × side = s²
  • Example: A square garden with side 5 m. Area = 5 m × 5 m = 25 m².

🎙️ The formula for the area of a square is simply 'side multiplied by side', or 's squared'. If you have a square garden with each side measuring 5 meters, its area would be 5 times 5, which is 25 square meters. Easy, right?

Rectangle's Area: Length x Breadth

  • Formula: Area = length × breadth (or width) = l × b
  • Example: A rectangular playground is 20 m long and 10 m wide. Area = 20 m × 10 m = 200 m².

🎙️ The area of a rectangle is found by multiplying its length by its breadth. So, for a playground that's 20 meters long and 10 meters wide, its area would be 20 multiplied by 10, giving us 200 square meters. Simple as that!

Triangle's Area: Half Base x Height

  • Formula: Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × b × h
  • Example: A triangular garden has a base of 10 m and a height of 6 m. Area = ½ × 10 m × 6 m = 30 m².

🎙️ The formula for the area of a triangle is 'half times base times height'. The base is one of its sides, and the height is the perpendicular distance from that base to the opposite vertex. For our garden example, the area is 30 square meters.

Rhombus's Area: Half Product of Diagonals

  • Formula: Area = ½ × d1 × d2 (where d1, d2 are diagonals)
  • Example: A rhombus has diagonals of 8 cm and 6 cm. Area = ½ × 8 cm × 6 cm = 24 cm².

🎙️ The area of a rhombus is half the product of its diagonals. If a rhombus has diagonals measuring 8 cm and 6 cm, you multiply them together (48) and then divide by two, giving an area of 24 square centimeters.

Trapezium's Area: Half Sum of Parallel Sides x Height

  • Formula: Area = ½ × (a + b) × h (a, b are parallel sides, h is height)
  • Example: A trapezium has parallel sides 10 cm and 12 cm, height 5 cm. Area = ½ × (10+12) × 5 = ½ × 22 × 5 = 55 cm².

🎙️ The formula for a trapezium's area involves adding the lengths of the two parallel sides, multiplying by the height, and then dividing by two. For our example, the sum of parallel sides is 22 cm, multiplied by 5 cm height and divided by 2 gives 55 square centimeters.

Quadrilateral Area: Step-by-Step

  • 1. Draw a diagonal to split into two triangles.
  • 2. Measure the diagonal (base) and heights to opposite vertices.
  • 3. Calculate Area = ½ × d × (h1 + h2).
  • Example: Diagonal = 10cm, h1=4cm, h2=3cm. Area = ½ × 10 × (4+3) = 35 cm².

🎙️ Here's how to do it: First, draw a diagonal. This diagonal becomes the common base for both triangles. Then, measure the perpendicular heights from the other two vertices to this diagonal. Finally, use the triangle area formula for each and add them. There's also a combined formula for this method.

Area of a Circle: The Round Space!

  • A circle is a special shape without straight sides.
  • Its area depends on its radius (r).
  • Formula: Area = πr² (where π ≈ 3.14 or 22/7).
  • Example: A circular pond has a radius of 7 m. Area = (22/7) × 7² = 154 m².

🎙️ Circles are unique because they don't have straight sides, so we can't use base and height. Instead, their area is calculated using the radius and a special constant called Pi (π). The formula is Pi times the radius squared. For a pond with a 7m radius, its area is 154 square meters.

Paths Around Shapes: A Practical Use!

  • Often, there's a path built around a garden or inside a field.
  • To find the path's area, subtract the inner area from the outer area.
  • Example: Path around 10x8m garden. Outer 12x10m. Path Area = (120 - 80) = 40 m².

🎙️ Area calculations are very useful for real-world scenarios, like finding the area of a path around a garden. You simply find the area of the larger shape (garden + path) and subtract the area of the smaller shape (just the garden). This gives you the area of the path itself.

Costing Projects: Area Helps Budget!

  • Want to tile a floor or paint a wall?
  • First, find the area to be covered.
  • Then, multiply the area by the cost per unit area (e.g., ₹ per m²).
  • Example: 50 m² floor, ₹100/m². Total cost = 50 × 100 = ₹5000.

🎙️ Area is directly linked to costs in many projects. If you know the area of a surface you need to cover, like a floor or a wall, and you know the cost per square unit for tiles or paint, you can easily calculate the total cost. This helps in budgeting and planning.

Common Mistakes & Exam Tips!

  • Avoid mixing perimeter & area formulas.
  • Always use correct square units (cm², m²).
  • Draw diagrams for complex problems.
  • Write formulas & show steps for full marks.

🎙️ Many students confuse area with perimeter, or forget to use square units. Always draw a diagram for complex shapes to visualize the problem. In exams, always write down the formula you're using and show your steps clearly to get full marks, even if the final answer has a minor error.
