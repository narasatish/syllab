Parts of a Right-Angled Triangle

  • Hypotenuse: The longest side, opposite the right angle.
  • Legs: The two shorter sides forming the right angle.

🎙️ Every right-angled triangle has three distinct sides with specific names. The side directly opposite the 90-degree angle is called the hypotenuse, and it's always the longest side. The other two sides, which form the right angle, are called the legs.

Baudhayana's Theorem: The Statement

  • The diagonal of a rectangle produces both areas which the two sides separately produce.
  • This means the square on the diagonal equals the sum of squares on its sides.
  • A profound observation about right triangles.

🎙️ Baudhayana's original statement is quite elegant. He described how the area of the square built on the diagonal of a rectangle is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares built on its two sides. This is essentially the theorem we know today, applied to a rectangle divided into two right triangles.

The Pythagorean Theorem: The Formula

  • In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (c) is equal to...
  • ...the sum of the squares of the other two sides (a and b).
  • Expressed as: a² + b² = c².

🎙️ The Pythagorean Theorem gives us a neat formula. If 'a' and 'b' are the lengths of the legs and 'c' is the length of the hypotenuse, then a squared plus b squared equals c squared. This simple equation unlocks many geometric problems.

Why 'Baudhayana-Pythagoras' Theorem?

  • Baudhayana stated the principle centuries before Pythagoras.
  • Pythagoras (or his followers) provided a formal proof.
  • Acknowledges contributions from both traditions.

🎙️ It's important to recognize that the core principle was known in India long before Pythagoras. While Pythagoras and his school are credited with a formal proof, Baudhayana's earlier work demonstrates the theorem's independent discovery. Naming it 'Baudhayana-Pythagoras' honors both historical contributions.

Worked Example 2: Finding a Leg

  • Problem: Hypotenuse is 10 cm, one leg is 6 cm.
  • We need to find the length of the other leg.
  • Rearrange the formula: a² = c² - b².

🎙️ What if we know the hypotenuse and one leg, but need to find the other leg? The formula can be rearranged. For example, if the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one leg is 6 cm, we can find the missing leg by subtracting the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse.

Pythagorean Triplets

  • A set of three positive integers (a, b, c).
  • They satisfy the equation a² + b² = c².
  • Examples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17).

🎙️ Pythagorean triplets are special sets of three whole numbers that perfectly fit the theorem. If you have a triangle with sides matching these numbers, you know it's a right-angled triangle. They are very useful for quickly solving problems or identifying right triangles.
