What is a Right-Angled Triangle?

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The 90-degree angle is also called a 'right angle'.

🎙️ A right-angled triangle is super special because one of its corners forms a perfect square corner, like the corner of a book or a wall. This 90-degree angle is crucial for the theorem we're studying.

Naming the Sides of a Right Triangle

  • Hypotenuse: The longest side, opposite the right angle.
  • Legs (or Cathetus): The two shorter sides forming the right angle.

🎙️ Each side has a unique name. The hypotenuse is always the longest and is directly across from the 90-degree angle. The other two sides are called legs, and they are the ones that actually create that right angle.

Baudhayana's Statement of the Theorem

  • He stated: 'The diagonal of an oblong produces by itself the same area as produced by both the sides (its length and breadth) separately.'

🎙️ This might sound a bit complex, but it's a beautiful way of describing what we now call the Pythagorean theorem. 'Produces by itself' means squaring the length, and 'same area as produced by both sides separately' refers to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

Understanding 'Oblong' in Geometry

🎙️ Imagine a rectangle. If you draw a line from one corner to the opposite corner, that's the diagonal. This diagonal splits the rectangle into two identical right-angled triangles, making it easy to apply the theorem.

The Pythagorean Theorem: The Formula

  • In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (c) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (a and b).
  • Mathematically: a² + b² = c².

🎙️ This is the famous formula! 'a' and 'b' are the lengths of the legs, and 'c' is the length of the hypotenuse. It's a powerful tool for finding unknown side lengths in right triangles.

🎙️ It's fascinating that two great minds, from different parts of the world and different eras, discovered the same fundamental mathematical truth. Baudhayana's work was more about practical application, while Pythagoras focused on formal proof.

Worked Example 1: Finding the Hypotenuse

  • A right triangle has legs of 3m and 4m. What is the length of the hypotenuse?
  • Solution: 3² + 4² = c² => 9 + 16 = c² => 25 = c² => c = 5m.

🎙️ Here, the two legs are given as 3m and 4m. We need to find the hypotenuse, 'c'. Using a² + b² = c², we can easily calculate that the hypotenuse is 5 meters.

Real-Life Application: Shortest Path

  • This diagonal path is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.

🎙️ Imagine you're at one corner of a rectangular park and want to get to the opposite corner. You can walk along the edges, or you can cut across diagonally. The diagonal path is always the shortest, and we can find its length using our theorem.

Worked Example 2: Finding a Leg

  • A ladder is 10m long and reaches 8m high on a wall. How far is its base from the wall?
  • Solution: x² + 8² = 10² => x² + 64 = 100 => x² = 36 => x = 6m.

🎙️ In this example, the ladder is the hypotenuse (c=10), and the height on the wall is one leg (b=8). We need to find the distance from the wall (a). The base of the ladder is 6 meters from the wall.

Pythagorean Triplets: Special Numbers

  • A set of three positive integers (a, b, c) that satisfy a² + b² = c².
  • Examples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17).

🎙️ These are special sets of numbers that perfectly fit the theorem. Knowing common triplets like (3, 4, 5) can save you time in calculations, as any triangle with sides in these ratios will be a right-angled triangle.
