Naming the Sides

  • The longest side is the Hypotenuse.
  • It's always opposite the 90-degree angle.
  • The other two sides are Legs (Perpendicular & Base).

🎙️ Every right-angled triangle has three sides, but they have special names. The side that's always longest and always opposite the 90-degree angle is called the 'hypotenuse'. The other two sides, which form the 90-degree angle, are called 'legs' – often referred to as the perpendicular and the base.

Baudhayana's Statement

  • 'The diagonal of a rectangle produces by itself both the areas which the two sides separately produce.'
  • Sounds complex? Let's break it down!
  • This means: square on diagonal = sum of squares on sides.

🎙️ Baudhayana's original statement might sound a bit like a riddle, but it's actually very elegant. He observed a fundamental relationship between the sides of a right-angled triangle (which is half a rectangle). He realised that if you make squares on each side, their areas are connected.

Baudhayana's Insight Explained

  • Imagine squares built on each side of a right triangle.

🎙️ Let's make it simpler. If you draw a square on the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, and then draw squares on the other two shorter sides, the area of the big square will be exactly equal to the combined areas of the two smaller squares. This was a groundbreaking observation!

The Famous Formula

  • In a right-angled triangle...
  • The square of the hypotenuse (c) is equal to...
  • ...the sum of the squares of the other two sides (a and b).

🎙️ This is the most common way you'll see the theorem stated: a² + b² = c². Here, 'c' always represents the hypotenuse, the longest side, and 'a' and 'b' represent the other two shorter sides, or legs. It's a simple yet incredibly powerful equation.

➕
a² + b² = c²

Breaking Down the Formula

  • 'a' and 'b' are the lengths of the legs.
  • 'c' is the length of the hypotenuse.
  • Squaring means multiplying a number by itself (e.g., 3² = 3x3 = 9).

🎙️ Let's clarify the terms. 'a' and 'b' are interchangeable for the perpendicular and base. 'c' is *always* the hypotenuse. The little '2' means 'squared'. So, if side 'a' is 3 units long, 'a squared' is 9. This formula links the lengths of the sides in a very specific way.

The Magical 3-4-5 Triangle

  • If legs are 3 units and 4 units long...
  • The hypotenuse will be exactly 5 units long.
  • Check: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, and 5² = 25!

🎙️ The 3-4-5 triangle is the simplest and most famous example of this theorem. It's often used by builders even today to check if an angle is truly 90 degrees. If you measure sides of 3, 4, and 5 units, you're guaranteed a perfect right angle!

Problem: Find the Hypotenuse

  • A triangle has legs of 6 cm and 8 cm.
  • What is the length of its hypotenuse?
  • Let's use a² + b² = c²!

🎙️ Let's try a practical example. Imagine you have a right-angled triangle where you know the lengths of the two shorter sides. How do you find the longest side? The Baudhayana-Pythagoras Theorem is our tool!

Step-by-Step Solution

  • Step 1: Write the formula: a² + b² = c².
  • Step 2: Substitute values: 6² + 8² = c².
  • Step 3: Calculate: 36 + 64 = c², so 100 = c².
  • Step 4: Find 'c': c = √100 = 10 cm.

🎙️ Here's how we solve it: First, write down the formula. Then, plug in the given values for 'a' and 'b'. Calculate the squares and add them. Finally, to find 'c', you need to take the square root of the sum. So, the hypotenuse is 10 cm long.

Problem: Find a Missing Leg

  • A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm.
  • One leg is 5 cm long. Find the other leg.
  • This time, we rearrange the formula!

🎙️ Sometimes, you might know the hypotenuse and one leg, and need to find the other leg. The theorem still applies, but we'll need to do a little algebraic rearrangement. Let's see how.

22 / 30

Step-by-Step Solution

  • Step 1: Formula: a² + b² = c².
  • Step 2: Substitute: 5² + b² = 13².
  • Step 3: Calculate: 25 + b² = 169.
  • Step 4: Isolate b²: b² = 169 - 25 = 144.
  • Step 5: Find 'b': b = √144 = 12 cm.

🎙️ Here, we substitute the known values. Since we're looking for a leg, we need to subtract the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse. Then, take the square root to find the length of the missing leg. So, the other leg is 12 cm.

Meet the Pythagorean Triplets

  • Sets of three positive integers (a, b, c).
  • They satisfy a² + b² = c².
  • Examples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17).

🎙️ Pythagorean triplets are special groups of three whole numbers that perfectly fit the theorem. Knowing some common triplets can save you time in calculations. They are frequently seen in math problems and real-world applications.
