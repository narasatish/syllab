Multiplying Monomials: Simple Steps

  • A monomial is an expression with one term (e.g., 3x, 5y²)
  • Multiply the numerical coefficients first
  • Then multiply the variable parts (using exponent rules)

🎙️ When multiplying monomials, like (3x) by (2y), we follow a simple two-step process. First, multiply the numbers in front of the variables, which are called coefficients. Second, multiply the variables themselves. Remember, x times x is x²!

Let's Try One: Monomial × Monomial

  • Example: (4x) × (5y)
  • Step 1: Multiply coefficients (4 × 5 = 20)
  • Step 2: Multiply variables (x × y = xy)
  • Result: 20xy

🎙️ Let's work through an example. To multiply 4x by 5y, we first multiply the coefficients 4 and 5, which gives us 20. Then, we multiply the variables x and y, which simply gives us xy. Combining these, our final answer is 20xy. Simple, isn't it?

Monomial by Binomial: Distribute the Joy!

  • A binomial has two terms (e.g., x + 2, 3y - 5)
  • Multiply the monomial by EACH term in the binomial
  • This is the distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac

🎙️ Now, what if we have one term multiplying two terms? Imagine you have one gift (the monomial) and you want to give a part of it to two friends (the binomial terms). You distribute the gift to each friend. Similarly, you multiply the monomial by each term inside the binomial separately.

A Practical Look: Monomial × Binomial

  • Example: 3x (2x + 5)
  • Step 1: 3x × 2x = 6x²
  • Step 2: 3x × 5 = 15x
  • Result: 6x² + 15x

🎙️ Let's apply the distributive property. For 3x multiplied by (2x + 5), we first multiply 3x by 2x, which gives us 6x². Then, we multiply 3x by 5, which results in 15x. Finally, we add these results to get 6x² + 15x. Remember to handle signs carefully!

Binomial by Binomial: Double the Distribution!

  • Each term of the first binomial multiplies EACH term of the second
  • Think of it as distributing twice!
  • A common method is FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last)

🎙️ When multiplying a binomial by another binomial, it's like having two people, and each person shakes hands with two other people. Every term in the first binomial must multiply every term in the second. The FOIL method is a handy way to remember all the combinations.

Step-by-Step: Binomial × Binomial

  • Example: (x + 2)(x + 3)
  • F (First): x × x = x²
  • O (Outer): x × 3 = 3x
  • I (Inner): 2 × x = 2x
  • L (Last): 2 × 3 = 6
  • Combine like terms: x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6

🎙️ Let's use the FOIL method for (x + 2)(x + 3). First, multiply the 'First' terms (x * x = x²). Next, the 'Outer' terms (x * 3 = 3x). Then, the 'Inner' terms (2 * x = 2x). Finally, the 'Last' terms (2 * 3 = 6). After finding all four products, combine any like terms to get your final simplified expression.

Binomial by Trinomial: Expanding Our Horizons

  • Each term of the binomial multiplies EACH term of the trinomial
  • It's an extension of the distributive property

🎙️ What if we have a binomial multiplying a trinomial? The principle remains the same: every term in the first expression multiplies every term in the second expression. It just means you'll have more individual multiplications to perform before combining like terms.

Let's Tackle It: Binomial × Trinomial

  • Example: (x + 1)(x² + 2x + 3)
  • Step 1: x(x² + 2x + 3) = x³ + 2x² + 3x
  • Step 2: +1(x² + 2x + 3) = x² + 2x + 3
  • Step 3: Combine like terms: x³ + (2x² + x²) + (3x + 2x) + 3
  • Result: x³ + 3x² + 5x + 3

🎙️ For (x + 1)(x² + 2x + 3), we first take 'x' from the binomial and multiply it by each term in the trinomial. Then, we take '+1' from the binomial and multiply it by each term in the trinomial. After these two sets of multiplications, we gather all the terms and combine any that are 'like terms' to simplify the expression.

Now, The Reverse: What is Division?

  • Division is the inverse operation of multiplication
  • If 3 × 4 = 12, then 12 ÷ 4 = 3
  • In algebra, we 'cancel out' common factors

🎙️ Just like subtraction is the opposite of addition, division is the inverse of multiplication. If we multiply two algebraic expressions, we can often divide the product by one of the expressions to get the other. The key here is to look for and cancel out common factors.

Dividing Monomials: Simplifying Terms

  • Divide the numerical coefficients
  • Divide the variable parts (using exponent rules: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ)
  • Combine the results

🎙️ Dividing monomials is much like multiplying them, but in reverse. First, divide the numerical coefficients. Then, divide the variable parts. Remember the rule for exponents: when dividing powers with the same base, subtract their exponents. For example, x⁵ divided by x² is x³.

Let's Divide: Monomial ÷ Monomial

  • Example: (12x³y²) ÷ (3xy)
  • Step 1: Divide coefficients (12 ÷ 3 = 4)
  • Step 2: Divide variables (x³ ÷ x = x², y² ÷ y = y)
  • Result: 4x²y

🎙️ Let's take an example: (12x³y²) divided by (3xy). We divide 12 by 3 to get 4. Then, for the variables, x³ divided by x gives us x raised to the power (3-1), which is x². Similarly, y² divided by y gives y raised to the power (2-1), which is y. So, the final answer is 4x²y.

Dividing a Polynomial by a Monomial

  • Example: (6x² + 9x) ÷ (3x)
  • Step 1: Divide first term: (6x²) ÷ (3x) = 2x
  • Step 2: Divide second term: (9x) ÷ (3x) = 3
  • Result: 2x + 3

🎙️ Let's see how this works. For (6x² + 9x) divided by (3x), we first divide 6x² by 3x, which simplifies to 2x. Then, we divide 9x by 3x, which simplifies to 3. Since the original terms were added, we add the results, giving us 2x + 3. Each term gets its turn!

Polynomial by Binomial: A Longer Journey

  • This often involves polynomial long division
  • Similar to long division with numbers
  • Requires careful step-by-step execution

🎙️ Dividing a polynomial by a binomial is a bit more involved, often requiring a method similar to the long division you learned for numbers. It's a systematic process where you divide, multiply, subtract, and bring down terms repeatedly until you get a remainder or zero.

Polynomial by Binomial: Step 1 - Setup

  • Arrange dividend and divisor in descending powers of variables.
  • Divide the first term of the dividend by the first term of the divisor.
  • Write the result as the first term of the quotient.

🎙️ Let's begin with polynomial long division. First, make sure both your dividend and divisor are written in descending order of the powers of the variable. Then, focus on the very first term of the dividend and divide it by the very first term of the divisor. This gives you the first part of your answer, the quotient.

Polynomial by Binomial: Step 2 - Repeat

  • Multiply the quotient term by the entire divisor.
  • Subtract the result from the dividend.
  • Bring down the next term and repeat the process.
  • Continue until the remainder's degree is less than the divisor's.

🎙️ After finding the first quotient term, multiply it by the *entire* divisor. Write this product below the dividend and subtract it carefully, paying attention to signs. Then, bring down the next term of the dividend and repeat the whole process until you can no longer divide, meaning the remainder's degree is less than the divisor's.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Sign errors during multiplication or subtraction
  • Not distributing to ALL terms (e.g., monomial by binomial)
  • Incorrectly combining unlike terms
  • Forgetting exponent rules for multiplication and division

🎙️ Be careful! Many students make mistakes with negative signs, especially during subtraction in long division. Always ensure you distribute the monomial to *every* term in the polynomial. And remember, you can only combine like terms; don't add x² to x. Finally, brush up on your exponent rules!
