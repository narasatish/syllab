Numbers in General Form: Two-Digit Numbers

  • A two-digit number 'ab' actually means 10a + b.
  • Here, 'a' is the digit in the tens place and 'b' is the digit in the units place.
  • Remember, 'a' cannot be zero.

🎙️ Let's start with how we write numbers in a general form. A two-digit number, like 'ab', is not 'a multiplied by b'. It represents 'a' tens and 'b' units. For instance, 45 is 4 tens and 5 units, so 10 times 4 plus 5.

Example: Representing 57 in General Form

  • The number is 57.
  • Here, 'a' (tens digit) = 5.
  • And 'b' (units digit) = 7.
  • So, 57 = (10 × 5) + 7 = 50 + 7.

🎙️ Consider the number 57. The digit in the tens place is 5, and the digit in the units place is 7. Using our general form, this means 10 multiplied by 5, plus 7, which indeed gives us 50 plus 7, or 57.

Numbers in General Form: Three-Digit Numbers

  • A three-digit number 'abc' means 100a + 10b + c.
  • Here, 'a' is hundreds, 'b' is tens, and 'c' is units.
  • 'a' cannot be zero.

🎙️ Similarly, for a three-digit number like 'abc', 'a' is in the hundreds place, 'b' in the tens, and 'c' in the units place. So, it's 100 times 'a', plus 10 times 'b', plus 'c'. This general form helps us understand number properties better.

Example: Representing 345 in General Form

  • The number is 345.
  • 'a' (hundreds digit) = 3.
  • 'b' (tens digit) = 4.
  • 'c' (units digit) = 5.
  • So, 345 = (100 × 3) + (10 × 4) + 5 = 300 + 40 + 5.

🎙️ Let's take the number 345. The digit 3 is in the hundreds place, 4 in the tens place, and 5 in the units place. Applying the general form, we get 100 times 3, plus 10 times 4, plus 5, which correctly equals 300 plus 40 plus 5, or 345.

Game 1: Reversing Digits (Two-Digit Numbers)

  • Start with a two-digit number 'ab' (10a + b).
  • Reverse its digits to get 'ba' (10b + a).
  • Add or subtract the original and reversed numbers.
  • Observe interesting patterns!

🎙️ Now for some fun games! Take any two-digit number. Reverse its digits to form a new number. What happens if you add these two numbers? Or subtract them? You'll find some neat properties related to divisibility.

Worked Example: Reversing 63

  • Original number: 63 (10 × 6 + 3).
  • Reversed number: 36 (10 × 3 + 6).
  • Sum: 63 + 36 = 99. (Always divisible by 11 and 9)
  • Difference: 63 - 36 = 27. (Always divisible by 9)

🎙️ Let's try with 63. The reversed number is 36. When we add them, 63 + 36 = 99. Notice that 99 is divisible by both 11 and 9. If we subtract them, 63 - 36 = 27, which is divisible by 9. These patterns hold true for any two-digit number!

Divisibility Rule for 10

  • A number is divisible by 10 if its units digit is 0.
  • This is because any number ending in 0 can be written as 10 multiplied by some other number.
  • Example: 70, 120, 500.

🎙️ Now, let's refresh our memory on divisibility rules, which are shortcuts to check if a number is divisible by another without actual division. For 10, it's very simple: if the last digit is a zero, it's divisible by 10. Think of counting in tens – 10, 20, 30... they all end in zero.

Divisibility Rule for 5

  • A number is divisible by 5 if its units digit is 0 or 5.
  • Numbers ending in 0 or 5 are multiples of 5.
  • Example: 15, 40, 75.

🎙️ Next, the rule for 5. A number is divisible by 5 if its last digit is either 0 or 5. This is because when you count by fives, all numbers end in one of these two digits. Think of a clock face and the minutes hand.

Divisibility Rule for 2

  • A number is divisible by 2 if its units digit is an even number (0, 2, 4, 6, or 8).
  • These are called even numbers.
  • Example: 12, 54, 106.

🎙️ The rule for 2 is also straightforward. If a number is even, meaning its last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8, then it's divisible by 2. We often use this rule to quickly identify even and odd numbers.

Divisibility Rule for 3

  • A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3.
  • This rule works for numbers of any length.
  • Example: 12 (1+2=3), 108 (1+0+8=9).

🎙️ The divisibility rule for 3 is a bit different. Instead of looking at the last digit, we sum all the digits of the number. If that sum is divisible by 3, then the original number is also divisible by 3. This is a very powerful rule!

Example: Numbers Divisible by 3

  • Is 75 divisible by 3? Sum = 7+5=12. Yes, 12 is divisible by 3.
  • Is 134 divisible by 3? Sum = 1+3+4=8. No, 8 is not divisible by 3.
  • Is 522 divisible by 3? Sum = 5+2+2=9. Yes, 9 is divisible by 3.

🎙️ For 75, the sum of digits is 12, which is divisible by 3, so 75 is divisible by 3. For 134, the sum is 8, not divisible by 3. For 522, the sum is 9, which is divisible by 3. This rule is very elegant and useful.

Divisibility Rule for 9

  • This rule is very similar to the rule for 3.

Worked Example: Find A and B

  • A B
  • + 3 8
  • -----
  • C 1

🎙️ Let's solve this puzzle. In the units column, B plus 8 must end in 1. This means B has to be 3, making it 11, so we carry over 1. In the tens column, A plus 3 plus the carried-over 1 must equal C. If C is 1, then A+4=11, making A=7. So, A is 7 and B is 3. The sum is 73 + 38 = 111.

1Units place: B + 8 ends in 1. So, B must be 3 (3+8=11). Carry over 1.
2Tens place: A + 3 + 1 (carry-over) = C. If C=1, A+4=11. So A=7. C=1.
3Solution: A=7, B=3. (73 + 38 = 111)
