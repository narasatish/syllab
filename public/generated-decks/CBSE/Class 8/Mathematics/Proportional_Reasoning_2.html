Recap: Direct Proportion

  • As one quantity increases, the other also increases.
  • As one quantity decreases, the other also decreases.
  • The ratio between the two quantities remains constant.

🎙️ Remember direct proportion? It's when two quantities move in the same direction. If you buy more pens, you pay more money – both go up. This relationship is often expressed as x/y = k.

Understanding Inverse Proportion

  • If quantity 'x' increases, quantity 'y' decreases.
  • If quantity 'x' decreases, quantity 'y' increases.
  • The product x * y = k (a constant).

🎙️ Imagine travelling a fixed distance. If you increase your speed, the time taken to cover that distance will decrease. They are inversely proportional, as shown by the comparison of bar lengths.

Worked Example 1: Inverse Proportion

  • If 6 pipes can fill a tank in 8 hours,
  • how long will 4 pipes take to fill the same tank?

🎙️ Let's try an example together. Here, the number of pipes and the time taken to fill the tank are inversely proportional. More pipes mean less time, as the work to fill the tank is constant.

9 / 25

Solution 1

  • Let x be the time taken by 4 pipes.
  • Pipes (x₁=6, x₂=4). Time (y₁=8, y₂=x).
  • Using x₁y₁ = x₂y₂: 6 * 8 = 4 * x.
  • 48 = 4x => x = 12 hours.

🎙️ So, with 6 pipes, it takes 8 hours. With fewer pipes (4), it will naturally take more time. Our calculation shows it will take 12 hours to fill the tank.

Worked Example 2: Compound Proportion

  • If 5 men can reap 10 acres of field in 10 days,
  • how many men will be required to reap 15 acres in 5 days?

🎙️ This problem has men, acres (representing work done), and days. We need to find the number of men. We'll compare men with acres and men with days to set up our solution.

17 / 25

Solution 2

  • Let x be the number of men required.
  • Men and Acres (Direct Proportion): (M₁/A₁) = (M₂/A₂)
  • Men and Days (Inverse Proportion): M₁D₁ = M₂D₂
  • Combined: (M₁D₁)/A₁ = (M₂D₂)/A₂ => (5 * 10) / 10 = (x * 5) / 15
  • 5 = x/3 => x = 15 men.

🎙️ By combining the direct relationship between men and acres, and the inverse relationship between men and days, we arrive at the answer. 15 men are needed to complete the larger task in fewer days.

Worked Example 3: Time and Work

  • A can do a piece of work in 10 days.
  • B can do the same work in 15 days.
  • How long will they take if they work together?

🎙️ This problem involves finding the combined work rate. We'll calculate the amount of work each person does in one day and then add them up to find their combined daily output.

20 / 25

Solution 3

  • A's 1 day's work = 1/10 of the total work.
  • B's 1 day's work = 1/15 of the total work.
  • (A+B)'s 1 day's work = 1/10 + 1/15 = (3+2)/30 = 5/30 = 1/6.
  • So, A and B together will take 6 days to complete the work.

🎙️ When working together, their individual work rates combine. Since they complete 1/6th of the work in one day, it will take them 6 days to finish the entire work when collaborating.

Worked Example 4: Pipes and Cisterns

  • A pipe can fill a tank in 12 hours.
  • Another pipe can empty it in 18 hours.
  • If both are opened, how long will it take to fill the tank?

🎙️ Here, one pipe is filling, and another is emptying. The emptying pipe does 'negative work' by reducing the water level. We need to find the net filling rate when both are operating.

23 / 25

Solution 4

  • Filling pipe's 1 hour work = 1/12 of the tank.
  • Emptying pipe's 1 hour work = -1/18 of the tank.
  • Net 1 hour work = 1/12 - 1/18 = (3-2)/36 = 1/36 of the tank.
  • So, the tank will be filled in 36 hours.

🎙️ When both pipes are open, the tank fills at a slower rate due to the emptying pipe. The net rate is 1/36th of the tank per hour, meaning it will take 36 hours to fill the entire tank.
