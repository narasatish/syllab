Direct Proportion: An Example

  • Problem: If 5 pens cost ₹50, what will 8 pens cost?
  • Solution: Let 8 pens cost ₹x. Then 5/50 = 8/x.
  • Cross-multiply: 5x = 8 * 50.
  • Result: x = 400/5 = ₹80.

🎙️ Let's quickly solve an example. We set up the ratios, cross-multiply, and find the unknown value. Here, as the number of pens increases, the cost also increases proportionally.

Understanding Inverse Proportion

  • When 'x' and 'y' are in inverse proportion, their product is constant (x * y = k).
  • If x doubles, y halves.
  • They move in opposite directions, maintaining a constant product.

🎙️ In inverse proportion, the product of the two quantities remains constant. This means if one quantity doubles, the other halves. They move in opposite directions, maintaining a balance.

Inverse Proportion: Workers & Time

  • Problem: 10 workers can build a wall in 20 days. How many days will 8 workers take?
  • Let x be the number of days. 10 * 20 = 8 * x.
  • Result: 200 = 8x, so x = 200/8 = 25 days.

🎙️ Let's apply this. Here, the number of workers and the days taken are inversely proportional. Less workers mean more days. We use the constant product rule to solve.

Inverse Proportion: Speed & Time

  • Problem: A car travelling at 60 km/h takes 2 hours to reach a destination.
  • How long will it take if it travels at 40 km/h?
  • Let t be the new time. 60 * 2 = 40 * t.
  • Result: 120 = 40t, so t = 120/40 = 3 hours.

🎙️ Another classic example is speed and time for a fixed distance. If you drive slower, it will take you longer to reach the same place. This is also an inverse relationship.

Time and Work: Individual Rates

  • Problem: A can do a piece of work in 10 days. B can do the same work in 15 days.
  • A's 1-day work = 1/10 of the work.
  • B's 1-day work = 1/15 of the work.

🎙️ When solving time and work problems, it's often helpful to think about the fraction of work done in one day. This helps us combine their efforts or compare their speeds.

Time and Work: Combined Efforts

  • Problem: If A does 1/10 work/day and B does 1/15 work/day, how long will they take working together?
  • Combined 1-day work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6.
  • Result: They will take 6 days to complete the work together.

🎙️ Now, if A and B work together, their daily work rates add up. Once we find their combined daily work, we can easily find the total time taken to complete the entire work.

Time and Distance: Inverse Proportion

  • Problem: A train covers a certain distance in 3 hours at a speed of 80 km/h.
  • How much time will it take to cover the same distance at 60 km/h?
  • Let t be the new time. 80 * 3 = 60 * t.
  • Result: 240 = 60t, so t = 240/60 = 4 hours.

🎙️ Here, the distance is constant. We have to find the new time when the speed changes. Use the inverse proportion rule (Speed1 × Time1 = Speed2 × Time2).

Combined Problem: Example

  • Problem: If 5 workers can build 10 chairs in 2 days, how many chairs can 8 workers build in 3 days?
  • Work per worker per day = 10 chairs / (5 workers * 2 days) = 1 chair/worker/day.
  • Chairs built by 8 workers in 3 days = 8 workers * 3 days * 1 chair/worker/day = 24 chairs.

🎙️ This is a classic 'man-days-work' type problem. By finding the work done by one person in one day, we can easily solve for any number of workers and days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing Direct and Inverse Proportion: Always analyze the relationship.
  • Calculation Errors: Double-check your arithmetic carefully.
  • Units Mismatch: Ensure all quantities are in consistent units.
  • Not showing steps: Present your work clearly for partial credit.

🎙️ Be careful not to mix up direct and inverse proportion. A simple mistake here can lead to a completely wrong answer. Always read the problem carefully and think about the relationship between the quantities.

Exam Tips for Proportional Reasoning

  • Clearly state if it's direct or inverse proportion at the beginning.
  • Write down the formula or relationship (e.g., x1/y1 = x2/y2 or x1y1 = x2y2).
  • Show all steps of your calculation for full marks.
  • Include appropriate units in your final answer.

🎙️ In exams, showing your steps is crucial. Even if your final answer is wrong, you might get partial credit for the correct method. Also, always remember to include the correct units in your answer.
