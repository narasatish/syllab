Revisiting Types of Proportions

Direct Proportion: The 'Together' Relationship

  • If x and y are in direct proportion, then x/y = k (constant).
  • As x increases, y increases proportionally.
  • As x decreases, y decreases proportionally.

🎙️ In direct proportion, if one quantity goes up, the other goes up by the same factor. Think of it like buying more items, you pay more money. Simple, right?

Direct Proportion in Action: Buying Groceries

🎙️ This is a classic example. The quantity of apples and their total cost are directly proportional. If you buy half the apples, you pay half the price.

Worked Example 1: Rice Cost

  • Problem: If 5 kg of rice costs ₹250, what will be the cost of 8 kg of rice?
  • Given: 5 kg rice = ₹250.
  • Find: Cost of 8 kg rice.

🎙️ Here, as the quantity of rice increases, its cost will also increase. This is a clear case of direct proportion. Let's solve this using the unitary method.

Rice Quantity

Solution: Rice Cost

  • Cost of 5 kg rice = ₹250.
  • Cost of 1 kg rice = ₹250 / 5 = ₹50.
  • Cost of 8 kg rice = ₹50 × 8 = ₹400.

🎙️ By finding the cost per kilogram, we can easily calculate the cost for any other quantity. This demonstrates the power of the unitary method in direct proportion.

Inverse Proportion: The 'Opposite' Relationship

  • If x and y are in inverse proportion, then x × y = k (constant).
  • As x increases, y decreases proportionally.
  • As x decreases, y increases proportionally.

🎙️ Unlike direct proportion, here the quantities move in opposite directions. If you have more workers, a job gets done faster, meaning less time.

Inverse Proportion in Action: Workers and Time

  • If 2 people take 10 hours to paint a room, 4 people will take less time.
  • More workers = Less time to complete work.
  • Fewer workers = More time to complete work.

🎙️ Imagine a construction project. If you double the number of skilled workers, theoretically, the time taken to complete the project should halve. This is a classic inverse relationship.

Worked Example 3: Finishing a Road

  • Problem: 15 workers can build a road in 48 days. How many days will 9 workers take to build the same road?
  • Given: 15 workers = 48 days.
  • Find: Days for 9 workers.

🎙️ Here, as the number of workers decreases, the time taken to build the road will increase. This is an inverse proportion problem. Let's solve it.

Solution: Finishing a Road

  • 15 workers take 48 days.
  • Total work units = 15 workers × 48 days = 720 work-days.
  • For 9 workers, days needed = 720 / 9 = 80 days.

🎙️ The total amount of 'work' remains constant. By calculating the total work-days, we can easily find the time required for a different number of workers.

Worked Example 5: Digging a Trench

  • Problem: If 12 men can dig a trench 100m long in 8 days, how many men can dig a trench 200m long in 4 days?
  • Solution Steps:
  • 1. Men & Length: Direct Proportion (More length, more men).
  • 2. Men & Days: Inverse Proportion (Fewer days, more men).
  • Equation: (M1 × D1 / L1) = (M2 × D2 / L2)
  • (12 × 8 / 100) = (M2 × 4 / 200)
  • M2 = (12 × 8 × 200) / (100 × 4) = 48 men.

🎙️ By carefully analyzing the direct and inverse relationships, we can set up a combined proportion. Remember, for direct, the quantity goes in the denominator if the other is in the numerator, and for inverse, it goes in the numerator.

Proportionality in Speed, Time & Distance

  • Distance = Speed × Time.
  • If speed is constant, Distance ∝ Time (Direct).
  • If time is constant, Distance ∝ Speed (Direct).
  • If distance is constant, Speed ∝ 1/Time (Inverse).

🎙️ These three quantities are fundamentally linked. Depending on which one is constant, the relationship between the other two changes. This is a very practical application in daily life.

Worked Example 6: Filling a Tank (Combined Work)

  • Problem: A tap fills a tank in 6 hours. Another tap fills it in 12 hours. How long will they take to fill the tank together?
  • Solution Steps:
  • 1. Tap 1's rate: 1/6 tank per hour.
  • 2. Tap 2's rate: 1/12 tank per hour.
  • 3. Combined rate = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4 tank per hour.
  • 4. Time to fill together = 1 / (combined rate) = 1 / (1/4) = 4 hours.

🎙️ When working together, their individual rates add up. The total time taken is the reciprocal of their combined work rate. This is a common type of problem in time and work.
