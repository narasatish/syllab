What are Exponents?

  • A shorthand for repeated multiplication.
  • Shows how many times a number (base) is multiplied by itself.
  • Example: 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 = 2⁵

🎙️ Exponents are a powerful way to write repeated multiplication in a much shorter form. Instead of writing '2 multiplied by itself five times', we can simply write '2 to the power of 5'. Here, 2 is the base, and 5 is the exponent.

Understanding the Parts

  • Base: The number being multiplied repeatedly.
  • Exponent (or Power): How many times the base is multiplied.
  • Result: The value obtained is called the 'power'.

🎙️ Let's look at an example like 3 raised to the power of 4, or 3⁴. Here, 3 is our 'base', the number that gets multiplied. The small 4 written above it is the 'exponent', telling us to multiply 3 by itself 4 times. The entire expression, 3⁴, is called the 'power'.

Why Do We Need Exponents?

  • To write very large numbers compactly.
  • To express very small numbers conveniently.
  • Simplifies calculations and understanding.

🎙️ Imagine writing the distance from Earth to the Sun: 150,000,000,000 meters. That's a lot of zeros! Exponents help us write this as 1.5 x 10¹¹. Similarly, for tiny things like the size of a virus, exponents make it manageable. They are incredibly useful in science, engineering, and daily life.

Law 1: Multiplying Powers with the Same Base

  • When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents.
  • Formula: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
  • Example: 2³ × 2² = 2³⁺² = 2⁵

🎙️ Our first law is about multiplying powers. If you have two powers with the same base, like 2³ and 2², and you want to multiply them, you simply add their exponents. So, 2³ × 2² becomes 2 to the power of (3+2), which is 2⁵. Simple, right?

Real Life: Bacterial Growth

  • A bacterium doubles every hour.
  • After 2 hours: 2¹ × 2¹ = 2² bacteria.
  • After 3 hours: 2² × 2¹ = 2³ bacteria.

🎙️ Think about bacteria multiplying. If one bacterium doubles every hour, after one hour you have 2¹ bacteria. After another hour, each of those doubles, so you have 2¹ × 2¹ = 2² bacteria. If it continues for 3 hours, it's 2³ bacteria. This shows how exponents help track rapid growth.

Worked Example: Product Rule

  • Simplify: 3⁴ × 3⁵
  • Solution: 3⁴ × 3⁵ = 3⁴⁺⁵ = 3⁹
  • Result: 3⁹ = 19683

🎙️ Let's try an example. To simplify 3⁴ × 3⁵, we first notice the base is the same (3). Then, we add the exponents: 4 + 5 = 9. So, the answer is 3⁹. Calculating 3⁹ means 3 multiplied by itself 9 times, which gives us 19683.

Law 2: Dividing Powers with the Same Base

  • When dividing powers with the same base, subtract the exponents.
  • Formula: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ (where a ≠ 0)
  • Example: 5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁶⁻² = 5⁴

🎙️ Our second law deals with division. When you divide powers that have the same base, you subtract the exponent of the denominator from the exponent of the numerator. So, 5⁶ divided by 5² becomes 5 to the power of (6-2), which is 5⁴.

Real Life: Sharing equally

  • You have 2⁵ candies.
  • You want to share them among 2² friends.
  • Each friend gets 2⁵⁻² = 2³ candies.

🎙️ Imagine you have a big jar with 2⁵, or 32, candies. You want to share these equally among 2², or 4, friends. How many candies does each friend get? Using the quotient rule, it's 2⁵ ÷ 2² = 2⁵⁻² = 2³ candies per friend, which is 8 candies each.

Law 3: Power of a Power

  • When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.
  • Formula: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
  • Example: (3²)⁴ = 3²ˣ⁴ = 3⁸

🎙️ Our third law is about a 'power of a power'. If you have an expression like (3²)⁴, it means 3² is multiplied by itself 4 times. Instead of writing that out, you simply multiply the exponents: 2 multiplied by 4 equals 8. So, (3²)⁴ becomes 3⁸.

Real Life: Area Scaling

  • A square field has side length 2 meters.
  • If we double the side length to (2 x 2) meters, new side is 2².
  • New area becomes (2²)² = 2⁴ square meters.

🎙️ Imagine a square plot of land with sides of 2 meters. Its area is 2². If you decide to make a new plot where each side is doubled, meaning each side is now (2 x 2) or 2² meters, the new area would be (2²)², which simplifies to 2⁴ square meters. This shows how exponents grow quickly.

Law 4: The Zero Exponent

  • Any non-zero number raised to the power of zero is 1.
  • Formula: a⁰ = 1 (where a ≠ 0)
  • Example: 100⁰ = 1, (-5)⁰ = 1

🎙️ This is a fun one! Any number, except zero itself, raised to the power of zero is always equal to 1. Whether it's a big number like 100 or a negative number like -5, if its exponent is zero, the answer is 1. This rule comes directly from the quotient rule, where aᵐ/aᵐ = aᵐ⁻ᵐ = a⁰ = 1.

Law 5: Negative Exponents

  • A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the base raised to the positive exponent.
  • Formula: a⁻ᵐ = 1/aᵐ (where a ≠ 0)
  • Example: 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

🎙️ Negative exponents don't mean the number is negative! They mean you take the reciprocal of the base and make the exponent positive. So, 2⁻³ means 1 divided by 2³, which is 1/8. This is very useful for writing very small numbers.

Worked Example: Negative Exponents

  • Simplify: 5⁻²
  • Solution: 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25
  • Simplify: (1/3)⁻³
  • Solution: (1/3)⁻³ = 3³ = 27

🎙️ Let's simplify 5⁻². Since the exponent is negative, we take the reciprocal of 5 (which is 1/5) and make the exponent positive, so it becomes 1/5², or 1/25. For (1/3)⁻³, the reciprocal of 1/3 is 3, so it becomes 3³, which is 27.

Standard Form: Scientific Notation

  • A way to write very large or very small numbers.
  • Expressed as k × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ k < 10 and n is an integer.
  • Example: 3,000,000 = 3 × 10⁶

🎙️ Standard form, also known as scientific notation, is a special application of exponents. It's used to write extremely large or small numbers in a compact and easy-to-read way. The number is written as a product of a number between 1 and 10 (including 1) and a power of 10.

Worked Example: Standard Form

  • Write 45,000,000 in standard form.
  • Solution: Move decimal 7 places left. 4.5 × 10⁷
  • Write 0.000000082 in standard form.
  • Solution: Move decimal 8 places right. 8.2 × 10⁻⁸

🎙️ To write 45,000,000 in standard form, we move the decimal point until we have a number between 1 and 10, which is 4.5. We moved it 7 places to the left, so it's 4.5 × 10⁷. For 0.000000082, we move the decimal 8 places to the right to get 8.2. Since we moved right, the exponent is negative: 8.2 × 10⁻⁸.
