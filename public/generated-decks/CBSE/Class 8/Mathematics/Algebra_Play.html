Variables & Constants: The Known & Unknown

  • Think of a variable as a 'mystery box' (x, y, a).
  • Its value can change or vary.
  • A constant is a fixed number (5, 100, -3).
  • Its value never changes.

🎙️ Just like in our ladoo example, 'x' can represent the unknown number of ladoos. Constants are like the fixed number of ladoos your friend gave you – that number doesn't change.

Terms, Factors, & Coefficients: Breaking It Down

  • Terms: Parts of an expression separated by + or - (e.g., in 3x+5, terms are 3x and 5).
  • Factors: Products that form a term (e.g., in 3x, factors are 3 and x).
  • Coefficient: The numerical factor of a term (e.g., in 3x, 3 is the coefficient).

🎙️ Understanding these parts is crucial. Think of an expression as a building, terms as rooms, factors as walls, and coefficients as the paint color on those walls!

Monomials & Binomials: Counting Terms

  • Monomial: An expression with only ONE term. (e.g., 5x, 7y², -3)
  • Binomial: An expression with TWO terms. (e.g., x + 5, 2a - 3b)

🎙️ The prefix 'mono' means one, and 'bi' means two. So, it's easy to remember: monomial has one term, binomial has two terms.

Trinomials & Polynomials: More Terms!

  • Trinomial: An expression with THREE terms. (e.g., x² + 2x + 1, a - b + c)
  • Polynomial: An expression with ONE or MORE terms. (e.g., 4x³ - 2x² + x - 5)

🎙️ Now, 'tri' means three, so a trinomial has three terms. 'Poly' means many, so a polynomial is a general term for any expression with one or more terms. All monomials, binomials, and trinomials are also polynomials!

Like & Unlike Terms: Friends or Strangers?

  • Like Terms: Terms with the SAME variables raised to the SAME powers. (e.g., 3x and 5x, 2y² and -7y²)
  • Unlike Terms: Terms with DIFFERENT variables or different powers. (e.g., 3x and 5y, 2x² and 2x³)

🎙️ Think of it like sorting fruits: you can add apples to apples, but you can't add apples to oranges directly. Only like terms can be added or subtracted!

Adding Algebraic Expressions: Grouping Friends

  • Step 1: Write the expressions, placing like terms together.
  • Step 2: Add the coefficients of the like terms.
  • Example: (3x + 5) + (2x - 3)
  • Solution: (3x + 2x) + (5 - 3) = 5x + 2

🎙️ When adding, we simply combine the 'friends' – the like terms. It's like counting how many apples you have in total after combining two baskets.

Subtracting Algebraic Expressions: Changing Signs

  • Step 1: Change the sign of each term in the expression being subtracted.
  • Step 2: Then, proceed as in addition (group like terms and add coefficients).
  • Example: (5y + 7) - (2y + 3)
  • Solution: (5y + 7) + (-2y - 3) = (5y - 2y) + (7 - 3) = 3y + 4

🎙️ Subtraction can be tricky! Remember to distribute the minus sign to EVERY term in the second expression. It's like turning a positive into a negative, or vice-versa, before combining.

Monomial by Polynomial: Distribute the Joy!

  • Multiply the monomial by EACH term of the polynomial.
  • Use the distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

  • Solution: (2x * 3x) + (2x * 5) = 6x² + 10x

Polynomial by Polynomial: Every Term Matters!

  • Multiply EACH term of the first polynomial by EACH term of the second polynomial.
  • Then, combine any like terms.
  • Example: (x + 2)(x + 3)
  • Solution: x(x+3) + 2(x+3) = x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6

🎙️ This is like a proper introduction! Each person from the first group shakes hands with every person from the second group. Make sure you don't miss any combinations!

Dividing Monomials: Sharing Equally!

  • Divide the numerical coefficients.
  • Divide the variables. Remember aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ.

Dividing Polynomial by Monomial: Term by Term!

  • Divide EACH term of the polynomial by the monomial.
  • Example: (6x³ + 4x² - 2x) / (2x)
  • Solution: (6x³/2x) + (4x²/2x) - (2x/2x) = 3x² + 2x - 1

🎙️ Just like distributing in multiplication, here you're 'undistributing' in division. Each term in the polynomial gets divided separately by the monomial.

Algebraic Identities: Smart Shortcuts!

  • An identity is an equation that is true for ALL values of its variables.
  • They are powerful tools to simplify calculations and factorize expressions.
  • Learning them saves time and effort in solving problems.

🎙️ Think of identities as secret formulas that always work! Once you know them, you can solve complex problems much faster than doing long multiplications.

Identity 1: (a+b)² = a²+2ab+b²

  • (a+b)² means (a+b) * (a+b).
  • Expanding it gives a² + ab + ba + b² = a² + 2ab + b².
  • Example: (x+3)² = x² + 2(x)(3) + 3² = x² + 6x + 9

🎙️ This identity is often used. Imagine a square with side length (a+b). Its area is (a+b)². You can divide it into smaller squares and rectangles to see how a²+2ab+b² comes about.

Identity 2: (a-b)² = a²-2ab+b²

  • (a-b)² means (a-b) * (a-b).
  • Expanding it gives a² - ab - ba + b² = a² - 2ab + b².
  • Example: (y-5)² = y² - 2(y)(5) + 5² = y² - 10y + 25

🎙️ Similar to the previous identity, but with a minus sign. Be careful with the signs – the middle term is negative, but the last term (b²) is still positive because (-b)*(-b) is +b².

Identity 3: (a+b)(a-b) = a²-b²

  • (a+b)(a-b) expands to a² - ab + ba - b².
  • The middle terms cancel out, leaving a² - b².
  • Example: (2x+7)(2x-7) = (2x)² - 7² = 4x² - 49

🎙️ This is a very elegant identity! It shows that when you multiply a sum and a difference of the same two terms, the middle terms always vanish, leaving you with the difference of their squares.

Identity 4: (x+a)(x+b) = x²+(a+b)x+ab

  • This identity helps when two binomials have one common term (x).
  • Expanding it gives x² + xb + ax + ab = x² + (a+b)x + ab.
  • Example: (y+2)(y+5) = y² + (2+5)y + (2*5) = y² + 7y + 10

🎙️ This identity is useful when you have binomials with different constant terms. It provides a quick way to multiply them without doing the full polynomial multiplication.

Algebraic Equations: The Balance Act!

  • An equation is a statement that two expressions are equal.
  • It always contains an 'equals' sign (=).
  • Example: x + 5 = 12 (from our ladoo problem).
  • The goal is to find the value of the unknown (variable).

🎙️ Unlike expressions, equations are like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it balanced. We are looking for the specific value of the variable that makes the statement true.

Solving Linear Equations: Finding the Unknown!

  • Step 1: Isolate the term with the variable.
  • Step 2: Perform opposite operations to both sides.
  • Step 3: Simplify to find the variable's value.
  • Example: 2x + 3 = 11
  • Solution: 2x = 11 - 3 => 2x = 8 => x = 8/2 => x = 4

🎙️ Remember the golden rule of equations: 'Do unto one side what you do unto the other!' This ensures the balance is maintained and you find the correct value for the unknown.

Common Mistakes: Don't Get Tricked!

  • Sign Errors: Forgetting to change signs in subtraction or multiplication.
  • Combining Unlike Terms: Trying to add '3x' and '2y'.
  • Distributive Property: Not multiplying *all* terms in a polynomial.
  • Identity Confusion: Mixing up (a+b)² and (a-b)² formulas.
  • Equation Balance: Performing an operation on only one side.

🎙️ These are the pitfalls where students often lose marks. Always double-check your signs, make sure you're only combining like terms, and apply the distributive property correctly. Practice makes perfect!
