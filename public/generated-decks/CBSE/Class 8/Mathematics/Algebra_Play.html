What is Algebra? The Language of Unknowns

  • Algebra uses letters (like x, y, a) to represent unknown numbers.
  • It helps us solve problems where we don't know all the values.
  • Think of it as a way to generalize math.

🎙️ Algebra is essentially a branch of mathematics where we use symbols, usually letters, to represent numbers that we don't know yet. It's like a secret code that helps us find those missing numbers. For example, if you have some chocolates and someone gives you 3 more, and now you have 7, algebra helps you find out how many you started with.

Variables and Constants: The Changing & The Fixed

  • Variables: Letters that can take different values (e.g., x, y).
  • Constants: Numbers that have a fixed value (e.g., 5, -2, 100).
  • Variables 'vary', constants stay 'constant'.

🎙️ Imagine a car's speed on a journey; it can change, right? That's a variable. But the number of wheels on a standard car is always 4 – that's a constant. So, variables are like x or y, whose values can change, while constants are like 5 or 10, whose values are fixed and never change.

Algebraic Expressions: Building Blocks of Algebra

  • A combination of variables, constants, and mathematical operations (+, -, *, /).
  • Examples: 2x + 5, y - 7, 3ab.
  • Expressions do NOT have an 'equal to' sign.

🎙️ Just like we use words to make sentences, in algebra, we combine variables and constants using operations to form expressions. Think of '2x + 5' as a phrase. It has numbers, a letter, and an operation. It's not a complete sentence yet, as it doesn't state equality to anything.

Terms: The Parts of an Expression

  • Terms are separated by '+' or '-' signs.
  • In 2x + 5, '2x' is one term and '5' is another.
  • Each term is a product of factors.

🎙️ When you look at an expression like '3x + 4y - 7', each piece separated by a plus or minus sign is called a term. So, '3x', '4y', and '-7' are the individual terms here. It's important to identify terms correctly when simplifying expressions.

Coefficients: The Number Partner

  • The numerical factor of a term with a variable.
  • In '5x', 5 is the coefficient of x.
  • In 'y', the coefficient is 1 (as 1y = y).

🎙️ The coefficient is simply the number that multiplies a variable in a term. If you have '7 apples', 7 is the coefficient of apples. Similarly, in '7x', 7 is the coefficient of x. If there's no number written, like in 'z', the coefficient is always 1, because '1z' is the same as 'z'.

Like and Unlike Terms: Grouping Friends

  • Like terms have the same variables raised to the same power (e.g., 2x and 5x).
  • Unlike terms have different variables or different powers (e.g., 2x and 5y, or 2x and 5x²).
  • Only like terms can be added or subtracted.

🎙️ Imagine you have a basket of fruits. You can easily add apples to apples, but you can't directly add apples to oranges and get 'appleoranges'. Similarly, in algebra, 'like terms' are terms that have the exact same variable part, like '3x' and '7x'. 'Unlike terms' have different variable parts, like '3x' and '7y', and cannot be combined directly.

Adding Expressions: Combine the Likes!

  • Identify and group all the like terms.
  • Add their coefficients, keeping the variable part the same.
  • Example: (2x + 3) + (4x - 1)

🎙️ When adding expressions, the golden rule is to only combine terms that are 'like' each other. First, you'll want to rearrange the expression so that all the 'x' terms are together, all the 'y' terms are together, and all the constant terms are together. Then, simply add their numerical coefficients.

Worked Example: Adding Expressions

  • Add: (5x + 3y) and (2x - y)
  • Step 1: Group like terms: (5x + 2x) + (3y - y)
  • Step 2: Add coefficients: (5+2)x + (3-1)y
  • Result: 7x + 2y

🎙️ Let's walk through an example. To add (5x + 3y) and (2x - y), we first put the 'x' terms together and the 'y' terms together. So, we have (5x + 2x) and (3y - y). Now, we just add the numbers in front of the variables: 5 plus 2 is 7, so we get 7x. And 3 minus 1 is 2, so we get 2y. The final answer is 7x + 2y.

Subtracting Expressions: Change and Combine

  • Change the sign of each term in the expression being subtracted.
  • Then, follow the rules for addition of expressions.
  • Example: (5x + 3) - (2x - 1)

🎙️ Subtracting expressions has a small trick! When you subtract an expression, you essentially add the opposite of each term in that expression. This means changing the sign of every term inside the parentheses that comes after the minus sign. After changing signs, it becomes an addition problem, and you combine like terms as before.

Worked Example: Subtracting Expressions

  • Subtract (2x - y) from (5x + 3y)
  • Step 1: Change signs of (2x - y) to (-2x + y)
  • Step 2: Add: (5x + 3y) + (-2x + y)
  • Step 3: Group: (5x - 2x) + (3y + y)
  • Result: 3x + 4y

🎙️ Let's subtract (2x - y) from (5x + 3y). First, we change the signs of the terms in (2x - y), so it becomes -2x + y. Now, we add (5x + 3y) and (-2x + y). Grouping like terms, we get (5x - 2x) + (3y + y). This simplifies to 3x + 4y. Remember that crucial step of changing the signs!

Real Life: Shopping with Algebra!

  • You buy 'x' shirts for ₹200 each and 'y' pants for ₹500 each.
  • Total cost expression: 200x + 500y.
  • If x=2, y=1, total cost = 200(2) + 500(1) = 400 + 500 = ₹900.

🎙️ Algebra is super useful even when you go shopping! If you don't know exactly how many shirts or pants you'll buy, you can use variables. For example, if each shirt costs ₹200 and each pant costs ₹500, then the total cost can be expressed as 200x + 500y. Later, when you decide to buy 2 shirts and 1 pant, you just substitute x=2 and y=1 to find the exact total.

Equations: The Balancing Act

  • An algebraic expression with an 'equal to' sign (=).
  • It states that two expressions are equal.
  • Example: 2x + 5 = 11.
  • The goal is to find the value of the unknown variable.

🎙️ Unlike expressions, equations are like complete mathematical sentences because they have an 'equal to' sign. Think of an equation as a balance scale. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it balanced. Our main goal with equations is usually to find the value of the variable that makes the equation true.

Solving Equations: The Balancing Method

  • Perform the same operation on both sides of the equation.
  • Aim to isolate the variable on one side.
  • Example: x + 3 = 7

🎙️ The balancing method is very intuitive. If you have x + 3 = 7, and you want to find 'x', you need to get rid of the '+3'. To do this, you subtract 3 from the left side. But to keep the scale balanced, you *must* also subtract 3 from the right side. So, x + 3 - 3 = 7 - 3, which gives x = 4.

Worked Example: Balancing Method

  • Solve: 3x - 4 = 11
  • Step 1: Add 4 to both sides: 3x - 4 + 4 = 11 + 4 => 3x = 15
  • Step 2: Divide both sides by 3: 3x / 3 = 15 / 3
  • Result: x = 5

🎙️ Let's solve 3x - 4 = 11 using the balancing method. First, to get rid of the -4, we add 4 to both sides, which gives us 3x = 15. Next, to isolate 'x', we need to get rid of the '3' that's multiplying 'x'. So, we divide both sides by 3. This results in x = 5. You can always check your answer by plugging it back into the original equation.

Transposing Method: Moving Terms

  • Move a term from one side of the equation to the other.
  • When moving, change its sign (from + to -, or - to +).
  • This is a shortcut for the balancing method.
  • Example: x + 3 = 7 => x = 7 - 3

🎙️ The transposing method is a quicker way to solve equations, especially once you understand the balancing method. It's like taking a term and 'transporting' it across the equal sign. But remember, when a term crosses the equal sign, it changes its operation – addition becomes subtraction, and multiplication becomes division, and vice versa.

Worked Example: Transposing Method

  • Solve: 2x + 6 = 18
  • Step 1: Transpose +6 to the right side (becomes -6): 2x = 18 - 6
  • Step 2: Simplify: 2x = 12
  • Step 3: Transpose 2 (multiplying) to the right side (becomes dividing): x = 12 / 2
  • Result: x = 6

🎙️ Let's solve 2x + 6 = 18 using transposition. First, we move the +6 to the right side, changing it to -6. So, we have 2x = 18 - 6, which simplifies to 2x = 12. Next, the '2' is multiplying 'x'. When we transpose it to the right side, it becomes division. So, x = 12 / 2, which gives us x = 6. Isn't that much faster?

Algebra in Geometry: Finding Perimeters

  • The perimeter of a rectangle with length 'l' and breadth 'b' is P = 2(l + b).
  • If length = x + 3 and breadth = x, P = 2((x+3) + x) = 2(2x + 3) = 4x + 6.
  • Algebra helps us write general formulas and solve for unknown dimensions.

🎙️ Algebra isn't just for abstract numbers; it's super useful in geometry too! For instance, we know the perimeter of a rectangle is 2 times (length + breadth). If we express length and breadth using variables, say length is x+3 and breadth is x, we can write the perimeter as an algebraic expression. This helps us find the perimeter for any value of x.

Algebra in Everyday Problems: Age Puzzles

  • Rohan is 'x' years old. His father is 3 times his age.
  • Father's age expression: 3x.
  • If Rohan is 10, father is 3(10) = 30 years old.
  • If father is 45, 3x = 45 => x = 15 (Rohan's age).

🎙️ Algebra can help us solve many real-life puzzles, like age problems! If we say Rohan's age is 'x', and his father is 3 times his age, then the father's age can be written as '3x'. If we know the father's age, we can set up an equation to find Rohan's age. This shows how algebra helps us represent relationships and solve for unknowns.
