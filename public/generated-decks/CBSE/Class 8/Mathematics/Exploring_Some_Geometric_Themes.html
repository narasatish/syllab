What is Geometry?

  • It helps us understand the space we live in.

Understanding Polygons

  • A closed figure made up of three or more line segments.
  • Segments are called sides, and they meet at vertices.
  • No curves, and sides only intersect at endpoints.

🎙️ Polygons are the building blocks of many 2D shapes. Think of a triangle or a square – these are perfect examples of polygons. Remember, they must be closed and made only of straight lines.

Concave vs. Convex Polygons

  • Convex: All interior angles are less than 180°.
  • Concave: At least one interior angle is greater than 180°.
  • A line segment connecting any two points lies entirely inside a convex polygon.

🎙️ This distinction is important! Imagine drawing a line between any two points inside the polygon. If the line always stays inside, it's convex. If it ever goes outside, it's concave. Think of a 'cave' for concave polygons.

Angles in a Triangle

  • A triangle is the simplest polygon (3 sides).
  • The sum of its interior angles is always 180°.
  • This is a foundational concept in geometry.

🎙️ We've learned this before, and it's super important! No matter the shape or size of a triangle, if you add up its three interior angles, you'll always get 180 degrees. This property is key to understanding other polygons.

Angles in a Quadrilateral

  • A quadrilateral has 4 sides and 4 angles.
  • It can be divided into two triangles by a diagonal.
  • Sum of interior angles = 2 × 180° = 360°.

🎙️ See how we can use what we know about triangles? By drawing a single diagonal in any quadrilateral, we divide it into two triangles. Since each triangle's angles sum to 180°, a quadrilateral's angles sum to 360°.

Sum of Interior Angles (Formula)

  • For a polygon with 'n' sides, it can be divided into (n-2) triangles.
  • Sum of interior angles = (n - 2) × 180°.
  • This formula works for ANY polygon!

🎙️ This is a powerful formula! If you know the number of sides of any polygon, you can instantly find the sum of all its interior angles. Just subtract 2 from the number of sides and multiply by 180 degrees. Let's try an example.

Example: Sum of Pentagon Angles

  • A pentagon has 5 sides (n=5).
  • Number of triangles = (5 - 2) = 3.
  • Sum of interior angles = 3 × 180° = 540°.

🎙️ So, for a pentagon, we have 5 sides. Using our formula, (5-2) gives us 3 triangles. Three times 180 degrees is 540 degrees. This means all the angles inside any pentagon will always add up to 540 degrees.

Regular vs. Irregular

  • Regular Polygon: All sides are equal AND all angles are equal.
  • Irregular Polygon: Sides or angles (or both) are not equal.
  • Examples: Square is regular, rectangle is irregular.

🎙️ This distinction is crucial. A square is a perfect example of a regular polygon because all its sides are equal and all its angles are 90 degrees. A rectangle, while having equal angles, usually doesn't have equal sides, making it irregular.

Exterior Angles

  • An exterior angle is formed by one side and the extension of an adjacent side.
  • Each interior angle and its corresponding exterior angle form a linear pair (sum to 180°).
  • There is one exterior angle at each vertex.

🎙️ Imagine walking around the perimeter of a polygon. At each corner, you 'turn' a certain amount. That turn is the exterior angle. It's supplementary to the interior angle at that same vertex.

↩️

Sum of Exterior Angles

  • The sum of all exterior angles of ANY convex polygon is always 360°.
  • This is true regardless of the number of sides!
  • Imagine rotating 360° as you walk around the polygon.

🎙️ This is a beautiful and simple property! Whether it's a triangle, a pentagon, or a 100-sided polygon, if you add up all its exterior angles, you will always get 360 degrees. It's like making a full turn as you complete a loop.

🎙️ Quadrilaterals are a very diverse family of polygons. From simple squares to more complex shapes, they all share the common trait of having four sides. We'll now look at some of their specific characteristics.

The Parallelogram

  • Opposite sides are parallel and equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other.

🎙️ A parallelogram is a quadrilateral where opposite sides are parallel. This simple rule leads to several important properties: opposite sides are also equal, opposite angles are equal, and its diagonals cut each other exactly in half.

Special Parallelograms

  • Rhombus: All four sides are equal.
  • Rectangle: All four angles are 90°.
  • Square: All sides equal AND all angles 90° (both rhombus and rectangle).

🎙️ These are special cases of parallelograms. A rhombus adds the condition of equal sides. A rectangle adds the condition of right angles. And a square combines both, making it the 'most regular' of these quadrilaterals.

Other Quadrilaterals

  • Trapezium (or Trapezoid): At least one pair of opposite sides is parallel.
  • Kite: Two distinct pairs of equal-length adjacent sides.
  • Diagonals of a kite are perpendicular.

🎙️ Not all quadrilaterals are parallelograms. A trapezium has at least one pair of parallel sides. A kite, as the name suggests, resembles a flying kite, with its unique property of having two pairs of equal adjacent sides.

Exploring 3D Shapes (Solids)

  • Shapes that have length, width, and height (depth).
  • Also called solids or polyhedra (if made of flat faces).
  • Key features: Faces, Edges, Vertices.

🎙️ Until now, we've mostly discussed 2D shapes that lie flat. But the world around us is 3D! These shapes have volume and occupy space. We describe them using faces (flat surfaces), edges (where faces meet), and vertices (corners).

Euler's Formula

  • For any simple polyhedron (no holes), the formula holds true.
  • Faces (F) + Vertices (V) - Edges (E) = 2.
  • F + V - E = 2 is a powerful relationship.

🎙️ This is a fascinating formula discovered by the mathematician Euler! It provides a simple relationship between the number of faces, vertices, and edges of any polyhedron. Let's try it for a cube: 6 faces + 8 vertices - 12 edges = 2. It works!
