Introducing Polygons

  • Closed figures
  • Made up of three or more line segments
  • Segments meet only at endpoints
  • No curves or open ends

🎙️ Now, let's talk about polygons. The word 'polygon' comes from 'poly' meaning many, and 'gon' meaning angle. They are closed figures formed by straight lines.

Elements of a Polygon

  • Sides (line segments)
  • Vertices (points where sides meet)
  • Diagonals (segments connecting non-adjacent vertices)
  • Interior Angles (inside the polygon)

🎙️ Every polygon has specific parts that define it. The sides form its boundary, vertices are its corners, and diagonals connect corners that aren't next to each other.

Convex and Concave Polygons

  • Convex: All interior angles less than 180°, all diagonals inside
  • Concave: At least one interior angle greater than 180°, at least one diagonal outside

🎙️ Polygons can be further classified as convex or concave. Think of it like this: a convex polygon 'bulges' outwards, while a concave one 'caves in' somewhere.

Regular and Irregular Polygons

  • Regular Polygon: All sides are equal AND all angles are equal
  • Irregular Polygon: Sides are not all equal OR angles are not all equal

🎙️ A special type of polygon is a regular polygon, where everything is perfectly balanced – all sides and all angles are the same. Otherwise, it's irregular.

Sum of Interior Angles

  • Formula: (n - 2) × 180°, where 'n' is the number of sides
  • We can divide any polygon into (n-2) triangles
  • Each triangle's angles sum to 180°

🎙️ How do we find the total measure of all angles inside a polygon? There's a clever trick: we can break any polygon into triangles, and we know the sum of angles in a triangle.

Example: Interior Angles of a Pentagon

  • A pentagon has 5 sides (n=5)
  • Sum of interior angles = (5 - 2) × 180°
  • Sum = 3 × 180° = 540°

🎙️ Let's apply the formula to a pentagon. Since it has 5 sides, we substitute 'n' with 5 and perform the calculation. You'll find the total is 540 degrees.

Exterior Angles of a Polygon

  • Formed by extending one side of the polygon
  • An interior angle and its adjacent exterior angle form a linear pair (sum to 180°)

🎙️ Just as there are angles inside a polygon, there are also angles outside. If you extend a side, the angle formed with the next side is called an exterior angle.

↩️
Outside Turn
13 / 25

Sum of Exterior Angles: A Special Property

  • For ANY convex polygon, the sum of all its exterior angles (one at each vertex) is always 360°
  • This holds true regardless of the number of sides

🎙️ This is a truly remarkable property! Imagine walking around the perimeter of a polygon; each exterior angle represents how much you turn at each corner. By the time you get back to your starting point, you've made a full 360-degree turn.

🌍
Full Circle
14 / 25

Example: Exterior Angles of a Hexagon

  • A hexagon has 6 sides
  • Sum of exterior angles is always 360°
  • If it's a regular hexagon, each exterior angle = 360° / 6 = 60°

🎙️ Let's see this in action with a hexagon. Whether it's regular or irregular, the sum of its exterior angles will always be 360 degrees. For a regular one, finding each angle is simple division.

Introducing Quadrilaterals

  • A polygon with exactly four sides
  • The sum of its interior angles is always 360°
  • Derived from 'quadri' (four) and 'latus' (side)

🎙️ Now, let's focus on a very common and important type of polygon: the quadrilateral. These are four-sided figures, and they have some unique properties.

Types of Quadrilaterals

  • Parallelogram: Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel

Properties of a Parallelogram

  • Opposite sides are parallel and equal
  • Opposite angles are equal
  • Consecutive angles are supplementary (add to 180°)
  • Diagonals bisect each other

🎙️ The parallelogram is a very versatile quadrilateral. Remember these four key properties; they are crucial for solving problems related to parallelograms.

Properties of a Rectangle

  • All properties of a parallelogram
  • All four angles are right angles (90°)
  • Diagonals are equal in length

🎙️ A rectangle is essentially a parallelogram with right angles. This simple addition gives it two very important extra properties, especially regarding its diagonals.

▭

Properties of a Rhombus

  • All properties of a parallelogram
  • All four sides are equal in length
  • Diagonals bisect each other at right angles
  • Diagonals bisect the angles at the vertices

🎙️ The rhombus is a parallelogram where all sides are equal. Its diagonals have some fascinating properties, intersecting at 90 degrees and cutting the vertex angles in half.

◇

Properties of a Square

  • All properties of a parallelogram, rectangle, AND rhombus
  • All four sides are equal
  • All four angles are right angles (90°)
  • Diagonals are equal and bisect each other at right angles

🎙️ The square is the superstar of quadrilaterals! It combines all the best features of parallelograms, rectangles, and rhombuses, making it a highly symmetrical and useful shape.
