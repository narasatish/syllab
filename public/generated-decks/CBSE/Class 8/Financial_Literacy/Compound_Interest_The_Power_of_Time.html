Understanding Simple Interest (S.I.)

  • Interest calculated ONLY on the original amount.
  • The interest amount remains fixed.
  • It's straightforward and easy to calculate.

🎙️ Simple Interest is the basic way to calculate the interest on a principal amount. It's calculated only on the original amount of money you invested or borrowed, and it remains fixed for the entire period. It's like earning the same amount of extra money every year.

The S.I. Formula: P x R x T

  • Formula: SI = (P x R x T) / 100
  • P = Principal (original money)
  • R = Rate of Interest (per annum)
  • T = Time (in years)

🎙️ To calculate Simple Interest, we use a simple formula: P multiplied by R multiplied by T, all divided by 100. Here, 'P' is the Principal (original money), 'R' is the Rate of Interest (percentage per year), and 'T' is the Time (in years).

S.I. Example: Saving for a Bicycle

  • Deposit ₹5000 for 2 years.
  • Rate: 10% Simple Interest per annum.
  • Interest = ₹1000.
  • Total Amount = ₹6000.

🎙️ Let's say you deposit ₹5000 in a bank for 2 years at a 10% simple interest rate per annum. Using the formula, your interest would be ₹5000 x 10 x 2 / 100, which equals ₹1000. So, after 2 years, you'll have ₹5000 + ₹1000 = ₹6000.

S.I. vs. C.I.: What's the BIG Difference?

  • S.I. calculates interest only on Principal.
  • C.I. calculates interest on Principal + accumulated Interest.
  • C.I. leads to much higher growth.

🎙️ The main difference is that Simple Interest is calculated only on the original principal. But Compound Interest is calculated on the principal AND the accumulated interest from previous periods. This means your interest starts earning interest!

C.I. Step-by-Step: Year 1

  • Principal (P) = ₹1000, Rate = 10%.
  • Interest (Year 1) = ₹100.
  • Amount (End of Year 1) = ₹1100.

🎙️ Let's take ₹1000 at 10% C.I. for 3 years. For Year 1, the interest is calculated on the initial principal of ₹1000. So, I = (1000 x 10 x 1)/100 = ₹100. The amount at the end of Year 1 is ₹1000 + ₹100 = ₹1100.

C.I. Step-by-Step: Year 2

  • New Principal = ₹1100 (from Year 1 amount).
  • Interest (Year 2) = ₹110.
  • Amount (End of Year 2) = ₹1210.

🎙️ Now, for Year 2, the principal changes! It becomes the amount from the end of Year 1, which is ₹1100. So, I = (1100 x 10 x 1)/100 = ₹110. The amount at the end of Year 2 is ₹1100 + ₹110 = ₹1210. Notice how the interest earned is more than in Year 1!

The C.I. Formula: Finding the Amount (A)

  • Formula for Amount (A): A = P (1 + R/100)^N
  • Used for annual compounding.
  • Directly calculates the total amount.

🎙️ Calculating year-by-year can be long. Luckily, there's a formula for the final Amount (A) when interest is compounded annually: A = P (1 + R/100)^N. This formula directly gives you the total amount after 'N' years.

Breaking Down the C.I. Formula Terms

  • P = Principal (initial amount).
  • R = Rate of Interest (per annum).
  • N = Number of Compounding Periods (years).

🎙️ In the formula A = P (1 + R/100)^N: 'P' is the Principal (initial amount), 'R' is the Rate of Interest (per annum), and 'N' is the Number of Compounding Periods (usually years). Make sure 'R' is in percentage form and 'N' is in years.

C.I. Example: Using the Amount Formula

  • Find Amount on ₹5000 at 8% C.I. for 2 years.
  • P=₹5000, R=8, N=2.
  • A = ₹5000 (1 + 8/100)^2 = ₹5832.

🎙️ Find the Amount on ₹5000 at 8% per annum compounded annually for 2 years. Here, P=₹5000, R=8, N=2. A = 5000 (1 + 8/100)^2 = 5000 x 1.1664 = ₹5832. This is the total amount you'll have.

Finding the Actual Compound Interest

  • Once 'A' is found, C.I. is easy.
  • Formula: C.I. = A - P.
  • Subtract original principal from final amount.

🎙️ Once you've found the total Amount (A) using the formula, calculating the actual Compound Interest (C.I.) is simple. Just subtract the original Principal (P) from the final Amount (A). So, C.I. = A - P.

C.I. Example: Finding the Interest

  • From previous example: A = ₹5832, P = ₹5000.
  • Compound Interest = ₹5832 - ₹5000.
  • Total C.I. earned = ₹832.

🎙️ Using the previous example, where P=₹5000, R=8%, N=2 years, and the Amount (A) was ₹5832. The Compound Interest earned would be A - P = ₹5832 - ₹5000 = ₹832. This is the extra money you gained.

When Does Interest Get Added? Compounding Period

  • Interest can be compounded annually, half-yearly, or quarterly.
  • More frequent compounding leads to faster growth.
  • Interest is added and starts earning sooner.

🎙️ Interest isn't always compounded annually. It can also be compounded half-yearly (twice a year) or quarterly (four times a year). The more frequently it's compounded, the faster your money grows, because interest is added and starts earning sooner!

Half-Yearly Compounding: An Example

  • Rate (R) becomes R/2.
  • Time (N) becomes N x 2.
  • Apply formula: A = P (1 + (R/2)/100)^(N*2).

🎙️ If interest is compounded half-yearly, the annual rate (R) is divided by 2, and the number of periods (N) is multiplied by 2. So, for 1 year, it's 2 periods. For 2 years, it's 4 periods. The formula remains A = P (1 + (R/2)/100)^(N*2).

Watch Out! Common Mistakes in C.I.
