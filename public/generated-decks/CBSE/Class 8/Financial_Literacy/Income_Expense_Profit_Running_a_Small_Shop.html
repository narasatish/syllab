Understanding Income

  • Income is the money a shop receives.
  • It's the earnings from selling goods or services.
  • It's the total money coming INTO the business.

🎙️ Think of income as all the money that comes into the shop. When a customer buys something, the money they pay becomes the shop's income. It's the 'plus' side of a shop's finances.

⬆️

Income Example: Selling Biscuits

  • A shop sells 5 packets of biscuits.
  • Each packet costs ₹10.
  • Total Income = 5 packets * ₹10/packet = ₹50.

🎙️ Let's say a customer comes to your shop and buys 5 packets of biscuits, and each packet costs ₹10. The total money you receive from this sale is ₹50. This ₹50 is your income from that transaction.

🎙️ For most small shops, the biggest source of income comes directly from selling their goods. A grocer earns from selling vegetables, a tailor from stitching clothes, and a barber from giving haircuts. This is called sales income.

Understanding Expenses

  • Expenses are the money a shop spends.
  • Costs incurred to run the business.
  • It's the total money going OUT of the business.

🎙️ Now, let's look at the other side of the coin: expenses. These are all the costs that the shop has to pay to operate. Just like you spend money on school supplies, a shop spends money on things it needs to function.

⬇️
Money Going Out

Expense Example: Buying Bread

  • A shop buys 3 loaves of bread.
  • Each loaf costs ₹30.
  • Total Expense = 3 loaves * ₹30/loaf = ₹90.

🎙️ If your shop sells bread, you first need to buy it from a supplier. If you buy 3 loaves of bread at ₹30 each, you spend ₹90. This ₹90 is an expense because it's money going out of your shop to get products to sell.

Types of Expenses (Part 1)

  • Rent: Money paid for using the shop space.
  • Salaries/Wages: Money paid to employees.
  • Cost of Goods Sold: Money paid for items to sell.

🎙️ There are many types of expenses. Rent is what you pay to use the shop building. If you have helpers, you pay them salaries. And most importantly, you spend money to buy the products you plan to sell to customers.

What is Profit?

  • Profit occurs when Income is MORE than Expenses.
  • It's the money left AFTER paying all costs.
  • Profit means the shop is doing well!

🎙️ This is the goal for any shopkeeper! Profit means that the money you earned from selling goods is more than the money you spent to run the shop. It's the positive difference, showing that your business is successful.

✅
Success!

The Profit Formula

  • Profit = Total Income - Total Expenses
  • This formula helps calculate how much money is gained.
  • It's a simple subtraction!

🎙️ To find out if your shop made a profit, you simply subtract your total expenses from your total income. If the result is a positive number, congratulations, you've made a profit!

Profit Example Calculation

  • A small fruit shop's Income = ₹500 (from selling fruits).
  • Its Expenses = ₹300 (to buy fruits, pay rent).
  • Profit = ₹500 - ₹300 = ₹200.

🎙️ Here's a simple example: If a fruit shop earns ₹500 in a day and spends ₹300 on buying fruits and other costs, then the profit for that day is ₹200. This ₹200 is the actual gain for the shopkeeper.

What is Loss?

  • Loss occurs when Expenses are MORE than Income.
  • It means the shop spent more than it earned.
  • A loss means the shop is struggling.

🎙️ Unfortunately, not all businesses make a profit all the time. Sometimes, a shop might spend more money than it earns. When this happens, it's called a loss. It's the opposite of profit and means the business is not doing well financially.

❌
Problem!

The Loss Formula

  • Loss = Total Expenses - Total Income
  • This formula helps calculate how much money is lost.
  • It also uses simple subtraction!

🎙️ If you find that your expenses are higher than your income, you calculate the loss by subtracting the total income from the total expenses. The result will be the amount of money the shop has lost.

Loss Example Calculation

Worked Example: Stationery Shop

  • Monthly Sales: ₹10,000
  • Cost of goods bought: ₹4,000
  • Rent: ₹2,000, Electricity: ₹500, Helper's Salary: ₹1,500

🎙️ Let's calculate for our stationery shop. Total Income is ₹10,000. Total Expenses = ₹4,000 (goods) + ₹2,000 (rent) + ₹500 (electricity) + ₹1,500 (salary) = ₹8,000. So, Profit = ₹10,000 - ₹8,000 = ₹2,000. The shop made a profit!

Why Track Income and Expenses?

  • To know if the shop is making money (profit) or losing money (loss).
  • To make smart decisions about the business.
  • To plan for the future and growth.

🎙️ Keeping track of all income and expenses is crucial. It's like keeping a score in a game. It tells the shop owner exactly how their business is performing, helping them understand where money is coming from and where it's going, and how to improve.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Income: Money received by the shop.
  • Expenses: Money spent by the shop.
  • Profit: Income > Expenses.
  • Loss: Expenses > Income.

🎙️ Let's quickly review what we've learned. Remember, income is money coming in, expenses are money going out. If income is more, it's a profit – good for the shop! If expenses are more, it's a loss – a challenge for the shop.
