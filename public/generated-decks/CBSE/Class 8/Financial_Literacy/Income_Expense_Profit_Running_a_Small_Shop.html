What is a Small Shop?

  • A place where goods or services are sold.
  • Like your local kirana store or a juice stall.
  • They aim to serve customers and earn money.

🎙️ Think about the shops you visit every day – the grocery store, the stationery shop, or even a small tea stall. These are all small shops, and they play a big role in our community.

What is Income?

  • Money received by selling goods or services.
  • It's the total money a shop earns.
  • Also called 'Revenue' or 'Sales'.

🎙️ Income is simply the money that comes *into* the shop. When you buy a packet of chips, the money you pay becomes income for the shop owner. It's the first step to understanding how a business works.

Income for a Samosa Shop

  • A shop sells 100 samosas at ₹10 each.
  • Total money earned = 100 samosas × ₹10.
  • Income = ₹1000.

🎙️ Imagine a small shop selling delicious samosas. If they sell 100 samosas in a day, and each samosa costs ₹10, their total income for that day from samosas would be ₹1000. Simple, right?

What are Expenses?

  • Money spent to run the shop.
  • Costs involved in making or selling products.
  • Examples: rent, raw materials, salaries.

🎙️ Now, to make those samosas or keep the shop open, the owner has to spend money. These are called expenses. Think of it as the cost of doing business.

🧾
Bills to Pay

Expenses for the Samosa Shop

  • Flour, potatoes, spices: ₹300
  • Cooking oil: ₹100
  • Shop rent (per day): ₹50
  • Total expenses = ₹300 + ₹100 + ₹50 = ₹450.

🎙️ For our samosa shop, the owner needs to buy ingredients like flour, potatoes, and spices. They also pay for cooking oil and a portion of the shop's rent for the day. All these are expenses.

Fixed vs. Variable Expenses

  • Expenses can be categorised.
  • Fixed expenses don't change with sales.
  • Variable expenses change with sales volume.

🎙️ Not all expenses are the same. Some costs remain constant no matter how many samosas are sold, while others directly depend on the number of items produced or sold. Let's look closer.

Understanding Fixed Expenses

  • Costs that remain the same.
  • Don't depend on how much is sold.
  • Examples: shop rent, insurance, owner's salary.

🎙️ Fixed expenses are like the steady bills you pay every month. Whether the samosa shop sells 10 samosas or 1000, the shop rent for the month will remain the same. These are predictable costs.

🏠
Constant Costs
9 / 25

Understanding Variable Expenses

  • Costs that change with production/sales.
  • More sales = more variable expense.
  • Examples: raw materials, packaging, delivery.

🎙️ Variable expenses, on the other hand, fluctuate. If the samosa shop makes more samosas, it will need more flour, more potatoes, and more cooking oil. These costs vary directly with the business activity.

What is Profit?

  • The money left after all expenses are paid.
  • Income - Expenses = Profit.
  • The main goal for most businesses.

🎙️ After a shop owner earns income and pays all their expenses, if there's money remaining, that's called profit! It's the reward for running the business successfully and efficiently.

📈
Growing Money

Calculating Samosa Shop Profit

  • Income = ₹1000
  • Expenses = ₹450
  • Profit = Income - Expenses
  • Profit = ₹1000 - ₹450 = ₹550.

🎙️ Using our samosa shop example, we can now calculate the profit. With an income of ₹1000 and expenses of ₹450, the shop owner made a profit of ₹550. This is a good day's work!

What is a Loss?

  • When expenses are more than income.
  • Expenses > Income = Loss.
  • A shop cannot survive long with continuous losses.

🎙️ Sometimes, a shop might spend more money than it earns. When this happens, it's called a loss. A shop owner always tries to avoid losses, as it means they are losing money.

📉
Decreasing Money
14 / 25

When a Shop Faces Loss

  • Imagine a shop's Income = ₹800
  • But its Expenses = ₹1000
  • Loss = Expenses - Income
  • Loss = ₹1000 - ₹800 = ₹200.

🎙️ Consider a different scenario. If a shop only earns ₹800 but has expenses of ₹1000, it means the shop has a loss of ₹200. This is a situation the owner would want to fix quickly.

Managing Your Shop's Finances

  • Track all income carefully.
  • Keep a record of every expense.
  • Regularly calculate profit or loss.

🎙️ To run a successful small shop, good financial management is key. This means diligently noting down every sale and every purchase, and regularly checking if the shop is making money or losing it.

Rohan's Profit or Loss?

How to Increase Profit

  • Increase Income: Sell more, offer new products.
  • Reduce Expenses: Buy raw materials wisely, save electricity.
  • Manage inventory efficiently to avoid waste.

🎙️ Every shop owner wants to increase their profit. There are two main ways: either earn more money by selling more or at a better price, or spend less money by being smart about expenses. It's a balance!
