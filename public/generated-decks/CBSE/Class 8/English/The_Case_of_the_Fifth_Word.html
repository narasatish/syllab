How to Approach a Mysterious Text

Example: Changing Word Order, Changing Meaning

  • Sentence 1: 'Only I saw the dog barking.' (No one else did)
  • Sentence 2: 'I only saw the dog barking.' (Didn't hear it, just saw)
  • The word 'only' changes its impact.
  • Position determines the focus of the sentence.

🎙️ Let's look at a simple example. Consider 'Only I saw the dog barking.' This means I was the sole person who witnessed it. Now, compare it to 'I only saw the dog barking.' This implies I saw it, but perhaps I didn't hear it, or I didn't do anything else. The small word 'only' completely changes the emphasis and meaning just by moving its position.

Understanding Context with an Example

  • Word: 'Bank'.
  • Context 1: 'He sat by the river bank.' (Side of a river)
  • Context 2: 'He went to the bank to withdraw money.' (Financial institution)
  • The surrounding words clarify the meaning.

🎙️ Let's take the word 'bank'. By itself, it has multiple meanings. But if I say, 'He sat by the river bank,' you immediately know I mean the land alongside a river. If I say, 'He went to the bank to withdraw money,' you understand I'm referring to a financial institution. The words around 'bank' provide the necessary context to choose the correct meaning.
