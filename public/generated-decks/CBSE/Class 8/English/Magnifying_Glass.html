What Does 'Magnifying' Mean in English?

  • It means to examine text very closely.
  • Focus on specific words, phrases, and sentences.
  • Uncover deeper meanings and connections.

🎙️ When we 'magnify' a text, we're not just reading it once. We're going back, re-reading, and asking questions about every little detail. Why did the author use *that* word? What does *this* sentence imply?

🔍
Deep Dive into Text
6 / 30

Example: Magnifying a Sentence

  • Original: 'The old man walked home.'
  • Magnified: 'The *frail*, *elderly* man *shuffled* home, his shoulders *stooped*.'
  • Notice the added details and imagery.

🎙️ See how the second sentence, though longer, gives us so much more information? We don't just know he's old; we know he's frail, elderly, walks slowly, and is perhaps burdened. That's the power of magnifying!

One Sentence, Many Details

How to Observe Textual Details

  • Read actively, not passively.
  • Highlight or underline key words.
  • Ask 'who, what, where, when, why, how?' about small parts.

🎙️ Don't just let your eyes glide over the words. Stop, think, and question. What adjectives are used? What actions are described? Every word is a choice the author made, and that choice has meaning.

Example: Observing Character Appearance

  • Text: 'She wore a *faded* saree and carried a *worn* jute bag.'

🎙️ Instead of just noting 'she wore a saree,' we zoom in on 'faded' and 'worn.' These adjectives aren't accidental. They paint a picture of her circumstances and can hint at her character or struggles.

Magnifying the Plot: Key Events

🎙️ Don't just list events. Ask yourself: 'What *caused* this event?' and 'What *effect* did it have on the characters or the story?' This helps you understand the flow and logic of the narrative.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not reading the text carefully enough.
  • Jumping to conclusions without evidence.
  • Ignoring smaller details as unimportant.
  • Summarizing instead of analyzing.

🎙️ Even experienced detectives can make mistakes! The biggest one is rushing. Always take your time, go back to the text, and make sure your observations are supported by what the author actually wrote, not just what you *think*.

29 / 30

Exam Tips: Acing Your Analysis

  • Read the question *twice*.
  • Underline keywords in the text.
  • Support your answers with direct quotes.
  • Structure your answer (point, evidence, explanation).

🎙️ In exams, it's not enough to just understand; you need to show your understanding clearly. Always refer back to the text. Your 'magnified' observations are most valuable when you can prove them with evidence from the story.
