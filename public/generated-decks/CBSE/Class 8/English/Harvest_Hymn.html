The Music of Words: Rhyme Scheme

  • The pattern of rhymes at the end of lines in a poem.

  • Adds musicality and rhythm to the poem.

Identifying Rhyme in the Hymn

  • "The fields are ripe, the harvest **near** (A)"
  • "With golden grain, so bright and **clear** (A)"
  • "We thank the Lord for all His **grace** (B)"
  • "And bounty shown in every **place** (B)"

🎙️ Let's look at a stanza from our poem and identify its rhyme scheme. You'll often find simple, consistent patterns in hymns, like the AABB pattern we see here, which makes them pleasant to read and remember.

Giving Life to Objects: Personification

  • Attributing human qualities or actions to inanimate objects or animals.
  • Makes descriptions more vivid and relatable.
  • Helps us connect with non-human elements.

🎙️ Have you ever heard the wind 'whisper' or the sun 'smile'? That's personification! It's a clever way poets give human characteristics to non-human things, making them seem alive and expressive.

🎭
Human Traits
14 / 25

Personification in the Hymn

  • "The fields rejoice in golden **hue**." (Fields cannot rejoice like humans).
  • "The sun, a watchful eye, looks **down**." (Sun does not have eyes).

🎙️ In 'Harvest Hymn', we might find instances of personification. For example, if the poem says 'the fields rejoice', it's giving the fields a human emotion, making the scene more lively and impactful.
