Vocabulary Focus: 'Parched'

  • Meaning: Extremely dry, especially from heat; thirsty.
  • Example: The parched earth cracked under the scorching sun.
  • Analogy: Like a throat that has had no water for hours.

🎙️ Imagine walking on a desert floor, that's how dry 'parched' feels. It's often used to describe land or throats, emphasizing an urgent need for water.

🏜️
Dry Desert

Vocabulary Focus: 'Languish'

  • Meaning: To suffer from being forced to remain in an unpleasant place or situation.
  • Example: The plants began to languish in the prolonged heat.
  • Analogy: Like a student who feels bored and loses interest.

🎙️ When something 'languishes', it loses energy, becomes weak, and often fades away due to harsh or unfavorable conditions. It suggests a slow decline.

Literary Device: Personification

  • Giving human qualities or actions to inanimate objects or animals.
  • Example: 'The thirsty earth cried out for water.'
  • It adds emotion and life to descriptions.

🎙️ It's like making non-human things act or feel like people. This device helps us connect with nature on a deeper, more emotional level.

Literary Device: Simile

  • Comparing two different things using 'like' or 'as'.
  • Example: 'The clouds gathered like dark, heavy blankets.'
  • Makes descriptions more vivid and understandable.

🎙️ Similes are direct comparisons that help us understand something new by relating it to something familiar. They create clear, imaginative pictures.

⚖️
Comparison with 'Like/As'
19 / 30

Literary Device: Metaphor

  • Directly stating one thing *is* another, without 'like' or 'as'.
  • Example: 'The rain was a silver blessing from the sky.'
  • Creates a strong, imaginative identification between two things.

🎙️ Metaphors are stronger comparisons. They say something *is* something else, creating a powerful image that can be more impactful than a simile.
