  • Example: The country suffered a severe drought last year.

Vocabulary 2: Parched

  • Meaning: Extremely dry, thirsty
  • Often used to describe land or throats
  • Example: The parched earth cracked under the relentless sun.

🎙️ When something is parched, it's not just a little dry; it's severely lacking moisture. This word evokes a strong sense of discomfort and need for water.

Literary Device: Personification

  • Meaning: Giving human qualities to inanimate objects or animals
  • Makes descriptions more engaging and relatable
  • Example: 'The wind whispered secrets through the dry leaves.'

🎙️ Personification helps us connect with non-human elements as if they have feelings or actions like us. The author might describe the rain as a 'long-awaited friend'.

Grammar Focus: Simple Past Tense

  • Used for actions completed in the past
  • Often uses '-ed' ending for regular verbs
  • Example: 'The sun *shone* brightly yesterday.'

🎙️ Many narratives, including 'Waiting for the Rain,' are told in the simple past tense. It describes events that have already happened, moving the story forward.

Example: Verbs in Simple Past

  • Wait -> Waited (regular)
  • Hope -> Hoped (regular)
  • Come -> Came (irregular)
  • Feel -> Felt (irregular)

🎙️ Remember that not all verbs follow the regular '-ed' rule. Pay attention to irregular verbs like 'come' and 'feel' when you are reading or writing in the past tense.

Grammar Focus: Adjectives for Description

🎙️ Adjectives are crucial for painting those vivid pictures we discussed with imagery. They help us understand the state of the land, the feelings of the people, and the nature of the rain.
