Decentralisation: Sharing the Power

  • Shifting power from central to local bodies.
  • Making governance more accessible and responsive.
  • Empowering communities to manage their own affairs.

🎙️ Decentralisation is like sharing a big cake. Instead of one person holding all the power, it's divided and distributed to smaller, local groups. This ensures that decisions are made closer to the people who are directly affected by them.

Steps to Decentralisation in India

  • 1. Identify local issues and needs.
  • 2. Form local self-governing bodies.
  • 3. Transfer powers and funds to these bodies.
  • 4. Enable local communities to participate directly.

🎙️ In India, decentralisation wasn't always present. It was a conscious effort through constitutional amendments to ensure that local communities had a formal structure and the necessary authority to govern themselves effectively.

The Panchayati Raj System: India's Local Governance 🏛️

  • A system of local self-governance in rural India.
  • Established to empower villages and districts.
  • Made mandatory by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

🎙️ The Panchayati Raj System is the backbone of grassroots democracy in rural India. It's a three-tier system designed to ensure that governance reaches every village, block, and district, giving power to the people at the local level.

🇮🇳
India's Local System

Gram Sabha: The Village Assembly

  • The general assembly of all adult voters (18+) in a village.
  • The most fundamental unit of the Panchayati Raj System.
  • Acts as the 'watchdog' over the Gram Panchayat.

🎙️ Think of the Gram Sabha as the parliament of the village. Every adult who lives in the village and is registered to vote is a member of the Gram Sabha. It's where direct democracy truly happens at the local level.

👥
Village Voters
11 / 30

How the Gram Sabha Works

  • Meets at least two to four times a year.
  • Discusses village development plans and budgets.
  • Approves the work of the Gram Panchayat.
  • Ensures transparency and accountability.

🎙️ During Gram Sabha meetings, villagers can directly question their elected representatives, review financial statements, and suggest new projects. This direct participation is key to making local governance effective and democratic.

🎙️ The Gram Sabha empowers ordinary villagers to hold their elected representatives accountable. It's a powerful tool to ensure that development projects truly benefit the community and that funds are used wisely.

Gram Panchayat: The Village Council

  • Elected body responsible for implementing development programs.
  • Consists of a Sarpanch (President) and Ward Members (Panchs).
  • Elected for a term of five years.

🎙️ While the Gram Sabha is the assembly of all voters, the Gram Panchayat is the executive committee. These are the people you elect to actually carry out the decisions and manage the day-to-day affairs of the village.

🏛️
Local Governing Body

Who Makes Up the Gram Panchayat?

  • Sarpanch/Pradhan: The head of the Gram Panchayat, directly elected.
  • Ward Members (Panchs): Elected from different wards (smaller areas) of the village.
  • Gram Sachiv (Secretary): Appointed by the government, not elected.

🎙️ The Sarpanch and Panchs are the elected representatives, directly chosen by the villagers. The Gram Sachiv is a government employee who helps with administrative tasks, record-keeping, and calling Gram Sabha meetings.

What Does the Gram Panchayat Do?

  • Construction and maintenance of water sources, roads, drains.
  • Levying and collecting local taxes.
  • Implementing government schemes for employment and poverty.
  • Maintaining village records and resolving minor disputes.

🎙️ From ensuring clean drinking water to building roads and managing local markets, the Gram Panchayat has a wide range of responsibilities that directly impact the daily lives of villagers. They are the frontline service providers.

Panchayat Samiti: The Block Level

  • The second tier of the Panchayati Raj System.
  • Works at the 'block' or 'mandal' level (group of villages).
  • Coordinates the work of several Gram Panchayats.

🎙️ Above the individual Gram Panchayats, we have the Panchayat Samiti. It acts as a link between the Gram Panchayats and the Zila Parishad, helping to coordinate projects and distribute resources across a larger area.

🔗
Connecting Villages

Zila Parishad: The District Level

  • The highest tier of the Panchayati Raj System.
  • Works at the 'district' level.
  • Makes development plans for the entire district.
  • Supervises the work of Panchayat Samitis and Gram Panchayats.

🎙️ The Zila Parishad is like the district-level parliament. It oversees the entire district's development, allocates funds, and ensures that policies are implemented effectively across all blocks and villages.

Common Mistakes to Avoid! ⚠️

  • Confusing Gram Sabha with Gram Panchayat. (One is an assembly, other is a council)
  • Forgetting the 73rd Amendment Act (1992) for Panchayati Raj.
  • Not understanding the three-tier structure (Village, Block, District).

🎙️ These are common pitfalls. Remember, the Gram Sabha is *all* the voters, while the Gram Panchayat is the *elected body*. And the 73rd Amendment is historically significant for making this system mandatory.
