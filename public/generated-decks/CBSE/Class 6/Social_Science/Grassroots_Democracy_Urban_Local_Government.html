What is Urban Local Government (ULG)?

  • It's a system of self-governance in urban areas.
  • Manages public services and local affairs.
  • Brings democracy to the grassroots level.

🎙️ Urban Local Government, or ULG, refers to the local bodies that govern cities and towns. They are elected by the people living in those areas to manage their local needs and provide essential services. It's democracy working right where you live!

🏛️
Local Governance

Why ULG is Important: Bringing Power Closer

  • Local problems are best understood and solved locally.
  • It empowers citizens to participate in decision-making.
  • Ensures accountability to the local population.

🎙️ Imagine if decisions about your local park were made by someone sitting far away in the capital city! ULG ensures that local issues, like waste management or street lighting, are handled by people who live in the community and understand its specific needs. Types of Urban Areas in India

  • India has different types of urban settlements.
  • Based on population size and development level.
  • Each type has a specific local governing body.

🎙️ Just like homes come in different sizes, from small flats to large bungalows, urban areas also vary greatly. In India, we classify urban areas into three main types based on their population and how developed they are. Each type has its own specific local governing body.

1. Nagar Panchayat (Town Area Committee)

  • For areas transitioning from rural to urban.
  • Has a population between 10,000 and 20,000.
  • Manages basic services for these evolving towns.

🎙️ The Nagar Panchayat is for areas that are not fully rural anymore, but not quite a big city either. Think of a growing village that's becoming a small town. It helps these areas develop and provides essential services as they transition.

🏘️
Transitioning Towns
11 / 30

2. Municipal Council (Nagar Palika Parishad)

  • Governs smaller cities or larger towns.
  • Population generally between 20,000 and 10 Lakhs.
  • Provides a wider range of services than a Nagar Panchayat.

🎙️ The Municipal Council, also known as Nagar Palika Parishad, is responsible for the administration of smaller cities. These are more developed than Nagar Panchayats and require more complex services, from public health to education.

🏢
Smaller Cities
12 / 30

3. Municipal Corporation (Mahanagar Palika)

  • For large metropolitan cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata.
  • Population usually above 10 Lakhs (1 million).
  • Has extensive powers and resources to manage vast urban areas.

🎙️ The Municipal Corporation, or Mahanagar Palika, is the biggest and most powerful urban local body. It governs our largest cities, like Bengaluru, Chennai, or Hyderabad, managing their complex needs, vast infrastructure, and diverse populations.

🌆
Mega Cities

How ULG Works: The Ward System

  • Each city/town is divided into smaller areas called 'Wards'.
  • Each ward elects one representative, called a 'Councillor'.
  • Ensures every part of the city has a voice.

🎙️ To manage a large city effectively, it's divided into smaller units called wards. Each ward is like a neighbourhood. This system allows for better representation and ensures that the specific needs of different areas within the city are addressed.

🗺️
City Divisions
15 / 30

Elections: Choosing Our Leaders

  • Citizens aged 18 and above vote in ULG elections.
  • They elect Ward Councillors and sometimes the Mayor directly.
  • This is how people choose their local representatives.

🎙️ Just like we vote for our state and national leaders, we also vote for our local leaders in ULG elections. Every adult citizen has the right to vote and choose who will represent their ward and manage their city. This is the essence of democracy at the local level.

🗳️
Your Vote
16 / 30

Steps to Elect a Councillor

  • The election process is simple and democratic.

🎙️ The process of electing a councillor involves several clear steps. First, the city is divided into wards. Then, candidates who want to represent a ward file their nominations. On election day, eligible voters cast their votes, and the candidate who receives the most votes becomes the Ward Councillor.

Role of a Councillor: Your Local Voice

  • Represents the people of their ward.
  • Raises local issues in ULG meetings.
  • Works to solve problems like sanitation, water, and roads.

🎙️ Your Ward Councillor is your direct link to the Urban Local Government. They listen to the problems and needs of the people in their ward, bring these issues to the attention of the ULG, and work towards finding solutions for your neighbourhood.

Local Voice

The Mayor or Chairperson: Head of the ULG

  • The Mayor (in Municipal Corporations) or Chairperson (in Municipal Councils/Nagar Panchayats) is the head.
  • Presides over meetings and guides the ULG's work.
  • Acts as the first citizen of the city/town.

🎙️ While councillors represent their wards, the Mayor or Chairperson is the overall head of the Urban Local Government. They lead the meetings, oversee the administration, and represent the entire city or town. For example, in Delhi, the Mayor of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi leads the city's civic body.

Sources of Income for ULG: How They Fund Services

  • Property tax on houses and buildings.
  • Water tax, professional tax.
  • Grants from State Government.
  • Fees for licenses and services.

🎙️ Providing all these services costs money! Urban Local Governments collect funds from various sources. A major source is property tax, which is paid by property owners. They also receive grants from the state government and collect fees for various services they provide.

Real Indian Example: Swachh Bharat Abhiyan & ULBs

  • A national cleanliness campaign launched in 2014.
  • Urban Local Bodies played a crucial role in its success.
  • They managed waste collection, built public toilets, and promoted cleanliness.
  • Example: Indore has been consistently ranked India's cleanest city by its ULG efforts.

🎙️ The Swachh Bharat Abhiyan is a great example of how ULGs work. This nationwide campaign focused on cleanliness, and it was the Municipal Corporations and Councils that were at the forefront, implementing strategies, managing waste, and educating citizens, making cities like Indore shining examples.
