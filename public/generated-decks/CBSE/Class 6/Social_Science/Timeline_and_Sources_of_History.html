Understanding BC (Before Christ) / BCE (Before Common Era)

  • Counts backwards from a central point (Year 0)
  • Larger numbers mean earlier events
  • Example: 500 BCE is before 200 BCE

🎙️ Think of a number line. BC or BCE counts backwards from a central point, traditionally the birth of Jesus Christ (Year 0). The larger the number, the earlier the event.

Understanding AD (Anno Domini) / CE (Common Era)

  • Counts forwards from the central point (Year 0)
  • Larger numbers mean more recent events
  • Example: 1000 CE is before 2000 CE

🎙️ AD or CE counts forwards from that same central point. Most events we discuss today fall within the Common Era. The larger the number, the more recent the event.

BC vs. AD: The Flow of Time

  • BC events precede AD events
  • Time moves from larger BC numbers to smaller BC numbers, then to larger AD numbers
  • Essential for chronological understanding

🎙️ It's important to remember that BC events happened *before* AD events. For example, the Indus Valley Civilization existed thousands of years before the Mughal Empire.

Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500 BCE)
Mauryan Empire (c. 322 BCE)
Gupta Empire (c. 320 CE)
Mughal Empire (c. 1526 CE)

Don't Get Confused! Common Mistakes

  • Confusing BC and AD (larger BC is older)
  • Misclassifying sources (e.g., calling a monument 'literary')
  • Not providing specific examples for answers
  • Forgetting to explain 'why' or 'how' in answers

🎙️ Students often confuse BC and AD, thinking a larger BC number is more recent. Another common mistake is misclassifying sources, like calling a monument a literary source.

Ace Your Exams: Important Points

  • Focus on 'Why' and 'How' questions (e.g., 'Why are coins important?')
  • Always provide concrete Indian examples for each source type
  • Practice chronological ordering of events (BC/AD)
  • Clearly define and differentiate between source types

🎙️ Expect 'Why' and 'How' questions. For example, 'Why are inscriptions important sources?' or 'How do archaeologists help us understand history?' Always provide examples to support your answers.
