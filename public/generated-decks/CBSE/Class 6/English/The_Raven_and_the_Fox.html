What is a Fable?

  • Short stories with animals as characters.
  • Animals often talk and act like humans.
  • Always teaches a moral lesson.

🎙️ A fable is a special kind of story, usually short, where animals play the main roles. These animals behave and speak like people, and the most important part is that every fable has a moral, a lesson for us to learn.

📚
Story Book

Key Elements of a Fable

  • Characters: Usually animals.
  • Plot: Simple, clear events.
  • Moral: A lesson for life.

🎙️ Fables have distinct parts. They feature animal characters, a straightforward plot that's easy to follow, and always conclude with a clear moral. The moral is often stated directly at the end of the story.

Understanding Flattery

  • Excessive praise, often insincere.
  • Used to gain favour or advantage.
  • Can make people feel good, but be careful!

🎙️ Flattery is when someone gives you a lot of praise, sometimes too much, and it might not be entirely true. People use flattery to get something they want from you. It's important to recognize it.

Lesson 1: Beware of Flattery

  • Don't trust everyone who praises you.
  • Some praise might have a hidden motive.
  • Think critically about compliments.

🎙️ The first lesson is to be cautious of flattery. When someone praises you excessively, especially if it feels insincere, consider if they might want something from you. Always think about their intentions.

⚠️
Warning Sign
15 / 25

Real-Life Example: Flattery

  • A friend says, "You're the best artist! Can you draw my whole project?"
  • A shopkeeper says, "This dress is perfect for you! You look like a model!" (even if it doesn't fit well).
  • Someone praises your work only when they need help.

🎙️ Imagine someone suddenly praising your drawing skills, then immediately asking you to do their homework art. Or a shopkeeper complimenting you endlessly to make a sale. These are examples where flattery might be used for personal gain.

16 / 25

Lesson 2: Use Your Brain Wisely

  • Don't let pride cloud your judgment.
  • Think about the consequences of your actions.
  • A wise person is not easily tricked.

🎙️ The second lesson is about wisdom. The raven's pride made him forget his good sense. Always use your brain to think things through and consider what might happen before you act, especially when someone is trying to influence you.

🧠
Wise Brain
17 / 25

Real-Life Example: Wisdom

  • Before sharing a secret, think if the person is trustworthy.
  • Before buying something expensive, research it and compare prices.
  • Don't believe everything you hear online without checking facts.

🎙️ In daily life, wisdom means making smart choices. For instance, if someone tells you exciting news, a wise person would check if it's true before spreading it. It's about being thoughtful and not impulsive.
