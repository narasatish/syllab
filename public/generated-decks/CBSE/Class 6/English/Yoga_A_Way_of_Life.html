The Ancient Roots of Yoga

  • Yoga originated in ancient India, dating back over 5,000 years.
  • It was developed by Rishis (sages) who sought well-being and enlightenment.
  • The earliest teachings were found in ancient scriptures like the Vedas.

🎙️ Imagine people thousands of years ago, observing nature and their own bodies, trying to find ways to live healthier and happier. That's how Yoga began, right here in India.

Ashtanga Yoga: The Eightfold Path

  • Patanjali's Yoga Sutras describe these 8 steps, known as Ashtanga Yoga.
  • These steps are like a roadmap for achieving inner peace and well-being.
  • They are not just physical exercises but a holistic way of living.

🎙️ These 8 limbs cover everything from how we behave with others to how we control our minds. They are a systematic approach to spiritual growth and self-realization.

Yama: How We Act Towards Others

  • Yama means 'moral discipline' or 'ethical conduct'.
  • It's about how we behave with the world and other people.
  • Examples: Non-violence (Ahimsa), truthfulness (Satya), honesty (Asteya).

🎙️ Yama teaches us to be good citizens and kind human beings. When we follow these principles, we create a more peaceful environment around us.

⚖️
Justice Scales
18 / 30

Niyama: How We Treat Ourselves

  • Niyama means 'self-observation' or 'personal discipline'.
  • It's about our inner discipline, habits, and self-care.
  • Examples: Cleanliness (Saucha), contentment (Santosha), self-study (Svadhyaya).

🎙️ While Yama focuses outwards, Niyama focuses inwards. It's about developing good habits and a positive attitude towards ourselves, which is essential for inner happiness.

🤔
Self-Reflection

Asana: The Yoga Poses

  • Asana means 'posture' or 'pose'. These are the physical exercises in Yoga.
  • They make our body strong, flexible, and steady, preparing it for deeper practices.
  • Examples include Tadasana (Mountain Pose) or Vrikshasana (Tree Pose).

🎙️ Asanas are what most people think of when they hear 'Yoga'. These poses are designed to make our bodies comfortable and healthy, so we can sit still and focus for longer.

🤸‍♀️

Pranayama: The Power of Breath

  • Pranayama means 'breath control' or 'extension of life force'.
  • It involves special breathing techniques, like deep or alternate nostril breathing.
  • It helps calm the mind, energize the body, and improve focus.

🎙️ Our breath is powerful! By learning to control it, we can control our energy levels and emotions. Pranayama is a fantastic way to feel instantly calmer or more alert.

🌬️
Deep Breath

Pratyahara: Turning Inward

  • Pratyahara means 'withdrawal of the senses'.
  • It's about pulling our attention away from outside distractions like sounds or sights.
  • Imagine closing your eyes to focus only on an inner thought or feeling.

🎙️ In our busy world, our senses are constantly bombarded. Pratyahara teaches us to temporarily disconnect from external stimuli to better hear our inner voice.

🧘
Inner Focus
23 / 30

Dharana: Focused Attention

  • Dharana means 'concentration' or 'holding the mind'.
  • It's about holding your mind steadily on one single point or object.
  • Like focusing intently on a single candle flame or a specific sound.

🎙️ Have you ever tried to focus really hard on just one thing? That's Dharana. It's the practice of training our minds to stay put, rather than jumping from thought to thought.

🎯
Target Focus
24 / 30

Dhyana: Deep Meditation

  • Dhyana means 'meditation'. It's a continuous, effortless flow of concentration.
  • When your mind is quiet, peaceful, and fully absorbed, you are in Dhyana.
  • It's a state of deep awareness without distraction, like watching a calm river.

🎙️ Dhyana is a deeper state than Dharana. It's when your concentration becomes so natural that you don't even try to focus; your mind just stays still and aware.

🧘‍♂️
Meditating Person
25 / 30

Samadhi: Ultimate Union

  • Samadhi means 'absorption' or 'enlightenment'. It's the highest state of Yoga.
  • It's a feeling of complete oneness, pure bliss, and profound peace.
  • In this state, the meditator merges with the object of meditation, experiencing ultimate harmony.

🎙️ Samadhi is the goal of Yoga, a state where all distractions vanish, and you experience pure consciousness and joy. It's a profound feeling of being connected to everything.

🌌
Universe/Oneness

Common Mistakes in Yoga

  • Thinking Yoga is only for very flexible people – it's for everyone!
  • Not breathing properly during poses, which reduces benefits.
  • Doing advanced Yoga without proper guidance, which can lead to injury.

🎙️ It's natural to make mistakes when starting something new. Remember, Yoga is a journey, not a competition. Listen to your body and always seek guidance from a qualified teacher.
