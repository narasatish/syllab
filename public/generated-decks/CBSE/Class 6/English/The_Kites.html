How Kites Fly: The Power of Wind!

  • Wind is the primary force that lifts a kite.
  • It pushes against the kite's surface.
  • A steady breeze is perfect for flying kites.

🎙️ Imagine the wind as an invisible hand pushing against the kite. This push, when strong enough, helps the kite overcome gravity and rise into the sky. Without wind, a kite cannot fly!

How Kites Fly: Lift and Drag

  • Lift: The upward force created by wind moving over the kite.
  • Drag: The resistance force that slows the kite down.
  • A good kite balances lift and drag to stay airborne.

🎙️ When wind flows over the kite, it creates an upward pull called 'lift'. At the same time, the air resists the kite's movement, creating 'drag'. For a kite to fly well, the lift must be greater than the drag and gravity.

Lift vs. Gravity

Fly Safe, Play Safe!

  • Always fly kites in open spaces, away from roads.
  • Avoid power lines and electric poles – they are dangerous!
  • Don't use metal or wet strings.
  • Have an adult supervise young children.

🎙️ While kite flying is fun, safety is very important. Always choose a safe, open area like a park. Never fly near electricity wires, as it can be extremely dangerous. Always prioritize your safety and the safety of others.

Similes: Painting Pictures with Words

  • A simile compares two different things.
  • Uses 'like' or 'as' to make the comparison.
  • Example: 'It pulls like a ship / With only a sail'.

🎙️ Similes help writers create vivid images. When the poem says the kite 'pulls like a ship', it helps us imagine the strong tug of the kite, similar to how a ship's sail catches the wind. It's a powerful comparison!

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing simile with metaphor.
  • Not explaining literary devices in your own words.
  • Giving one-word answers instead of complete sentences.
  • Ignoring the poem's overall message/theme.

🎙️ Many students make these common errors. Remember, similes use 'like' or 'as'. Always explain what a literary device means and how it's used. And always provide full, well-explained answers in your exams.
