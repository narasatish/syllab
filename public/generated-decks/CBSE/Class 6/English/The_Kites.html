What is a Kite?

  • An aircraft tethered to the ground.
  • Flies on wind power.
  • Loved by children and adults.

🎙️ A kite is essentially a tethered aircraft, meaning it's tied to the ground by a string. It uses the power of the wind to lift and stay in the air. Kites are a source of great joy and entertainment for people of all ages.

  • Originated in ancient China.
  • Used for fishing, measuring, signals.
  • Spread to other parts of the world.

🎙️ Did you know kites have a long and fascinating history? They were first invented in ancient China, thousands of years ago. People used them for various purposes, from fishing to sending signals, long before they became popular toys.

Anatomy of a Kite

  • Sail/Body: The main fabric.
  • Spars: The frame that gives shape.
  • Bridle/Line: Connects to the string.

🎙️ Just like any machine, a kite has different parts that work together. The 'sail' is the colorful fabric part, and 'spars' are the sticks that form its skeleton. The 'bridle' is the set of lines that connect to your main flying string.

How Kites Take Flight

  • Wind pushes the kite up.
  • Shape creates 'lift'.
  • String keeps it from flying away.

🎙️ How does a kite actually fly? It's all about the wind! The wind pushes against the kite's surface, and its special shape creates an upward force called 'lift'. The string is crucial, as it controls the kite and prevents it from disappearing into the sky.

"The Kites" - Our Poem

  • A beautiful description of a kite's journey.
  • Written by Harry Behn.
  • Let's read the first stanza.

🎙️ Now, let's dive into our poem, 'The Kites', written by Harry Behn. This poem beautifully captures the excitement and movements of a kite in the sky. We'll read it stanza by stanza to understand its meaning.

8 / 25

Vocabulary - Stanza 1

  • Dips: Goes down quickly.
  • Dives: Plunges headfirst.
  • Soars: Flies high in the air.

🎙️ In the first stanza, you'll find words like 'dips' and 'dives'. 'Dips' means to go down quickly and then come back up, like a bird touching the water. 'Dives' is a more forceful downward movement, similar to how a swimmer dives into a pool. 'Soars' means to fly very high.

Finding the Rhyme

  • Words that sound similar.
  • Creates a musical effect.
  • Helps remember the poem.

🎙️ Poets often use rhyming words to make their poems sound pleasant and rhythmic. Listen for words at the end of lines that have similar sounds. This pattern of rhyming words is called the rhyme scheme, and it adds to the poem's musicality.

Stanza 2 - The Kite's Dance

  • The kite struggles against the wind.
  • It flaps and flutters.
  • A beautiful, active image.

🎙️ As we move to the second stanza, pay attention to how the poet describes the kite's movements. It's not just flying; it's almost as if it's dancing and fighting with the wind, creating a very vivid picture in our minds.

More New Words

  • Rags: Old, torn pieces of cloth.
  • Flaps: Moves up and down quickly.
  • Shakes: Moves back and forth rapidly.

🎙️ In this stanza, 'rags' refers to old, worn-out pieces of cloth, which might describe how the kite looks after much flying. 'Flaps' means to move something up and down, like a bird flapping its wings. The kite 'shakes' as it struggles in the wind.

🎙️ Imagery is a powerful tool poets use. It's when words create vivid pictures in our minds, appealing to our senses. In this stanza, the poet uses imagery to make us almost see and feel the kite's struggle and graceful movements in the sky.

Stanza 3 - The Kite's Freedom

  • The kite is truly free.
  • It rides the wind effortlessly.
  • A moment of pure joy.

🎙️ In the third and final stanza, the poem reaches a beautiful conclusion. The kite, after its struggle, seems to find its rhythm and truly embrace its freedom in the sky. Let's read and appreciate this moment of liberation.

14 / 25

Key Terms from Stanza 3

  • Tugs: Pulls sharply or suddenly.
  • Rides: Travels on something, like the wind.
  • Sail: To move smoothly through the air.

🎙️ Here, 'tugs' describes a sharp pull, perhaps the string pulling the kite or the wind pulling the kite. When the kite 'rides' the wind, it means it's traveling along with the wind's current. To 'sail' implies moving gracefully and smoothly, much like a boat on water.

Giving Life to the Kite

  • Giving human qualities to non-human things.
  • Makes the poem more lively.
  • The kite acts like a person.

🎙️ One of the beautiful literary devices used in this poem is personification. This is when an inanimate object, like the kite, is given human actions or feelings. It makes the kite seem alive and allows us to connect with it on a deeper level.

Fly High, Fly Safe!

  • Avoid power lines and busy roads.
  • Use strong, safe string.
  • Fly in open areas, away from trees.

🎙️ While kite flying is fun, safety is very important. Always choose an open area away from electrical wires and roads. Using strong, non-metallic string is crucial to prevent accidents. Remember, safety first!

Uttarayan - The Kite Festival
