Introducing Prime Numbers

  • Have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves.
  • Cannot be divided evenly by other numbers (except 1 and itself).

🎙️ Prime numbers are very special! They have only two friends: the number 1 and themselves. No other number can divide them perfectly.

Introducing Composite Numbers

  • Have more than two factors.
  • Can be divided evenly by other numbers.

🎙️ Composite numbers are the opposite of prime numbers. They are 'composed' of many factors, meaning they can be divided by more than just 1 and themselves.

The Special Case of Number 1

  • Has only ONE factor: itself.
  • Not prime (needs two factors).
  • Not composite (needs more than two factors).

🎙️ Number 1 is unique! It only has one factor, which is 1 itself. Because it doesn't have exactly two factors, it's not prime. And because it doesn't have more than two factors, it's not composite. It's in a category of its own!

🎙️ Mathematicians developed clever ways to find prime numbers. One ancient and very useful method is called the Sieve of Eratosthenes. It's like a filter for numbers!

Sieve of Eratosthenes (Simplified)

  • 1. List numbers.
  • 2. Cross out 1. Circle 2, cross out its multiples.
  • 3. Circle next uncrossed number (3), cross out its multiples. Repeat.

🎙️ Imagine a grid of numbers. You start by crossing out 1. Then, you circle 2, and cross out all numbers that are multiples of 2. Next, you find the smallest uncrossed number, which is 3, circle it, and cross out all its multiples. You keep doing this, and the circled numbers are your primes!

1List numbers 1-20
2Cross out 1
3Circle 2, cross out 4,6,8...

Real-Life Example: Arranging Objects

  • 7 candies: Only 1 row of 7 or 7 rows of 1. (Prime)
  • 10 candies: 1x10, 2x5, 5x2, 10x1. (Composite)

🎙️ Think about arranging candies. If you have 7 candies, you can only arrange them in one long row or seven single piles. That's a prime number. But if you have 10 candies, you can make rows of 1, 2, 5, or 10. That's a composite number!

Method: Factor Tree

  • Start with the number.
  • Find two factors.
  • Continue branching until all factors are prime.

🎙️ The factor tree method is a visual way to do prime factorization. You start with your number at the top, and draw branches down to its factors. You keep going until all the numbers at the ends of your branches are prime numbers.

1Start with the number
2Find two factors (any)
3Keep branching until all are prime

Worked Example: Prime Factorization of 36

  • 36 = 2 x 18
  • 18 = 2 x 9
  • 9 = 3 x 3

🎙️ Let's try 36. We can write 36 as 2 multiplied by 18. 2 is prime, so we stop there. Now break 18 into 2 times 9. Again, 2 is prime. Finally, 9 breaks into 3 times 3. Both 3s are prime. So, the prime factors of 36 are 2, 2, 3, and 3.
