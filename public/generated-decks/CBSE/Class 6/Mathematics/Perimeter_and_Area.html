Understanding Perimeter

  • The total length of the boundary of a closed figure.
  • It's like walking around the edge of a shape.
  • Measured in units of length (cm, m, km).

🎙️ Perimeter comes from 'peri' meaning around, and 'meter' meaning measure. So, it literally means to 'measure around'. Think of it as drawing an outline or putting a border around something.

🚶

Perimeter of a Rectangle

  • A rectangle has two lengths (l) and two breadths (b).
  • Perimeter = length + breadth + length + breadth
  • Formula: P = 2 × (length + breadth) or P = 2(l + b)

🎙️ Look at the rectangle. You have a longer side, which we call length, and a shorter side, which is breadth. If you walk all around it, you cover one length, then one breadth, then another length, and finally another breadth. That's why the formula is twice the sum of length and breadth.

Example: Fencing a Garden

  • A rectangular garden is 10 m long and 5 m wide.
  • How much fence wire is needed?
  • P = 2 × (10 m + 5 m) = 2 × 15 m = 30 m

🎙️ Here's a real-life example. If your garden is 10 meters long and 5 meters wide, you'd need 30 meters of wire to fence it completely. This is a practical application of finding the perimeter.

Perimeter of a Square

  • A square has four equal sides (s).
  • Perimeter = side + side + side + side
  • Formula: P = 4 × side or P = 4s

🎙️ A square is a special type of rectangle where all four sides are equal. So, instead of adding four different lengths, we can simply multiply the length of one side by four to find its perimeter.

Example: Picture Frame

  • A square picture has a side of 20 cm.
  • What is the length of the frame needed?
  • P = 4 × 20 cm = 80 cm

🎙️ If you have a square picture and want to put a frame around it, and each side of the picture is 20 cm, you'll need 80 cm of framing material. Simple, right?

Perimeter of Other Polygons

  • For any polygon, sum of the lengths of all its sides.
  • Triangle: P = a + b + c
  • Pentagon: P = s1 + s2 + s3 + s4 + s5

🎙️ The general rule for finding the perimeter of any closed shape with straight sides, called a polygon, is to just add up the lengths of all its sides. No special formula needed, just careful addition!

Understanding Area

  • The amount of surface covered by a closed figure.
  • It's the space inside the boundary.
  • Measured in square units (cm², m², km²).

🎙️ Now, let's move on to Area. While perimeter is about the boundary, area is about the space *inside* that boundary. Imagine painting a wall; the amount of paint needed depends on the wall's area.

Units of Area

  • Since area is 2-dimensional (length × breadth), its units are 'square units'.
  • If sides are in cm, area is in cm² (square centimetres).
  • If sides are in m, area is in m² (square metres).

🎙️ Because we are multiplying two lengths together (length and breadth), the unit for area becomes a 'square unit'. So, we say square centimetres or square metres, often written as cm² or m².

Area of a Rectangle

  • Imagine covering the rectangle with small squares.
  • The number of squares inside is its area.
  • Formula: Area = length × breadth (A = l × b)

🎙️ Think of a rectangle made of unit squares. If you have 4 squares along the length and 2 squares along the breadth, you have 4 × 2 = 8 squares in total. This gives us the simple formula: length times breadth.

Example: Tiling a Floor

  • A rectangular room is 8 m long and 6 m wide.
  • What is the area of the floor to be tiled?
  • A = 8 m × 6 m = 48 m²

🎙️ If you want to tile a room that is 8 meters long and 6 meters wide, you need to calculate the area of the floor. In this case, it's 48 square meters. This tells you how many square meter tiles you'd need.

Area of a Square

  • A square has all sides equal (s).
  • Area = side × side
  • Formula: A = s × s or A = s²

🎙️ For a square, since length and breadth are the same (both are 'side'), the area formula simplifies to side multiplied by side, or side squared. It's a very straightforward calculation.

Example: Chessboard

  • A chessboard has a side length of 40 cm.
  • What is the area of the chessboard?
  • A = 40 cm × 40 cm = 1600 cm²

🎙️ A chessboard is a perfect example of a square. If one side is 40 cm, its entire playing surface area is 1600 square centimetres. This helps us understand how much space it takes up.

Perimeter vs. Area

  • Perimeter: Measures the boundary (1D).
  • Area: Measures the surface inside (2D).
  • Different concepts, different units.

🎙️ It's important not to confuse perimeter and area. Perimeter is like the fence around a field, a one-dimensional measurement. Area is like the grass *within* the field, a two-dimensional measurement. They are distinct concepts.
