What is Perimeter?

  • The total length of the boundary of a closed figure.
  • It's like measuring the 'outline' of a shape.
  • Measured in units like cm, m, km.

🎙️ Perimeter is simply the total length of the boundary of any closed shape. If you were to 'unfold' the boundary and measure it with a ruler, that's the perimeter. It's always measured in linear units like centimetres, metres, or kilometres.

Perimeter of a Rectangle

  • A rectangle has two lengths and two widths.
  • Perimeter = Length + Width + Length + Width
  • Simplified: Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width)

🎙️ A rectangle has four sides, with opposite sides being equal in length. So, to find its perimeter, we add up all four sides. We can simplify this to twice the sum of its length and width, as shown in the formula.

Example: Fencing a Garden

  • A rectangular garden is 10 m long and 5 m wide.
  • Perimeter = 2 × (10 m + 5 m)
  • Perimeter = 2 × 15 m = 30 m

🎙️ Let's apply the formula! If a garden is 10 metres long and 5 metres wide, we first add the length and width, which is 15 metres. Then, we multiply this by 2 to get the total perimeter, which is 30 metres. This is how much fencing is needed!

Perimeter of a Square

  • A square has four equal sides.
  • Perimeter = Side + Side + Side + Side
  • Simplified: Perimeter = 4 × Side

🎙️ For a square, all four sides are exactly the same length. So, instead of adding the same number four times, we can simply multiply the length of one side by 4. This gives us the perimeter of the square.

What is Area?

  • The amount of surface enclosed by a closed figure.
  • It's the 'space inside' the boundary.
  • Measured in square units (cm², m²).

🎙️ Area is the measure of the flat surface a closed shape covers. It's literally the 'space inside' the perimeter. Unlike perimeter, which is a length, area is measured in square units, such as square centimetres or square metres.

🖼️

Understanding Square Units

  • A square centimetre (cm²) is the area of a square with 1 cm side.
  • A square metre (m²) is the area of a square with 1 m side.
  • We count how many unit squares fit inside a shape.

🎙️ Why 'square' units? Because we imagine covering the surface with tiny squares. If a square has a side of 1 cm, its area is 1 square cm. If it's 1 metre, it's 1 square metre. We're essentially counting how many of these unit squares can fit inside our shape.

Area of a Rectangle

  • Imagine rows of unit squares inside a rectangle.
  • Area = Length × Width
  • The product of its two different sides.

🎙️ To find the area of a rectangle, it's quite simple. We multiply its length by its width. Think of it as counting the number of unit squares in each row (length) and then multiplying by the number of rows (width). This gives you the total number of squares inside.

Area of a Square

  • A square has all sides equal.
  • Area = Side × Side
  • Also written as Side² (Side squared).

🎙️ Since all sides of a square are equal, its length and width are the same. So, to find the area of a square, we simply multiply the side length by itself. This is often written as 'side squared' because it's a side multiplied by itself.

Perimeter vs. Area: Key Differences

  • Perimeter: Measures boundary (length).
  • Area: Measures surface (space inside).
  • Perimeter units: cm, m. Area units: cm², m².

🎙️ It's important not to confuse perimeter and area. Perimeter is about the 'outside' boundary, like a fence. Area is about the 'inside' space, like a carpet. They measure different aspects and have different units.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Perimeter and Area.
  • Using wrong units (e.g., cm for area, cm² for perimeter).
  • Forgetting to multiply by 2 for rectangle perimeter.
  • Not adding all sides for irregular shapes.

🎙️ Many students mix up perimeter and area, especially their units. Always double-check what the question is asking for. Remember the formulas and units carefully to avoid these common errors.
