What is a Fraction?

  • A fraction represents a part of a whole.
  • It shows how many pieces of a whole we have.
  • The whole must be divided into equal parts.

🎙️ Imagine a delicious pizza! If you take one slice, you're taking a fraction of the whole pizza. A fraction helps us describe that specific part.

The Numerator and Denominator

  • A fraction has two main parts.
  • Numerator: The top number, tells us how many parts we HAVE.
  • Denominator: The bottom number, tells us total EQUAL parts in the whole.

🎙️ Think of a fraction like a small mathematical sandwich! The top slice is the numerator, showing what you have, and the bottom slice is the denominator, showing the total number of slices possible.

How to Write a Fraction

  • Write the numerator (parts you have) on top.
  • Draw a horizontal line (fraction bar) below it.
  • Write the denominator (total equal parts) below the line.

🎙️ It's like building a small structure. The numerator sits on top, the denominator holds it up, and the fraction bar is the bridge between them. Always remember the order!

1Numerator (Parts taken)
2Fraction Bar
3Denominator (Total parts)

Types of Fractions: Proper Fractions

  • A fraction where the Numerator is LESS than the Denominator.
  • Represents a part that is less than a whole.
  • Examples: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8.

🎙️ Proper fractions are like having part of something, but not the whole thing. The top number is always smaller than the bottom number, meaning you haven't completed a full unit yet.

✅
Less than a whole

Types of Fractions: Improper Fractions

  • A fraction where the Numerator is GREATER than or EQUAL to the Denominator.
  • Represents a value equal to or more than one whole.
  • Examples: 5/4, 7/3, 8/8.

🎙️ Improper fractions are when you have enough parts to make one whole, or even more than one whole! The numerator is either equal to or larger than the denominator.

More than a whole

Types of Fractions: Mixed Fractions

  • A combination of a whole number and a proper fraction.
  • Another way to express improper fractions.
  • Examples: 1 1/2, 2 3/4.

🎙️ Mixed fractions are a neat way to write improper fractions. They clearly show how many whole things you have, plus any leftover parts. It's a whole number and a proper fraction together.

Converting Improper to Mixed Fractions

  • Divide the numerator by the denominator.
  • The quotient is the whole number part.
  • The remainder is the new numerator, with the original denominator.

🎙️ This is like figuring out how many full cakes you can make from a pile of slices. You divide the total slices by the number of slices per cake to find the whole cakes and any leftover slices.

1Divide Numerator by Denominator
2Quotient = Whole Number
3Remainder = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same

Worked Example: Improper to Mixed

  • Convert 7/3 to a mixed fraction.
  • 7 ÷ 3 = 2 with a remainder of 1.
  • So, 7/3 = 2 1/3.

🎙️ Let's try an example. If you have 7 cookies and you want to group them into sets of 3, you can make 2 full groups, and you'll have 1 cookie left over. So, 7/3 is 2 whole and 1/3.

🍪🍪🍪🍪🍪🍪🍪

Converting Mixed to Improper Fractions

  • Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
  • Add the numerator to this product.
  • This sum becomes the new numerator, over the original denominator.

🎙️ Now, let's go the other way. If you have a mixed fraction, you can convert it back to an improper fraction. This is useful when you need to perform calculations with fractions.

2+ Numerator

Worked Example: Mixed to Improper

  • Convert 2 3/4 to an improper fraction.
  • (2 x 4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11.
  • So, 2 3/4 = 11/4.

🎙️ Imagine you have 2 whole oranges, each with 4 segments, and 3 extra segments from another orange. That's (2 * 4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11 segments in total, with each whole orange being 4 segments. So, 11/4.

Equivalent Fractions

  • Fractions that represent the same value, even if they look different.
  • Obtained by multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • Examples: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6.

🎙️ Equivalent fractions are like different names for the same amount. Just like 50 paise and half a rupee are the same value, 1/2 and 2/4 represent the same portion.

Equal Value

Simplest Form of a Fraction

  • A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1.
  • Also known as lowest terms.
  • To simplify, divide both by their highest common factor (HCF).

🎙️ The simplest form is like giving a fraction its shortest possible name. It means you can't divide both the top and bottom numbers by any number other than 1 and still get whole numbers.

🤏
Smallest Parts
24 / 25

Worked Example: Simplest Form

  • Simplify the fraction 6/12.
  • Common factors of 6 and 12 are 2, 3, 6.
  • The HCF is 6. Divide both by 6.
  • 6 ÷ 6 = 1, 12 ÷ 6 = 2. So, 6/12 simplifies to 1/2.

🎙️ Let's simplify 6/12. We find the largest number that can divide both 6 and 12, which is 6. Dividing both by 6 gives us 1/2. This means 6 parts out of 12 is the same as 1 part out of 2.
