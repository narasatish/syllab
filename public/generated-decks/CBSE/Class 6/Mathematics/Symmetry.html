What is Symmetry?

  • A shape has symmetry if it looks the same after a transformation.
  • It's about balance and proportion.
  • Imagine folding a shape exactly in half.

🎙️ Symmetry means that one part of an object is identical to another part. Think of a butterfly's wings – they are mirror images of each other. If you fold it along its body, both sides match perfectly.

The Line of Symmetry

  • An imaginary line that divides a figure into two identical halves.
  • If you fold along this line, the halves match perfectly.
  • Also called the 'axis of symmetry'.

🎙️ The most common type of symmetry we study is called line symmetry. The line of symmetry is like a mirror. Whatever is on one side of the line, is exactly replicated on the other side.

↔️
A line dividing two equal parts.

How to Find a Line of Symmetry

  • Imagine folding the shape.
  • The fold line where both halves match is the line of symmetry.
  • A shape can have one, many, or no lines of symmetry.

🎙️ To practically find a line of symmetry, you can use the folding method. If you can fold a shape along a line and the two resulting halves perfectly overlap, then that fold line is a line of symmetry.

1Take a paper shape
2Fold it exactly in half
3Check if both sides match
4The crease is the line

Drawing Lines of Symmetry

  • Use a ruler to draw a straight line.
  • Ensure it passes through the center or divides the shape equally.
  • Practice with various geometric figures.

🎙️ Once you understand the concept, you can draw the lines of symmetry directly onto a figure. Remember, the line must divide the shape into two parts that are exact mirror images of each other.

Example: Square

  • A square has four equal sides and four right angles.
  • It has lines of symmetry passing through midpoints of opposite sides.
  • It also has lines of symmetry along its diagonals.

🎙️ Let's take a square. You can fold it vertically, horizontally, and along both diagonals. Each fold will make the halves match. So, a square has four lines of symmetry.

⏹️
A square has 4 lines of symmetry.

Example: Rectangle

  • A rectangle has opposite sides equal and four right angles.
  • It has lines of symmetry passing through the midpoints of opposite sides.
  • It does NOT have lines of symmetry along its diagonals.

🎙️ Now consider a rectangle. You can fold it horizontally and vertically to get matching halves. However, if you fold it along its diagonal, the corners won't meet perfectly. Thus, a rectangle has two lines of symmetry.

▭
A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry.

Example: Isosceles Triangle

  • An isosceles triangle has two equal sides and two equal angles.
  • Its line of symmetry passes from the vertex between the equal sides.
  • It divides the base into two equal parts.

🎙️ An isosceles triangle has only one line of symmetry. This line starts from the vertex where the two equal sides meet and goes down to the midpoint of the base. Try folding a paper isosceles triangle to see this.

📐
An isosceles triangle has 1 line of symmetry.
11 / 25

Example: Equilateral Triangle

  • An equilateral triangle has all three sides equal and all three angles equal.
  • It has three lines of symmetry.
  • Each line passes from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

🎙️ Unlike an isosceles triangle, an equilateral triangle is perfectly symmetrical from all three sides. Each vertex can be the starting point for a line of symmetry that goes to the midpoint of the opposite side, giving it three lines of symmetry.

🔺
An equilateral triangle has 3 lines of symmetry.

Example: Circle

  • A circle is perfectly round.
  • Any line passing through its center is a line of symmetry.
  • Therefore, a circle has an infinite number of lines of symmetry.

🎙️ The circle is special! Imagine folding a circle. No matter how you fold it, as long as the fold passes through the center, the two halves will always match. This means it has an endless, or infinite, number of lines of symmetry.

⚪
A circle has infinite lines of symmetry.

Objects with One Line of Symmetry

  • Examples: A heart shape, a human face, a kite.
  • They can only be folded one way to match perfectly.
  • Many common objects fall into this category.

🎙️ Many everyday objects have just one line of symmetry. Think of a heart; you can only fold it vertically to get two matching halves. A human face, when viewed straight on, also has one vertical line of symmetry.

❤️
A heart shape has one line of symmetry.

Objects with Two Lines of Symmetry

  • Examples: A rectangle, a plus sign (+), an oval.
  • These shapes can be folded in two different ways.
  • Both folds result in identical halves.

🎙️ Some shapes can be divided perfectly in two different ways. A plus sign, for instance, has a horizontal and a vertical line of symmetry. An oval shape also has two lines of symmetry, one along its longer axis and one along its shorter axis.

➕➕
A plus sign has two lines of symmetry.

Objects with More Than Two Lines of Symmetry

  • Examples: A square (4), a regular pentagon (5), a regular hexagon (6).
  • These are often called 'regular polygons'.
  • The number of lines equals the number of sides for regular polygons.

🎙️ Regular polygons, like a square or a regular pentagon, have many lines of symmetry. A regular pentagon has five lines, and a regular hexagon has six. A five-pointed star, if it's regular, also has five lines of symmetry.

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
A regular star has multiple lines of symmetry.

Objects with NO Line of Symmetry

  • These shapes are called 'asymmetrical'.
  • No matter how you fold them, the halves won't match.
  • Examples: A scalene triangle, a comma, a human hand.

🎙️ Not all objects are symmetrical. A scalene triangle, where all sides and angles are different, has no line of symmetry. Your left hand, for example, cannot be folded to perfectly match your right hand; it's an asymmetrical shape.

Symmetry in Alphabets

  • Some capital letters have vertical lines of symmetry (e.g., A, M, T).
  • Some have horizontal lines of symmetry (e.g., B, C, E).
  • Some have both (e.g., H, I, X, O).
  • Some have no lines of symmetry (e.g., F, G, J, P).

🎙️ Let's look at the English alphabet. The letter 'A' has a vertical line of symmetry. The letter 'B' has a horizontal line. 'H' has both horizontal and vertical lines. And letters like 'F' or 'P' have no lines of symmetry.

🅰️
Letter 'A' has one vertical line of symmetry.

Symmetry in Numbers

  • Some digits have vertical lines of symmetry (e.g., 1, 8).
  • Some have horizontal lines of symmetry (e.g., 0, 3, 8).
  • The digit '8' has both vertical and horizontal lines.
  • Many digits like 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 are asymmetrical.

🎙️ Numbers also display symmetry! The digit '1' has a vertical line of symmetry if written carefully. '0' and '3' have horizontal lines. The digit '8' is unique as it has both a vertical and a horizontal line of symmetry. Think about writing these numbers and folding them.

Mirror Images and Symmetry

  • Line symmetry is also called reflectional symmetry.
  • One half of the shape is a mirror image of the other.
  • The line of symmetry acts like a mirror.

🎙️ The concept of a mirror image is very closely related to line symmetry. When you look in a mirror, you see your reflection. Similarly, one side of a symmetrical object is a perfect reflection of the other side across the line of symmetry.
