What is Symmetry?

  • Imagine folding a paper in half.
  • If both halves match perfectly, it's symmetrical.
  • It's about balance and identical parts.

🎙️ Symmetry is when one half of an object or shape is a mirror image of the other half. Think of it like a perfectly balanced seesaw or a paper folded exactly in half, where both sides are identical.

Symmetry is like perfect balance!

Introducing the Line of Symmetry

  • The imaginary line that divides a figure into two identical halves.
  • If you fold along this line, the halves overlap perfectly.
  • Also called the axis of symmetry.

🎙️ The line of symmetry is a very important concept. It's an imaginary line that cuts a shape into two parts that are exact mirror images of each other. If you were to fold the shape along this line, both halves would match up perfectly.

The dividing line for perfect halves

Understanding Reflection Symmetry

  • When one half is the mirror image of the other.
  • The line of symmetry acts like a mirror.
  • Each point on one side has a corresponding point on the other.

🎙️ Reflection symmetry is exactly what it sounds like – one side is a perfect reflection of the other. The line of symmetry serves as the mirror, and every point on one side has an exact twin on the other side, at the same distance from the line.

🪞
Like looking in a mirror!
10 / 30

How Reflection Works

  • The mirror line is perpendicular to the line joining a point and its image.
  • The distance from the point to the mirror line is equal to the distance from the image to the mirror line.

🎙️ Imagine a point 'A' and a mirror line. Its reflection, 'A prime', will be on the opposite side of the mirror line. The important thing is that the distance from 'A' to the mirror line is exactly the same as the distance from 'A prime' to the mirror line.

Asymmetrical Figures

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Confusing diagonals with lines of symmetry.
  • Missing some lines of symmetry in complex figures.
  • Not understanding that reflections must be exact.

🎙️ Watch out for these common errors! Remember, a diagonal is not always a line of symmetry. Also, be careful to find ALL lines of symmetry, especially in figures like a square. And always ensure the reflection is an exact mirror image.
