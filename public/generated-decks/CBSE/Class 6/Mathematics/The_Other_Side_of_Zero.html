The Positive Side: Positive Integers

  • Numbers greater than zero.
  • They can be written with a '+' sign (e.g., +5) or no sign (e.g., 5).
  • Examples: Profit, height above sea level, temperature above 0°C.

🎙️ Positive integers are just like our natural numbers, representing quantities that are 'more than zero'. Think of them as moving upwards or gaining something. We usually don't write the '+' sign, but it's understood.

The Negative Side: Negative Integers

  • Numbers less than zero.
  • Always written with a '-' sign (e.g., -5).
  • Examples: Loss, depth below sea level, temperature below 0°C.

🎙️ Negative integers are the 'other side' of zero. They represent quantities that are 'less than zero', like a decrease or a debt. It's crucial to always write the '-' sign for negative numbers.

Zero: Neither Positive Nor Negative

  • Zero is a special integer.
  • It acts as the bridge between positive and negative numbers.
  • It is neither positive nor negative.

🎙️ Zero holds a unique position in the integer family. It's the starting point, the neutral ground, separating the positive numbers on one side and the negative numbers on the other. It doesn't have a sign.

Representing Integers on a Number Line

Comparing Integers: Who's Bigger?

  • On a number line, the integer to the right is always greater.
  • The integer to the left is always smaller.
  • Positive numbers are always greater than negative numbers.

🎙️ Comparing integers can sometimes be tricky, especially with negative numbers. The number line is your best friend here: whichever number is further to the right is the larger one. Simple as that!

Absolute Value: How Far From Zero?

  • The distance of a number from zero on the number line.
  • It is always a positive value (or zero).
  • Denoted by two vertical bars: |number|.

🎙️ Absolute value tells us 'how far' a number is from zero, regardless of direction. Think of it as taking steps from zero – whether you step left or right, the number of steps is always positive.

📏
Measuring Distance
17 / 25

Absolute Value: Example

  • What is the absolute value of -5? |-5| = 5.
  • What is the absolute value of 5? |5| = 5.
  • Both -5 and 5 are 5 units away from zero.

🎙️ Whether you go 5 steps to the left (-5) or 5 steps to the right (+5) from zero, the distance covered is still 5 steps. That's what absolute value represents: the magnitude, not the direction.

Adding Integers: Rules of the Game

  • Same signs: Add the absolute values and keep the common sign.
  • Different signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

🎙️ Now, let's learn how to add integers. There are two main rules to remember, depending on whether the numbers have the same sign or different signs. Pay close attention to the signs!

Adding Integers: Example (Same Signs)

  • Example 1: (+3) + (+5) = +8 (Add 3 and 5, keep '+' sign).
  • Example 2: (-3) + (-5) = -8 (Add 3 and 5, keep '-' sign).

🎙️ When integers have the same sign, it's straightforward: just add their magnitudes and attach that common sign. Think of it as combining two gains or combining two losses.

Adding Integers: Example (Different Signs)

🎙️ When signs are different, it's like a tug-of-war! The stronger side (the number with the larger absolute value) wins, and you subtract the smaller force. The result takes the sign of the winner.

Subtracting Integers: Add the Opposite!

  • Subtraction of integers can be converted into addition.
  • Change the subtraction sign to an addition sign.
  • Change the sign of the second number (find its additive inverse).
  • Then, follow the rules for adding integers!

🎙️ Subtracting integers has a clever trick! Instead of subtracting, we 'add the opposite'. This means you flip the sign of the number you are subtracting, and then just apply the addition rules we just learned. It makes things much simpler!
