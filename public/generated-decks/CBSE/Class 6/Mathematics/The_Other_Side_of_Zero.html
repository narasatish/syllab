What are Integers?

  • The collection of positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.
  • No fractions or decimals.
  • Represented by the symbol 'Z'.

🎙️ Integers are a big family of numbers! They include all the whole numbers you know, their negative counterparts, and zero. The key is, they are 'whole' numbers, no parts or pieces.

Integers in Real Life: Temperature

  • Above 0°C (positive integers).
  • Below 0°C (negative integers).
  • 0°C is the freezing point of water.

🎙️ When you hear the weather report, sometimes it says 'minus 5 degrees Celsius'. This 'minus 5' is a negative integer, telling us it's very cold, 5 degrees below zero.

Plotting Integers on a Number Line

  • Start at zero.
  • Move right for positive integers.
  • Move left for negative integers.
  • Mark the point clearly.

🎙️ To plot an integer, find zero first. Then, count the correct number of steps to the right for positive numbers, or to the left for negative numbers. Always make a clear mark!

🎙️ Positive integers are what you've been working with since kindergarten! They represent increases, gains, or movement to the right. The plus sign is optional, but it's always there implicitly.

The Special Role of Zero

  • Neither positive nor negative.
  • The origin point on the number line.
  • A neutral value, representing 'nothing' or 'no change'.

🎙️ Zero is unique! It's the boundary between positive and negative numbers. It's like the starting line in a race, or the balance point on a seesaw.

Ordering Integers: Who is Bigger?

  • On a number line, numbers to the right are always greater.
  • Numbers to the left are always smaller.
  • Every positive integer is greater than every negative integer.

🎙️ Just like with whole numbers, we can compare integers. The rule is simple: the further right a number is on the number line, the larger its value. The further left, the smaller.

📈
Increasing Value

  • Compare 5 and 2.
  • Both are to the right of zero.
  • 5 is further to the right than 2, so 5 > 2.

🎙️ This is a comparison you're already familiar with. Five is clearly larger than two. On the number line, 5 is to the right of 2.

Comparing Integers (Example 2): Negative vs Negative

  • Compare -5 and -2.
  • Both are to the left of zero.
  • -2 is further to the right than -5, so -2 > -5.

🎙️ This can be tricky! Remember, with negative numbers, the number closer to zero is greater. Think of debt: owing 2 rupees is better than owing 5 rupees.

Comparing Integers (Example 3): Positive vs Negative

  • Compare 3 and -4.
  • 3 is positive (right of zero).
  • -4 is negative (left of zero).
  • Any positive integer is greater than any negative integer, so 3 > -4.

🎙️ This is the easiest comparison! No matter how large a negative number looks, it will always be smaller than any positive number. Having 3 rupees is always better than owing 4 rupees!

Absolute Value: Distance from Zero

  • The distance of an integer from zero on the number line.
  • Always a positive value or zero.
  • Represented by two vertical bars, e.g., |-3|.

🎙️ Absolute value tells us 'how far' a number is from zero, regardless of its direction. Think of it as always giving you a positive answer, like a distance.

📏
Measuring Distance
18 / 25

Absolute Value: Worked Example

  • Find the absolute value of 5: |5| = 5.
  • Find the absolute value of -5: |-5| = 5.
  • Both 5 and -5 are 5 units away from zero.

🎙️ Whether you walk 5 steps to the right or 5 steps to the left, you've still walked 5 steps. That's the idea behind absolute value – it's just the magnitude.
