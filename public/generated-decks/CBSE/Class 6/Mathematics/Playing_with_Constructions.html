Basic Construction 3: Line Segment of Given Length

  • 1. Draw a line 'l' and mark a point 'A' on it.
  • 2. Open compass to the given length.
  • 3. Place compass needle at 'A'.
  • 4. Draw an arc intersecting 'l' at 'B'. 'AB' is the segment.

🎙️ To draw a line segment of a specific length, first draw a long line and mark a starting point 'A'. Then, open your compass to the desired length. Place the needle at 'A' and draw an arc that cuts the line. The point where it cuts is 'B', and 'AB' is your segment.

1Draw Line & Point
2Set Compass Length
3Mark Arc

Basic Construction 4: Copying a Line Segment

  • 1. Draw a line 'l' and mark a point 'C' on it.
  • 2. Use compass to measure length of original segment 'AB'.
  • 3. Place compass needle at 'C'.
  • 4. Draw an arc intersecting 'l' at 'D'. 'CD' is the copy.

🎙️ Sometimes we need to copy an existing line segment. First, draw a new line and mark a starting point 'C'. Then, use your compass to measure the length of the original segment. Finally, place the compass needle at 'C' and draw an arc to mark point 'D', creating an exact copy 'CD'.

Basic Construction 5: Perpendicular Bisector

  • 1. Draw segment AB. With A as centre, draw arcs (radius > half AB).
  • 2. With B as centre, draw arcs (same radius) intersecting first arcs.
  • 3. Join the intersection points P and Q. Line PQ is the bisector.

🎙️ This is a slightly more advanced construction. To draw a perpendicular bisector of segment AB, first draw AB. Then, from point A, draw two arcs above and below AB with a radius more than half of AB. Repeat from point B with the same radius. Finally, join the two points where the arcs intersect – this line is your perpendicular bisector.

1Draw Arcs from A
2Draw Arcs from B
3Join Intersections

Worked Example: Perpendicular Bisector of PQ

  • 1. Draw line segment PQ.
  • 2. From P, draw arcs (radius > 1/2 PQ).
  • 3. From Q, draw arcs (same radius) intersecting first arcs.
  • 4. Join the intersection points to get the bisector.

🎙️ Let's work through constructing the perpendicular bisector of a line segment PQ. Draw PQ. From P, use your compass to draw arcs above and below PQ, with a radius greater than half of PQ. Do the same from Q with the same radius. The line joining where these arcs cross is your perpendicular bisector.
