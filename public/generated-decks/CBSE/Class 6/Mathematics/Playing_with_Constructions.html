Constructing a Circle

  • A closed curve with all points equidistant from center
  • This distance is called the radius
  • Compass helps us draw perfect circles

🎙️ A circle is a fundamental shape. To draw one, we first decide how big it should be by setting the radius on our compass. Then, we fix the compass's pointer at the center and rotate the pencil end.

1Set radius on compass
2Place pointer at center
3Rotate pencil end

Example: Draw a Circle of Radius 3 cm

  • 1. Open compass to 3 cm using a ruler.
  • 2. Mark a center point 'O' on your paper.
  • 3. Place compass pointer on 'O'.
  • 4. Rotate the compass to draw the circle.

🎙️ Let's try drawing a circle of 3 cm radius. First, use your ruler to open your compass to exactly 3 cm. Then, mark a point on your paper as the center. Place the compass pointer on this center and carefully rotate the pencil end to draw the circle.

1Measure 3cm
2Fix center 'O'
3Draw circle

🎙️ A line segment is simply a straight path between two points, like the edge of your book. We can draw a line segment of any desired length using our ruler and compass, ensuring it's perfectly straight and accurate.

Constructing a Segment of Given Length

  • 1. Draw a random line 'l'.
  • 2. Mark a point 'A' on line 'l'.
  • 3. Open compass to the desired length.
  • 4. Place pointer on 'A', draw arc on 'l' at 'B'.

🎙️ To construct a line segment of a specific length, say 6 cm, we first draw a long line. Then, we mark a starting point 'A' on it. We open our compass to 6 cm, place the pointer on 'A', and draw an arc that cuts the line. This intersection point is 'B', giving us segment AB.

1Draw a line
2Mark point A
3Set compass length
4Mark point B

Example: Construct a Line Segment of 5 cm

  • 1. Draw a line 'm'.
  • 2. Mark point 'P' on 'm'.
  • 3. Open compass to 5 cm using ruler.
  • 4. With 'P' as center, draw an arc intersecting 'm' at 'Q'. PQ is 5 cm.

🎙️ Let's construct a 5 cm line segment. Draw a long line. Mark a point 'P' on it. Take your compass, measure 5 cm using your ruler, and then place the compass pointer on 'P'. Draw an arc that cuts your line at a new point, 'Q'. The segment PQ is exactly 5 cm long.

1Draw line 'm'
2Mark P

What is a Perpendicular Bisector?

  • A line that cuts another line segment exactly in half
  • It also forms a 90-degree angle with the segment
  • 'Perpendicular' means 90 degrees, 'Bisector' means cuts in half

🎙️ Imagine folding a line segment exactly in half, and the fold line is perfectly straight and crosses the segment at a right angle. That fold line is the perpendicular bisector. It's a very useful construction!

⊥
Halving at 90°
12 / 25

Steps to Construct a Perpendicular Bisector

  • 1. Draw line segment AB.
  • 2. With A as center, radius > half AB, draw arcs above and below AB.
  • 3. With B as center, same radius, draw arcs intersecting previous arcs at C and D.
  • 4. Join C and D. This line is the perpendicular bisector.

🎙️ To draw a perpendicular bisector for a segment AB, first, open your compass to more than half the length of AB. From point A, draw arcs above and below the segment. Repeat this from point B with the same compass opening. The two points where the arcs intersect, when joined, form the perpendicular bisector.

1Draw segment
2Arcs from A
3Arcs from B
4Join intersection
13 / 25

Example: Bisect a 7 cm Line Segment

  • 1. Draw AB = 7 cm.
  • 2. Set compass radius > 3.5 cm (e.g., 4 cm).
  • 3. From A, draw arcs above/below.
  • 4. From B, draw arcs intersecting previous ones. Join intersection points P and Q. PQ is the bisector.

🎙️ Let's bisect a 7 cm line segment. Draw the segment AB. Open your compass to a radius slightly more than 3.5 cm. From A, draw arcs. From B, with the same radius, draw intersecting arcs. Connect the points where these arcs meet. This line will divide the 7 cm segment exactly in half and be perpendicular to it.

1Draw 7cm
2Open compass >3.5cm
3Arcs from A & B
4Draw bisector

Constructing a 60-Degree Angle

  • This is a fundamental construction
  • Forms the basis for many other angles
  • Uses only ruler and compass, no protractor

🎙️ A 60-degree angle is special because it can be constructed very easily with just a compass. It's like a building block for other angles. Once you master this, you can construct 30, 90, 120 degrees, and more!

1Draw ray OA
2Arc from O
3Arc from B
4Join OC
16 / 25

Example: Construct a 60° Angle

  • 1. Draw a ray OA.
  • 2. With O as center, draw an arc cutting OA at B.
  • 3. With B as center, same radius, draw an arc cutting the first arc at C.
  • 4. Join OC. Angle COA is 60°.

🎙️ Let's construct a 60-degree angle. First, draw a ray OA. Then, with O as the center and any convenient radius, draw an arc that cuts OA at point B. Now, keeping the same radius, place the compass pointer on B and draw another arc that intersects the first arc at point C. Draw a line from O to C. Angle COA is your 60-degree angle!

2First arc
3Second arc
4Draw line

Bisecting an Angle

  • Dividing an angle into two equal parts
  • The bisector passes through the vertex
  • Creates two smaller, equal angles

🎙️ Just like we bisected a line segment, we can also bisect an angle. This means drawing a ray that perfectly cuts the angle into two smaller angles of equal measure. This is very useful when you need to create specific angles that are half of others.

✂️
Cutting in Half
18 / 25

Steps to Construct an Angle Bisector

  • 1. Draw an angle ABC.
  • 2. With B as center, draw an arc cutting BA at P and BC at Q.
  • 3. With P as center, radius > half PQ, draw an arc.
  • 4. With Q as center, same radius, draw an arc intersecting the previous one at R. Join BR. BR is the bisector.

🎙️ To bisect an angle, say angle ABC, first draw an arc from the vertex B that cuts both arms of the angle at points P and Q. Then, from P and Q, with a radius greater than half the distance PQ, draw two intersecting arcs. The line from B through this intersection point is the angle bisector.

1Draw angle
2Arc from vertex
3Arcs from points
4Draw bisector
19 / 25

Example: Bisect any Given Angle

  • 1. Draw any angle, e.g., 80°.
  • 2. Follow steps: draw arc from vertex.
  • 3. Draw intersecting arcs from points on arms.
  • 4. Draw the bisector ray. Verify with protractor.

🎙️ Let's take any angle you've drawn, perhaps an 80-degree angle. Now, apply the steps we just learned. Draw an arc from the vertex, then from the points where this arc cuts the arms, draw two more intersecting arcs. Connect the vertex to this intersection point, and you have successfully bisected your angle!

1Draw an angle
2Arc from vertex
3Arcs from intersections
4Draw bisecting ray
