Steps to Construct a Line Segment (e.g., 6 cm)

  • Place ruler on paper.
  • Mark point A at 0 cm.
  • Mark point B at 6 cm.
  • Join A and B.

🎙️ Let's construct a line segment of 6 cm. First, use your ruler to mark a starting point at zero. Then, find the 6 cm mark and place another point. Finally, draw a line connecting them.

1Place Ruler
2Mark Point A
3Mark Point B
4Join A and B

Steps to Copy a Line Segment

  • Draw line 'l' and mark point C.
  • Open compass to length of AB.
  • Place needle at C, draw arc intersecting 'l' at D.
  • CD is your copy!

🎙️ First, draw a random line and mark a point on it. Then, use your compass to measure the length of the original segment. Without changing the compass opening, place its needle on your marked point and draw an arc to get the copied segment.

1Draw base line & point C
2Measure AB with compass
3Draw arc from C
4Mark D, CD is copy

Constructing a Circle

  • Like drawing a perfect bangle or a coin.
  • Needs a centre and a radius.

🎙️ Think about drawing a perfect 'roti' or a 'chappati'. A circle is a perfectly round shape, and to draw it accurately, we need two things: its center and its radius.

💍
Perfect Round Shape
18 / 30

Steps to Construct a Circle (Radius 3 cm)

  • Mark a point O (centre).
  • Open compass to 3 cm using ruler.
  • Place needle at O, rotate pencil arm.
  • A perfect circle appears!

🎙️ To draw a circle with a 3 cm radius, first mark a point for the center. Then, open your compass so the distance between the needle and pencil is exactly 3 cm. Place the needle on the center point and rotate the pencil arm completely to draw your circle.

1Mark Centre O
2Set compass to 3 cm
3Place needle at O
4Rotate pencil arm

Perpendicular Bisector of a Line Segment

  • A line that cuts another line exactly in half.
  • Forms a perfect 90-degree angle.
  • Think of folding a paper exactly in half!

🎙️ Imagine you have a piece of string, and you want to cut it exactly in the middle with a straight cut. A perpendicular bisector does just that – it cuts a line segment into two equal halves at a right angle.

✂️
Cutting in Half
20 / 30

Steps: Perpendicular Bisector of AB

  • Open compass more than half of AB.
  • Draw arcs from A (above & below AB).
  • Draw arcs from B (same radius, intersect previous).
  • Join intersection points P and Q.

🎙️ To construct a perpendicular bisector, open your compass to more than half the length of the line segment. From each endpoint, draw arcs above and below the line. The line connecting the points where these arcs intersect is your perpendicular bisector.

1Open compass > half AB
2Draw arcs from A
3Draw arcs from B
4Join intersection P & Q

Steps to Construct an Angle (e.g., 60°)

  • Draw a ray OA.
  • Place protractor center at O, base along OA.
  • Mark point B at 60°.
  • Join O to B.

🎙️ To draw a 60-degree angle, first draw a straight line (ray). Place the center of your protractor on the starting point of the ray, aligning the base. Find 60 degrees on the scale and make a mark. Finally, draw a line from the starting point to your mark.

1Draw ray OA
2Place protractor
3Mark point B at 60°
4Join OB

Angle Bisector: Dividing an Angle

  • A ray that divides an angle into two equal parts.
  • Like cutting a slice of cake exactly in half!

🎙️ Imagine you have a slice of cake, and you want to share it equally with a friend. An angle bisector does just that – it cuts an angle into two perfectly equal smaller angles.

🍰
Dividing a Slice
25 / 30

Steps to Construct an Angle Bisector

  • Draw angle ABC.
  • With B as center, draw arc intersecting BA, BC at D, E.
  • With D, E as centers (same radius), draw intersecting arcs at F.
  • Join B to F. BF is the bisector.

🎙️ First, draw any angle. Then, from the vertex, draw an arc that cuts both arms of the angle. From these intersection points, draw two more arcs that meet. The line from the vertex to this new intersection point is your angle bisector.

1Draw angle ABC
2Draw arc from B (D, E)
3Draw arcs from D, E (F)
4Join B to F

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Loose compass pencil: causes inaccurate circles.
  • Not sharpening pencil: thick lines, hard to be precise.
  • Measuring from wrong ruler mark (not 0).
  • Not opening compass enough for bisectors.

🎙️ Even experienced constructors make small errors! Always ensure your compass pencil is tight and sharp. Remember to start measuring from the '0' mark on your ruler. And for bisectors, make sure your compass opening is wide enough!
