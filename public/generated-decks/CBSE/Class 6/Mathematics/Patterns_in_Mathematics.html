Arithmetic Patterns: Counting Steps

  • Each number increases or decreases by a constant value.
  • Like climbing stairs, one step at a time.
  • The difference between consecutive terms is fixed.

🎙️ Imagine climbing a staircase where each step is the same height. In arithmetic patterns, we add or subtract the same number each time. This fixed number is called the common difference.

Example: Adding a Constant

  • Pattern: 5, 8, 11, 14, __
  • Rule: Add 3 to the previous number.
  • Next term: 14 + 3 = 17

🎙️ Let's look at this example. From 5 to 8, we add 3. From 8 to 11, we add 3 again. So the rule is 'add 3'. To find the next number, we simply add 3 to 14, which gives us 17.

Becoming a Pattern Detective: Finding the Rule

  • Step 1: Look at the first two terms.
  • Step 2: Ask: Is it adding/subtracting or multiplying/dividing?
  • Step 3: Test your rule with the next terms.

🎙️ The most important skill in patterns is finding the rule! It's like being a detective. First, compare the first two numbers. Are they getting bigger or smaller? By how much? Then, test your guess with the rest of the numbers to be sure.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Jumping to conclusions: Always test the rule with multiple terms.
  • Ignoring the type: Is it adding/subtracting or multiplying/dividing?
  • Not seeing rotations: Shapes can turn, not just change.

🎙️ Even pattern experts make mistakes! The most common one is guessing a rule after only two terms and not checking it for the rest. Always verify your rule! Also, remember to consider all types of changes – addition, multiplication, and even rotations for shapes.
