Income & Expenses - The Basics

  • Income: Money you receive.
  • Expenses: Money you spend.
  • Budgeting balances these two.

🎙️ These are two very important words in budgeting. Income is the money coming in, like your pocket money. Expenses are the money going out, like when you buy snacks. A good budget makes sure your income is more than or equal to your expenses.

The Budgeting Process - Step 1: Track Income

  • Write down all money you receive.
  • Note the amount and date.
  • Know your total income.

🎙️ The very first step is to know how much money you have. Keep a small notebook or a simple sheet to write down every rupee that comes into your hands, and when it arrived.

The Budgeting Process - Step 3: Categorize Expenses

  • Group similar expenses.
  • Distinguish between 'Needs' and 'Wants'.
  • This helps prioritize spending.

🎙️ Once you have your list, group your expenses. A crucial part of this is figuring out what are 'needs' – things you must have – and what are 'wants' – things you'd like to have but can live without. This helps you decide what's most important.

Needs vs. Wants - Explained

  • Needs: Essential for survival/well-being (e.g., food, school supplies).
  • Wants: Desirable but not essential (e.g., toys, extra snacks).
  • Prioritize needs over wants.

🎙️ This is a very important concept. Food, water, shelter, and school books are needs. A new video game or an extra packet of chips are wants. Always make sure your needs are covered before spending on wants.

The Budgeting Process - Step 4: Create a Plan

  • Allocate money for different categories.
  • Decide how much to save.
  • Ensure your spending doesn't exceed income.

🎙️ Now, put it all together! Decide how much money you will spend on needs, how much on wants, and how much you will save. Make sure the total of your spending and saving isn't more than your total income.

Worked Example: Rohan's Budget

  • Income: ₹200 (weekly pocket money).
  • Needs: ₹50 (bus fare), ₹30 (school snack).
  • Wants: ₹70 (comic book).
  • Savings: ₹50 for a new cricket bat.

🎙️ Rohan gets ₹200. He plans to spend ₹50 on bus fare (need) and ₹30 on a snack (need). He wants a comic for ₹70 (want). That leaves him with ₹50, which he smartly decides to save for a cricket bat. Total spent + saved = ₹200. Perfect!

Tips for Smart Budgeting

  • Start small, be consistent.
  • Save before you spend.
  • Review your budget often.

🎙️ Here are some extra tips to help you become a budgeting pro! Don't try to be perfect from day one. Always try to put some money aside for savings first, before you start spending. And keep checking your budget to see how you're doing.

Common Budgeting Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not tracking all spending.
  • Being unrealistic with savings.
  • Giving up too soon.

🎙️ Watch out for these common pitfalls! Make sure you write down ALL your expenses, even small ones. Don't set impossible saving goals, start small. And if you make a mistake, don't give up – just adjust and try again!
