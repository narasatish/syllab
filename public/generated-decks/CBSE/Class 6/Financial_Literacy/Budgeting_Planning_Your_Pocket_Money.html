What is Pocket Money?

  • Money given to you by parents or guardians.
  • It's usually for small, personal expenses.
  • It helps you learn about money management early.

🎙️ Pocket money is the small amount of cash you receive regularly from your family. It's meant for your personal use, like buying snacks or small toys. It's a great way to start understanding how money works.

  • Ensures you don't run out of money too quickly.
  • Allows you to save for bigger things you want.

🎙️ Budgeting is like having a map for your money. It guides you to spend wisely and reach your financial goals. Without a budget, money can disappear quickly, and you might wonder where it all went!

Understanding Income

  • Income is the money you receive.
  • For you, it's mostly your pocket money.
  • It can be weekly, monthly, or on special occasions.

🎙️ Income is simply the money that comes to you. For most of you, this is your regular pocket money. It's the starting point of your budget, as it dictates how much you have to work with.

Understanding Expenses

  • Expenses are the money you spend.
  • This includes everything you buy or pay for.
  • Tracking expenses shows where your money goes.

🎙️ Expenses are the opposite of income – it's all the money that leaves your pocket. From buying a snack to saving for a gift, every rupee spent is an expense. Knowing your expenses is key to managing your money.

⬇️

Types of Expenses: Needs vs. Wants

  • Needs are things essential for survival or well-being (e.g., food, school supplies).
  • Wants are things you desire but can live without (e.g., toys, extra sweets).
  • Distinguishing them helps in smart spending.

🎙️ This is a very important distinction. Needs are things you absolutely must have, like your lunch or a pen for school. Wants are things that would be nice to have, like a new video game or extra chocolates. Learning to tell them apart is crucial for budgeting.

Budgeting Process: Step 1 - Track Your Income

  • Know exactly how much money you receive.
  • Note down the amount and when you receive it.
  • This is your starting point for the budget.

🎙️ The first step in creating a budget is to clearly know your income. Write down how much pocket money you get and when. For example, '₹100 every Sunday'. This gives you a clear picture of your total funds.

🗓️
Record Your Money
10 / 25

Budgeting Process: Step 2 - Track Your Expenses

  • Keep a record of every rupee you spend.
  • Note down what you bought and how much it cost.
  • You can use a small notebook or a phone app.

🎙️ Next, become a detective for your money! Every time you spend, no matter how small the amount, write it down. This might seem tedious at first, but it's the only way to truly see where your money is going.

📝
Write It Down
11 / 25

Budgeting Process: Step 3 - Create a Plan

  • Allocate your income to different categories.
  • Decide how much to spend on needs, wants, and savings.
  • Give every rupee a 'job'.

🎙️ Now comes the planning part. Based on your income and typical expenses, decide how much you want to spend on different things. For example, '₹50 for snacks, ₹30 for saving, ₹20 for fun'. This is your actual budget.

📊
Allocate Funds
12 / 25

Budgeting Process: Step 4 - Review and Adjust

  • At the end of the week/month, check your budget.
  • Compare what you planned vs. what you actually spent.
  • Adjust your plan for the next period if needed.

🎙️ Budgeting isn't a one-time thing; it's an ongoing process. Regularly look at your budget. Did you stick to it? Did you overspend in one area? Learn from your spending habits and make changes for the next week or month.

The Power of Saving Money

  • Saving means keeping some money aside for future use.
  • It helps you buy bigger, more expensive things later.
  • Even small amounts saved regularly add up over time.

🎙️ Saving money is one of the most powerful habits you can develop. It means you don't spend all your money right away, but put some aside. This allows you to achieve bigger goals, like buying a bicycle or a special gift.

Setting Saving Goals

  • Decide what you want to save for (e.g., a new toy, a book).
  • Set a target amount and a timeline.
  • Break down big goals into smaller, manageable savings.

🎙️ To make saving easier, set clear goals. Do you want a new toy? How much does it cost? By when do you want to buy it? Breaking it down makes the goal feel achievable and motivates you to save regularly.

Tips for Smart Spending

  • Always compare prices before buying.
  • Think 'Do I really need this?' before making a purchase.
  • Avoid impulse buying (buying without thinking).

🎙️ Being a smart spender means making thoughtful decisions. Don't just buy the first thing you see. Compare options, ask yourself if it's a need or a want, and avoid buying things just because they look appealing at the moment.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Budgeting helps you manage your pocket money wisely.
  • Understand income, expenses, needs, and wants.
  • Follow the 4 steps: Track Income, Track Expenses, Plan, Review.
  • Saving money helps you achieve your goals.

🎙️ To summarize, budgeting is a vital skill. Remember to always know your income and expenses, distinguish between needs and wants, and follow the simple four-step process. Most importantly, start saving for your future goals now!
