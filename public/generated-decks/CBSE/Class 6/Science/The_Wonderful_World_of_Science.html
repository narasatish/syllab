What is Science?

  • It's a way of understanding the world.
  • Based on observation and experiments.
  • Helps us find answers to our questions.

🎙️ So, what exactly is science? It's simply a special way of understanding the world around us. Scientists don't just guess; they observe things carefully, ask questions, and then do experiments to find answers. It's all about discovering how things work!

Observing the World Around Us

  • Science starts with careful observation.
  • Using our senses to notice details.
  • Example: Watching a plant grow.

🎙️ The first step to being a scientist is to observe! This means using all your senses – sight, hearing, smell, touch, and even taste (safely!) – to notice details about things. For example, watching a tiny seed sprout and grow into a big plant is a great observation.

The Scientific Method: Step 1 - Observe

  • Notice something interesting.
  • Gather information with your senses.
  • Example: A leaf falling from a tree.

🎙️ The scientific method is like a roadmap for scientists. Step one is 'Observe'. You notice something, like a leaf falling from a tree. You gather facts using your eyes, ears, and other senses. This careful watching is crucial.

The Scientific Method: Step 2 - Ask a Question

  • Based on your observation.
  • What do you want to know?
  • Example: Why do leaves change colour in autumn?

🎙️ Once you've observed, the next logical step is to 'Ask a Question'. If you saw leaves falling, you might wonder, 'Why do leaves change colour in autumn?' This question guides your entire investigation. It's the puzzle you want to solve.

2Ask a Question

The Scientific Method: Step 3 - Form a Hypothesis

  • An educated guess or prediction.
  • A possible answer to your question.
  • Example: 'Leaves change colour because of colder weather.'

🎙️ After asking a question, you 'Form a Hypothesis'. This is your best guess or a prediction about the answer to your question. For our leaf example, a hypothesis could be: 'Leaves change colour because of colder weather.' It's a statement you can test.

2Ask a Question

The Scientific Method: Step 4 - Experiment

  • Test your hypothesis carefully.
  • Design a fair test.
  • Example: Grow plants in different temperature conditions.

🎙️ Now comes the exciting part: 'Experiment'! This is where you design and carry out a test to see if your hypothesis is correct. For our leaf example, you might grow plants in different temperature conditions to see if temperature affects leaf colour change. It must be a fair test.

The Scientific Method: Step 5 - Analyze & Conclude

  • Look at your results.
  • Was your hypothesis correct?
  • Share your findings with others.

🎙️ Finally, you 'Analyze your results and Conclude'. You look at all the data you collected during your experiment. Did your experiment support your hypothesis, or did it show something different? Then, you share what you've learned with others. This completes one cycle of the scientific method.

Physics: The Study of Energy and Motion

  • Deals with forces, energy, motion.
  • How things move and interact.
  • Example: A ball rolling down a slope.

🎙️ One major branch is Physics. Physics helps us understand how things move, why they fall, and how energy works. Think about a ball rolling down a slope – physics explains the force of gravity and the ball's motion. It's all about how the universe works!

⚛️

Chemistry: The Study of Matter

  • Focuses on what things are made of.
  • How substances change and react.
  • Example: Baking a cake (ingredients change).

🎙️ Another important branch is Chemistry. Chemistry is all about matter – what everything around us is made of – and how different substances change or react with each other. When you bake a cake, the ingredients change and combine to form something new; that's chemistry in action!

Biology: The Study of Life

  • Explores all living things.
  • Plants, animals, humans, tiny microbes.
  • Example: How a seed grows into a plant.

🎙️ Then there is Biology, which is the study of life itself! Biologists learn about plants, animals, humans, and even tiny microbes. Understanding how a seed grows into a plant or how our own bodies work are all part of biology. It helps us appreciate the diversity of life.

Measurement: Being Accurate in Science

  • Essential for accurate observations.
  • Using standard units and tools.
  • Example: How tall is a tree?

🎙️ In science, being precise is very important. That's why we use 'Measurement'. It involves using standard units and tools to find out the size, quantity, or amount of something. Knowing exactly how tall a tree is, for instance, requires careful measurement.

Measuring Length

  • How long or short something is.
  • Units: centimetre (cm), metre (m), kilometre (km).
  • Tools: Ruler, measuring tape.

🎙️ Length tells us how long or short an object is, or the distance between two points. We use units like centimetres, metres, and kilometres. Common tools for measuring length are a ruler for small objects and a measuring tape for larger distances.

Measuring Mass

  • How much 'stuff' is in an object.
  • Units: gram (g), kilogram (kg).
  • Tools: Weighing balance, electronic scale.

🎙️ Mass tells us how much 'stuff' or matter an object contains. We usually measure mass in grams or kilograms. A weighing balance or an electronic scale are the tools we use to find out the mass of different objects, like fruits or vegetables.

Measuring Time

  • How long an event lasts.
  • Units: second (s), minute (min), hour (h).
  • Tools: Clock, stopwatch.

🎙️ Time helps us understand how long an event lasts or when something happens. We measure time in seconds, minutes, and hours. A clock tells us the current time, while a stopwatch is used to measure the duration of an event, like a race.
