The Moon: Earth's Celestial Companion

  • Earth's only natural satellite
  • Orbits our planet
  • Reflects sunlight, doesn't produce its own light

🎙️ The Moon is our closest neighbour in space, constantly circling around Earth. It shines so brightly because it acts like a mirror, reflecting the Sun's light towards us.

  • Occurs over about a month

🎙️ Have you noticed the Moon looking different on various nights? These are called phases, and they happen as the Moon orbits Earth, changing how much of its sunlit side we can observe.

Full Moon and New Moon

  • Full Moon: Entire side visible
  • New Moon: Not visible from Earth
  • Cycle repeats every ~29.5 days

🎙️ The Full Moon is when we see the entire illuminated face. The New Moon is when the Moon is between the Sun and Earth, appearing invisible from our perspective. This cycle repeats approximately every month.

Meteors and Meteorites: Shooting Stars

  • Meteor: Rock burning in atmosphere
  • Meteorite: Rock that reaches Earth's surface
  • Often seen at night

🎙️ When a small piece of rock enters Earth's atmosphere, it burns up due to friction, creating a bright streak of light we call a 'shooting star' or meteor. If it survives and hits the ground, it's a meteorite.

  • Develop a tail when near the Sun
  • Have very elliptical orbits

🎙️ Comets are like dirty snowballs from the outer reaches of our Solar System. As they get closer to the Sun, the ice melts and forms a beautiful, glowing tail that points away from the Sun.
