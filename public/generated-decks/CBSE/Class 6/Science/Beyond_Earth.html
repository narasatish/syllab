The Moon - Earth's Companion

  • Earth's only natural satellite.
  • Orbits Earth, taking about 27 days to complete one revolution.
  • Has no light of its own; reflects sunlight.

🎙️ Our Moon is Earth's loyal companion, constantly orbiting around us. It doesn't produce its own light, but we see it shine because it reflects the Sun's light.

Phases of the Moon

Asteroids - Space Rocks

  • Small, rocky objects orbiting the Sun.
  • Most are found in the Asteroid Belt between Mars and Jupiter.
  • Vary in size from pebbles to hundreds of kilometers wide.

🎙️ Asteroids are like giant space rocks, left over from the formation of our Solar System. Most of them hang out together in a 'belt' between Mars and Jupiter.

Meteors and Meteorites - Shooting Stars

  • Meteor: A streak of light when a space rock enters Earth's atmosphere.
  • Often called a 'shooting star'.
  • Meteorite: A meteor that survives the burn and hits Earth's surface.

🎙️ When a small piece of space rock burns up in our atmosphere, it creates a bright streak we call a meteor. If it's big enough to survive and land on Earth, it's called a meteorite.

Comets - Icy Visitors

  • Balls of ice, dust, and rock.
  • Develop a glowing 'tail' when they get close to the Sun.
  • Their tails always point away from the Sun due to solar wind.

🎙️ Comets are often called 'dirty snowballs' because they are made of ice and dust. As they get closer to the Sun, the ice melts and forms a beautiful, long tail that always points away from the Sun.
