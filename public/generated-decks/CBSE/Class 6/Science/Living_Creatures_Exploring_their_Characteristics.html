What Defines "Living"?

  • Living things show life processes.
  • They interact with their surroundings.
  • They are made of cells.

🎙️ Imagine a tiny seed. It looks still, but it has the potential for life. Living things are dynamic; they are constantly performing various activities to sustain themselves. These activities are what we call life processes.

Living Things Grow

  • Increase in size and mass.
  • Irreversible change.
  • Happens throughout life or up to a certain age.

🎙️ Think about yourself! You were once a baby, and now you are a child. This increase in size is called growth. Similarly, a small seed grows into a big tree over time. Non-living things, like a stone, don't grow on their own.

Growth in Animals and Plants

🎙️ Growth is a fundamental characteristic. A small puppy doesn't just stay small; it grows into a full-sized dog. Similarly, a tiny mustard seed can grow into a tall plant. This growth is orderly and programmed.

Living Things Move

  • Change position from one place to another.
  • Show internal movements.
  • Plants show movement towards light or water.

🎙️ You can walk, run, and jump – that's movement! Animals move around to find food, shelter, or escape danger. Even plants, though rooted, show movement, like turning their leaves towards the sun or roots growing towards water.

Different Ways of Movement

  • Birds fly using wings.
  • Fish swim using fins.
  • Plants bend towards sunlight (phototropism).

🎙️ Movement can be very diverse. A bird soaring high in the sky, a fish gracefully moving through water, or even a sunflower slowly turning its head to follow the sun – these are all examples of movement in living things. A chair, however, won't move unless you push it.

Living Things Breathe (Respire)

  • Take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide.
  • Release energy from food.
  • Essential for survival.

🎙️ Just like a car needs fuel to run, our bodies need energy to perform all activities. Respiration is the process where living things take in air, use the oxygen to break down food, and release energy. They also give out carbon dioxide as a waste product.

🌬️
Taking a Breath

How Animals and Plants Breathe

  • Humans breathe through lungs.
  • Fish breathe through gills.
  • Plants breathe through tiny pores called stomata.

🎙️ While the process is similar, the organs for respiration vary. Humans and many animals use lungs. Fish have gills to extract oxygen from water. Plants have tiny openings, mostly on their leaves, called stomata, for gas exchange.

Living Things Need Food (Nutrition)

  • Obtain nutrients for energy and growth.
  • Plants make their own food (autotrophs).
  • Animals get food from plants or other animals (heterotrophs).

🎙️ Food is our fuel! Living things need food to get energy for all their activities, to grow, and to repair their bodies. Without food, no living thing can survive for long. This process of obtaining food is called nutrition.

How Different Organisms Get Food

  • Mango tree makes food using sunlight.
  • Cow eats grass.
  • Lion hunts deer for food.

🎙️ Plants are amazing because they can make their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide – a process called photosynthesis. Animals, on the other hand, cannot make their own food; they depend on plants or other animals for their nutrition.

Living Things Remove Waste (Excrete)

  • Get rid of harmful waste products.
  • Keeps the body healthy.
  • Waste can be solid, liquid, or gas.

🎙️ Just like we clean our houses and throw out garbage, our bodies also produce waste products during life processes. Excretion is the process of removing these harmful waste materials from the body. If waste builds up, it can be dangerous.

Waste Removal in Living Beings

  • Humans sweat and pass urine.
  • Plants release oxygen during photosynthesis.
  • Some plants shed old leaves.

🎙️ Our kidneys filter waste from our blood, and we sweat through our skin. Plants release excess water vapour through transpiration and can even shed leaves that have accumulated waste. Even the oxygen plants release is a 'waste' product for them!

Living Things Produce Young Ones (Reproduce)

  • Produce offspring of their own kind.
  • Ensures continuation of species.
  • Can be sexual or asexual.

🎙️ Imagine if animals and plants never had babies! Their species would disappear. Reproduction is the amazing ability of living organisms to produce new individuals similar to themselves, ensuring that life continues from generation to generation.

Reproduction in Animals and Plants

  • Birds lay eggs, which hatch into chicks.
  • Dogs give birth to puppies.
  • Plants produce seeds that grow into new plants.

🎙️ Animals reproduce in different ways; some lay eggs, while others give birth to live young. Plants reproduce through seeds, spores, or even parts of their body like stems and roots. This ensures that their kind doesn't vanish from Earth.

Living Things Respond to Changes (Stimuli)

  • React to changes in their surroundings.
  • Changes are called stimuli (e.g., light, heat, touch).
  • Helps them survive and adapt.

🎙️ If you accidentally touch a hot stove, you immediately pull your hand back, right? That's a response to a stimulus (heat). Living things are aware of their environment and react to changes around them, which is crucial for their survival.

Examples of Response

  • Mimosa (touch-me-not) plant closes its leaves when touched.
  • Cockroaches run away from light.
  • We shiver when it's cold.

🎙️ A sunflower turning towards the sun, a 'touch-me-not' plant folding its leaves when touched, or even our eyes blinking when a bright light flashes – these are all examples of living things responding to stimuli. Non-living things don't react this way.

Living Things Have a Life Span

  • Born, grow, reproduce, and eventually die.
  • Each organism has a specific duration of life.
  • From birth to natural death.

🎙️ Every living thing, from a tiny insect to a giant tree, has a defined period for which it lives. This period, from birth to natural death, is called its life span. Non-living things do not have a life span; they don't 'die'.

Plants Are Living Too!

  • Show growth (get taller, wider).
  • Respire through stomata.
  • Reproduce through seeds, spores, etc.

🎙️ Sometimes, because plants don't move like animals, it's easy to forget they are living. But they definitely are! They grow, breathe, need food (which they make!), reproduce, and respond to their environment, just in their own unique ways.

Animals: Diverse Living Forms

  • Show clear movement.
  • Need food from external sources.
  • Reproduce and have a life cycle.

🎙️ From the smallest ant to the largest elephant, animals are incredibly diverse. They all share the common characteristics of living things, although how they express them can vary greatly. They are constantly interacting with their environment to survive.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing growth of crystals with growth of living things.
  • Thinking all movement means living (e.g., a rolling ball).
  • Believing plants don't move or respond.

🎙️ It's easy to get confused sometimes! Remember that the 'growth' of a crystal is just adding material to its surface, not growing from within. A ball moves only when pushed. Plants indeed move and respond, just often slowly or in subtle ways.
