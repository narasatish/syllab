Understanding Living Things

  • Things that show specific life processes.
  • They are also called organisms.
  • From tiny bacteria to giant elephants.

🎙️ Living things are all around us, from the smallest microbes we can't see to the largest animals. They all share certain characteristics that define them as alive. We will learn about these fascinating characteristics one by one.

Characteristic 1: Growth

  • Increase in size over time.
  • From a baby to an adult.
  • Plants grow taller, animals grow bigger.

🎙️ One of the most noticeable characteristics of living things is that they grow. Think about how a tiny seed grows into a large tree, or how a human baby grows into an adult. This increase in size and complexity is a fundamental part of life.

Example of Growth: A Plant's Journey

  • A small mango seed grows into a large mango tree.
  • It absorbs water and nutrients.

🎙️ A great example of growth is a plant. A tiny seed, given the right conditions like water, sunlight, and soil, will sprout and gradually grow into a tall, strong tree, bearing fruits and flowers. This transformation is a continuous process throughout its life.

Characteristic 2: Movement

  • Ability to change position or place.
  • Animals move to find food or escape danger.
  • Plants show subtle movements like turning towards sun.

🎙️ Living things have the ability to move. Animals move from one place to another using their legs, wings, or fins. Even plants, though rooted, show movements like their leaves turning towards the sun or flowers opening and closing.

Example of Movement: A Bird in Flight

  • A bird flies from one tree to another.
  • It uses its wings to propel itself.
  • This movement helps it find food and build nests.

🎙️ Observe birds flying high in the sky or hopping between branches. This active movement is crucial for their survival, allowing them to search for food, find mates, and escape from predators. It's a clear sign of life.

Characteristic 3: Need for Food

  • All living things need energy to live and grow.
  • Food provides this energy.
  • Plants make their own food, animals eat other organisms.

🎙️ Just like a car needs fuel, living things need food to get energy. This energy is used for all life processes, including growth, movement, and maintaining body functions. Plants are unique as they produce their own food using sunlight.

Example of Food: Humans Eating

  • This food is digested to release energy.

Characteristic 4: Respiration (Breathing)

  • Process of taking in oxygen and giving out carbon dioxide.
  • Releases energy from food.
  • Occurs in all living things, even plants.

🎙️ Respiration is often called breathing, but it's more than just taking air in and out. It's the process where living things use oxygen to break down food and release the stored energy. This happens continuously, day and night, in all living organisms.

🌬️
Breathing
11 / 25

Example of Respiration: Fish Breathing

  • Fish use gills to breathe oxygen dissolved in water.
  • Water enters through their mouth and passes over gills.
  • Gills extract oxygen and release carbon dioxide.

🎙️ Fish, living underwater, have a special way of breathing. They don't have lungs like us, but they have gills. Gills are feathery organs that absorb oxygen directly from the water, allowing the fish to respire and survive.

Characteristic 5: Response to Stimuli

  • Ability to react to changes in the environment.
  • A stimulus is anything that causes a reaction.
  • Like light, sound, touch, or temperature.

🎙️ Living things are aware of their surroundings and can react to changes. If it's too hot, we sweat. If we hear a loud noise, we might jump. These reactions to stimuli are essential for survival and protection.

Example of Response: Touch-Me-Not Plant

  • The Mimosa Pudica plant (touch-me-not) folds its leaves when touched.
  • This is a rapid response to a physical stimulus.
  • It protects itself from potential harm.

🎙️ A fascinating example is the 'touch-me-not' plant. When you gently touch its leaves, they quickly fold inward. This is a remarkable defense mechanism and a clear demonstration of how even plants respond to external stimuli.

Characteristic 6: Excretion

  • Removal of waste products from the body.
  • Waste can be harmful if not removed.
  • Animals excrete urine, sweat; plants release oxygen.

🎙️ As living things carry out various life processes, waste products are generated. Excretion is the process by which these harmful waste materials are removed from the body, keeping the organism healthy and functioning properly.

Example of Excretion: Sweating

  • Humans sweat to remove excess water and salts.
  • Sweating also helps to cool down the body.
  • It's a way our body cleanses itself.

🎙️ When we play or exercise, we often sweat. Sweating is an excretory process where our skin releases water and some dissolved salts. It not only removes waste but also plays a vital role in regulating our body temperature.

Characteristic 7: Reproduction

  • Ability to produce young ones of their own kind.
  • Ensures the continuation of a species.
  • Can be sexual (two parents) or asexual (one parent).

🎙️ Reproduction is the process by which living organisms create offspring. This characteristic is crucial for the survival of a species over generations. Without reproduction, a species would eventually die out.

Example of Reproduction: Birds Laying Eggs

  • A hen lays eggs, from which chicks hatch.
  • This is how birds create new generations.
  • Ensures the population of birds continues.

🎙️ A common example of reproduction is birds laying eggs. The eggs contain developing embryos, and after a period of incubation, baby chicks hatch, growing to become adult birds themselves. This cycle ensures the continuity of bird life.

What are Non-Living Things?

  • Objects that do not show any life processes.
  • They do not grow, move on their own, or need food.
  • Examples: rocks, tables, cars, clouds.

🎙️ In contrast to living things, non-living things are inanimate. They don't breathe, eat, grow, or reproduce. A chair will remain a chair and won't suddenly grow taller or have baby chairs!

Worked Example: Identifying Living or Non-Living

Recap: The Seven Characteristics of Life

  • 1. Growth: Increase in size.
  • 2. Movement: Change in position.
  • 3. Food: Need for energy.
  • 4. Respiration: Breathing to release energy.
  • 5. Response to Stimuli: Reacting to environment.
  • 6. Excretion: Removing waste.
  • 7. Reproduction: Producing young ones.

🎙️ We've covered a lot today! Remember these seven key characteristics. They are the fundamental processes that distinguish all living organisms from non-living matter. Each one plays a vital role in sustaining life.
