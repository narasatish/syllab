What Makes Something Alive?

  • They can grow and change.
  • They move on their own.
  • They need food, water, and air.

🎙️ Look around you. You see plants, animals, and even yourself! What do all these have in common? They are alive! Living things show certain special features that non-living things do not.

Living vs. Non-Living Things

  • Living things have life.
  • Non-living things do not.
  • Can you tell the difference?

🎙️ It's important to distinguish between living and non-living things. A chair, a rock, or a toy car are non-living. They don't grow, breathe, or move on their own. But a bird, a tree, or you, are living!

Characteristic 1: Growth

  • Living things increase in size.
  • They also develop and mature.
  • It's a gradual process.

🎙️ The first amazing characteristic is growth. Think about a tiny seedling becoming a big tree, or a baby growing into an adult. This increase in size and complexity is what we call growth.

🎙️ You can see growth everywhere! From a tiny seed becoming a huge banyan tree to a little puppy transforming into a big, strong dog. Even you are growing every single day!

Characteristic 2: Movement

  • Living things can move parts of their body.
  • Animals can move from one place to another.
  • Plants show movement towards light or water.

🎙️ Another key feature is movement. Animals move around to find food, shelter, or escape danger. Plants also show movement, like turning their leaves towards the sun, even if they don't walk around.

Characteristic 3: Respiration

  • Living things breathe in air.
  • They use oxygen to get energy from food.
  • They release carbon dioxide.

🎙️ Respiration is how living things get energy. We breathe in oxygen, which helps our bodies release energy from the food we eat. Then, we breathe out carbon dioxide, a waste gas.

🌬️
Breathing
9 / 25

Example: Respiration

  • Humans breathe through lungs.
  • Fish breathe through gills.
  • Plants breathe through tiny pores called stomata.

🎙️ Different living things have different ways to respire. Humans use lungs, fish use gills, and even plants 'breathe' through tiny openings on their leaves called stomata, taking in and giving out gases.

Characteristic 4: Response to Stimuli

  • Living things react to changes around them.
  • These changes are called stimuli.
  • Responses help them survive.

🎙️ Imagine touching something hot – you quickly pull your hand away! This reaction to a change (heat) is called responding to a stimulus. Living things constantly react to their environment.

👀
Seeing and reacting

Example: Responding to Stimuli

  • A plant turns towards sunlight.
  • You blink when dust enters your eye.
  • Animals run away from danger.

🎙️ A sunflower turning to face the sun, or a deer running away when it hears a loud noise are perfect examples. Even your eyes blinking when something comes near is a response to a stimulus.

Characteristic 5: Excretion

  • Living things produce waste materials.
  • They need to remove these wastes.
  • Excretion is the process of waste removal.

🎙️ Just like a factory produces waste products, living organisms also produce waste during their life processes. Excretion is the essential process of getting rid of these harmful waste materials from the body.

🚽
Waste removal

Example of Excretion

  • Humans sweat and urinate.
  • Plants release excess water vapor.
  • Animals release solid waste.

🎙️ When you sweat, your body is excreting waste. Plants release extra water as vapour through their leaves, which is also a form of excretion. All living things have ways to remove waste.

Characteristic 6: Reproduction

  • Living things produce young ones.
  • This ensures the continuation of their kind.
  • It can be through eggs, seeds, or live birth.

🎙️ Reproduction is the amazing ability of living organisms to create new individuals of their own species. This ensures that life continues from one generation to the next, preventing species from disappearing.

Example of Reproduction

  • Birds lay eggs that hatch into chicks.
  • Animals give birth to babies.
  • Plants produce seeds that grow into new plants.

🎙️ Think of a hen laying eggs, which then hatch into cute chicks. Or a mother dog giving birth to puppies. Even a tiny seed from a mango tree can grow into a new mango tree. These are all examples of reproduction.

Habitat: Home Sweet Home

  • The place where an organism lives is its habitat.
  • It provides food, water, shelter, and space.
  • Different organisms live in different habitats.

🎙️ Every living creature needs a place to live, find food, and raise its young. This natural home is called its habitat. A habitat is like a living thing's personal apartment, providing everything it needs.

🏡
A cozy home
19 / 25

Different Types of Habitats

  • Terrestrial habitats: on land (forests, deserts).
  • Aquatic habitats: in water (ponds, oceans).
  • Arboreal habitats: in trees.

🎙️ Habitats can be broadly categorized. Terrestrial habitats are on land, like deserts or mountains. Aquatic habitats are in water, such as rivers or oceans. Some creatures even spend most of their lives in the air or trees!

Adaptations: Fitting into the Habitat

  • Special features that help an organism survive.
  • These features develop over many generations.
  • They can be physical or behavioural.

🎙️ Living things don't just 'live' in their habitat; they are perfectly designed for it! Adaptations are special characteristics that help an organism survive and thrive in its specific environment. Think of a camel in a desert.

🦒
Long neck for high leaves

Example of Adaptations

  • Camels have humps to store water and fat.
  • Fish have gills to breathe underwater.
  • Polar bears have thick fur for cold climates.

🎙️ A cactus has thick, waxy skin and spines to prevent water loss in the desert. Fish have streamlined bodies and gills to move and breathe efficiently in water. These are all amazing adaptations!
