Solid State: Hard and Fixed

  • Has a definite shape.
  • Has a definite volume.
  • Particles are tightly packed.

🎙️ In its solid state, water is called ice. It's hard and holds its own shape, like an ice cube. This is because its particles are very close together and vibrate in fixed positions.

Liquid State: Flowing and Flexible

  • Takes the shape of its container.
  • Has a definite volume.

Gaseous State: Invisible and Free

  • No definite shape or volume.
  • Spreads out to fill any container.
  • Particles are far apart and move freely.

🎙️ When water turns into a gas, it's called water vapour. It's usually invisible and spreads out everywhere. Its particles are very far apart and move very fast and freely.

From Solid to Liquid: Melting

  • Heat causes ice to turn into water.
  • Particles gain energy and move more freely.
  • This change occurs at the melting point.

🎙️ When you add heat to ice, it begins to change from solid to liquid. This process is called melting. The ice particles absorb energy, start moving faster, and break free from their fixed positions.

From Liquid to Solid: Freezing

  • Cooling water turns it into ice.
  • Particles lose energy and slow down.
  • This change occurs at the freezing point.

🎙️ The opposite of melting is freezing. When you cool liquid water, it loses heat energy, and its particles slow down and arrange themselves into a fixed pattern, becoming solid ice.

2Heat Removed (Cooling)

From Liquid to Gas: Evaporation

  • Heat causes liquid water to turn into water vapour.
  • Particles gain a lot of energy and escape into the air.
  • Happens even below boiling point.

🎙️ When liquid water turns into a gas, it's called evaporation. This happens when water gains enough heat energy for its particles to break away from the liquid surface and become airborne gas particles.

1Water (Liquid)
2Heat Added
3Water Vapour (Gas)

Evaporation in Our Daily Lives

  • Wet clothes drying on a line.
  • Puddles disappearing after rain.
  • Sweat drying from our skin.

🎙️ Have you ever noticed how wet clothes dry when you hang them out? That's evaporation! The water in the clothes turns into water vapour and mixes with the air.

👕
Drying Clothes

From Gas to Liquid: Condensation

  • Cooling water vapour turns it into liquid water.
  • Particles lose energy and come closer together.
  • Forms tiny water droplets.

🎙️ The opposite of evaporation is condensation. When water vapour cools down, its particles lose energy, slow down, and come closer together, forming tiny liquid water droplets.

1Water Vapour (Gas)
2Heat Removed (Cooling)
3Water (Liquid)

Condensation in Our Daily Lives

  • Water droplets on a cold glass.
  • Fog and dew forming in the morning.
  • Clouds forming in the sky.

🎙️ Next time you take a cold drink out of the fridge, notice the water droplets on the outside of the glass. That's condensation! The water vapour in the air cools down and turns into liquid on the cold surface.

The Amazing Water Cycle

  • Continuous movement of water.
  • Involves evaporation, condensation, precipitation.
  • Keeps water circulating on Earth.

🎙️ All these changes of state are part of a bigger process called the Water Cycle. Water continuously moves from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere and back, ensuring a constant supply of fresh water.

The water cycle describes how water circulates through Earth's atmosphere, land, and ocean
