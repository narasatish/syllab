What is Temperature?

Units of Temperature: Celsius Scale

  • Temperature is measured in units, similar to how length is measured in meters.
  • The most common unit used in India and globally is degrees Celsius (°C).
  • Water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C.

🎙️ When you hear the weather forecast, temperatures are usually given in degrees Celsius. This scale is widely used because it's based on two very common reference points: the freezing and boiling points of water, making it easy to understand.

🌡️
Degrees Celsius (°C)

Units of Temperature: Fahrenheit Scale

  • Another common unit for temperature is degrees Fahrenheit (°F).
  • It is primarily used in countries like the United States.
  • Water freezes at 32°F and boils at 212°F.

🎙️ While Celsius is more prevalent, it's good to know about the Fahrenheit scale. You might encounter it in international news or older texts. Both scales measure the same thing, just with different numerical values for the same hotness or coldness.

The Clinical Thermometer: Doctor's Best Friend

  • This is the thermometer you see doctors use to measure body temperature.

🎙️ The clinical thermometer is a very familiar sight, especially when you're not feeling well. Its specific design and limited range are perfect for accurately measuring the relatively narrow range of human body temperatures without being overly complex.

Clinical Thermometer: Its Structure

  • It is a long, narrow glass tube with a bulb at one end.
  • The bulb contains mercury, a liquid metal.
  • Crucially, it has a small 'kink' or constriction near the bulb.

🎙️ Look closely at a clinical thermometer. You'll notice the silver thread of mercury and that small bend just above the bulb. This kink is a very important feature that makes it suitable for measuring body temperature, as we'll see next.

Clinical Thermometer: How it Works

  • When the bulb touches a hot object (like your body), mercury expands.
  • The expanding mercury rises up the glass tube.
  • The kink prevents mercury from falling back immediately, allowing time to read.

🎙️ The mercury expands when it gets warm, pushing it up the narrow tube. The ingenious part is the kink: it acts like a valve, stopping the mercury from dropping down as soon as you remove the thermometer from your body. This gives you time to read the temperature accurately.

Mercury rises with heat

Reading a Clinical Thermometer: Getting the Right Number

  • First, wash the thermometer and give it a few jerks.
  • Place the bulb under the tongue or armpit for about one minute.
  • Remove and read the mercury level, keeping it at eye level.

🎙️ It's important to follow these steps to get an accurate reading. Jerking the thermometer ensures the mercury is below 35°C before use. Reading at eye level prevents parallax error, where the reading appears different depending on your viewing angle.

2Place under tongue
3Wait 1 minute
4Read at eye level

Precautions for Clinical Thermometer: Handle with Care

  • Wash the thermometer with antiseptic solution before and after use.
  • Ensure the mercury level is below 35°C before taking a reading.
  • Do not hold the thermometer by the bulb while reading it.
  • Read at eye level to avoid errors.

🎙️ Just like any medical instrument, a clinical thermometer needs to be handled carefully. Washing it ensures hygiene, and checking the mercury level confirms it's ready for an accurate measurement. Holding it correctly prevents damage and ensures a true reading.

Normal Human Body Temperature: Just Right

  • The normal human body temperature is around 37°C.
  • In Fahrenheit, this is approximately 98.6°F.
  • Temperatures much higher or lower than this indicate a fever or other health issues.

🎙️ Our bodies maintain a very precise internal temperature for all our systems to function correctly. A slight deviation from 37°C can signal that our body is fighting an infection or experiencing some other problem, which is why measuring it is so important.

🎙️ Unlike the clinical thermometer for humans, the laboratory thermometer is your go-to tool for experiments. If you're heating water in a beaker or observing a chemical reaction, this is the thermometer you'd use to track temperature changes.

Laboratory Thermometer: Its Structure & Range

  • It is typically longer and has a much wider temperature range.
  • Common range: from -10°C to 110°C.
  • Unlike the clinical thermometer, it does NOT have a kink or constriction.

🎙️ The wider range of a laboratory thermometer makes it versatile for many experiments where temperatures can vary greatly, from very cold to very hot. The absence of a kink is a key difference, impacting how it's used.

Laboratory Thermometer: How it Works & Precautions

  • The bulb must be fully immersed in the substance being measured.
  • The thermometer must be kept upright, not tilted.
  • Read the temperature while the thermometer is still immersed in the substance.
  • Since there's no kink, mercury falls immediately upon removal.

🎙️ Because it lacks a kink, you must read a laboratory thermometer while it's still in the substance. If you pull it out, the mercury level will quickly drop, giving you an inaccurate reading. Proper immersion ensures the bulb is fully exposed to the substance's temperature.

  • Kink: Clinical has it, Lab does not.
  • Reading: Clinical read after removal, Lab read while immersed.

Digital Thermometers: The Modern Way

  • These thermometers use electronic sensors instead of mercury.
  • They display temperature digitally, making them very easy to read.
  • They are safe as they do not contain mercury, which is toxic.

🎙️ Digital thermometers have become very popular due to their ease of use and safety. You often see them in homes and hospitals. They provide quick, clear readings without the worry of mercury spills.

Common Mistakes: Watch Out For These!

  • Using a laboratory thermometer to measure human body temperature.
  • Reading a clinical thermometer by holding the bulb.
  • Not jerking the clinical thermometer before use.
  • Removing a laboratory thermometer from the substance before reading.

🎙️ It's easy to make mistakes, especially when you're just learning. Being aware of these common errors will help you use thermometers correctly and get accurate results every time. Always remember the specific features and uses of each type.
