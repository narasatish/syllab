Introduction: What is Temperature?

  • We often feel if something is hot or cold.
  • How do we describe 'how hot' or 'how cold' something is?
  • Temperature is a measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object.

🎙️ Think about a cup of tea or an ice cream. We instantly know if they are hot or cold. But how do we express exactly 'how' hot or 'how' cold they are? This is where temperature comes in. It gives us a precise way to describe this feeling.

Hot and Cold: Our Senses

  • Our sense of touch tells us if an object is hot or cold.
  • Example: Touching a hot pan feels 'hot', touching an ice cube feels 'cold'.

🎙️ From a very young age, we learn to distinguish between hot and cold using our hands. This natural ability helps us navigate the world safely, like knowing not to touch a flame or a very cold surface.

Is Our Sense of Touch Reliable?

  • Our sense of touch is not always a reliable measure of temperature.
  • It can be misleading in certain situations.

🎙️ Imagine you have one hand in hot water and the other in cold water. Now, put both hands into lukewarm water. The hand from hot water will feel the lukewarm water as cold, while the hand from cold water will feel it as hot! This shows our senses can be tricked.

Why Do We Need to Measure Temperature?

  • To get an accurate and objective measure of hotness or coldness.
  • For scientific experiments, cooking, weather forecasting, and health.

🎙️ Since our sense of touch isn't always accurate, we need a way to measure temperature precisely. This is crucial in many fields, like when a doctor checks your body temperature or when a chef needs to bake a cake at a specific temperature.

Temperature: A Scientific Definition

  • Temperature is the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance.
  • Higher temperature means particles are moving faster.

🎙️ At a microscopic level, everything is made of tiny particles that are constantly moving. When something is hot, these particles are vibrating or moving very quickly. When it's cold, they move slower. Temperature is essentially a measure of this average movement.

Heat vs. Temperature: Key Differences

  • Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder one.
  • Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of an object.

🎙️ This is a common point of confusion. Think of heat as the total energy contained in a substance due to its particle movement, while temperature is like the intensity of that energy. A large bucket of lukewarm water might have more total heat energy than a small cup of boiling water, but the boiling water has a higher temperature.

Units of Temperature Measurement

  • Kelvin (K) is used in scientific research.

🎙️ Just like we measure length in meters or feet, we measure temperature using specific scales. Celsius is widely used in India and many other countries, while Fahrenheit is common in the United States. Kelvin is the absolute temperature scale used by scientists.

Measuring Temperature: The Thermometer

  • A device used to measure temperature accurately.
  • It uses the principle of expansion and contraction of liquids.

🎙️ The most common tool for measuring temperature is the thermometer. Most thermometers work by using a liquid, like mercury or alcohol, that expands when heated and contracts when cooled. This change in volume is then shown on a calibrated scale.

🎙️ While all thermometers measure temperature, they are designed differently for specific purposes. The two main types we will learn about are the clinical thermometer, which you might have seen at home, and the laboratory thermometer, used in science classes.

Clinical Thermometer: Structure

  • Long, narrow glass tube with a bulb at one end.
  • Contains mercury (silver liquid) or alcohol (coloured liquid).
  • Has a kink or constriction near the bulb to prevent mercury from falling back.

🎙️ Take a closer look at a clinical thermometer. You'll see a small, shiny thread inside, which is mercury. The kink is very important because it holds the mercury level, allowing you to read the temperature even after taking the thermometer out of the mouth.

For body temperature

Clinical Thermometer: Temperature Range

  • Measures temperature from 35°C to 42°C.
  • This range covers the normal human body temperature.

🎙️ The human body temperature rarely goes below 35°C or above 42°C. So, the clinical thermometer is designed with this specific, narrow range to be very precise for medical use. Normal body temperature is around 37°C.

Using a Clinical Thermometer

  • Wash with antiseptic solution.
  • Hold firmly and give a few jerks to bring mercury below 35°C.
  • Place under tongue or armpit for a minute.
  • Read the temperature while keeping the level of mercury along your line of sight.

🎙️ It's important to follow these steps carefully to get an accurate reading. The jerking action helps to push the mercury back into the bulb, ready for the next measurement. Always make sure your eye is level with the mercury column for the most accurate reading.

Precautions with Clinical Thermometer

  • Do not use to measure temperature of any object other than human body.
  • Handle with care, it is made of glass.
  • Do not hold the thermometer by the bulb while reading it.

🎙️ Clinical thermometers are delicate and designed for a specific purpose. Using them for other objects can damage them or give inaccurate readings. Holding by the bulb can affect the mercury level, making your reading incorrect.

Laboratory Thermometer: Structure

  • Longer and wider than a clinical thermometer.
  • No kink or constriction in the tube.
  • Contains mercury or alcohol.

🎙️ Unlike the clinical thermometer, the laboratory thermometer is built for a different environment. The absence of a kink means the mercury level will fall back as soon as it's removed from the hot object, so you must read it while it's still in contact with the substance.

Laboratory Thermometer: Temperature Range

  • Measures temperature from -10°C to 110°C.
  • This wide range is suitable for various experiments in a lab.

🎙️ Laboratory experiments often involve substances that are much colder than human body temperature or much hotter, like boiling water. Therefore, a laboratory thermometer needs a much broader range to accommodate these different temperatures.

Using a Laboratory Thermometer

  • Immerse the bulb completely in the substance to be measured.
  • Hold thermometer vertically, do not tilt it.
  • Read the temperature without removing it from the substance.

🎙️ It's crucial to keep the thermometer in the substance while reading to get an accurate temperature. If you remove it, the temperature will immediately start changing. Also, ensure the bulb doesn't touch the bottom or sides of the container.

Precautions with Laboratory Thermometer

  • Do not use to measure body temperature.
  • Handle with care, it is made of glass.
  • Ensure bulb is fully immersed and not touching container walls.

🎙️ Just like clinical thermometers, laboratory thermometers have their specific uses and precautions. Never use a laboratory thermometer to measure your body temperature; its range is too wide and it lacks the kink, making it unsuitable for that purpose.

Digital Thermometers

  • Modern thermometers that display temperature digitally.
  • Do not use mercury, making them safer and easier to read.
  • Common for clinical and household use.

🎙️ With advancements in technology, digital thermometers have become very popular. They are much safer as they don't contain mercury, which is a toxic substance. They are also very convenient because the temperature is shown directly as a number, making it easy to read.

Worked Example: Reading a Thermometer Scale

  • Observe the major markings (e.g., 36, 37).
  • Count the number of small divisions between two major markings (e.g., 10 divisions).
  • Calculate the value of one small division (e.g., 1°C / 10 divisions = 0.1°C).
  • Read the mercury level carefully against the scale.

🎙️ Let's say a thermometer has major markings at 36°C and 37°C, and there are 10 small divisions between them. Each small division represents 0.1°C. If the mercury is at 36 and 5 small divisions, the reading is 36.5°C. Practice makes perfect!
