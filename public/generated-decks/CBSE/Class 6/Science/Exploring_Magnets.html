What are Magnets?

  • Objects that attract iron, nickel, and cobalt.
  • They create an invisible force around them.
  • Used in many devices we use every day.

🎙️ A magnet is any object that produces its own persistent magnetic field. This field is responsible for the force that pulls on other ferromagnetic materials like iron, and attracts or repels other magnets.

Poles of a Magnet

  • Every magnet has two poles: North and South.
  • The magnetic force is strongest at the poles.
  • These poles are always at the ends of a magnet.

🎙️ No matter how small or large a magnet is, it will always have two ends where its magnetic strength is concentrated. We call these the North Pole and the South Pole. These poles are crucial for understanding how magnets interact.

🔀
North and South poles

Like Poles Repel, Unlike Poles Attract

  • North pole attracts a South pole (and vice-versa).
  • North pole repels another North pole.
  • South pole repels another South pole.

🎙️ This is a fundamental law of magnetism. If you bring two similar poles together, like two North poles, they will push each other away. But if you bring opposite poles together, like a North and a South pole, they will pull towards each other.

N-S (Attract)

Poles Always Exist in Pairs

  • You cannot have a magnet with only one pole.
  • If you break a magnet, each piece becomes a new magnet.
  • Each new piece will have its own North and South poles.

🎙️ It's impossible to isolate a single pole of a magnet. Even if you break a bar magnet into tiny pieces, each small piece will instantly develop its own North and South poles. The magnetic poles always come in pairs.

Making Your Own Magnet: Rubbing Method

  • Take an iron nail and a strong magnet.
  • Rub the magnet along the nail in one direction.
  • Repeat 30-40 times carefully.
  • The nail will become a temporary magnet.

🎙️ You can create a temporary magnet at home! By stroking an iron object like a nail with a strong magnet repeatedly in the same direction, you can align the tiny magnetic particles within the nail, turning it into a weak magnet.

Non-Magnetic Materials

  • Materials that are NOT attracted by a magnet.

Strength of Magnetic Field

  • The magnetic field is strongest near the poles.
  • It is weakest in the middle of the magnet.
  • This is why poles have the greatest attractive force.

🎙️ The force of a magnet isn't uniform everywhere. It's most powerful at its North and South poles, which is why objects are most strongly attracted there. The strength gradually decreases as you move towards the center of the magnet.

How Magnets Lose Their Properties (Demagnetization)

  • Heating a magnet can destroy its magnetism.
  • Hammering or dropping a magnet repeatedly.
  • Improper storage can also weaken magnets.

🎙️ Magnets can lose their magnetic properties, a process called demagnetization. High heat, strong impacts like hammering or dropping, and even incorrect storage can cause the internal alignment of magnetic particles to get disturbed, weakening the magnet.

Storing Magnets Safely

  • Keep bar magnets in pairs with opposite poles together.
  • Use wooden or plastic 'keepers' at the ends.
  • Keep magnets away from electronics and credit cards.

🎙️ To ensure your magnets last long and retain their strength, proper storage is key. Bar magnets should be stored in pairs with opposite poles facing each other, separated by wooden or plastic pieces called keepers. Also, keep them away from sensitive electronics.
