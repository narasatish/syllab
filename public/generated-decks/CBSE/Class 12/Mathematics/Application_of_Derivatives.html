What is a Derivative? A Quick Recall

  • Measures instantaneous rate of change of one quantity w.r.t. another.
  • Geometrically, it represents the slope of the tangent to a curve.
  • Expressed as dy/dx for y = f(x).

🎙️ Before diving into applications, let's quickly recall what a derivative truly signifies. It's essentially about how one quantity changes with respect to another, at a specific instant. Geometrically, it's the steepness of a curve.

Application 1: Rate of Change of Quantities

  • If y = f(x), then dy/dx is the rate of change of y with respect to x.
  • Units are crucial for interpreting the result correctly.
  • Often involves time, denoted as dy/dt.

🎙️ The derivative is a powerful tool for measuring how quickly something changes. Think of speed, which is the rate of change of distance with respect to time. We'll apply this to various geometric and physical quantities.

⏱️
Measuring Change

Worked Example 1: Rate of Change of Area

  • Problem: The radius of a circle is increasing at 0.7 cm/s. Find the rate of increase of its area when r = 10 cm.
  • Solution: A = πr² => dA/dt = 2πr (dr/dt).
  • Substitute: dA/dt = 2π(10)(0.7) = 14π cm²/s.

🎙️ Here, we need to find dA/dt given dr/dt. Remember to differentiate the area formula with respect to time 't' using the chain rule. Always include correct units.

6 / 25

Worked Example 2: Rate of Change of Volume

  • Problem: A cube's side is increasing at 3 cm/s. Find the rate at which its volume is increasing when side is 10 cm.
  • Solution: V = s³ => dV/dt = 3s² (ds/dt).
  • Substitute: dV/dt = 3(10)²(3) = 900 cm³/s.

🎙️ Similar approach to the previous example: write down the volume formula, differentiate with respect to time, and substitute the given values. Units are cubic centimeters per second.

7 / 25

Common Mistakes: Rate of Change Problems

  • Forgetting chain rule (e.g., d/dt (r²) = 2r dr/dt).
  • Substituting values *before* differentiation.
  • Incorrectly identifying variables or constants.

🎙️ Students often make these errors. Always differentiate first, then substitute the specific values given. And remember the chain rule; it's fundamental here for implicit differentiation with respect to time.

Application 2: Increasing & Decreasing Functions

  • f(x) is increasing on an interval if f'(x) > 0.
  • f(x) is decreasing on an interval if f'(x) < 0.
  • f(x) is constant on an interval if f'(x) = 0.

🎙️ Derivatives tell us about the 'behavior' of a function. If the slope of the tangent is positive, the function is going up; if negative, it's going down. This helps us sketch graphs and understand function trends.

Worked Example 1: Intervals of Increase/Decrease

  • Problem: Find intervals where f(x) = x² - 4x + 6 is increasing or decreasing.
  • Solution: f'(x) = 2x - 4.
  • For increasing: 2x - 4 > 0 => x > 2. Interval: (2, ∞).
  • For decreasing: 2x - 4 < 0 => x < 2. Interval: (-∞, 2).

🎙️ To solve this, first find the derivative f'(x). Then, set f'(x) > 0 for increasing and f'(x) < 0 for decreasing. This will give you the required intervals based on the sign of the derivative.

11 / 25

Worked Example 2: Inc/Dec with Trigonometric Functions

  • Problem: Find intervals where f(x) = sin x is increasing or decreasing in [0, 2π].
  • Solution: f'(x) = cos x.
  • Increasing: cos x > 0 in [0, π/2) U (3π/2, 2π].
  • Decreasing: cos x < 0 in (π/2, 3π/2).

🎙️ This involves trigonometric functions. Remember the derivative of sin x is cos x. Then, analyze the sign of cos x in the given interval [0, 2π]. Knowledge of quadrant signs is key.

12 / 25

Common Mistakes: Inc/Dec Functions

  • Incorrectly solving inequalities after finding f'(x).
  • Not considering domain restrictions (e.g., for log functions).
  • Confusing f(x) with f'(x) when interpreting results.

🎙️ Be careful with inequality signs and interval notation. Always refer back to the original function's domain if there are any restrictions. And remember, we analyze the sign of f'(x), not f(x).

Application 3: Tangents & Normals to a Curve

  • Slope of tangent at (x₀, y₀) is m_T = f'(x₀).
  • Equation of tangent: y - y₀ = m_T(x - x₀).
  • Slope of normal m_N = -1/m_T (if m_T ≠ 0).
  • Equation of normal: y - y₀ = m_N(x - x₀).

🎙️ Derivatives are crucial for understanding the geometry of curves. They give us the exact slope of the tangent line at any point, which is fundamental for finding equations of both tangent and normal lines.

📏
Curve Geometry
15 / 25

Worked Example 1: Equation of Tangent

  • Problem: Find the equation of the tangent to y = x³ - x + 1 at (2, 7).
  • Solution: dy/dx = 3x² - 1. At (2, 7), m_T = 3(2)² - 1 = 11.
  • Equation: y - 7 = 11(x - 2) => y = 11x - 15.

🎙️ First, find the derivative of the function. Then, calculate the slope of the tangent at the given point by substituting x=2. Finally, use the point-slope form of a line to get the equation.

16 / 25

Worked Example 2: Tangent Parallel to a Line

  • Problem: Find points on y = x³ - 11x + 5 where the tangent is parallel to y = x - 11.
  • Solution: Slope of y = x - 11 is 1. dy/dx = 3x² - 11.
  • Set 3x² - 11 = 1 => 3x² = 12 => x = ±2.
  • Points: (2, -9) and (-2, 19).

🎙️ If the tangent is parallel to the given line, their slopes must be equal. Find the derivative of the curve, equate it to the slope of the given line, and solve for x. Remember to find the corresponding y values.

Common Mistakes: Tangents & Normals

  • Forgetting to find y-coordinate after finding x for the point.
  • Incorrectly calculating the slope of the normal (m_N = -1/m_T).
  • Algebraic errors in solving equations or substitution.

🎙️ A common slip is finding x but forgetting the corresponding y. Also, ensure you correctly apply the negative reciprocal for the normal's slope. Double-check your algebra for accuracy.

Application 4: Maxima & Minima (Optimization)

  • Used to find the largest or smallest values of a function.
  • Critical points occur where f'(x) = 0 or f'(x) is undefined.
  • Differentiate between local (relative) and absolute extrema.

🎙️ This is perhaps the most practical application, used extensively in engineering and economics. We use derivatives to find the 'peaks' and 'valleys' of a function, which correspond to maximum or minimum values.

⛰️
Peaks & Valleys
19 / 25

First Derivative Test for Local Extrema

  • 1. Find critical points (f'(x) = 0 or undefined).
  • 2. Check sign of f'(x) around critical points.
  • 3. If f'(x) changes from (+) to (-), it's a local maximum.
  • 4. If f'(x) changes from (-) to (+), it's a local minimum.

🎙️ The First Derivative Test helps us classify critical points. It's about observing the function's behavior – whether it's increasing then decreasing (max) or decreasing then increasing (min).

1Find f'(x) & C.P.
2Check f'(x) sign change
3(+) to (-) = Max
4(-) to (+) = Min
20 / 25

Second Derivative Test for Local Extrema

  • 1. Find critical points (f'(x) = 0).
  • 2. Find f''(x).
  • 3. If f''(c) < 0 at critical point c, it's a local maximum.
  • 4. If f''(c) > 0 at critical point c, it's a local minimum. (If f''(c) = 0, test fails).

🎙️ The Second Derivative Test is often quicker if it doesn't fail. It uses the concavity of the function. A negative second derivative means concave down (maximum), positive means concave up (minimum).

1Find f'(x) & C.P.
2Find f''(x)
3Evaluate f''(c)
4Conclude max/min

Worked Example 1: Finding Local Extrema

  • Problem: Find local maximum and minimum values of f(x) = x³ - 3x.
  • Solution: f'(x) = 3x² - 3 = 0 => x = ±1 (Critical Points).
  • f''(x) = 6x. At x = 1, f''(1) = 6 > 0 => Local Min: f(1) = -2.
  • At x = -1, f''(-1) = -6 < 0 => Local Max: f(-1) = 2.

🎙️ Here, we apply the Second Derivative Test. After finding critical points by setting f'(x)=0, we evaluate f''(x) at these points to classify them as local maxima or minima. Remember to find the actual values of the function.

22 / 25

Worked Example 2: Optimization Problem

  • Problem: Find two positive numbers whose sum is 15 and the sum of whose squares is minimum.
  • Solution: Let numbers be x, (15-x). Sum of squares S(x) = x² + (15-x)².
  • S'(x) = 2x - 2(15-x) = 4x - 30. Set S'(x) = 0 => x = 7.5.
  • S''(x) = 4 > 0 (minimum). Numbers are 7.5 and 7.5.

🎙️ This is a classic optimization problem. First, set up a function that represents the quantity to be minimized in terms of a single variable. Then, use derivatives to find its minimum value.

Common Mistakes: Maxima & Minima

  • Not checking boundary points for absolute extrema on a closed interval.
  • Forgetting to find the actual maximum/minimum *value* of the function.
  • Incorrectly interpreting f''(c) = 0 (test fails, use First Derivative Test).

🎙️ Remember, local extrema are not always absolute extrema, especially on a closed interval. Always substitute critical points and boundary points into the *original* function to find the actual values. If the second derivative test fails, switch to the first derivative test.
