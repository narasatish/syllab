Coordinates of a Point in Space (x, y, z)

  • Extending 2D (x,y) to 3D (x,y,z).
  • Origin O (0,0,0) as the reference point.
  • Three mutually perpendicular axes: X, Y, Z.

🎙️ Just like we locate a point on a plane using two coordinates, in 3D space, we need three: x, y, and z. These coordinates represent the perpendicular distances from the YZ, XZ, and XY planes respectively. Understanding this system is the first step to mastering 3D geometry.

Distance Between Two Points in 3D

  • Generalisation of 2D distance formula.
  • Let P(x1, y1, z1) and Q(x2, y2, z2).
  • PQ = sqrt((x2-x1)² + (y2-y1)² + (z2-z1)²).

🎙️ The distance formula in 3D is a direct extension of its 2D counterpart, derived using the Pythagorean theorem. This formula is fundamental for many problems, so ensure you understand its application well for your exams and future topics.

Section Formula: Internal Division

  • Point R divides line segment PQ internally in ratio m:n.
  • R = ((nx1+mx2)/(m+n), (ny1+my2)/(m+n), (nz1+mz2)/(m+n)).
  • Crucial for finding coordinates of points along a line segment.

🎙️ The section formula helps us find the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment joining two given points in a specific ratio. Remember the 'cross-multiplication' pattern: m multiplies with the coordinates of Q, and n with P. This is a frequently tested concept.

Section Formula: External Division & Midpoint

  • External Division: Replace '+' with '-' in internal formula.
  • R = ((nx1-mx2)/(n-m), (ny1-my2)/(n-m), (nz1-mz2)/(n-m)).
  • Midpoint: Special case of internal division (m=n=1).

🎙️ External division is similar to internal, just with a sign change in the formula. The midpoint formula is a very important special case, where the ratio is 1:1, essentially averaging the coordinates. Be careful with the order of m and n in the denominator for external division.

Direction Cosines of a Line

  • Cosines of angles made by a line with positive X, Y, Z axes.
  • Denoted by l, m, n (l=cosα, m=cosβ, n=cosγ).
  • Fundamental Property: l² + m² + n² = 1.

🎙️ Direction cosines tell us about the orientation of a line in 3D space. They are the cosines of the angles the line makes with the positive directions of the coordinate axes. The fundamental property l² + m² + n² = 1 is very important and frequently tested in exams.

Direction Ratios of a Line

  • Any three numbers proportional to the direction cosines.
  • Denoted by a, b, c.
  • If (a,b,c) are DRs, then l = ±a/√(a²+b²+c²), etc.

🎙️ Direction ratios are simply any set of three numbers that are proportional to the direction cosines. While direction cosines uniquely define a direction, direction ratios can be scaled. Understanding their relationship is key for solving problems involving lines and their orientation.

Equation of a Line in Space: Vector Form

  • Line passing through a point 'a' and parallel to vector 'b'.
  • Vector equation: r = a + λb, where λ is a scalar.
  • 'a' is the position vector of a known point on the line.

🎙️ The vector form of a line's equation is intuitive. It states that any point 'r' on the line can be reached by starting at a known point 'a' on the line and moving some scalar multiple (λ) along the direction vector 'b'. The vector 'b' gives the line its orientation in space.

Equation of a Line in Space: Cartesian Form

  • Derived from r = a + λb (r=xi+yj+zk, a=x1i+y1j+z1k, b=ai+bj+ck).
  • (x-x1)/a = (y-y1)/b = (z-z1)/c = λ.
  • Here (x1,y1,z1) is a point and (a,b,c) are DRs of the line.

🎙️ The Cartesian form is derived directly from the vector form by equating the components. Here, (x1, y1, z1) are the coordinates of a known point on the line, and (a, b, c) are its direction ratios. This form is often easier for calculations involving points and planes.

Shortest Distance between Two Skew Lines

  • Skew lines are non-parallel and non-intersecting lines in 3D.
  • Formula: |(a2-a1).(b1xb2)| / |b1xb2|.
  • a1, a2 are position vectors; b1, b2 are direction vectors.

🎙️ Skew lines are a unique concept in 3D geometry. The shortest distance between them is the length of the common perpendicular, which can be found using this formula. The formula involves the scalar triple product and the magnitude of the cross product, so a good grasp of vector algebra is essential here.

Shortest Distance between Parallel Lines

  • Lines are parallel if their direction vectors are proportional (b1 = λb2).
  • Formula: |(a2-a1) x b| / |b|.
  • Here, 'b' is the common direction vector for both lines.

🎙️ For parallel lines, the shortest distance is simply the perpendicular distance between them. The formula is simpler than for skew lines, involving a cross product and the magnitude of the direction vector. Ensure you correctly identify 'a1', 'a2', and the common 'b' vector.

Equation of a Plane: Normal Form

  • Vector Form: r . n̂ = d (n̂ is unit normal vector, d is distance from origin).
  • Cartesian Form: lx + my + nz = d (l, m, n are DCs of the normal).
  • The normal vector is always perpendicular to the plane.

🎙️ The normal form is fundamental for defining a plane. It uses a vector perpendicular to the plane (the normal vector) and its distance from the origin. Understanding the normal vector's role is key, as it dictates the plane's orientation in space.

Equation of a Plane: Intercept & Point Forms

  • Intercept Form: x/a + y/b + z/c = 1 (a,b,c are x,y,z intercepts).
  • Plane passing through (x1,y1,z1) and normal to vector N(A,B,C): A(x-x1) + B(y-y1) + C(z-z1) = 0.
  • Here (A,B,C) are Direction Ratios of the normal vector.

🎙️ The intercept form is very useful when the plane's intersections with the axes are known. The point-normal form allows us to define a plane using a point it passes through and its normal vector, which is a very versatile and commonly used method.

Equation of a Plane: Through Three Points

  • A unique plane passes through three non-collinear points P, Q, R.
  • Vector Form: (r - P) . ((Q - P) x (R - P)) = 0.
  • Cartesian Form: Determinant method involving coordinates of the points.

🎙️ Just like two points define a line, three non-collinear points define a unique plane. The vector form uses the idea that the vector from a point on the plane to any other point on the plane must be coplanar with two vectors formed by the three given points. This is a powerful concept.
