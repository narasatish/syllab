The 3D Coordinate System: Your Room's Corner

  • Three mutually perpendicular axes: X, Y, Z.
  • Origin (0,0,0) is their intersection point.
  • These axes divide space into eight octants.

🎙️ Imagine a corner of your room. The floor edges meeting at the corner can be X and Y axes, and the wall edge going up from that corner is the Z axis. This is your 3D coordinate system.

Coordinates of a Point in Space

  • Any point P in space is represented by (x, y, z).
  • x, y, z are perpendicular distances from YZ, XZ, XY planes respectively.
  • Each coordinate specifies a position along an axis.

🎙️ Just like in 2D, where a point is (x,y), in 3D, we add a 'z' coordinate. These three numbers uniquely identify any point in space, giving its exact location.

Distance Formula in 3D

  • Extension of the 2D distance formula.
  • Distance between P(x₁, y₁, z₁) and Q(x₂, y₂, z₂).
  • Formula: √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)² + (z₂-z₁)²].

🎙️ The distance formula in 3D is a natural extension of its 2D counterpart. We simply add the squared difference of the z-coordinates. Remember to take the square root at the end!

Direction Cosines: A Compass in 3D

  • Cosines of the angles a line makes with positive X, Y, Z axes.
  • Denoted by l, m, n (cos α, cos β, cos γ).
  • They define the orientation of a line in space.

🎙️ Just as a compass tells you direction in 2D, direction cosines tell you the exact orientation of a line in 3D space. They are the cosines of the angles the line makes with the coordinate axes.

Fundamental Relation of Direction Cosines

  • For any line, the sum of squares of its direction cosines is always 1.
  • l² + m² + n² = 1.
  • This property is crucial for solving problems.

🎙️ This identity, l² + m² + n² = 1, is a cornerstone of 3D geometry. It's like a built-in check for direction cosines and is frequently used in derivations and problem-solving.

✅
l² + m² + n² = 1

Direction Ratios: Scaled Directions

  • Any three numbers a, b, c proportional to the direction cosines l, m, n.
  • They also define the direction, but not uniquely.
  • If (a, b, c) are DRs, then (ka, kb, kc) are also DRs for any k ≠ 0.

🎙️ Direction ratios are like a scaled version of direction cosines. They give the same direction but without the unit magnitude constraint. Think of them as components of a vector along the axes.

Proportional to DCs

Connecting Direction Cosines and Ratios

  • If (a, b, c) are DRs, then DCs are l = ± a/√(a²+b²+c²), m = ± b/√(a²+b²+c²), n = ± c/√(a²+b²+c²).
  • The sign depends on the direction of the line.
  • This link allows conversion between DRs and DCs.

🎙️ This formula provides the bridge between direction ratios and direction cosines. It allows you to find the unique direction cosines from any given set of direction ratios, by normalizing them.

🔗
DRs ↔️ DCs

Equation of a Line in Space: Vector Form

  • Analogy: A train leaving a station (point 'a') in a specific direction (vector 'b').
  • Equation: **r** = **a** + λ**b**.
  • **a** is position vector of a known point on the line, **b** is vector parallel to the line, λ is a scalar parameter.

🎙️ Think of the vector 'a' as the starting point, and 'b' as the direction the line extends in. Lambda (λ) allows you to reach any point on that line by scaling the direction vector.

Train on a track

Equation of a Line in Space: Cartesian Form

  • Derived from the vector form.
  • Equation: (x - x₁)/a = (y - y₁)/b = (z - z₁)/c.
  • (x₁, y₁, z₁) is a point on the line, (a, b, c) are its direction ratios.

🎙️ The Cartesian form is often more intuitive for calculations involving coordinates. It directly uses a point on the line and the direction ratios to define the line's path.

The path of a line

Line Passing Through Two Given Points

  • If a line passes through A(**a**) and B(**b**).
  • Vector Form: **r** = **a** + λ(**b** - **a**).
  • Cartesian Form: (x-x₁)/(x₂-x₁) = (y-y₁)/(y₂-y₁) = (z-z₁)/(z₂-z₁).

🎙️ When you have two points, you can easily find the direction vector by subtracting their position vectors. This direction vector, along with one of the points, allows you to write the line's equation.

Angle Between Two Lines

  • The angle between two lines is the angle between their direction vectors.
  • If lines have DRs (a₁,b₁,c₁) and (a₂,b₂,c₂).
  • cos θ = |(a₁a₂ + b₁b₂ + c₁c₂)| / [√(a₁²+b₁²+c₁²)√(a₂²+b₂²+c₂²)].

🎙️ This formula uses the dot product concept. The absolute value ensures we get the acute angle. Remember that if cos θ = 0, the lines are perpendicular, and if cos θ = 1, they are parallel.

Shortest Distance Between Two Skew Lines

  • Skew lines: non-parallel, non-intersecting lines in 3D space.
  • Analogy: Two airplanes flying at different altitudes and paths without colliding.
  • Formula (vector): d = |(**a₂** - **a₁**) . (**b₁** x **b₂**)| / |**b₁** x **b₂**|.

🎙️ Skew lines are unique to 3D. The shortest distance between them is along a line perpendicular to both. The formula involves the scalar triple product and the magnitude of the cross product.

Equation of a Plane: Normal Form

  • Analogy: A wall in a room, defined by its perpendicular distance from the origin and its orientation.
  • Vector Form: **r** . **n̂** = d.
  • **n̂** is unit normal vector to the plane, d is perpendicular distance from origin.

🎙️ A plane is uniquely determined by its distance from the origin and the direction of its normal vector. The normal vector is perpendicular to every line in the plane.

Equation of a Plane: Cartesian Form

  • General form: Ax + By + Cz + D = 0.
  • A, B, C are direction ratios of the normal to the plane.
  • This is the most common form for solving plane problems.

🎙️ The coefficients A, B, C in the Cartesian equation are directly the direction ratios of the normal to the plane. This form is very versatile for various plane-related calculations.

Plane Passing Through Three Non-Collinear Points

  • Three non-collinear points uniquely define a plane.
  • Method: Use a general point (x,y,z) and the three given points.
  • Formulate vectors and use scalar triple product or determinant.

🎙️ Just like two points define a line, three non-collinear points define a plane. The key idea is that the three vectors formed by these points must be coplanar, meaning their scalar triple product is zero.

Common Mistakes in 3D Geometry

  • Sign errors in coordinate calculations.
  • Confusing Direction Cosines with Direction Ratios.
  • Incorrectly applying vector vs. Cartesian forms.
  • Errors in cross product or dot product calculations.

🎙️ Be meticulous with signs, especially when subtracting coordinates or dealing with negative numbers. Always double-check which formula applies to DCs or DRs, and ensure you're using the correct vector operations.
