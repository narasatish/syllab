Recall: What is a Definite Integral?

  • Represents the net signed area under a curve between two limits.
  • Evaluated over a specific interval [a, b].
  • Fundamental Theorem of Calculus is key for evaluation.

🎙️ Before we jump into applications, let's quickly recall what a definite integral signifies. It's essentially the 'sum' of infinitely many tiny parts, giving us the area under a curve, taking into account its sign.

Area Bounded by a Curve and the X-axis

  • Consider a continuous function y = f(x) over [a, b].
  • The area is found by integrating f(x) with respect to x.
  • Region is bounded by the curve, the x-axis, and vertical lines x=a, x=b.

🎙️ The most fundamental application is finding the area under a curve. When the curve y=f(x) is above the x-axis, the integral from 'a' to 'b' directly gives us this area.

📏
Measuring Area
5 / 25

Formula: Area under y = f(x) (x-axis)

  • Area A = ∫[a,b] f(x) dx.
  • Ensure f(x) ≥ 0 for the interval for positive area.
  • If f(x) < 0, area for that part is |∫ f(x) dx|.

🎙️ This is our first key formula. Remember, area is always a positive quantity, so if your function dips below the x-axis, you'll need to handle those parts separately or take the absolute value for that segment.

Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-axis

  • Consider a continuous function x = g(y) over [c, d].
  • The area is found by integrating g(y) with respect to y.
  • Region bounded by the curve, the y-axis, and horizontal lines y=c, y=d.

🎙️ Similarly, we can find the area when the curve is defined as x in terms of y, and bounded by the y-axis. Here, we integrate with respect to y, using y-limits.

Formula: Area under x = g(y) (y-axis)

  • Area A = ∫[c,d] g(y) dy.
  • Ensure g(y) ≥ 0 for the interval (region to the right of y-axis).
  • If g(y) < 0 (region to the left), area for that part is |∫ g(y) dy|.

🎙️ This formula is symmetric to the x-axis case. Just remember to express x in terms of y and integrate with respect to y, using y-limits 'c' and 'd'.

Common Mistake 1: Handling Negative Area

  • A definite integral gives the net signed area, which can be negative.
  • Actual geometric area is always positive.
  • For regions below x-axis (or left of y-axis), take the absolute value of the integral for that segment.

🎙️ A crucial point for exams! If a part of the curve lies below the x-axis, the integral will yield a negative value for that segment. Since area cannot be negative, we must take the absolute value of such integrals.

Area Bounded Between Two Curves (x-axis)

  • Find area between y = f(x) and y = g(x) over [a, b].
  • Assume f(x) ≥ g(x) throughout the interval.
  • The area is the difference between the areas under each curve.

🎙️ What if we need to find the area enclosed by two curves? The principle remains the same: it's the area under the upper curve minus the area under the lower curve.

↔️
Gap between

Formula: Area Between Two Curves (x-axis)

  • Area A = ∫[a,b] [f(x) - g(x)] dx.
  • f(x) is the upper curve, g(x) is the lower curve in [a,b].
  • Limits 'a' and 'b' are often intersection points or given boundaries.

🎙️ This is a very common scenario in exams. The formula is straightforward: integral of (upper function minus lower function). The challenge often lies in correctly identifying which function is 'upper' and determining the correct limits.

Essential Step: Finding Intersection Points

  • Set f(x) = g(x) to find the x-coordinates of intersection.
  • These points often define the limits of integration for the area.
  • Critical for regions bounded solely by the curves themselves.

🎙️ When the region is bounded *only* by the curves themselves, the points where they intersect become your limits of integration. This is a critical first step for many problems involving areas between curves.

Common Mistake 2: Swapping Upper and Lower Curves

  • Incorrect order of subtraction [g(x) - f(x)] leads to a negative area.
  • Always verify which function is greater (upper) in the given interval.
  • A quick sketch of the curves helps immensely to avoid this error.

🎙️ Another common pitfall! If you accidentally subtract the upper curve from the lower, your result will be negative. Always draw a rough sketch or test a point within the interval to confirm the upper and lower functions.

Key Formulas at a Glance

  • Area under y=f(x) (x-axis): ∫[a,b] f(x) dx.
  • Area under x=g(y) (y-axis): ∫[c,d] g(y) dy.
  • Area between f(x) and g(x): ∫[a,b] [f(x)-g(x)] dx.

Recap & Exam Strategy

  • Always draw a rough sketch of the region.
  • Identify correct limits of integration (x or y).
  • Pay attention to upper/lower or right/left curves.
  • Handle negative areas carefully by taking absolute values of segments.

🎙️ To ace this chapter, always start with a sketch. It helps visualize the region and correctly identify limits and the 'difference' function. Be mindful of areas below the axis or to the left of the y-axis!
