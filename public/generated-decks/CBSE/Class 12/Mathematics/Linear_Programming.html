What is a Linear Programming Problem (LPP)?

  • A method to achieve the best outcome.
  • Optimise a linear objective function.
  • Subject to linear equality and inequality constraints.
  • Variables are non-negative.

🎙️ Linear Programming is essentially a mathematical technique to find the 'best' solution. This 'best' could mean maximizing profit or minimizing cost. The key is that all relationships involved are linear.

Constraints: The Limitations

Non-negativity Restrictions

  • Decision variables cannot take negative values.
  • E.g., number of items produced, amount of resources used.
  • Mathematically: x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.

🎙️ In most practical scenarios, quantities like the number of products, hours worked, or units of material cannot be negative. This fundamental assumption simplifies the problem and ensures realistic solutions.

The Feasible Region

  • The common region satisfying all constraints.
  • Represents all possible solutions to the LPP.
  • Can be bounded (enclosed) or unbounded.
  • Every point in this region is a feasible solution.

🎙️ The feasible region is the area on your graph where all your constraints overlap. Any point within this region, including its boundaries, is a valid solution that respects all the limitations of your problem.

The Corner Point Method Theorem

  • The optimal solution (if it exists) lies at a corner point.
  • A corner point is an intersection of two boundary lines.
  • It's also called a vertex of the feasible region.
  • Reduces the search for optimal solution to a few points.

🎙️ This theorem is the backbone of the graphical method. It tells us that we don't need to check every single point in the vast feasible region. We only need to evaluate the objective function at the 'corners' of this region.

Steps for Solving LPP Graphically

  • 1. Formulate the LPP.
  • 2. Graph each constraint as an equation.
  • 3. Identify the feasible region.
  • 4. Determine the corner points.
  • 5. Evaluate the objective function at each corner point.
  • 6. Identify the optimal solution.

🎙️ These six steps provide a clear roadmap for solving any LPP using the graphical method. Make sure to follow them meticulously to avoid errors, especially when plotting the inequalities and finding intersection points.

Worked Example 1: Graphing Constraints

  • Convert inequalities to equations: x + y = 50, x = 20.
  • Plot these lines on the graph.
  • Test a point (e.g., origin) to find the feasible side of inequalities.
  • Remember x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0 means first quadrant.

Worked Example 1: Feasible Region & Corner Points

  • Shade the common region satisfying all constraints.
  • Identify the vertices (corner points) of this region.
  • E.g., (0,0), (20,0), (20,30), (0,50) (assuming x+y<=50 and x<=20 are the only specific constraints, and y>=0)
  • Calculate coordinates of intersection points.

🎙️ The shaded area is your feasible region. Now, carefully identify all the points where the boundary lines intersect within or on the edge of this feasible region. These are your crucial corner points.

Worked Example 1: Evaluating Objective Function

  • Substitute coordinates of each corner point into Z = 4x + y.
  • At (0,0), Z = 0.
  • At (20,0), Z = 80.
  • At (20,30), Z = 110.
  • At (0,50), Z = 50.
  • Maximum Z = 110 at (20,30).

🎙️ Finally, we plug the coordinates of each corner point into our objective function Z. The point that yields the highest value for Z is our optimal solution for a maximisation problem. In this case, 110 is the maximum profit.

Special Cases in LPP

  • Unbounded Solution: Objective function can be increased/decreased infinitely.
  • No Feasible Solution: Constraints are contradictory, no common region.
  • Multiple Optimal Solutions: Objective function value is same at two corner points.

🎙️ While most LPPs have a unique optimal solution, it's important to be aware of these special cases. An unbounded solution means there are no limits to the objective function, while no feasible solution means the problem itself is impossible to satisfy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Incorrectly plotting inequalities.
  • Wrongly identifying the feasible region.
  • Missing or miscalculating corner points.
  • Errors in evaluating the objective function.
  • Forgetting non-negativity restrictions.

🎙️ Accuracy is key in LPP. The most frequent errors occur during graphing and identifying the feasible region. Always double-check your shaded area and the coordinates of your corner points to ensure precision.
