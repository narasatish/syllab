Defining a Differential Equation

  • An equation involving derivatives of a dependent variable.
  • With respect to one or more independent variables.

🎙️ Formally, a differential equation is an equation that contains an unknown function and one or more of its derivatives. It's the mathematical expression of how things are dynamically interconnected.

Understanding Order

  • It is the order of the highest order derivative present in the differential equation.
  • Always a positive integer.

🎙️ The order is simply the order of the highest derivative appearing in the equation. For example, dy/dx has order 1, while d²y/dx² has order 2. It's a fundamental characteristic.

1Identify all derivatives
2Find the highest order derivative
3That's the order!

🎙️ Now, once you've identified the 'highest rank' derivative (the order), the 'degree' is like the power or exponent to which that highest rank derivative is raised. It tells us about the structure of the equation.

Deciphering the Degree

  • The highest power of the highest order derivative.
  • When the equation is a polynomial in derivatives.

🎙️ The degree is defined as the highest power of the highest order derivative, *provided* the differential equation is expressible as a polynomial in derivatives. If not, the degree is undefined. This is a common point of confusion!

Finding the 'Family' vs. 'Specific' Story

  • General solution: a family of curves.
  • Particular solution: a unique curve from that family.

🎙️ Think of solving a differential equation as finding a family of curves, like a family tree. The 'general solution' gives you the whole family. A 'particular solution' is like picking out one specific member of that family, with its own unique story.

The General Solution

  • Contains arbitrary constants.
  • Represents a family of solutions.

🎙️ A general solution to a differential equation is a solution that contains arbitrary constants. These constants allow it to represent an entire family of curves, each satisfying the differential equation.

The Particular Solution

  • Obtained from the general solution.
  • By assigning specific values to constants.
  • Uses given initial or boundary conditions.

🎙️ When you're given specific conditions, like an initial value (e.g., y(0)=1), you can determine the exact values of those arbitrary constants. This specific solution, free of arbitrary constants, is called the particular solution.

How to Form a DE

  • Identify arbitrary constants (A, B, C...).
  • Differentiate the equation repeatedly.
  • Eliminate the constants using the original and differentiated equations.

🎙️ The process involves differentiating the given equation as many times as there are arbitrary constants. Then, use algebraic manipulation to eliminate those constants, leaving you with the differential equation.

1Count constants
2Differentiate N times
3Eliminate constants

Sorting Your Laundry!

  • Group 'x' terms with 'dx', 'y' terms with 'dy'.
  • Then integrate both sides separately.

🎙️ Imagine you're doing laundry and need to separate whites from colours. The 'variable separable' method is similar: you separate all terms involving 'x' with 'dx' on one side, and all 'y' terms with 'dy' on the other. Then, you integrate!

🧺
Separating items
19 / 30

Solving by Variable Separation

  • Rearrange the equation to f(x)dx = g(y)dy.
  • Integrate both sides: ∫f(x)dx = ∫g(y)dy.
  • Add an arbitrary constant C.

🎙️ This is often the simplest method. The key is to algebraically manipulate the equation so that all terms involving one variable are on one side with its differential, and the other variable's terms are on the other side.

1Separate variables
2Integrate both sides
3Add constant C

Uniformity in Powers

  • A function f(x, y) is homogeneous of degree n if f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x, y).
  • A DE dy/dx = f(x,y) is homogeneous if f(x,y) is a homogeneous function of degree zero.

🎙️ Homogeneous differential equations have a special property: if you replace x with tx and y with ty, the 't' factor cancels out, leaving the function unchanged. This uniformity allows for a specific substitution to solve them.

⚖️
Balance/Uniformity

Solving Homogeneous DEs

  • Substitute y = vx, so dy/dx = v + x(dv/dx).
  • The equation transforms into variable separable form.
  • Solve for v, then substitute back y/x for v.

🎙️ The magic trick for homogeneous equations is the substitution y = vx. This substitution cleverly converts the homogeneous equation into a variable separable form, which we already know how to solve. Don't forget to substitute back!

1Substitute y=vx
2Separate variables
3Integrate, then substitute back

The 'Straightforward' DEs

  • A DE is linear if it's of the form dy/dx + Py = Q or dx/dy + Px = Q.
  • P and Q are functions of x (or y).

🎙️ Linear differential equations are a very important class because they have a standard, straightforward method of solution. They don't have products of y and its derivatives, nor powers of y or its derivatives greater than one.

📏
Straight line

Solving Linear DEs

  • Write in standard form: dy/dx + Py = Q.
  • Find Integrating Factor (IF) = e^(∫Pdx).
  • Solution is y(IF) = ∫Q(IF)dx + C.

🎙️ The key to solving linear DEs is the 'Integrating Factor'. Once you calculate this factor, the solution simply follows a direct formula. Master these three steps, and you'll ace these problems!

1Standard Form
2Calculate IF
3Apply Solution Formula
