Formal Definition: Limits

  • Left-Hand Limit (LHL): x approaches 'a' from left (x < a)
  • Right-Hand Limit (RHL): x approaches 'a' from right (x > a)
  • Limit exists if LHL = RHL

🎙️ For a limit to exist at a point, the function must approach the *same* value whether you come from the left side or the right side. If these two values are different, the limit does not exist.

Continuity: The Formal Definition

  • A function f(x) is continuous at x=a if:
  • 1. f(a) is defined
  • 2. lim f(x) as x→a exists
  • 3. lim f(x) as x→a = f(a)

🎙️ For a function to be continuous at a specific point 'a', three strict conditions must be met: the function must have a value there, the limit must exist there, and crucially, these two values must be identical.

Visualising Discontinuity

  • Jump Discontinuity: LHL ≠ RHL
  • Removable Discontinuity: Hole in graph (limit exists but ≠ f(a) or f(a) undefined)
  • Infinite Discontinuity: Vertical asymptote

🎙️ When any of the three conditions for continuity fail, we have a discontinuity. Think of a road that suddenly ends, or has a missing bridge, or a sharp drop-off – these are analogous to different types of discontinuities.

Continuity of Polynomial Functions

  • Functions like P(x) = ax^n + bx^(n-1) + ... + c
  • Always continuous for all real numbers
  • Their graphs are smooth curves
  • Examples: x^2, 3x+5, x^3-2x+1

🎙️ Polynomial functions are the 'well-behaved' members of the function family. Their graphs are always smooth and unbroken, meaning they are continuous everywhere. You never have to worry about breaks or jumps with polynomials.

Continuity of Rational Functions

  • Functions of the form f(x) = P(x)/Q(x)
  • Continuous everywhere *except* where Q(x) = 0
  • Discontinuity occurs at roots of the denominator
  • Example: 1/x is discontinuous at x=0

🎙️ Rational functions are continuous in their domain. The only points where they might be discontinuous are where the denominator becomes zero, as division by zero is undefined. These points often lead to vertical asymptotes.

Worked Example 1: Checking Continuity

  • Problem: Check continuity of f(x) = { x+2 if x ≤ 1, x^2 if x > 1 } at x=1
  • Step 1: Find f(1)
  • Step 2: Find LHL at x=1
  • Step 3: Find RHL at x=1; Solution: f(1)=3, LHL=3, RHL=1. Since LHL ≠ RHL, f(x) is discontinuous at x=1.

🎙️ This example demonstrates the critical check: all three conditions must hold. Here, the LHL and RHL approach different values, immediately telling us that the function has a jump discontinuity at x=1.

Worked Example 2: Finding 'k' for Continuity

  • Problem: Find k if f(x) = { kx+1 if x ≤ 5, 3x-5 if x > 5 } is continuous at x=5
  • For continuity, LHL = RHL = f(5)
  • LHL = lim (kx+1) as x→5- = 5k+1
  • RHL = lim (3x-5) as x→5+ = 3(5)-5 = 10; f(5) = 5k+1; Set 5k+1 = 10. Solve for k.

🎙️ This is a common exam-style question. If a function is given as continuous, we can use the definition to set up an equation. By equating the LHL, RHL, and function value, we can solve for the unknown constant 'k'.

Properties of Continuous Functions

  • If f and g are continuous at x=a, then:
  • f ± g is continuous at x=a
  • f * g is continuous at x=a; f / g is continuous at x=a (if g(a) ≠ 0)
  • (f o g) is continuous if f is continuous at g(a) and g is continuous at a

🎙️ These properties are very useful. They mean that if you combine continuous functions through basic arithmetic operations or composition, the resulting function will also be continuous, provided the denominators aren't zero.

🎙️ Intuitively, a function is differentiable if its graph is smooth, without any sharp points or corners. Imagine driving a car: differentiability means you can make a smooth turn at any point, without jerking the steering wheel.

Differentiability: The Formal Definition

  • A function f(x) is differentiable at x=a if the derivative f'(a) exists
  • f'(a) = lim [f(a+h) - f(a)] / h as h→0
  • This limit must exist and be finite
  • Left-Hand Derivative (LHD) = Right-Hand Derivative (RHD)

🎙️ Formally, differentiability means the limit of the difference quotient exists at that point. This implies that the slope of the tangent line is well-defined and unique, whether you approach from the left or the right.

Geometric Meaning of Derivative

  • The derivative f'(a) represents the slope of the tangent line
  • To the curve y=f(x) at the point (a, f(a))
  • It measures the instantaneous rate of change
  • How fast y is changing with respect to x at that exact point

🎙️ Geometrically, the derivative is simply the slope of the line that just touches the curve at a single point. This slope tells us how steeply the function is rising or falling at that precise instant.

Differentiability IMPLIES Continuity

  • If a function is differentiable at a point, it MUST be continuous at that point
  • Differentiability is a stronger condition than continuity
  • Think: a smooth curve (differentiable) cannot have breaks (discontinuous)
  • The converse is NOT true

🎙️ This is a key theorem! If a function is differentiable, it has no choice but to be continuous. A function cannot have a well-defined tangent if it has a jump or a hole. However, a continuous function might have sharp corners and thus not be differentiable.

Visualising Non-Differentiability

  • Sharp Corners/Cusps: e.g., f(x) = |x| at x=0
  • Vertical Tangents: e.g., f(x) = x^(1/3) at x=0
  • Discontinuities: If not continuous, cannot be differentiable
  • At these points, a unique tangent cannot be drawn.

🎙️ Points of non-differentiability are where the graph isn't 'smooth'. A sharp corner means you can draw infinitely many tangent lines, not a unique one. A vertical tangent has an undefined slope, hence no finite derivative.

Worked Example 3: Checking Differentiability

  • Problem: Check differentiability of f(x) = |x| at x=0
  • Left-Hand Derivative (LHD) at x=0: lim [f(0+h)-f(0)]/h as h→0- = lim |h|/h as h→0- = -1
  • Right-Hand Derivative (RHD) at x=0: lim [f(0+h)-f(0)]/h as h→0+ = lim |h|/h as h→0+ = 1
  • Since LHD ≠ RHD, f(x) = |x| is not differentiable at x=0.

🎙️ This is a classic example. Even though |x| is continuous at x=0, its sharp corner makes it non-differentiable. The LHD and RHD are different, indicating no unique tangent slope.

Worked Example 4: Find 'a' and 'b' for Differentiability

  • Problem: Find a and b if f(x) = { ax+b if x < 1, x^2 if x ≥ 1 } is differentiable at x=1
  • Condition 1: Continuity at x=1 (LHL=RHL=f(1)) => a+b=1
  • Condition 2: Differentiability at x=1 (LHD=RHD)
  • LHD = a; RHD = 2x evaluated at x=1 = 2; So, a=2. Substitute 'a' into a+b=1 to find b.

🎙️ For a piecewise function to be differentiable at the junction point, it must first be continuous there, and then its left and right derivatives must match. This gives us a system of equations to solve for the unknown constants.

Applications of Differentiability

  • Rates of Change: Velocity, acceleration, population growth

  • Physics and Engineering: Modeling motion, forces, fluid flow
