Microbes in Household Products: Curd

  • Curd is made by adding a 'starter' (inoculum) to warm milk.
  • The starter contains Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB), e.g., Lactobacillus.
  • LAB convert lactose sugar into lactic acid, coagulating milk proteins.

🎙️ Think of making curd at home. A small amount of old curd is added to warm milk. This 'starter' contains millions of Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB), like Lactobacillus. These bacteria multiply, converting lactose sugar in milk into lactic acid, which coagulates the milk proteins.

Benefits of Curd & Bread Making

  • Curd improves nutritional quality by increasing Vitamin B12.
  • LAB also check disease-causing microbes in the stomach.
  • Baker's Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) ferments dough, making bread fluffy due to CO2.

🎙️ Curd not only improves taste but also nutritional quality by increasing Vitamin B12. LAB also checks disease-causing microbes in our stomach. For bread, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (Baker's Yeast) ferments dough, producing CO2 that makes bread fluffy.

Cheese Making: Art & Science

  • Different microbes give distinct flavors and textures to cheese.
  • Swiss cheese's large holes are from CO2 by *Propionibacterium sharmanii*.
  • Roquefort cheese gets its unique flavor from fungi like *Penicillium roqueforti*.

🎙️ Cheese is another household product where microbes are essential. Different microbes give distinct flavors and textures. For example, large holes in Swiss cheese are due to CO2 produced by *Propionibacterium sharmanii*, while Roquefort cheese gets its characteristic flavor from fungi like *Penicillium roqueforti*.

Microbes in Industrial Products: Beverages

  • Microbes are used on a large scale to produce alcoholic beverages.
  • Brewer's yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) ferments malted cereals and fruit juices.
  • This fermentation produces ethanol in large fermentors.

🎙️ Microbes are used on a large scale to produce alcoholic beverages like beer, wine, whisky, brandy, or rum. *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, also known as brewer's yeast, is crucial for fermenting malted cereals and fruit juices to produce ethanol. This process is carried out in large fermentors.

Alcoholic Beverages: Distilled vs. Undistilled

  • Wine and beer are produced without distillation (undistilled).
  • They have relatively lower alcohol content.
  • Whisky, brandy, and rum are produced by distillation, increasing alcohol concentration.

🎙️ Wine and beer are produced without distillation, having lower alcohol content. Whisky, brandy, and rum are produced by distillation of the fermented broth, which increases their alcohol concentration significantly. This distinction is important for understanding the final product.

Antibiotics: The Microbial War

  • Antibiotics are chemicals produced by microbes to kill or inhibit other microbes.
  • Alexander Fleming discovered the first antibiotic, Penicillin, in 1928.
  • It was derived from the mold *Penicillium notatum*.

🎙️ Antibiotics are chemical substances produced by some microbes that can kill or retard the growth of other (disease-causing) microbes. The discovery of penicillin by Alexander Fleming in 1928, from *Penicillium notatum*, was a landmark event in medical history.

Key Antibiotics & Their Origins

  • Ernst Chain and Howard Florey established penicillin's full potential.
  • They received the Nobel Prize with Fleming in 1945.
  • Other antibiotics include Streptomycin (from *Streptomyces griseus*) and Tetracycline.

🎙️ Fleming's work was later fully established by Ernst Chain and Howard Florey, who received the Nobel Prize along with Fleming in 1945. Penicillin was widely used to treat American soldiers during World War II. Other important antibiotics include streptomycin (from *Streptomyces griseus*) and tetracycline.

Chemicals, Enzymes & Bioactive Molecules

  • Microbes produce organic acids: Citric acid (*Aspergillus niger*), Acetic acid (*Acetobacter aceti*).
  • They also produce enzymes like Lipases (in detergents) and Pectinases/Proteases (for fruit juice clarification).
  • These are essential for various industrial and household applications.

🎙️ Microbes are industrial powerhouses, producing various organic acids like citric acid (*Aspergillus niger*), acetic acid (*Acetobacter aceti*), and butyric acid (*Clostridium butylicum*). They also produce enzymes like lipases (used in detergents) and pectinases/proteases (for fruit juice clarification).

Microbes in Sewage Treatment: A Clean Future

  • Municipal wastewater (sewage) contains organic matter and pathogens.
  • Untreated sewage pollutes natural water bodies.
  • Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) use microbes to reduce pollution.

🎙️ Municipal wastewater, or sewage, contains large amounts of organic matter and pathogenic microbes. Untreated sewage cannot be discharged into natural water bodies. Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) use microbes to reduce pollution and make water safe for discharge.

Wastewater from homes and industries is treated in multiple stages to remove contaminants

STP - Step 1: Primary Treatment

  • This is a physical process, removing large and small particles.
  • Involves sequential filtration (floating debris) and grit removal.
  • Sedimentation allows solid particles to settle, forming 'primary sludge'.

🎙️ This initial physical process involves sequential filtration to remove floating debris and grit, followed by sedimentation. Solid particles settle to form the 'primary sludge', while the supernatant forms the 'effluent'. This effluent is then taken for secondary treatment.

STP - Step 2: Secondary Treatment (Biological)

  • Primary effluent is pumped into large aeration tanks.
  • Aerobic microbes ('flocs') grow rapidly, consuming organic matter.
  • This significantly reduces the Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) of the effluent.

🎙️ The primary effluent is pumped into large aeration tanks. Here, beneficial aerobic microbes, called 'flocs' (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments), grow rapidly. They consume the organic matter in the effluent, significantly reducing its Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD).

Aeration Tank

STP - Step 3: Anaerobic Sludge Digestion & Effluent

  • Flocs sediment to form 'activated sludge' in settling tanks.
  • Activated sludge is pumped into anaerobic digesters.
  • Anaerobic bacteria digest organic matter, producing biogas; treated effluent is released.

🎙️ Once BOD is reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank where bacterial flocs sediment, forming 'activated sludge'. A small part is returned to the aeration tank as inoculum. The remaining sludge is pumped into anaerobic sludge digesters, where anaerobic bacteria digest the organic matter and produce biogas. The treated effluent is then released.

Microbes in Biogas Production: Renewable Energy

The Role of Methanogens

  • Methanogens are anaerobic bacteria responsible for biogas production.
  • Example: *Methanobacterium*.
  • They are found in anaerobic sludge and in the rumen of cattle, aiding cellulose digestion.

🎙️ A group of anaerobic bacteria called Methanogens, especially *Methanobacterium*, are responsible for biogas production. They are found in the anaerobic sludge and also in the rumen of cattle, where they help digest cellulose and produce methane, which is why cattle dung is rich in methanogens.

Microbes as Biocontrol Agents: Nature's Pest Control

  • Biocontrol uses biological methods to control plant diseases and pests.
  • It reduces reliance on harmful chemical pesticides and herbicides.
  • Environmentally friendly approach, preserving non-target organisms.

🎙️ Biocontrol refers to the use of biological methods for controlling plant diseases and pests. This approach is environmentally friendly, reducing reliance on chemical pesticides and herbicides, which can be harmful to non-target organisms and the environment.

Biocontrol Examples: Bacteria & Fungi

  • *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) controls insect larvae (butterflies, moths).
  • Spores are sprayed on plants; larvae ingest them, and toxins kill them.
  • *Trichoderma* (fungus) is effective against plant root pathogens.

🎙️ *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) is a common bacterium used to control insect larvae, especially butterflies and moths. Dry spores are mixed with water and sprayed on vulnerable plants. The spores are ingested by the larvae, and the toxin released kills them. *Trichoderma* is a free-living fungus effective against plant root pathogens.

Biocontrol Examples: Viruses

  • Baculoviruses (genus *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*) are excellent biocontrol agents.
  • They are species-specific, narrow-spectrum insecticides.
  • No negative impact on non-target organisms; ideal for Integrated Pest Management (IPM).

🎙️ Baculoviruses, particularly the genus *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*, are excellent biocontrol agents. They are species-specific, narrow-spectrum insecticides, meaning they target only specific insects and have no negative impact on plants, mammals, birds, fish, or even non-target insects. This makes them ideal for Integrated Pest Management (IPM).

Biofertilizer Examples: Bacteria & Cyanobacteria

  • *Rhizobium* forms symbiotic associations with legumes, fixing nitrogen.
  • Free-living bacteria like *Azotobacter* and *Azospirillum* also fix nitrogen.
  • Cyanobacteria (*Anabaena*, *Nostoc*) are vital in paddy fields for nitrogen fixation.

🎙️ *Rhizobium* forms symbiotic associations with leguminous plants, fixing atmospheric nitrogen. Free-living bacteria like *Azotobacter* and *Azospirillum* also fix nitrogen. Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) like *Anabaena* and *Nostoc* are important biofertilizers, especially in paddy fields, fixing nitrogen and adding organic matter to the soil.

Common Mistakes in Microbes in Human Welfare

  • Confusing primary vs. secondary sewage treatment.
  • Mixing up specific microbes with their exact products (e.g., Swiss vs. Roquefort cheese microbes).
  • Misunderstanding the concept and significance of BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand).

🎙️ Students often confuse primary and secondary sewage treatment processes, or the specific microbes with their products (e.g., *Propionibacterium* for Swiss cheese vs. *Penicillium roqueforti* for Roquefort). Also, confusing distilled and undistilled beverages, or misinterpreting BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) are common pitfalls. Pay close attention to these details.
