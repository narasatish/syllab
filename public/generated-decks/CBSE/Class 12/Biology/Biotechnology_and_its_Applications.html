Worked Example: Bt Cotton Action

  • Q: Explain how Bt cotton protects itself from pests.
  • A: Bt gene from Bacillus thuringiensis is inserted into cotton. Plant produces protoxin. Insect ingests, alkaline gut activates toxin. Toxin binds to midgut epithelial cells, creates pores, causes lysis, insect dies.

🎙️ This is a common exam question. Remember the key steps: ingestion, activation in alkaline gut, binding to midgut cells, pore formation, and ultimately, the death of the insect.

1Protoxin ingested
2Alkaline gut activates
3Binds to gut cells
4Pores & Lysis
5Insect dies

Worked Example: Gene Therapy Logic

  • Q: Why is a retrovirus often used as a vector in gene therapy?
  • A: Retroviruses naturally integrate their genetic material into the host cell's genome, ensuring stable and long-term expression of the therapeutic gene. They are modified to be non-pathogenic.

🎙️ The choice of vector is crucial. Retroviruses are effective because they integrate the gene permanently, but they must be engineered to be safe and non-disease causing.
