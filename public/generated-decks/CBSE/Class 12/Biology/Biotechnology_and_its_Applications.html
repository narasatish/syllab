Core Principles of Biotechnology

  • Genetic Engineering: Altering genetic material.
  • Bioprocess Engineering: Maintaining sterile conditions.
  • Together, they enable large-scale production.

🎙️ Biotechnology relies on two main principles. Genetic engineering is the manipulation of DNA to introduce new characteristics. Bioprocess engineering ensures that organisms can grow in large, clean environments to produce the desired product efficiently.

Application in Agriculture: Bt Cotton

  • India faces significant crop losses due to pests.
  • Bt Cotton is a genetically modified crop.
  • It produces its own insecticide against bollworms.

🎙️ Let's start with agriculture. Bt Cotton is a prime example of how biotechnology helps farmers. Instead of spraying pesticides, the plant itself produces a natural toxin, protecting it from common pests like the cotton bollworm, which can devastate crops.

How Bt Cotton Works: A Natural Shield

  • Gene from bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt).
  • Produces 'Cry' protein, toxic to specific insects.
  • Toxin activated in insect gut, creating pores.

🎙️ The 'Bt' in Bt Cotton comes from a soil bacterium. Scientists isolated the gene responsible for producing an insecticidal protein, called Cry protein, and inserted it into cotton plants. When a bollworm eats the cotton, the toxin becomes active in its alkaline gut, leading to its death.

Case Study: Golden Rice for Vitamin A

  • Biofortified rice, genetically engineered.
  • Produces beta-carotene, a precursor to Vitamin A.
  • Aims to combat Vitamin A deficiency in developing countries.

🎙️ Golden Rice is a powerful example of biofortification. Many populations rely on rice as a staple, but it lacks Vitamin A. By adding genes from daffodils and bacteria, Golden Rice produces beta-carotene, which converts to Vitamin A in the body, helping prevent blindness and other health issues.

Producing Human Insulin: 'Humulin'

  • Eli Lilly (1983) first produced human insulin.
  • A and B peptide chains produced separately in E. coli.
  • Chains extracted and combined via disulfide bonds.

🎙️ The process involves genetically engineering bacteria, typically E. coli, to produce the two main chains of human insulin (A and B chains) separately. These chains are then chemically linked to form the functional human insulin, which is marketed as 'Humulin'.

2Gene for Chain B inserted into E.coli

4Chains combined (disulfide bonds)
5Functional Human Insulin

Gene Therapy: Fixing Faulty Genes

  • Correction of a defective gene that causes disease.
  • First successful gene therapy in 1990.
  • Treats Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency.

🎙️ Imagine being able to fix the root cause of a genetic disease! That's the promise of gene therapy. It involves introducing a functional gene into a patient's cells to replace or inactivate a faulty one. A classic example is the treatment for ADA deficiency, a severe combined immunodeficiency.

🛠️
Genetic Repair
14 / 30

Mechanism of ADA Gene Therapy

  • Lymphocytes from patient grown in culture.
  • Functional ADA cDNA introduced using a viral vector.
  • Reintroduced into patient; not a permanent cure.
  • Permanent cure: introducing gene into early embryonic stage.

🎙️ In ADA deficiency, lymphocytes are extracted, a functional ADA gene is inserted using a virus as a vehicle, and then these modified cells are returned to the patient. This requires periodic infusions. A more permanent solution would be to introduce the gene at a very early developmental stage.

1Isolate lymphocytes
2Introduce functional ADA gene (vector)
3Return modified lymphocytes to patient
4Periodic infusion needed

Molecular Diagnosis: Early Disease Detection

  • Molecular diagnosis detects pathogens/mutations early.

🎙️ Early detection is crucial for effective treatment. Molecular diagnosis allows us to identify diseases at a very early stage, even before symptoms appear. This is achieved by detecting specific genetic material or proteins associated with the disease.

PCR: Amplifying DNA for Detection

  • Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) amplifies DNA.
  • Detects very low concentrations of pathogens (HIV, TB).
  • Useful for detecting gene mutations in cancer.

🎙️ PCR is a powerful technique that can make millions of copies of a specific DNA segment. This amplification allows us to detect even tiny amounts of a pathogen's DNA or a specific gene mutation, making it invaluable for early diagnosis of infections like HIV or genetic disorders.

ELISA: Detecting Antigens/Antibodies

Biopiracy: Exploiting Traditional Knowledge

  • Use of bio-resources without proper authorization.
  • Often exploits traditional knowledge of indigenous communities.
  • Examples: Basmati rice, Turmeric, Neem.

🎙️ Biopiracy is an important ethical and legal issue. It's the unauthorized commercial exploitation of biological resources or traditional knowledge, often from developing countries, without fair compensation. The cases of Basmati rice, Turmeric, and Neem being patented by foreign entities sparked significant debate in India.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing gene therapy with vaccination.
  • Misunderstanding Bt toxin's activation mechanism.
  • Not distinguishing ethical vs. legal aspects of biopiracy.

🎙️ Students often mix up gene therapy, which fixes genes, with vaccination, which builds immunity. Also, remember Bt toxin is a protoxin activated by alkaline pH in the insect gut, not directly toxic. Finally, biopiracy has both ethical (fairness) and legal (patent law) dimensions.

Worked Example: Recombinant Insulin

  • Q: Explain the steps in the production of human insulin by recombinant DNA technology.
  • A: Isolate human insulin genes (A & B chains). Insert into separate plasmids of E. coli. Culture E. coli to produce chains. Extract and purify chains. Chemically link A & B chains via disulfide bonds to form functional insulin.

🎙️ When asked to explain a process, break it down into logical steps. For recombinant insulin, clearly mention the separate production of A and B chains in E. coli and their subsequent joining. This demonstrates a clear understanding of the technology.
