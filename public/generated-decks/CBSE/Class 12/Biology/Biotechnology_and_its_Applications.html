Understanding Biotechnology

  • Integrates natural science & engineering
  • Broad field with diverse applications

🎙️ Biotechnology is essentially technology based on biology. It involves using living systems and organisms to develop or make products, like medicines, food, or industrial enzymes. Think of it as harnessing life for human benefit.

Bt Cotton: A Case Study in Pest Resistance

  • Gene from bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) incorporated
  • Produces insecticidal protein (Cry protein)
  • Protects cotton plants from bollworms

🎙️ Bt cotton is a widely adopted GM crop. It's engineered with a gene from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis, which naturally produces a protein toxic to certain insect pests, particularly the cotton bollworm. This reduces crop damage significantly.

How Bt Toxin Works

  • Inactive protoxin ingested by insect
  • Activated by alkaline pH in insect gut
  • Binds to gut epithelial cells, creates pores, causes lysis

🎙️ The Bt toxin is produced as an inactive protoxin in the plant. When an insect eats the plant, the alkaline pH in its gut activates the toxin. This active toxin then binds to the gut lining, creating pores that lead to cell swelling and lysis, ultimately killing the insect.

RNA Interference (RNAi): A Novel Pest Control Strategy

  • Cellular defense mechanism in eukaryotes
  • Silences specific messenger RNA (mRNA)
  • Used to control nematode pests in plants

🎙️ RNA interference is a powerful biological process where RNA molecules inhibit gene expression or translation, by neutralizing targeted mRNA molecules. It's a natural defense mechanism in eukaryotes, and scientists have harnessed it for pest control.

🚫
Gene Silencing
10 / 25

RNAi in Action: Nematode Resistance

  • Nematode-specific genes introduced into host plant
  • Plant produces double-stranded RNA (dsRNA)
  • dsRNA silences nematode's mRNA, leading to its death

🎙️ For nematode resistance, specific genes are introduced into the plant that produce double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) complementary to the nematode's vital genes. When the nematode feeds on the plant, this dsRNA enters its body and silences its essential genes, preventing its survival.

Genetically Engineered Insulin: A Breakthrough

  • Previously animal-derived insulin caused allergies
  • Human gene for insulin cloned into bacteria (E. coli)
  • Mass production of pure, human insulin ('Humulin')

🎙️ Before genetic engineering, diabetic patients relied on insulin from slaughtered animals, often leading to allergic reactions. The ability to produce human insulin using recombinant DNA technology was a monumental achievement, providing a safe and abundant supply.

2Synthesize DNA sequences

Production of Human Insulin (Eli Lilly)

  • A and B chains synthesized separately
  • Cloned into different E. coli plasmids
  • Extracted and chemically combined to form active insulin

🎙️ Eli Lilly, an American company, achieved this milestone in 1983. They synthesized the A and B peptide chains of human insulin separately, inserted them into different E. coli plasmids, cultured the bacteria, and then extracted and chemically combined the chains to form active human insulin, named Humulin.

3Insert into separate plasmids

Gene Therapy: Correcting Genetic Defects

  • Method to correct a defective gene that causes disease
  • Involves delivering a functional gene into patient's cells
  • Potential cure for many inherited genetic diseases

🎙️ Gene therapy is a revolutionary technique aiming to treat genetic diseases by replacing, inactivating, or introducing genes into a patient's cells. It holds immense promise for conditions like cystic fibrosis, muscular dystrophy, and certain cancers.

First Gene Therapy: ADA Deficiency (SCID)

  • Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency causes SCID (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency)
  • T-lymphocytes removed, functional ADA gene inserted via retrovirus
  • Genetically modified cells reintroduced to patient

🎙️ The first successful gene therapy was performed in 1990 on a 4-year-old girl with Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID) due to ADA deficiency. Her lymphocytes were extracted, a functional ADA gene was introduced using a viral vector, and the modified cells were then returned to her body.

Molecular Diagnosis: Early & Accurate Detection

  • Detects genetic disorders, infectious diseases early
  • Traditional methods (serum/urine analysis) often slow or insensitive
  • Techniques include PCR, ELISA, Recombinant DNA technology

🎙️ Early and accurate diagnosis is critical for effective disease management. Molecular diagnostic techniques offer superior sensitivity and specificity compared to conventional methods, allowing for detection even when symptoms are not yet apparent.

Early Detection

Key Techniques in Molecular Diagnosis

  • PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction): Amplifies DNA/RNA for detection
  • ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay): Detects antigens/antibodies
  • Recombinant DNA: Identifies specific gene mutations/pathogens

🎙️ PCR is vital for amplifying minute amounts of pathogen DNA or RNA, enabling early detection of infections like HIV. ELISA detects antibodies or antigens, useful for AIDS and other infectious diseases. Recombinant DNA technology can identify specific gene mutations in genetic disorders.

Transgenic Animals: Engineered for Purpose

  • Animals with foreign DNA (transgene) incorporated into their genome
  • Modified to express a specific desirable trait or protein
  • Mice are the most common transgenic animals

🎙️ Transgenic animals are those whose genome has been altered by the introduction of a foreign gene. This allows scientists to study gene function, disease models, and produce valuable proteins. Mice are frequently used due to their short generation time and ease of manipulation.

🐁
Modified Organism
19 / 25

Uses of Transgenic Animals

  • Study of diseases (e.g., cancer, cystic fibrosis, Alzheimer's)
  • Production of biological products (e.g., human alpha-lactalbumin from 'Rosie')
  • Vaccine safety testing, chemical safety testing

🎙️ Transgenic animals serve various purposes. They help us understand how genes contribute to disease development. They can also be 'bioreactors' to produce valuable human proteins, like 'Rosie' the cow who produced human protein-enriched milk. They are also used for safety testing of vaccines and chemicals.

Biopiracy, the unauthorized exploitation of biological resources and traditional knowledge, is a significant issue for developing nations.
