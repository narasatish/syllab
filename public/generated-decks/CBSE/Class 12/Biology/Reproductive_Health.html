Defining Reproductive Health

  • Total well-being in all aspects of reproduction.
  • Physical, emotional, behavioural, and social health.
  • Ability to reproduce and regulate fertility safely.

🎙️ Reproductive health isn't just about absence of disease. It's a holistic concept encompassing physical health, emotional stability, responsible behavior, and positive social interactions related to reproduction.

The Population Explosion

  • Rapid increase in global population.
  • Decreased death rate, increased birth rate.
  • Strain on resources: food, water, housing.

🎙️ Post-independence, India witnessed a significant population boom due to better healthcare reducing death rates while birth rates remained high. This explosion puts immense pressure on our natural resources and infrastructure.

Controlling Population Growth

🎙️ Various strategies are employed to manage population growth. These include public awareness campaigns, legal measures like raising the minimum marriageable age, and widespread availability of family planning options.

Natural Family Planning

  • Avoids introduction of drugs/devices.
  • Periodic abstinence: avoiding coitus during fertile period.
  • Withdrawal (Coitus Interruptus): male withdraws before ejaculation.

🎙️ Natural methods rely on understanding the female reproductive cycle. Periodic abstinence requires discipline and accurate tracking of the menstrual cycle, while coitus interruptus has a higher failure rate.

Barrier Contraceptives

  • Prevent physical meeting of sperm and ovum.
  • Condoms (male & female): protect against STIs.
  • Diaphragms, Cervical Caps, Vaults: reusable, used with spermicides.

🎙️ Barrier methods physically block sperm from reaching the egg. Condoms are unique as they also offer protection against sexually transmitted infections, making them a dual-purpose contraceptive.

Intra-Uterine Devices (IUDs)

  • Inserted by doctors into the uterus.
  • Non-medicated (Lippes loop), Copper-releasing (CuT, Cu7), Hormone-releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20).
  • Increase phagocytosis of sperm, suppress sperm motility, make uterus unsuitable.

🎙️ IUDs are highly effective, long-term contraceptives. Their mechanism varies: copper ions suppress sperm, while hormone-releasing IUDs also thicken cervical mucus and thin the uterine lining.

Oral Contraceptive Pills

  • Progestogen or progestogen-estrogen combination.
  • Inhibit ovulation, alter cervical mucus.
  • Taken daily for 21 days, starting within 5 days of menstruation.

🎙️ Oral contraceptive pills are popular for their effectiveness. They work primarily by preventing the release of an egg (ovulation) and by making the uterus less hospitable for implantation.

Injectables & Implants

  • Progestogen-only or combined hormonal methods.
  • Injections given subcutaneously/intramuscularly (e.g., Depo-Provera).
  • Implants (e.g., Norplant) placed under skin, release hormones slowly.

🎙️ These methods offer long-acting contraception, lasting for several months to years, which is convenient for many. They work similarly to oral pills by inhibiting ovulation and altering cervical mucus.

Permanent Contraception

  • Highly effective, irreversible methods.
  • Vasectomy (males): vas deferens cut and tied.
  • Tubectomy (females): fallopian tubes cut and tied.

🎙️ Surgical sterilization is a permanent solution for individuals who have completed their family. Both vasectomy and tubectomy prevent gamete transport, thus preventing fertilization.

Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

  • Intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term.
  • Legalized in India in 1971 (MTP Act).
  • Performed by registered medical practitioners.

🎙️ MTP is a sensitive topic, but it is a legal and safe procedure when performed by qualified medical professionals under specific conditions. It's crucial for managing unwanted pregnancies due to various reasons.

MTP Safety & Timing

  • Safe in the first trimester (up to 12 weeks).
  • Riskier in the second trimester (12-24 weeks).
  • Illegal MTPs by quacks are extremely dangerous.

🎙️ The safety of MTP is highly dependent on the gestational age. Early MTPs are relatively safe, but risks increase significantly in the second trimester. Unqualified procedures can lead to severe complications or death.

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

  • Infections transmitted through sexual contact.
  • Can affect reproductive tract and other body parts.
  • Often asymptomatic, leading to delayed diagnosis.

🎙️ STIs are a major public health concern. Many STIs can be asymptomatic, meaning individuals may not know they are infected, leading to further transmission and potentially serious long-term health issues.

🦠🦠🦠🦠🦠🦠
Hidden Threats

Bacterial STIs

  • Chlamydia: often asymptomatic, can cause infertility.
  • Gonorrhea: painful urination, discharge, can lead to PID.
  • Syphilis: stages with chancres, rash, organ damage.

🎙️ Bacterial STIs like Chlamydia, Gonorrhea, and Syphilis are generally curable with antibiotics if detected early. However, untreated infections can lead to severe complications, including infertility.

Viral STIs

  • Genital Herpes: painful blisters, recurrent outbreaks.
  • Genital Warts: caused by HPV, can lead to cervical cancer.
  • Hepatitis B & HIV/AIDS: life-threatening, no cure.

🎙️ Viral STIs, such as Herpes, Genital Warts, Hepatitis B, and HIV, are generally incurable, though symptoms can often be managed. HIV/AIDS is particularly devastating, attacking the immune system.

Preventing STIs

  • Avoid sex with unknown partners or multiple partners.
  • Always use condoms during coitus.
  • Seek early diagnosis and complete treatment.

🎙️ Prevention is key for STIs. Practicing safe sex, limiting partners, and getting tested regularly are crucial steps. Early diagnosis and treatment are vital to prevent long-term health consequences.

Understanding Infertility

  • Inability to conceive after unprotected sexual cohabitation for 1 year.
  • Causes in males: low sperm count, erectile dysfunction.
  • Causes in females: ovulation problems, blocked fallopian tubes.

🎙️ Infertility affects a significant number of couples. It's important to remember that it can be due to factors in either the male or female partner, or a combination of both. Diagnosis requires thorough investigation.

Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

  • Techniques to help infertile couples conceive.
  • Involves handling gametes outside the body.
  • Offers hope to many struggling with infertility.

🎙️ ART encompasses a range of advanced medical procedures designed to overcome infertility. These technologies have revolutionized fertility treatment, allowing many couples to achieve parenthood.

IVF and Embryo Transfer

  • IVF (In Vitro Fertilization): 'test-tube baby' technique.
  • Fertilization occurs outside the body in a lab.
  • ET (Embryo Transfer): embryo transferred to uterus/fallopian tube.

🎙️ IVF is the most well-known ART. Eggs are retrieved and fertilized with sperm in a petri dish. The resulting embryos are then transferred back into the woman's uterus or fallopian tube for implantation.

Advanced ART Techniques

  • ZIFT (Zygote Intrafallopian Transfer): zygote transferred to fallopian tube.
  • IUT (Intrauterine Transfer): embryo transferred directly to uterus (blastocyst stage).
  • ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection): sperm directly injected into ovum.

🎙️ These are variations of ART. ZIFT and IUT are types of embryo transfer depending on the stage of the embryo. ICSI is particularly useful for male infertility, as it requires only a single sperm.

Artificial Insemination (AI)

  • AI: sperm collected and artificially introduced.
  • IUI (Intrauterine Insemination): sperm injected into uterus.
  • Used when male partner has low sperm count or inability to ejaculate.

🎙️ Artificial Insemination is a simpler ART, involving placing sperm directly into the female reproductive tract. IUI is a common form, increasing the chances of fertilization by bypassing the cervix.
