Defining Reproductive Health (WHO)

  • A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being.
  • Not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.
  • Pertains to all aspects of reproduction.
  • Encompasses reproductive processes, functions, and systems.

🎙️ The World Health Organization, or WHO, provides a comprehensive definition. It emphasizes that reproductive health is holistic, covering physical, mental, and social aspects, not just the absence of illness. This means a person should be able to have a satisfying and safe sex life, and the capability to reproduce, along with the freedom to decide if, when, and how often to do so.

The Population Boom: A Major Concern

  • World population crossed 6 billion in May 2000.
  • India's population reached 1 billion in May 2000.
  • Rapid increase in population after independence.
  • Projected to surpass China as most populous nation.

🎙️ The exponential growth in human population, often termed 'population explosion,' is a severe global concern. India's population growth rate, though declining, remains high. This rapid increase puts immense pressure on natural resources and infrastructure, leading to various socio-economic problems.

Causes of Population Explosion

  • Decline in mortality rates (IMR, MMR).
  • Increased longevity due to better healthcare.

🎙️ Several factors contribute to population explosion. 🎙️ Contraception refers to any method or device used to prevent pregnancy. It is a crucial component of reproductive health, enabling individuals and couples to make informed decisions about family size and spacing of children. We will now delve into the various types of contraceptive methods.

Natural Methods of Contraception

  • Work on the principle of avoiding sperm and ovum meeting.
  • Periodic abstinence: avoiding coitus during fertile period.
  • Coitus interruptus (withdrawal): male withdraws before ejaculation.
  • Lactational amenorrhea: absence of menstruation during intense lactation.

🎙️ Natural methods rely on understanding the female reproductive cycle to prevent conception. Periodic abstinence involves avoiding intercourse during the ovulation period, while coitus interruptus requires withdrawal before ejaculation. Lactational amenorrhea is effective only for up to six months post-delivery, provided the mother breastfeeds fully. These methods have no side effects but are less reliable.

Barrier Methods of Contraception

  • Physically prevent sperm from meeting the ovum.
  • Condoms: disposable, cover penis/vagina; protect against STIs.
  • Diaphragms, cervical caps, vaults: reusable, cover cervix.
  • Often used with spermicidal creams/jellies for increased efficacy.

🎙️ Barrier methods create a physical block between sperm and egg. Condoms are widely popular as they offer protection against both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. Diaphragms, cervical caps, and vaults are inserted into the female reproductive tract and are more effective when used with chemical spermicides.

Intra-Uterine Devices (IUDs)

  • Devices inserted by doctors into the uterus.
  • Non-medicated IUDs: Lippes Loop (increase phagocytosis of sperm).
  • Copper-releasing IUDs: CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375 (suppress sperm motility).
  • Hormone-releasing IUDs: Progestasert, LNG-20 (make uterus unsuitable for implantation).

🎙️ IUDs are highly effective and popular contraceptives, especially for women who want to delay pregnancy or space children. They are inserted by a medical professional and can remain in place for several years. Their mechanisms vary, from increasing phagocytosis of sperm to releasing hormones that alter the uterine environment.

Oral Contraceptives (Pills)

  • Hormonal preparations taken orally by females.
  • Contain progestogens or progestogen-estrogen combinations.
  • Inhibit ovulation and implantation.
  • Alter quality of cervical mucus to prevent sperm entry.

🎙️ Oral contraceptive pills are a very effective method, taken daily for 21 days starting within the first five days of menstruation. They primarily work by inhibiting ovulation and making the uterus unsuitable for implantation. 'Saheli' is a new oral contraceptive for females, a non-steroidal preparation taken once a week with fewer side effects.

Injectables, Implants, and Surgical Methods

  • Injectables/Implants: Progestogens or combinations, effective for longer periods.
  • Surgical methods (Sterilization): Permanent contraception.
  • Vasectomy (males): Vas deferens cut and tied.
  • Tubectomy (females): Fallopian tubes cut and tied.

🎙️ Beyond daily pills, longer-acting hormonal methods like injectables and implants offer convenience. Surgical methods, vasectomy for males and tubectomy for females, are terminal methods, meaning they are permanent and highly effective. These methods block gamete transport, preventing conception.

Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)

  • Intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term.
  • Also known as induced abortion.
  • Legalized in India in 1971 with strict conditions.
  • Up to 20 weeks of pregnancy, with approval of medical practitioners.

🎙️ Medical Termination of Pregnancy, or MTP, is a sensitive topic. It refers to the intentional termination of a pregnancy. In India, MTP was legalized in 1971 to prevent illegal abortions, which posed significant risks to women's health. However, there are strict legal guidelines regarding when and how it can be performed.

⚖️

MTP: When and How?

  • MTPs are relatively safe during the first trimester (up to 12 weeks).
  • Second trimester (12-20 weeks) MTPs are riskier.
  • Performed due to rape, incest, or threat to mother's/fetus's health.
  • Strict medical supervision is crucial to avoid complications.

🎙️ The safety of MTP largely depends on the gestational age. MTPs are considered relatively safe during the first trimester. However, they become significantly riskier during the second trimester. It's crucial to understand that MTP should only be performed by qualified medical professionals under legal conditions to ensure the safety of the woman.

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

  • Infections transmitted through sexual contact.
  • Also known as Venereal Diseases (VD) or Reproductive Tract Infections (RTIs).
  • Can be bacterial, viral, protozoan, or fungal.
  • Early symptoms often mild or absent, leading to delayed diagnosis.

🎙️ Sexually Transmitted Infections, or STIs, are a major public health concern. These infections spread primarily through sexual contact and can have severe long-term health consequences if left untreated. It's important to be aware of the common types and their symptoms for early detection and treatment.

Common STIs and Their Symptoms

  • Gonorrhea, Syphilis, Genital Herpes (viral), Chlamydiasis.
  • Hepatitis B, Genital Warts (viral), Trichomoniasis (protozoan).
  • HIV/AIDS (viral) is the most dangerous STI.
  • Symptoms: itching, fluid discharge, pain, swelling in genital region.

🎙️ Many STIs, especially in their early stages, might have very mild or no symptoms, making them difficult to detect. However, common symptoms can include itching, burning, discharge, or sores in the genital area. Untreated STIs can lead to serious complications like infertility, ectopic pregnancies, and even cancer.

Prevention and Early Detection of STIs

  • Avoid sex with unknown partners or multiple partners.
  • Always use condoms during coitus.
  • Avoid sharing needles (for Hepatitis B/HIV).
  • In case of doubt, consult a qualified doctor for early detection and treatment.

🎙️ Prevention is always better than cure, especially for STIs. Practicing safe sex, limiting partners, and consistent condom use are crucial. If you suspect exposure or experience any symptoms, immediate consultation with a doctor is vital for early diagnosis and effective treatment to prevent severe complications.

Infertility - Causes and Treatment

  • Inability to conceive or produce children after unprotected coitus.
  • Affects both males and females.
  • Causes: physical, congenital, diseases, drugs, immunological, psychological.
  • Specialized infertility clinics can help.

🎙️ Infertility is defined as the inability to conceive despite unprotected sexual cohabitation for a year or more. It can stem from various causes in either partner, ranging from physical issues to lifestyle factors. Fortunately, advancements in medical science offer several solutions for infertile couples.

Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART)

  • Techniques used to help infertile couples conceive.
  • IVF (In Vitro Fertilization): 'Test tube baby' technique, fertilization outside body.
  • ZIFT (Zygote Intra Fallopian Transfer): Zygote transferred to fallopian tube.
  • GIFT (Gamete Intra Fallopian Transfer): Ovum transferred to fallopian tube.
  • ICSI (Intra Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection): Sperm directly injected into ovum.

🎙️ ARTs have revolutionized infertility treatment. IVF, commonly known as the 'test tube baby' program, involves fertilization outside the body. ZIFT and GIFT are variations where zygotes or gametes are transferred into the fallopian tube. ICSI is particularly useful for male infertility, where a single sperm is injected into the ovum. These techniques offer hope to many couples.
