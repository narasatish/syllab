Rutherford's Alpha-Particle Scattering: Key Observations

  • Some deflected at small angles.
  • Very few (1 in 8000) deflected at large angles (>90°), some even bounced back.

🎙️ The observations were quite surprising. While most alpha particles went straight through, a significant number were scattered, and a tiny fraction even reversed their path, indicating a very strong repulsive force.

Rutherford's Model: The Nuclear Atom

  • Atom's mass and positive charge concentrated in a tiny nucleus.
  • Electrons revolve around the nucleus in orbits.
  • Most of the atom is empty space.

🎙️ Based on these observations, Rutherford concluded that the atom has a dense, positively charged core called the nucleus. Electrons orbit this nucleus, and the vast majority of the atom is empty space.

Distance of Closest Approach (r₀)

  • Minimum distance an alpha particle reaches the nucleus.
  • Kinetic energy converts to potential energy at this point.
  • Important for estimating nuclear size.

🎙️ When an alpha particle approaches the nucleus head-on, its kinetic energy is converted into electrostatic potential energy. The point where all kinetic energy is converted is the distance of closest approach, giving us an estimate of the nucleus's size.

Drawbacks of Rutherford's Atomic Model

  • Instability of atoms: Accelerating electrons should radiate energy and spiral into nucleus.
  • Could not explain the discrete atomic spectra observed.
  • Classical physics predicted a continuous spectrum, not line spectra.

🎙️ Despite its revolutionary insights, Rutherford's model had significant flaws. According to classical electromagnetism, orbiting electrons should continuously radiate energy and eventually collapse into the nucleus. It also failed to explain why atoms emit discrete line spectra instead of a continuous one.

Bohr's Model of the Hydrogen Atom: Postulate 1

  • Electrons revolve in certain stable, non-radiating orbits.
  • Each orbit has a definite, fixed energy.
  • Classical laws of electromagnetism do not apply here.

🎙️ Niels Bohr addressed Rutherford's drawbacks with three postulates. The first states that electrons can only exist in specific, stable orbits without radiating energy, a radical departure from classical physics.

Bohr's model of the atom proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy levels

Bohr's Model of the Hydrogen Atom: Postulate 2 (Quantization)

  • Electron's angular momentum is quantized.
  • L = mvr = n(h/2π), where n = 1, 2, 3...
  • Only discrete orbits, called stationary states, are allowed.

🎙️ Bohr's second postulate is about the quantization of angular momentum. It states that an electron can only orbit if its angular momentum is an integral multiple of h/2π, where 'h' is Planck's constant. This directly leads to discrete energy levels.

Bohr's Model: Derivation of Orbital Radius (Concept)

  • Centripetal force is provided by electrostatic force.
  • Combine this with angular momentum quantization.
  • Shows that orbital radius is proportional to n².

🎙️ By equating the centripetal force required for circular motion to the electrostatic force of attraction between the electron and nucleus, and incorporating the quantization condition, we can derive an expression for the radius of the nth orbit. This shows that radii are discrete and increase with n².

Bohr's Model: Energy Levels of Hydrogen Atom

  • Total energy (Kinetic + Potential) of the electron is quantized.
  • En = -13.6/n² eV for the hydrogen atom.
  • Ground state (n=1) is the lowest energy state.

🎙️ Similarly, the total energy of the electron in a Bohr orbit is also quantized. For hydrogen, the energy levels are given by En = -13.6/n² eV. The negative sign indicates that the electron is bound to the nucleus, and the lowest energy state is the ground state with n=1.

Worked Example 1: Calculate Bohr Radius for n=2

  • Given: Bohr radius (a₀) for n=1 is 0.529 Å.
  • Formula: rn = a₀ * n².
  • For n=2, r₂ = 0.529 * 2² = 2.116 Å.

🎙️ Let's apply the formula for Bohr radius. We know the radius of the first orbit, a₀. For the second orbit, we simply multiply a₀ by n², which is 2² or 4. This gives us 2.116 Angstroms.

Worked Example 2: Calculate Energy for n=3

  • Given: Ground state energy (E₁) for hydrogen is -13.6 eV.
  • Formula: En = E₁ / n².
  • For n=3, E₃ = -13.6 / 3² = -1.51 eV.

🎙️ Now for an energy calculation. We use the formula En = E₁/n². For the third energy level, n=3, so we divide -13.6 eV by 3 squared, which is 9. This results in an energy of -1.51 electron volts.

Common Mistakes in Bohr's Model Calculations

  • Forgetting 'Z²' for hydrogen-like ions (e.g., He⁺, Li²⁺).
  • Incorrectly using 'n' values for transitions (final vs. initial).
  • Units: Confusing eV with Joules, Angstrom with meters.

🎙️ Students often forget to include the atomic number squared (Z²) when dealing with hydrogen-like ions. Also, be careful with the 'n' values in transition calculations and always double-check your units – eV and Joules are both used, as are Angstroms and meters.

Limitations of Bohr's Model

  • Fails to explain spectra of multi-electron atoms.
  • Cannot explain the fine structure of spectral lines.
  • Doesn't explain Zeeman effect (splitting in magnetic field) or Stark effect (electric field).

🎙️ While a huge leap, Bohr's model isn't perfect. It couldn't explain the spectra of atoms with more than one electron. It also failed to account for the fine structure of spectral lines or their splitting in magnetic (Zeeman) and electric (Stark) fields.

De Broglie's Explanation of Bohr's Quantization

  • Electrons exhibit wave-like nature (wave-particle duality).
  • Stable orbits correspond to stationary standing waves.
  • Circumference of orbit = nλ (n times de Broglie wavelength).

🎙️ De Broglie provided a beautiful explanation for Bohr's second postulate. He proposed that electrons in stable orbits behave as standing waves. The condition for a stable orbit is that the circumference of the orbit must be an integral multiple of the electron's de Broglie wavelength.

Moseley's Law and Atomic Number

  • Relates frequency of characteristic X-rays to atomic number (Z).
  • √ν = a(Z - b), where a and b are constants.
  • Confirmed atomic number, not atomic mass, as fundamental property.

🎙️ Moseley's law was a pivotal discovery. It showed a direct relationship between the square root of the frequency of characteristic X-rays and the atomic number of the element, establishing atomic number as the more fundamental property than atomic mass.
