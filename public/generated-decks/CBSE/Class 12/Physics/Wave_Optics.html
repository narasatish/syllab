Huygens' Principle: Step-by-Step Construction

  • Each point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets.
  • These wavelets spread out in all directions with the speed of light.
  • The new wavefront is the envelope of all these secondary wavelets.

🎙️ Imagine a primary wavefront. Each point on it acts as a source for secondary wavelets. The envelope tangent to these wavelets in the forward direction gives the new wavefront at a later time.

Path Difference and Phase Difference: The Relation

  • Path difference (Δx): Difference in distance traveled by waves.
  • Phase difference (Δφ): Difference in phase of the waves.
  • Relation: Δφ = (2π/λ)Δx.

🎙️ The difference in distance traveled by waves from two sources to a point is the path difference. It's directly related to the phase difference by the formula: Δφ = (2π/λ)Δx.

Conditions for Constructive Interference (Bright Fringes)

  • Waves meet in phase, leading to maximum intensity.
  • Path difference: Δx = nλ (where n = 0, 1, 2, ...).
  • Phase difference: Δφ = 2nπ.

🎙️ Constructive interference occurs when the waves meet in phase, leading to maximum intensity (bright fringes). This happens when the path difference is an integral multiple of the wavelength (nλ).

Conditions for Destructive Interference (Dark Fringes)

  • Waves meet out of phase, leading to minimum intensity.
  • Path difference: Δx = (n + ½)λ (where n = 0, 1, 2, ...).
  • Phase difference: Δφ = (2n + 1)π.

🎙️ Destructive interference occurs when waves meet out of phase, leading to minimum intensity (dark fringes). This happens when the path difference is an odd multiple of half the wavelength ((n + 1/2)λ).

Fringe Width (β): Spacing the Fringes

  • Distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes.
  • Formula: β = λD/d.
  • λ = wavelength, D = distance to screen, d = slit separation.

🎙️ Fringe width is the distance between two consecutive bright or dark fringes. In YDSE, it's given by β = λD/d, where λ is wavelength, D is screen distance, and d is slit separation.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Fringe Width

  • Given: λ = 600 nm, D = 1.5 m, d = 0.2 mm.
  • Convert units: λ = 600 x 10⁻⁹ m, d = 0.2 x 10⁻³ m.
  • β = (600 x 10⁻⁹ * 1.5) / (0.2 x 10⁻³) = 4.5 x 10⁻³ m = 4.5 mm.

🎙️ Let's calculate the fringe width for a given setup: λ = 600 nm, D = 1.5 m, d = 0.2 mm. Remember to convert all units to SI before calculation. This type of problem is very common in exams.

Single Slit Diffraction: Minima and Maxima

  • Minima occur at sinθ = nλ/a (n = ±1, ±2, ...).
  • Secondary maxima occur approximately at sinθ = (2n+1)λ/2a.
  • Central maximum is twice as wide and most intense.

🎙️ For a single slit, minima occur at sinθ = nλ/a and maxima are approximately at sinθ = (2n+1)λ/2a, where 'a' is the slit width. The central maximum is twice as wide as other maxima.

Interference vs. Diffraction: Key Differences

  • Interference: Superposition from two distinct coherent sources.
  • Diffraction: Superposition of wavelets from different points of the same wavefront.
  • Fringes are equally spaced in interference, not in diffraction.

🎙️ While both involve superposition, interference is due to superposition of waves from two different wavefronts originating from two coherent sources, while diffraction is due to superposition of wavelets from different points of the same wavefront.

Unpolarized vs. Plane Polarized Light

  • Unpolarized: Vibrations in all directions perpendicular to propagation.
  • Plane Polarized: Vibrations restricted to a single plane.
  • Achieved by passing unpolarized light through a polarizer.

🎙️ Think of unpolarized light as having vibrations in all directions perpendicular to its path. A polarizer acts like a filter, allowing only vibrations in a specific plane to pass through, creating polarized light.

Brewster's Law: Angle of Polarization

  • When reflected light is completely plane-polarized.
  • Occurs at a specific angle of incidence, Brewster's angle (iₚ).
  • Formula: tan(iₚ) = n (refractive index of the medium).

🎙️ Brewster's Law states that when unpolarized light is incident at a specific angle (Brewster's angle, i_p) on a transparent medium, the reflected light is completely plane-polarized.
