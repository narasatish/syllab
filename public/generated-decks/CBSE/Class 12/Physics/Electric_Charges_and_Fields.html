What is Electric Charge?

  • It's an intrinsic property of matter.
  • Causes it to experience force in an electromagnetic field.
  • Scalar quantity, SI unit: Coulomb (C).
  • Two types: Positive (+) and Negative (-).

🎙️ Electric charge is a fundamental property, just like mass. It's responsible for all electrical phenomena we observe around us.

Quantization of Electric Charge

  • Analogy: Indian currency only comes in fixed denominations (₹1, ₹2, ₹5, etc.). You can't have ₹1.5.
  • Similarly, electric charge exists in discrete packets.
  • Any charge (q) is an integral multiple of elementary charge (e).
  • q = ±ne, where n = 1, 2, 3... and e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

🎙️ This means you can never find a charge that is, say, 0.5e. It's always a whole number multiple of the fundamental charge 'e'.

Conservation of Electric Charge

  • Analogy: Energy can't be created or destroyed, only transformed.
  • Total electric charge in an isolated system remains constant.
  • Charges can be transferred, but the net charge doesn't change.
  • Example: Rubbing glass rod with silk – one becomes +ve, other -ve, but total remains zero.

🎙️ This is a very powerful principle, similar to the conservation of mass and energy. It holds true for all electrical interactions.

Additivity of Electric Charge

  • Analogy: Adding numbers on a scale. +5kg + (-2kg) = +3kg.
  • Total charge of a system is the algebraic sum of all individual charges.
  • Treat positive charges as +ve numbers, negative charges as -ve numbers.
  • Example: A system has charges +3C, -2C, +5C. Total charge = (3 - 2 + 5)C = +6C.

🎙️ This property simplifies calculations for systems with multiple charges. Remember to consider the signs!

🎙️ Just like gravity, electric force is an inverse square law, but with a crucial difference: it can be both attractive and repulsive.

Coulomb's Law: The Formal Statement

  • Force between two point charges (q1, q2) is directly proportional to product of charges.
  • Inversely proportional to square of distance (r) between them.
  • F = k |q1q2| / r²
  • k = 1 / (4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ Nm²/C² (for vacuum/air)

🎙️ This fundamental law quantifies the electric force. Remember, like charges repel, unlike charges attract.

📏
Measuring Force
12 / 30

Coulomb's Law in Vector Form

  • Force is a vector, so direction matters.
  • F₂₁ = k (q₁q₂ / r₂₁²) r̂₂₁ (force on q₂ due to q₁)
  • r̂₂₁ is unit vector from q₁ to q₂.
  • If q₁q₂ > 0 (like charges), F₂₁ is along r̂₂₁ (repulsion).
  • If q₁q₂ < 0 (unlike charges), F₂₁ is opposite to r̂₂₁ (attraction).

🎙️ The vector form ensures we correctly account for the direction of the force, which is crucial for systems with multiple charges.

Role of Medium: Permittivity

  • The constant k (or ε₀) changes if charges are in a medium other than vacuum.
  • ε = ε₀εᵣ (absolute permittivity = vacuum permittivity × relative permittivity).
  • εᵣ (or K, dielectric constant) is always ≥ 1.
  • Force in medium F_m = F_air / εᵣ. Force decreases in a medium.

🎙️ The medium between charges weakens the electric force. Water, for instance, has a very high dielectric constant.

Worked Example: Coulomb's Law

  • Q: Two point charges, +3 μC and +5 μC, are placed 10 cm apart in air. Calculate the force between them.
  • Step 1: Convert units (μC to C, cm to m). q₁=3x10⁻⁶ C, q₂=5x10⁻⁶ C, r=0.1 m.
  • Step 2: Use F = k |q1q2| / r². k = 9x10⁹ Nm²/C².
  • Step 3: F = (9x10⁹)(3x10⁻⁶)(5x10⁻⁶) / (0.1)² = 13.5 N.
  • Since charges are positive, the force is repulsive.

🎙️ Always remember to convert units to SI before applying the formula. Also, state whether the force is attractive or repulsive.

Electric Field: The Definition

  • The space around a charge where its influence can be felt.
  • Defined as the electric force per unit positive test charge.
  • E = F / q₀ (where q₀ is a small positive test charge).
  • Vector quantity, SI unit: Newton/Coulomb (N/C) or Volt/meter (V/m).

🎙️ We use a small positive test charge to measure the field so that it doesn't disturb the source charge's field.

⚡

Electric Field due to a Point Charge

  • For a point charge Q at the origin, the field E at distance r is:
  • E = k Q / r² (magnitude)
  • E = k (Q / r²) r̂ (vector form, r̂ points away from +Q, towards -Q).
  • Direction is radially outward for positive charge, inward for negative charge.

🎙️ This formula allows us to calculate the strength and direction of the electric field at any point around a single charge.

Electric Field Lines: Visualizing the Field

  • Imaginary lines representing the direction of the electric field.

  • Closer lines indicate stronger field, farther lines weaker field.

🎙️ Field lines are a powerful tool to visualize the electric field patterns, especially for complex charge distributions.

Worked Example: Electric Field

  • Q: A point charge of +4 nC is placed at the origin. Find the electric field at a point P located 2 cm along the positive x-axis.
  • Step 1: Convert units. Q = 4x10⁻⁹ C, r = 0.02 m.
  • Step 2: Use E = k Q / r². k = 9x10⁹ Nm²/C².
  • Step 3: E = (9x10⁹)(4x10⁻⁹) / (0.02)² = 90,000 N/C.
  • Direction: Radially outward, so along +x-axis.

🎙️ Always specify the direction of the electric field as it's a vector quantity. For a positive charge, it's away from the charge.

Electric Dipole: A Special Arrangement

  • An electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite point charges.
  • Separated by a small distance (2a).
  • Example: HCl, H₂O molecules are dipoles.
  • Net charge of a dipole is zero, but its field is not.

🎙️ Dipoles are very important in understanding the behavior of molecules in electric fields. They have a unique electric field pattern.

Electric Dipole Moment (p)

  • A vector quantity that measures the strength of an electric dipole.
  • Magnitude: p = q × 2a (charge × distance between charges).
  • Direction: From negative charge to positive charge.
  • SI unit: Coulomb-meter (C m).

🎙️ The dipole moment is a crucial parameter for describing a dipole. Its direction is conventionally defined from negative to positive.

Dipole in a Uniform Electric Field

  • When a dipole is placed in a uniform E-field, net force on it is zero.
  • However, it experiences a torque.
  • Torque (τ) = p × E (vector cross product).
  • Magnitude: τ = pE sinθ (where θ is angle between p and E).

🎙️ The torque tends to align the dipole with the electric field. This is why polar molecules rotate in an electric field.

Introducing Electric Flux

  • Analogy: Imagine water flowing through a net.
  • More water flows if the net is larger or if the flow is stronger.
  • More water flows if the net is perpendicular to the flow.
  • Electric flux is similar: 'flow' of electric field through an area.

🎙️ Flux quantifies how many electric field lines pass through a given surface. It's a measure of the electric field piercing an area.

Electric Flux: The Definition

  • A measure of the number of electric field lines passing through a surface.
  • Scalar quantity, SI unit: N m²/C or V m.
  • For a uniform field E through a plane area A: Φ = E ⋅ A = EA cosθ.
  • θ is the angle between the electric field E and the area vector A.

🎙️ The area vector is always perpendicular to the surface. Flux is maximum when the field is perpendicular to the surface (θ=0), and zero when parallel (θ=90).

Worked Example: Electric Flux

  • Q: A uniform electric field E = 3 x 10³ N/C is directed along the positive x-axis. Find the flux through a square of side 10 cm if it is placed in the y-z plane.
  • Step 1: Identify E and A. E = 3 x 10³ N/C (along +x). Area = (0.1 m)² = 0.01 m².
  • Step 2: Determine angle θ. Square in y-z plane, so its area vector A is along +x-axis. E is also along +x-axis. So θ = 0°.
  • Step 3: Apply Φ = EA cosθ. Φ = (3 x 10³)(0.01) cos(0°) = 30 N m²/C.

🎙️ Understanding the direction of the area vector is crucial for calculating flux correctly. It's always normal to the surface.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing scalar and vector quantities (Charge vs. Field).
  • Incorrectly applying signs in Coulomb's Law (magnitude only, direction separate).
  • Forgetting to convert units (μC to C, cm to m).
  • Misinterpreting direction of electric field lines or area vector.

🎙️ Pay close attention to these details. A small error in sign or unit conversion can lead to a completely wrong answer in exams.
