Decoding 'Binomial'

  • "Bi" means two, like a bicycle has two wheels.
  • An algebraic expression with exactly two terms.
  • Examples: (a+b), (x-y), (2p+3q).

🎙️ Think of it as a pair of terms joined by addition or subtraction. Simple expressions like 'a' or 'a+b+c' are not binomials. It's strictly two terms.

Unveiling Patterns: Powers

  • In (a+b)^n, the power of 'a' decreases from 'n' to 0.
  • The power of 'b' increases from 0 to 'n'.
  • Sum of powers in each term is always 'n'.

🎙️ Look at (a+b)^3: a^3b^0, a^2b^1, a^1b^2, a^0b^3. Notice how 'a' goes down and 'b' goes up, always summing to the original power 'n'. This is a consistent pattern.

Pascal's Triangle Meets Combinations

  • The coefficients are actually combinations, denoted as nCr.
  • nCr = n! / (r! * (n-r)!)
  • For (a+b)^n, coefficients are nC0, nC1, nC2, ..., nCn.

🎙️ This is the crucial link! Pascal's Triangle numbers are precisely the values of nCr. For example, the coefficients for (a+b)^3 are 3C0, 3C1, 3C2, 3C3, which are 1, 3, 3, 1. This connection makes the theorem precise.

The Binomial Theorem for Positive Integer Index

  • For any positive integer 'n':
  • (a+b)^n = nC0 a^n b^0 + nC1 a^(n-1) b^1 + ... + nCr a^(n-r) b^r + ... + nCn a^0 b^n
  • This can be written as Σ (from r=0 to n) nCr a^(n-r) b^r.

🎙️ This is the core formula. Memorize it! Understand that 'r' represents the power of the second term 'b' in each term of the expansion. It's the foundation of everything we'll do.

Key Properties of (a+b)^n

  • Number of terms = n+1.
  • Sum of powers of 'a' and 'b' in each term is 'n'.
  • Coefficients nCr and nC(n-r) are equal (symmetry).

🎙️ These properties are very useful for quick checks and understanding the structure of the expansion. For (a+b)^3, there are 3+1 = 4 terms. Keep these in mind as you work through problems.

The General Term: Tr+1

  • The (r+1)th term in the expansion of (a+b)^n is given by:
  • Tr+1 = nCr a^(n-r) b^r
  • This formula is extremely powerful for finding any specific term.

🎙️ This term is called the general term because by varying 'r' from 0 to 'n', you can generate all terms of the expansion. Remember, if you need the 5th term, 'r' will be 4. This is a key insight.

Example: Find the 4th term in (x - 2y)^7

  • Here, a=x, b=(-2y), n=7. For the 4th term, r+1=4, so r=3.
  • T4 = T(3+1) = 7C3 x^(7-3) (-2y)^3
  • = 7C3 x^4 (-2)^3 y^3
  • 7C3 = (7*6*5)/(3*2*1) = 35
  • T4 = 35 * x^4 * (-8) * y^3 = -280 x^4 y^3

🎙️ Pay close attention to the sign of 'b'. If 'b' is negative, its powers will alternate in sign. Also, calculate nCr carefully. A small error in sign or calculation can lead to a wrong answer.

Locating the Middle Term(s)

  • If 'n' is even, there is one middle term: (n/2 + 1)th term.
  • If 'n' is odd, there are two middle terms: ((n+1)/2)th and ((n+3)/2)th terms.

🎙️ This depends on whether 'n' is even or odd, which in turn affects the total number of terms (n+1). If n+1 is odd, there's one middle term. If n+1 is even, there are two. This distinction is important.

Example: Find the middle term(s) of (x/3 + 9y)^8

  • Here, n=8 (even).
  • One middle term: (8/2 + 1)th = 5th term. So, r=4.
  • T5 = T(4+1) = 8C4 (x/3)^(8-4) (9y)^4
  • = 8C4 (x/3)^4 (9y)^4
  • 8C4 = (8*7*6*5)/(4*3*2*1) = 70
  • T5 = 70 * (x^4/81) * (6561*y^4) = 70 * x^4 * 81 * y^4 = 5670 x^4 y^4

🎙️ Remember to simplify powers carefully, especially when fractions or coefficients are involved. (9^4/3^4) can be simplified to (9/3)^4 = 3^4. This simplification can save a lot of calculation time.

Term Independent of x (Constant Term)

  • This is the term where the power of 'x' is zero.
  • Set the power of 'x' in the general term Tr+1 to zero.
  • Solve for 'r' and then substitute back into the Tr+1 formula.

🎙️ This is a common exam question. The key is to correctly write down the general term and then equate the exponent of 'x' to zero to find 'r'. This ensures the term is constant.

Example: Find the term independent of x in (x^2 + 1/x)^9

  • a=x^2, b=x^(-1), n=9.
  • Tr+1 = 9Cr (x^2)^(9-r) (x^-1)^r = 9Cr x^(18-2r) x^(-r) = 9Cr x^(18-3r)
  • For term independent of x, set 18-3r = 0 => 3r = 18 => r = 6.
  • The term is T(6+1) = T7.
  • T7 = 9C6 (x^2)^(9-6) (x^-1)^6 = 9C6 x^6 x^-6 = 9C6 x^0 = 9C6.
  • 9C6 = 9C3 = (9*8*7)/(3*2*1) = 3*4*7 = 84.

🎙️ Be careful when combining powers of 'x' using exponent rules. (x^m)^n = x^(mn) and x^m * x^n = x^(m+n). A common pitfall is misapplying these rules.

Sum of all Coefficients

  • To find the sum of all coefficients in (a+b)^n, substitute a=1 and b=1.
  • Sum = (1+1)^n = 2^n.
  • For (x+y)^n, sum = (1+1)^n = 2^n.
  • For (ax+by)^n, sum = (a*1 + b*1)^n = (a+b)^n.

🎙️ This is a neat trick! It works because when you set the variables to 1, each term's variable part becomes 1, leaving only its coefficient. This simplifies calculation significantly.

Special Expansions: Sum & Difference

  • (x+y)^n + (x-y)^n = 2 * [nC0 x^n y^0 + nC2 x^(n-2) y^2 + ...] (even terms of 'r')
  • (x+y)^n - (x-y)^n = 2 * [nC1 x^(n-1) y^1 + nC3 x^(n-3) y^3 + ...] (odd terms of 'r')
  • Useful for calculating specific sums or approximations.

🎙️ When you add the expansions, the terms with odd powers of 'y' cancel out. When you subtract, the terms with even powers of 'y' cancel out. This effectively halves the number of terms.

Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

  • **Sign Errors:** For (a-b)^n, treat 'b' as (-b) in Tr+1.
  • **Incorrect 'r':** For the k-th term, 'r' is (k-1), not k.
  • **Power of 'x' in Tr+1:** Combine powers carefully, especially with fractions or negative exponents.
  • **nCr Calculation:** Don't forget nCr = nC(n-r) for simpler calculations.

🎙️ These are the typical areas where students lose marks. Double-check your signs, 'r' value, and exponent simplifications. Practice helps in avoiding these common errors and improving accuracy.
