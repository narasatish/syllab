Understanding Angles: Degrees vs. Radians

  • An angle is a measure of rotation
  • Degree: 1/360th of a full rotation
  • Radian: Angle subtended at the center by an arc equal to the radius

🎙️ We typically measure angles in degrees, but in higher mathematics, radians are more natural. Think of degrees as a common language and radians as a more scientific one.

The Link: Degrees and Radians

  • Full circle: 360° = 2π radians
  • Half circle: 180° = π radians
  • 1 radian ≈ 57.29°, 1° ≈ 0.01745 radians

🎙️ The key relationship is 180 degrees equals π radians. This conversion factor is crucial for solving problems and understanding different units.

The Unit Circle: Your Trigo Map

  • A circle with radius 1 unit centered at the origin (0,0)
  • Point P(x,y) on the circle at angle θ from positive x-axis
  • x = cos θ, y = sin θ

🎙️ The unit circle is your best friend in trigonometry. It visually represents all trigonometric values for any angle, making it easier to understand their periodic nature.

Signs in Quadrants: ASTC Rule

  • All functions positive in 1st quadrant
  • Sine and Cosecant positive in 2nd
  • Tangent and Cotangent positive in 3rd
  • Cosine and Secant positive in 4th

🎙️ The 'All Students Take Coffee' (ASTC) rule is a handy mnemonic to remember which functions are positive in which quadrant. This is crucial for finding exact values.

Domain & Range: What's Allowed?

  • sin x, cos x: Domain R, Range [-1, 1]
  • tan x, sec x: Domain R - {(2n+1)π/2}, Range R (tan x), R - (-1, 1) (sec x)
  • cot x, cosec x: Domain R - {nπ}, Range R (cot x), R - (-1, 1) (cosec x)

🎙️ Understanding domain and range is critical. It tells you for which input values the function is defined and what output values it can produce. Pay attention to the restrictions for tan, cot, sec, and cosec.

Sum & Difference Formulas: Expanding Angles

  • sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
  • cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
  • tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B) / (1 ∓ tan A tan B)

🎙️ These formulas are incredibly powerful for finding exact values of angles not on our standard list, like 75° or 15°. Memorize them well!

Double & Half Angle Formulas

  • sin(2A) = 2 sin A cos A
  • cos(2A) = cos² A - sin² A (and other forms)
  • tan(2A) = (2 tan A) / (1 - tan² A)
  • Half-angle formulas derived from double angle

🎙️ These are special cases of the sum formulas where A = B. They are crucial for simplifying expressions and solving trigonometric equations.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing degree and radian measures
  • Incorrect signs in quadrants (ASTC)
  • Errors in applying allied angle rules
  • Forgetting domain restrictions for tan, cot, sec, cosec
  • Algebraic errors in identities

🎙️ Many students lose marks due to these common pitfalls. Double-check your signs, units, and algebraic manipulations. Practice is key to avoiding them.
