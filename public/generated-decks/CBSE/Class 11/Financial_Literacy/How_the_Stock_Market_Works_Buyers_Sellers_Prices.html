What is the Stock Market?

  • A marketplace for buying and selling company shares
  • Facilitates capital formation for businesses
  • Allows investors to grow wealth
  • Operates through exchanges like BSE and NSE

🎙️ The stock market is essentially a sophisticated marketplace. It's where parts of publicly listed companies, known as shares, are traded by investors. This mechanism is vital for companies to raise funds and for individuals to invest their savings.

Why Do Companies Issue Shares?

  • To raise capital for expansion or projects
  • Alternative to traditional loans
  • Dilutes ownership but brings in funds
  • Allows public participation in company growth

🎙️ Companies issue shares primarily to raise capital. Instead of borrowing money from banks, they offer ownership stakes to the public. This helps them fund growth initiatives, research, or even pay off existing debts, without incurring interest.

Key Players: Buyers & Sellers

  • The market functions on interactions between these two groups
  • Buyers are investors seeking ownership and returns
  • Sellers are existing shareholders or companies divesting
  • Their actions determine market dynamics

🎙️ At its core, the stock market is driven by two main types of participants: buyers and sellers. Buyers want to acquire shares, while sellers want to offload them. The constant interaction between these groups sets the prices and liquidity in the market.

  • Provide demand for shares

🎙️ Buyers, often called investors, are individuals or institutions who purchase shares. Their primary motivation is to see the value of their investment grow over time. By buying, they inject capital into companies, supporting their expansion.

What Exactly is a Share?

  • A unit of ownership in a company
  • Represents a claim on the company's assets and earnings
  • Gives voting rights in some cases
  • Can be bought and sold on stock exchanges

🎙️ Think of a share as a tiny piece of a large puzzle, where the puzzle is the company itself. Each share represents a fractional ownership in the company. Holding shares means you are a part-owner, with potential rights to dividends and voting.

Factors Affecting Share Price: Demand & Supply

  • The most fundamental economic principle at play
  • High demand + low supply = Price increase
  • Low demand + high supply = Price decrease
  • Market forces constantly seek equilibrium

🎙️ The core driver of share price changes is the basic economic law of demand and supply. If more people want to buy a particular share than sell it, its price will rise. Conversely, if more people want to sell than buy, the price will fall.

⚖️
Market Balance

Demand & Supply Explained

  • Demand: Number of shares investors want to buy
  • Supply: Number of shares available for sale
  • When demand > supply, prices tend to rise
  • When supply > demand, prices tend to fall

🎙️ Let's elaborate: Demand refers to the willingness and ability of buyers to purchase shares at various prices. Supply refers to the willingness and ability of sellers to offer shares. The imbalance between these two forces is what causes price movements.

Demand
Supply

Worked Example: Demand Exceeds Supply

  • Company announces excellent quarterly results
  • Many investors want to buy its shares (High Demand)
  • Few existing shareholders want to sell (Low Supply)
  • Result: Buyers bid higher, share price increases

🎙️ Imagine a company releases fantastic earnings. Everyone wants a piece of that success. With many buyers but limited shares available, buyers will compete by offering higher prices, leading to a surge in the share's value. This is a classic example of demand exceeding supply.

2More Buyers

Worked Example: Supply Exceeds Demand

  • Company involved in a major scandal
  • Many existing shareholders rush to sell (High Supply)
  • Few new investors want to buy (Low Demand)
  • Result: Sellers lower prices, share price decreases

🎙️ Now consider the opposite: a company faces a scandal. Existing shareholders panic and want to sell quickly. With a flood of shares on the market and few interested buyers, sellers must drop their prices to find a buyer, causing the share price to plummet.

2More Sellers

Equilibrium Price Concept

  • The price where demand equals supply
  • No excess buyers or sellers at this point
  • Market tends to move towards equilibrium
  • A temporary state, constantly shifting

🎙️ The equilibrium price is the theoretical point where the number of shares buyers wish to purchase perfectly matches the number of shares sellers wish to sell. While the market constantly strives for this balance, it's a dynamic point that rarely stays fixed for long due to new information and changing sentiments.

Common Mistake: Thinking Share Price is Fixed

  • Share prices are NOT static values
  • They change second-by-second during market hours
  • Influenced by news, sentiment, economic data
  • Understanding volatility is key for investors

🎙️ A very common misconception among new learners is that a share price is a set value. This is absolutely incorrect! Share prices are fluid and can change every moment the market is open, reflecting the continuous battle between buyers and sellers.

Market Orders vs. Limit Orders

  • Market Order: Buy/sell immediately at best available price
  • Limit Order: Buy/sell at a specified price or better
  • Market orders offer speed, limit orders offer price control
  • Crucial for executing trades effectively

🎙️ When placing a trade, you typically have two main options. A market order executes your trade instantly at whatever the current best price is. A limit order, however, allows you to set a specific price at which you are willing to buy or sell, giving you more control over the execution price.
