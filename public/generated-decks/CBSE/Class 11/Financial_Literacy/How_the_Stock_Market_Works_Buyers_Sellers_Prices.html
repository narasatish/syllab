Who are the Buyers in the Market?

  • Individuals: Small investors like you and me.
  • Institutional Investors: Large funds, banks, insurance companies.
  • Foreign Investors: Global entities seeking opportunities.

🎙️ Buyers are essentially investors who believe a company's share price will rise, or they want to own a piece of a profitable business. They put their money into the market hoping for future returns. It's a diverse group, from students saving for a future goal to massive financial institutions.

Who are the Sellers in the Market?

  • Companies: Issuing new shares to raise capital (IPO).
  • Existing Investors: Selling shares for profit or other needs.
  • Institutional Investors: Rebalancing portfolios.

🎙️ Sellers are those who offer shares for sale. This could be a company seeking funds for expansion, or an existing investor who wants to book profits or needs money for personal use. They are looking to exchange their ownership stake for cash.

Demand in the Stock Market

  • Refers to the total quantity of a stock investors are willing to buy.
  • Influenced by positive news, company performance, economic growth.
  • High demand tends to push stock prices UP.

🎙️ When many investors want to buy a particular stock, we say there is high demand. This usually happens when a company announces excellent results or there's positive market sentiment. High demand, like for mangoes, drives prices higher.

⬆️
Upward Pressure
12 / 30

Supply in the Stock Market

  • Refers to the total quantity of a stock available for sale.
  • Influenced by existing shareholders selling, company issuing new shares.
  • High supply tends to push stock prices DOWN.

🎙️ Conversely, supply is the number of shares available to be sold. If many shareholders decide to sell their shares, or a company issues a large number of new shares, the supply increases. High supply can lead to a fall in prices.

⬇️
Downward Pressure
13 / 30

Equilibrium Price: The Balancing Act

  • The price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.
  • A dynamic point, constantly shifting with market forces.
  • Represents a fair market value at that specific moment.

🎙️ Just like a perfect balance in a weighing scale, the equilibrium price is where buyers and sellers agree. It's the point where the number of shares people want to buy exactly matches the number of shares available for sale. This price is never static; it's always adjusting.

How Prices are Set: The Auction System

  • Buyers place 'bid' orders (max price they'll pay).
  • Sellers place 'ask' orders (min price they'll accept).
  • Trades occur when bid and ask prices match.

🎙️ The stock market essentially works like a continuous auction. Buyers are 'bidding' for shares, and sellers are 'asking' for a certain price. When these two meet, a trade is executed, and that's the current market price.

Primary Market: The First Sale (IPO)

  • Where companies sell new shares to the public for the first time.
  • Process is called an Initial Public Offering (IPO).
  • Companies raise capital directly from investors.

🎙️ Imagine a company like 'Chai Point' deciding to go public to fund its expansion. They would issue shares for the very first time in the Primary Market, through an IPO. This is where fresh capital is injected into the company.

🆕
New Issue
23 / 30

Secondary Market: Trading Existing Shares

  • Where investors buy and sell existing shares from each other.
  • Most daily stock trading happens here (e.g., NSE, BSE).
  • Company does not directly receive funds from these trades.

🎙️ Once shares are issued in the primary market, they are then traded among investors in the secondary market. This is what most people refer to when they talk about the 'stock market' – buying and selling shares that are already in circulation.

🔄

Primary vs. Secondary Market

  • Key differences in purpose and participants.
  • Both are vital for a functioning capital market.
  • Understanding them clarifies the flow of funds.

🎙️ The primary market is about new issues and capital raising for companies, while the secondary market is about liquidity and price discovery for investors. Both are crucial for the ecosystem of the stock market.

Role of Stock Brokers

  • Intermediaries who facilitate buying and selling of shares.
  • Provide platforms (apps/websites) for trading.
  • Charge a commission or 'brokerage' for their services.

🎙️ You can't directly go to the stock exchange to buy shares. You need a stockbroker, who acts as your agent. They provide the technology and services to place your orders and ensure they are executed on the exchange.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Confusing Demand with Supply: Always clarify their impact.
  • Ignoring Economic Factors: Focus only on company news.
  • Misunderstanding IPOs: Thinking they are secondary market trades.

🎙️ It's easy to mix up these concepts, especially under exam pressure. Pay close attention to the direction of influence for demand and supply, and remember that IPOs are exclusively primary market events.
