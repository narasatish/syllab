What is Crude Oil? The Black Gold

  • Unrefined petroleum extracted from the earth.
  • Primary source for petrol, diesel, kerosene, and other products.
  • Traded globally as a commodity, priced in US Dollars.

🎙️ Crude oil, often called 'black gold,' is the raw material from which most of our fuels are derived. It's a fossil fuel formed over millions of years. Understanding its nature is the first step to understanding petrol prices.

Components of Petrol Price in India

  • Crude Oil Cost: International price + freight.
  • Refinery Processing: Cost of converting crude to petrol.
  • Central & State Taxes: Excise duty and VAT.
  • Dealer Commission: Margin for petrol pump owners.

🎙️ The price you pay at the pump isn't just the cost of crude. It's a layered structure. The biggest components, after the crude cost itself, are the various taxes levied by both central and state governments, followed by refinery margins and dealer commissions.

Crude Oil Cost for India: Import Parity

  • India imports over 80% of its crude oil needs.
  • Domestic petrol prices are linked to international crude prices.
  • Import parity pricing is used, considering global benchmarks.

🎙️ Since India is a major importer of crude oil, its domestic petrol prices are highly susceptible to international crude price movements. The 'import parity' principle means our prices reflect what it would cost to import the finished product.

Taxes on Petrol in India: A Major Contributor

  • Central Excise Duty: Fixed by the Union Government.
  • State VAT (Value Added Tax): Varies from state to state.
  • Taxes are ad valorem (percentage) or specific (fixed per litre).

🎙️ Taxes form a substantial portion of the final petrol price. Both the central and state governments levy taxes, with VAT being a significant component that differs across states. This is why petrol prices are not uniform throughout India.

🎙️ It's crucial to understand that taxes are not a small part; they often make up more than half of what you pay for petrol. These revenues are vital for government spending, but any changes in tax rates have an immediate and direct impact on your wallet.

Currency Exchange Rate: INR vs. USD

  • Crude oil is traded in US Dollars globally.
  • A weaker Rupee (INR) against the Dollar makes imports more expensive.
  • Fluctuations in exchange rates directly impact import costs for India.

🎙️ Since India imports most of its crude oil and payments are made in US Dollars, the Rupee-Dollar exchange rate is a critical factor. If the Rupee weakens, India needs more Rupees to buy the same amount of Dollars, making crude imports costlier, even if the international crude price in USD remains stable.

OPEC Decisions: Production Cuts and Increases

  • OPEC members collectively control a large share of global oil exports.

🎙️ OPEC's collective decisions on production levels have a profound impact. If they decide to cut production, the reduced supply can drive up prices. Conversely, increasing production can lead to a surplus, pushing prices down. Their actions are closely watched by the market.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Petrol Price Components

  • Assume Crude Cost: ₹40/litre, Refining + Freight: ₹5/litre.
  • Central Excise Duty: ₹20/litre, State VAT: 15% on (Crude+Refining+Excise).
  • Dealer Commission: ₹3/litre. Calculate the final retail price.

🎙️ Let's work through a hypothetical example to understand the calculation. This will help you see how each component adds up to the final retail price. Pay attention to the order of applying taxes, especially VAT.

18 / 25

Solution: Worked Example 1

  • Base Price (Crude + Refining + Freight) = ₹40 + ₹5 = ₹45/litre.
  • Price after Excise Duty = ₹45 + ₹20 = ₹65/litre.
  • State VAT (15% on ₹65) = 0.15 * ₹65 = ₹9.75/litre.
  • Final Retail Price = ₹65 + ₹9.75 + ₹3 (Dealer Commission) = ₹77.75/litre.

🎙️ Here's the step-by-step solution. Notice how the State VAT is applied after the Central Excise Duty is added, as it's often calculated on the base price plus central taxes. This layering is crucial for accurate calculation.
