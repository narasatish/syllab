What is Crude Oil?

  • Naturally occurring fossil fuel.
  • Mixture of hydrocarbons.
  • Raw material for petrol, diesel, kerosene, etc.

🎙️ Crude oil, often called 'black gold,' is a vital fossil fuel extracted from the earth. It's a complex mix of hydrocarbons and serves as the primary raw material for a vast range of petroleum products, not just petrol. Its availability and price profoundly impact global economies.

From Crude to Petrol: The Refining Process

  • Crude oil is transported to refineries.
  • Fractional distillation separates components.
  • Petrol is one of many refined products.

🎙️ Once extracted, crude oil isn't directly usable. It's transported to massive refineries where it undergoes fractional distillation. This process separates crude oil into various components based on their boiling points, yielding products like petrol, diesel, aviation fuel, and even asphalt.

Key Players in the Oil Market

  • OPEC: Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries.
  • Non-OPEC Producers: USA, Russia, Canada, etc.
  • Major Consumers: China, USA, India, EU.

🎙️ The oil market is dominated by a few key players. OPEC, a cartel of oil-producing nations, significantly influences supply. Non-OPEC producers like the US and Russia also hold immense power. On the demand side, major industrial economies like China, the US, and India are the largest consumers.

Understanding Supply and Demand

  • Foundation of all market prices.
  • High supply + low demand = lower prices.
  • Low supply + high demand = higher prices.

🎙️ At its core, the price of oil, like any commodity, is determined by the fundamental economic principles of supply and demand. When supply outstrips demand, prices tend to fall. Conversely, when demand exceeds supply, prices rise. This balance, or imbalance, is constantly shifting.

Factors Affecting Global Supply (Part 1)

  • Geopolitical Tensions: Conflicts in oil-rich regions.
  • OPEC Decisions: Production cuts or increases.
  • Political Instability: Affects production capacity.

🎙️ Global oil supply is highly sensitive to geopolitical events. Conflicts in the Middle East or other major producing regions can disrupt production and transport. OPEC's collective decisions on output levels also play a huge role, as do political instabilities within individual producing nations.

The Role of Futures Markets

  • Contracts to buy/sell oil at a future date, fixed price.
  • Used for hedging (risk management) and speculation.
  • Key benchmarks: Brent Crude, WTI Crude.

🎙️ Oil prices are not just determined by current physical supply and demand, but also heavily by futures markets. These markets allow participants to buy or sell contracts for oil delivery at a future date for a predetermined price. This mechanism is used by producers and consumers to hedge against price volatility and by speculators to profit from anticipated price movements.

How Futures Prices Impact Spot Prices

  • Futures prices reflect market expectations.
  • These expectations influence current (spot) prices.
  • High futures prices can pull up spot prices.

🎙️ The futures market acts as a barometer for future supply and demand expectations. If futures traders anticipate a shortage or high demand in the coming months, futures prices will rise. This expectation, in turn, often pulls up the current 'spot' price of oil, as traders and refiners adjust their strategies based on future outlooks.

Components of Petrol Price in India

  • Crude Oil Cost: International benchmark price.
  • Refining & Marketing Costs: Processing, transport.
  • Taxes: Central Excise Duty, State VAT, Dealer Commission.

🎙️ When you pay for petrol in India, you're paying for much more than just the crude oil. The price is a composite of the international crude oil cost, the expenses incurred in refining and marketing it, and a significant portion comprises various taxes levied by both the Central and State governments, along with the dealer's commission.

Government Taxes and Duties

  • Central Excise Duty: Fixed per litre by Union Govt.
  • State VAT: Value Added Tax, percentage-based.
  • These are major revenue sources for governments.

🎙️ Taxes form a substantial part of petrol prices in India. The Central government levies an Excise Duty, which is a fixed amount per litre. State governments impose Value Added Tax (VAT), which is a percentage of the price and thus fluctuates. These taxes are crucial revenue streams for both the Union and State exchequers.

Impact of Exchange Rate (INR-USD)

  • Crude oil is traded in US Dollars globally.
  • Weaker Rupee (INR) means more Rupees per Dollar.
  • Higher import cost in Rupees, leading to higher petrol price.

🎙️ India imports over 80% of its crude oil, and all international transactions are in US Dollars. Therefore, the exchange rate between the Indian Rupee and the US Dollar significantly impacts petrol prices. If the Rupee weakens against the Dollar, India has to pay more Rupees for the same amount of Dollars, increasing the import cost and, consequently, the petrol price.

Worked Example: Calculating Tax Impact

  • Assume Crude Cost: ₹30/litre, Refining: ₹10/litre.
  • Central Excise Duty: ₹20/litre (fixed).
  • State VAT: 20% on (Crude + Refining + Excise).
  • Calculate final price.

🎙️ Let's put our knowledge into practice. If the base cost (crude + refining) is ₹40, and Excise Duty is ₹20, the subtotal is ₹60. A 20% VAT on this ₹60 would be ₹12. So, the final price would be ₹60 + ₹12 = ₹72 per litre. This shows how taxes significantly inflate the final price.

Common Mistake: Ignoring Global Factors

  • Mistake: Believing petrol prices are only domestic.
  • Reality: Heavily influenced by international crude prices.
  • Local taxes add to, but don't solely determine, price.

🎙️ A common misconception is that petrol prices are solely determined by domestic policies or costs. While Indian taxes are a significant component, the fundamental driver remains the international price of crude oil. Any major shift in global supply or demand will inevitably reflect in our local petrol pumps, even with stable taxes.

Real-Life Example: 2008 Financial Crisis & Oil

  • Global recession led to sharp fall in demand.
  • Crude oil prices plummeted from $147 to $30/barrel.
  • Demonstrates demand-side impact on prices.

🎙️ The 2008 global financial crisis serves as a stark example of demand-side impact. As economies worldwide plunged into recession, industrial activity slowed, and travel reduced drastically. This massive drop in demand caused crude oil prices to collapse from record highs, illustrating how global economic health directly affects oil prices.

Real-Life Example: COVID-19 Pandemic & Oil

At one point, WTI crude oil futures prices even turned negative, meaning producers were paying buyers to take oil due to overflowing storage, a truly historic event.

Impact on Indian Economy

  • Inflation: Higher petrol prices increase transport costs.
  • Fiscal Deficit: Higher import bill strains government.
  • Trade Balance: Large oil imports worsen trade deficit.

🎙️ High petrol prices have significant ripple effects on the Indian economy. They contribute to inflation, as increased transport costs translate to higher prices for goods and services. A larger oil import bill strains the government's finances, potentially widening the fiscal deficit, and it also negatively impacts our trade balance.

Government's Role in Price Management

  • Tax adjustments: Lowering/raising excise duties.
  • Subsidies: Direct support to consumers (historically).
  • Strategic Petroleum Reserves: Stockpiling for emergencies.

🎙️ Governments often try to manage the impact of volatile oil prices. They can adjust taxes, like excise duties, to cushion consumers from sharp price hikes or to boost revenue. Historically, subsidies were used, though less so now. India also maintains Strategic Petroleum Reserves to safeguard against supply disruptions.
