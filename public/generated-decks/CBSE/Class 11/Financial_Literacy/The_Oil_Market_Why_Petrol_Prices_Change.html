Concept 1: Crude Oil – The Black Gold

  • Crude oil is unprocessed petroleum, a fossil fuel.
  • It's a mixture of hydrocarbons, extracted from underground reservoirs.
  • Often called 'black gold' due to its immense economic value.

🎙️ Imagine crude oil as the raw material for many products, not just petrol. It's a foundational commodity, and its price affects almost everything else in the economy.

Concept 6: Currency Exchange Rates (USD-INR)

  • Crude oil is primarily traded in US Dollars (USD) globally.
  • India imports over 80% of its crude oil needs.
  • A weaker Indian Rupee (INR) against the USD makes imports more expensive.

🎙️ Even if the international crude oil price remains stable in USD, a depreciation of the Indian Rupee means we have to pay more INR for the same amount of oil. This directly impacts our petrol prices.

Worked Example: Exchange Rate Impact

  • Scenario 1: Crude oil at $80/barrel, USD-INR at 75. Cost: ₹6000.
  • Scenario 2: Crude oil at $80/barrel, USD-INR at 83. Cost: ₹6640.
  • A weaker Rupee (INR 83) means higher import cost even if crude price is same.

🎙️ This example clearly illustrates that a significant portion of petrol price changes in India can be attributed to the USD-INR exchange rate, even without a change in global crude prices. It's a critical domestic factor.

Concept 7: Taxes and Duties – Government's Share

  • Central Government levies Excise Duty and Road & Infrastructure Cess.
  • State Governments levy Value Added Tax (VAT) and other local taxes.
  • These taxes form a substantial part of the final petrol price.

🎙️ Both the central and state governments collect significant revenue from petrol sales. These taxes are often ad-valorem (percentage-based) or fixed, and their changes directly impact the retail price.

Concept 8: Refining & Distribution Costs

🎙️ After the crude is refined, there are still costs associated with getting the fuel to your vehicle. These include the operational costs of the refinery, logistics, and the profit margin for the petrol pump owner.

Concept 9: International Benchmarks – Brent & WTI

  • Brent Crude (North Sea) and WTI (West Texas Intermediate) are global benchmarks.
  • They represent different types of crude oil and are traded on futures markets.
  • Their prices serve as references for crude oil prices worldwide.

🎙️ Think of Brent and WTI as the gold standards for crude oil. Their prices are closely watched indicators of the global oil market's health and direction, influencing pricing for other crude varieties.

Concept 10: Futures Trading & Speculation

  • Oil is traded on futures markets, where buyers and sellers agree on future prices.
  • Speculators bet on future price movements, adding volatility.
  • High speculation can amplify price swings, disconnected from immediate supply/demand.

🎙️ Futures markets allow companies to hedge against price volatility, but they also attract speculators. These speculators, by buying or selling large volumes, can create significant price movements based on their expectations.

🎙️ Petrol price changes don't just affect vehicle owners. They cascade through the entire economy, impacting everything from food prices to manufacturing costs, leading to broader inflation.

Worked Example: Calculating Petrol Price Components (Simplified)

  • Assume Crude Cost: ₹40/liter, Refining/Freight: ₹5/liter, Dealer Comm: ₹3/liter.
  • Central Excise Duty (fixed): ₹20/liter, State VAT (30% of base + excise): ₹19.50/liter.
  • Total Retail Price = ₹40+₹5+₹3+₹20+₹19.50 = ₹87.50/liter.

🎙️ This simplified example demonstrates how each component adds up to the final price. Notice how taxes alone can nearly equal or even exceed the initial cost of crude oil.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Assuming petrol price only depends on international crude price.
  • Ignoring the impact of USD-INR exchange rate.
  • Underestimating the significant share of taxes in the final price.
  • Confusing global demand/supply with local market conditions.

🎙️ It's easy to oversimplify this complex topic. Remember to consider all layers of influence – global, national, and local – when analyzing petrol price changes.
