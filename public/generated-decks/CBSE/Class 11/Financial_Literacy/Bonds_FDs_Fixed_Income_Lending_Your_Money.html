What is Lending Your Money?

  • Giving money to someone else
  • Expecting it back with interest
  • Becoming a 'creditor' or 'lender'

🎙️ When you lend money, you're essentially providing funds to another entity – be it a bank, a company, or the government – for a specific period. In return, they pay you interest for using your money.

Fixed Deposits (FDs): The Basics

  • Money deposited for a fixed period
  • Earns a predetermined interest rate
  • Offered by banks and NBFCs

🎙️ FDs are one of the most popular and safest investment options in India. They are essentially a time deposit where you commit your money for a set duration.

Key Features of FDs

  • Fixed interest rate
  • Flexible tenure (7 days to 10 years)
  • Premature withdrawal option (with penalty)
  • Nomination facility available

🎙️ Understanding these features is vital. The fixed interest rate ensures predictability, while the flexible tenure allows you to align it with your financial goals.

FD Characteristics

Types of FDs

  • Cumulative FD: Interest compounded and paid at maturity
  • Non-cumulative FD: Interest paid out regularly (monthly, quarterly)

🎙️ Choose between cumulative if you want to grow your principal significantly over time, or non-cumulative if you need a regular income stream from your investment.

Advantages of FDs

  • High safety (DICGC insurance up to ₹5 lakhs)
  • Guaranteed returns
  • Simple to understand and invest
  • Tax benefits on certain FDs (Tax Saver FDs)

🎙️ The safety aspect, especially with DICGC insurance, makes FDs a preferred choice for risk-averse investors. The guaranteed returns eliminate market volatility concerns.

Disadvantages of FDs

  • Lower returns compared to market-linked instruments
  • Interest income is taxable
  • Liquidity constraints (premature withdrawal penalty)
  • Inflation risk (real return might be low)

🎙️ While safe, FDs might not always beat inflation, meaning your purchasing power could erode over time. Also, remember the tax implications on interest earned.

Worked Example: Calculating FD Returns

  • Invest ₹1,00,000 for 5 years at 7% p.a. (compounded annually).
  • Formula: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
  • Calculate maturity amount.

🎙️ Let's apply the compound interest formula. Here, P = 1,00,000, r = 0.07, n = 1 (annually), t = 5. The maturity amount will be ₹1,40,255.

What are Bonds?

  • Debt instruments issued by governments or companies
  • Borrower pays interest (coupon) to lender
  • Repays principal at maturity

🎙️ Think of a bond as an IOU (I Owe You) certificate. When you buy a bond, you're essentially lending money to the issuer, who promises to pay you back with interest.

📜
Debt Certificate
11 / 25

Key Terms in Bonds

  • Face Value (Par Value): The principal amount repaid at maturity.
  • Coupon Rate: The annual interest rate paid on the face value.
  • Maturity Date: The date when the principal is repaid.

🎙️ These three terms are fundamental to understanding bonds. The face value is what you get back, the coupon rate determines your income, and the maturity date tells you when your investment ends.

How Bonds Work (Process)

  • 1. Issuer sells bond to investor
  • 2. Investor pays face value
  • 3. Issuer pays periodic interest (coupon)
  • 4. Issuer repays face value at maturity

🎙️ This simple four-step process illustrates the lifecycle of a bond. It's a contractual agreement between the borrower and the lender.

2Receive Funds
3Pay Interest
4Repay Principal

Types of Bonds

  • Government Bonds (G-Secs): Issued by central/state governments, considered safest.
  • Corporate Bonds: Issued by companies, vary in risk based on company's creditworthiness.

🎙️ Government bonds are often called 'risk-free' because of the sovereign guarantee. Corporate bonds offer higher returns but come with associated credit risk.

Disadvantages of Bonds

  • Liquidity risk (some bonds may be hard to sell)

Worked Example: Bond Yield Calculation

  • A bond with Face Value ₹1000, Coupon Rate 8%, Market Price ₹950. Calculate Current Yield.
  • Current Yield = (Annual Interest Payment / Current Market Price) * 100

🎙️ The current yield gives you an idea of the return you get based on the bond's current market price, not just its face value. Here, Annual Interest = 8% of ₹1000 = ₹80. Current Yield = (80/950)*100 = 8.42%.

FD vs. Bonds: A Comparison

  • Safety: FDs generally higher (DICGC) vs. Bonds (depends on issuer).
  • Liquidity: FDs (premature withdrawal with penalty) vs. Bonds (can be traded on exchanges).
  • Returns: FDs (fixed, lower) vs. Bonds (can be higher, market-linked price).

🎙️ This comparison helps you decide which instrument aligns better with your risk appetite and financial goals. FDs are simpler, while bonds offer more complexity and potential for higher returns.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ignoring inflation when calculating real returns.
  • Not diversifying fixed income portfolio.
  • Overlooking credit risk in corporate bonds.
  • Not checking premature withdrawal penalties for FDs.

🎙️ Always consider the real return after inflation and taxes. Diversification is key even within fixed income to spread risk. Read the terms and conditions carefully before investing.

Recap & Key Takeaways
