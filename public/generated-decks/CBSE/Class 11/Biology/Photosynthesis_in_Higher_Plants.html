The Foundation of Life: What is Photosynthesis?

  • Process by which green plants synthesize food
  • Uses light energy, CO2, and water
  • Produces glucose and oxygen
  • Essential for all heterotrophs

🎙️ Photosynthesis is derived from Greek words: 'photo' meaning light, and 'synthesis' meaning to build. It's the primary energy-capturing reaction on our planet, converting light energy into chemical energy stored in organic matter.

Flow of Electrons: The Z-scheme (Non-Cyclic)

  • Non-cyclic photophosphorylation pathway
  • Electrons move from PSII to PSI
  • Involves several electron carriers (Plastoquinone, Cytochrome complex, Plastocyanin)
  • Creates a proton gradient

🎙️ This pathway is called the Z-scheme due to the characteristic zig-zag shape of the redox potential changes. It's crucial for producing both ATP and NADPH.

ATP Synthesis: Cyclic vs. Non-cyclic Photophosphorylation

  • Non-cyclic: Involves both PSI and PSII; Produces ATP and NADPH; Water is split, O2 released
  • Cyclic: Involves only PSI; Produces only ATP; No water splitting, no O2, no NADPH

🎙️ Non-cyclic photophosphorylation is the primary way to generate ATP and NADPH for the Calvin cycle. Cyclic photophosphorylation provides additional ATP when NADPH demand is low.

How ATP is Made: The Chemiosmotic Hypothesis

  • Proton gradient across thylakoid membrane
  • Protons accumulate in lumen
  • Due to water splitting, electron transport, and NADP+ reduction
  • Protons flow through ATP synthase to stroma, generating ATP

🎙️ This hypothesis explains how the energy from the electron transport chain is used to synthesize ATP, similar to how it works in mitochondria. It's a fundamental concept in bioenergetics.

The Second Stage: Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)

  • Occur in the stroma of chloroplasts
  • Does not directly require light
  • Uses ATP and NADPH from light reactions
  • Synthesizes glucose from CO2

🎙️ This is where the actual sugar is made! The energy and reducing power generated in the light reactions are now put to use to fix carbon dioxide.

Calvin Cycle - Step 1: Carboxylation (CO2 Fixation)

  • CO2 combines with RuBP (a 5-carbon sugar)
  • Catalyzed by RuBisCO enzyme
  • Forms 2 molecules of 3-PGA (3-phosphoglyceric acid)
  • This is the first stable product, hence C3 pathway

🎙️ RuBisCO is arguably the most abundant enzyme on Earth! It's critical for carbon fixation, but also has a problematic oxygenase activity we'll discuss later.

Calvin Cycle - Step 3: Regeneration of RuBP

Adapting to Heat: Introduction to the C4 Pathway

  • Found in plants adapted to hot, dry regions (e.g., maize, sugarcane)
  • Minimizes photorespiration
  • Has a specialized leaf anatomy
  • Uses PEP carboxylase for initial CO2 fixation

🎙️ C4 plants have evolved a clever mechanism to overcome the limitations of RuBisCO in hot environments, where photorespiration becomes a significant problem for C3 plants.

Kranz Anatomy: A Special Leaf Structure in C4 Plants

  • Bundle sheath cells: Large cells surrounding vascular bundles
  • Thick walls, no intercellular spaces; Many chloroplasts
  • Mesophyll cells: Surround bundle sheath cells
  • Spatially separates initial CO2 fixation from Calvin cycle

🎙️ Kranz anatomy, meaning 'wreath' in German, is characteristic of C4 plants. It spatially separates the initial carbon fixation from the Calvin cycle, creating a CO2-rich environment.

C4 Pathway: The Mechanism in Detail

  • 1. Mesophyll: CO2 + PEP -> OAA (4C) by PEPcase
  • 2. OAA -> Malate/Aspartate, transported to bundle sheath cells
  • 3. Bundle sheath: Malate/Aspartate decarboxylated, releasing CO2
  • 4. CO2 enters Calvin cycle; Pyruvate returns to mesophyll, regenerates PEP

🎙️ This two-step fixation process effectively concentrates CO2 around RuBisCO in the bundle sheath cells, drastically reducing photorespiration.

The Wasteful Process: Photorespiration in C3 Plants

  • Occurs in C3 plants when O2 levels are high, CO2 levels low
  • RuBisCO acts as an oxygenase, binding O2 to RuBP
  • Forms one molecule of PGA and one of phosphoglycolate
  • No ATP or NADPH produced, consumes ATP, releases CO2

🎙️ Photorespiration is a disadvantage for C3 plants, especially in hot and dry conditions when stomata close, reducing CO2 intake and increasing O2 concentration.

What Influences Photosynthesis? Limiting Factors

  • Light intensity: Essential for light reactions
  • CO2 concentration: Substrate for Calvin cycle
  • Temperature: Affects enzyme activity
  • Water: Reactant, affects stomatal opening

🎙️ Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors states that the rate of a process is limited by the factor that is in shortest supply. Understanding these factors is crucial for maximizing crop yields.

Recap & Summary: Photosynthesis - A Life-Sustaining Process

  • Photosynthesis converts light energy to chemical energy
  • Occurs in chloroplasts (light & dark reactions)
  • Light reactions produce ATP, NADPH, O2
  • Calvin cycle fixes CO2 to sugar
