  • Studies motion and rest of objects
  • Divided into three main parts

🎙️ Mechanics is the oldest branch of physics, dealing with how objects move or stay still. We classify it into Statics, Kinematics, and Dynamics. In this chapter, we'll focus on Kinematics, which describes motion without considering its causes.

Rest and Motion: Relative Concepts

  • Object is at rest if its position doesn't change
  • Object is in motion if its position changes
  • Both are relative to an observer or frame of reference

🎙️ An object is never truly at rest or in motion in an absolute sense. It always depends on the chosen frame of reference, or the observer. Think of passengers in a moving train: they are at rest relative to each other but in motion relative to the ground.

Path Length & Displacement

  • Path Length: Total distance covered by an object
  • Displacement: Shortest distance from initial to final position
  • Path Length is scalar, Displacement is vector

🎙️ These are two fundamental quantities describing motion. Path length is a scalar, always positive, representing the actual route taken. Displacement is a vector, indicating both magnitude and direction, focusing only on the net change in position.

Comparing Path Length and Displacement

  • Path Length: Scalar, always positive, depends on actual path
  • Displacement: Vector, can be zero/negative, independent of path
  • Displacement ≤ Path Length

🎙️ Remember, path length tells you how much ground was covered, while displacement tells you how far and in what direction you are from where you started. They are equal only for straight-line motion without a change in direction.

Worked Example 1: Path Length & Displacement

  • A person walks 5m East, then 3m West.
  • Calculate the total path length.
  • Calculate the net displacement.

🎙️ Let's apply what we just learned. For path length, we simply add the magnitudes of the distances covered. For displacement, we must consider the directions, typically assigning positive to one direction and negative to the opposite.

🚶
Walking problem
8 / 25

Solution: Path Length & Displacement

  • Path Length = 5m (East) + 3m (West) = 8m
  • Displacement = 5m (East) - 3m (West) = 2m (East)
  • Final position is 2m East of starting point

🎙️ The path length is the total distance covered, which is 8 meters. The displacement is the net change in position, which is 2 meters towards the East from the starting point. Note how direction matters for displacement.

Speed and Velocity: How Fast?

  • Speed: Rate of change of path length
  • Velocity: Rate of change of displacement
  • Speed is scalar, Velocity is vector

🎙️ Just like path length and displacement, speed and velocity are related but distinct. Speed is a scalar, telling you how quickly an object is moving. Velocity is a vector, indicating both the speed and the direction of motion.

Uniform and Non-uniform Motion

  • Uniform Motion: Equal distances in equal time intervals
  • Non-uniform Motion: Unequal distances in equal time intervals
  • Uniform velocity means constant speed and constant direction

🎙️ Uniform motion implies constant speed and constant direction, meaning constant velocity. Non-uniform motion means either the speed, direction, or both are changing. Most real-world motion you observe is non-uniform.

Average Speed and Average Velocity

  • Average Speed = Total path length / Total time taken
  • Average Velocity = Total displacement / Total time taken
  • Describe motion over a specific time interval

🎙️ These quantities describe the motion over a longer duration. Average speed doesn't care about the stops or turns, just the total distance and total time. Average velocity focuses on the net change in position over the time interval.

Worked Example 2: Average Speed & Velocity

  • Car travels 100km North in 2h
  • Then 50km South in 1h
  • Calculate its average speed and average velocity

🎙️ This example combines both concepts of average speed and average velocity. Remember to account for directions when calculating displacement and average velocity, while for average speed, we only sum the magnitudes of distances.

🚗
Car travel
13 / 25

Solution: Average Speed & Velocity

  • Total Path Length = 100km + 50km = 150km
  • Total Displacement = 100km (North) - 50km (South) = 50km (North)
  • Total Time = 2h + 1h = 3h

🎙️ Average Speed = 150km / 3h = 50 km/h. Average Velocity = 50km (North) / 3h = 16.67 km/h (North). Notice how the values and directions differ significantly due to the change in direction.

🎙️ Acceleration tells us how quickly an object's velocity is changing. This change can be in magnitude (speeding up or slowing down) or direction. A car turning a corner is accelerating even if its speed is constant.

Uniform and Non-uniform Acceleration

  • Uniform Acceleration: Velocity changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals
  • Non-uniform Acceleration: Velocity changes by unequal amounts
  • Constant acceleration simplifies motion analysis

🎙️ An object falling freely under gravity experiences uniform acceleration (ignoring air resistance). A car in city traffic typically experiences non-uniform acceleration due to varying speeds and braking. We primarily focus on uniform acceleration in this chapter.

The Three Kinematic Equations

  • 1. v = u + at
  • 2. s = ut + ½at²
  • 3. v² = u² + 2as

🎙️ Make sure you know these equations by heart and understand what each variable represents. 'u' is initial velocity, 'v' is final velocity, 'a' is acceleration, 't' is time, and 's' is displacement. Choose the correct equation based on your knowns and unknowns.

Worked Example 3: Applying Kinematic Equations

  • Car accelerates from rest at 2 m/s² for 5s.
  • Find its final velocity (v).
  • Find the distance covered (s).

🎙️ This is a classic problem that tests your ability to apply the kinematic equations. Remember that 'from rest' implies an initial velocity of zero. Identify your knowns and unknowns, then select the appropriate equations.

🏁
Start of a race
20 / 25

Solution: Kinematic Equations

  • Given: u=0, a=2 m/s², t=5s
  • Using v = u + at: v = 0 + (2)(5) = 10 m/s
  • Using s = ut + ½at²: s = (0)(5) + ½(2)(5)² = 25m

🎙️ The car reaches a final velocity of 10 m/s after 5 seconds of acceleration. During this time, it covers a displacement of 25 meters. Always double-check your units and calculations.

🏎️
Accelerating car

Motion Under Gravity: A Special Case

  • Free fall is uniformly accelerated motion
  • Acceleration 'a' becomes 'g' (approx. 9.8 m/s²)
  • Direction of 'g' is always downwards

🎙️ When objects fall or are thrown vertically, their motion is governed by gravity. We use the same kinematic equations, replacing 'a' with 'g'. Remember to choose a consistent sign convention for 'g' based on your chosen positive direction.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing scalar and vector quantities (e.g., speed vs. velocity)
  • Incorrect sign conventions for direction or 'g'
  • Not identifying 'from rest' (u=0) or 'comes to stop' (v=0)
  • Mixing units without conversion

🎙️ Pay close attention to the details in problem statements. Always list your knowns and unknowns with units. Consistency in sign convention for direction is paramount, especially in problems involving vertical motion or changes in direction.
