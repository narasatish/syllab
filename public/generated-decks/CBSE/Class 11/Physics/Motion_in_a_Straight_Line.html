  • Non-uniform motion: Changing velocity, non-zero acceleration
  • Uniformly accelerated motion: Constant acceleration

Worked Example 1: Car Acceleration

  • Problem: A car accelerates from rest at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds.
  • Find its final velocity and displacement.
  • Solution: Given u=0, a=2 m/s², t=5 s.
  • v = u + at = 0 + (2)(5) = 10 m/s.
  • s = ut + ½ at² = 0 + ½ (2)(5)² = 25 m.

🎙️ Here's a direct application of the kinematic equations. We identify the given values and then apply the appropriate equations to find the final velocity and the total displacement. Always clearly state your knowns and unknowns.

Worked Example 2: Ball Drop

  • Problem: A ball is dropped from a height of 20m. Find time to hit ground and final velocity.
  • Assume g = 10 m/s².
  • Solution: Given u=0, s=20m, a=g=10 m/s².
  • s = ut + ½ at² => 20 = 0 + ½ (10)t² => t = 2 s.
  • v = u + at = 0 + (10)(2) = 20 m/s.

🎙️ This example shows motion under gravity. Remember that 'dropped' implies an initial velocity of zero. We use the kinematic equations, substituting 'a' with 'g', and solve for time and final velocity.

Position-Time (x-t) Graphs

  • Slope of x-t graph gives velocity
  • Horizontal line: object at rest
  • Straight line with slope: uniform velocity
  • Curved line: non-uniform velocity (acceleration)

🎙️ Graphs are powerful tools in kinematics. The slope of a position-time graph directly tells us the object's velocity. A straight line means constant velocity, while a curve indicates changing velocity or acceleration.

Velocity-Time (v-t) Graphs

  • Slope of v-t graph gives acceleration
