The Gravitational Constant (G)

  • Proportionality constant in Newton's Law
  • Value: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²kg⁻²
  • Determined experimentally by Cavendish

🎙️ The 'G' in the formula is the Universal Gravitational Constant. It's a very small value, indicating that gravitational force is weak unless the masses are extremely large, like planets. Henry Cavendish first measured its value accurately.

Key Features of Gravitational Force

  • Always attractive in nature
  • It is a central force
  • Weakest fundamental force
  • Independent of intervening medium

🎙️ Gravitational force has distinct characteristics. It always pulls objects together, acts along the line joining their centres, and is the weakest of the four fundamental forces. Crucially, it doesn't care if there's air, water, or vacuum between the objects.

Common Mistake 1: G vs. g

  • G is Universal Gravitational Constant
  • g is acceleration due to gravity
  • G is constant everywhere, g varies

🎙️ This is a very common point of confusion. 'G' is a universal constant, same everywhere in the universe. 'g' is the acceleration experienced by an object due to gravity, and it changes depending on location, altitude, and even the planet.

Acceleration due to Gravity (g)

  • Acceleration experienced by a freely falling body
  • Caused by Earth's gravitational pull
  • Formula: g = GM/R²

🎙️ When you drop an object, it accelerates towards the Earth. This acceleration is 'g'. Its value is approximately 9.8 m/s² on Earth's surface. The formula shows it depends on the mass (M) and radius (R) of the planet.

'g' Variation with Altitude (Height)

  • 'g' decreases as we go up from Earth's surface
  • Formula: g_h = g(1 - 2h/R_e) (for h << R_e)
  • Significant for satellites and space travel

🎙️ As you move away from the Earth's surface, the distance 'r' in the GM/r² formula increases, causing 'g' to decrease. This means objects weigh less at higher altitudes. This effect is crucial for understanding satellite motion.

'g' Variation with Depth

  • 'g' also decreases as we go down into Earth
  • Formula: g_d = g(1 - d/R_e)
  • 'g' is zero at Earth's centre

🎙️ Surprisingly, 'g' decreases when you go *inside* the Earth too. This is because the mass attracting you becomes less. At the very centre of the Earth, 'g' becomes zero, as the gravitational forces from all directions balance out.

'g' Variation with Latitude

  • Earth's rotation causes centrifugal force
  • 'g' is maximum at poles, minimum at equator
  • Earth's bulge also contributes

🎙️ The Earth isn't perfectly spherical and it rotates. These factors cause 'g' to vary slightly with latitude. Due to the centrifugal effect, 'g' is slightly less at the equator than at the poles. This is a subtle but important variation.

Common Mistake 2: Forgetting Earth's Radius (R)

  • Distance 'r' is from centre of masses
  • For objects near Earth, r = R_e + h
  • Don't just use 'h' as the distance

🎙️ A frequent error is to use only 'h' (height above surface) as the distance in gravitational formulas. Remember, the 'r' in F=GMm/r² or g=GM/r² is the distance from the *centre* of the Earth, so it's always R_e + h.

Gravitational Potential Energy

  • Energy possessed by a mass due to its position in a gravitational field
  • Formula: U = -GMm/r
  • Always negative and zero at infinity

🎙️ Gravitational potential energy is the work done to bring a mass from infinity to a point in the gravitational field. It's always negative because gravity is an attractive force, and we define zero potential energy at infinity.

Gravitational Potential

  • Gravitational potential energy per unit mass
  • Formula: V = -GM/r
  • Scalar quantity, unit: J/kg

🎙️ Gravitational potential is similar to potential energy but defined per unit mass. It describes the 'strength' of the gravitational field at a point, independent of the mass you place there. Like potential energy, it's negative and zero at infinity.

Escape Velocity

  • Minimum velocity required for an object to escape Earth's gravitational field
  • Formula: v_e = √(2GM/R)
  • For Earth, approx. 11.2 km/s

🎙️ If you throw an object upwards with enough speed, it can completely leave Earth's gravity and never return. This minimum speed is the escape velocity. It depends on the planet's mass and radius, not the mass of the escaping object.

The Phenomenon of Weightlessness

  • Not absence of gravity, but absence of normal force
  • Occurs in freely falling frames
  • Astronauts in orbit are continuously falling

🎙️ Weightlessness in space isn't because there's no gravity – there's still significant gravity. It's because the satellite and everything inside it are continuously falling around the Earth, creating a sensation of weightlessness, just like in a freely falling elevator.
