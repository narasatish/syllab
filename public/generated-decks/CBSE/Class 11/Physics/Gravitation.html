Newton's Law: The Universal Attraction

  • Every particle attracts every other particle.
  • Force is directly proportional to the product of their masses.
  • Force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.

🎙️ This law, famously inspired by a falling apple, describes how any two objects with mass exert an attractive force on each other. It's a cornerstone of classical physics.

Decoding Newton's Gravitational Formula

  • F = G * (m₁m₂ / r²)
  • F: Gravitational force
  • m₁, m₂: Masses of the two objects
  • r: Distance between their centers

🎙️ The formula F = G * (m₁m₂ / r²) is crucial. Remember that 'r' is the distance between the *centers* of the objects, not their surfaces. The inverse square relationship means force drops rapidly with distance.

The Universal Gravitational Constant (G)

  • G is a universal constant, independent of medium or properties.
  • Value: 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².
  • Determined experimentally by Henry Cavendish.
  • Ensures the force calculation is dimensionally consistent.

🎙️ The value of G is extremely small, which explains why we don't feel the gravitational pull between everyday objects. It's a fundamental constant of nature.

Common Mistake: Differentiating 'g' and 'G'

  • 'G' is Universal Gravitational Constant.
  • 'g' is Acceleration due to Gravity.
  • They are distinct physical quantities.

🎙️ This is a frequent point of confusion for students. 'G' is a constant everywhere in the universe, while 'g' varies depending on location and altitude. Keep them separate!

Gravitation vs. Gravity: What's the Difference?

  • Gravitation: The general phenomenon of attraction between any two masses.
  • Gravity: The specific force of attraction exerted by a massive celestial body (like Earth) on objects near its surface.
  • Gravity is a specific case of gravitation.

🎙️ While often used interchangeably in casual talk, in physics, 'gravitation' is the broader term. 'Gravity' usually refers to the force experienced on a planet's surface.

Acceleration due to Gravity (g)

  • Acceleration experienced by an object due to Earth's gravitational pull.
  • Formula: g = GM/R² (near Earth's surface).
  • Approximate value on Earth's surface: 9.8 m/s².
  • It is a vector quantity, directed towards the center of Earth.

🎙️ This 'g' is what makes objects fall towards the Earth. It's not a constant everywhere; its value changes with location, altitude, and depth, which we will explore next.

Variation of 'g' with Altitude

  • As height (h) increases above Earth's surface, 'g' decreases.
  • Formula: g_h = g (1 - 2h/R) for h << R.
  • More generally: g_h = GM / (R+h)².
  • Objects weigh less at higher altitudes.

🎙️ Think about astronauts in space; they experience weightlessness because 'g' is significantly reduced at their orbital altitude. The force of gravity becomes weaker as you move away from the Earth's center.

Variation of 'g' with Depth

  • As depth (d) increases below Earth's surface, 'g' decreases.
  • Formula: g_d = g (1 - d/R).
  • At the center of Earth (d=R), 'g' becomes zero.
  • This is due to only the mass within radius (R-d) contributing to gravity.

🎙️ It's counter-intuitive, but 'g' also decreases as you go deeper into the Earth. This is because the mass of the Earth *above* you cancels out some of the gravitational pull.

Variation of 'g' with Latitude (Earth's Rotation)

  • Earth's rotation causes a centrifugal force, reducing effective 'g'.
  • Maximum 'g' at poles (no centrifugal effect).
  • Minimum 'g' at equator (maximum centrifugal effect).
  • Formula: g' = g - Rω²cos²λ (where λ is latitude).

🎙️ The Earth isn't a perfect sphere and it rotates. Both factors contribute to 'g' being slightly different at the poles versus the equator. This is due to the centrifugal force acting outwards.

Gravitational Potential Energy (U)

  • Energy possessed by a body due to its position in a gravitational field.
  • Formula: U = -GMm/r (where r is distance from center).
  • It is always negative, indicating an attractive force.
  • Zero potential energy is taken at infinite separation.

🎙️ Gravitational potential energy is a scalar quantity. The negative sign signifies that work must be done against the gravitational force to move objects apart. It's stored energy due to configuration.

Gravitational Potential (V)

  • Work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to a point in the gravitational field.
  • Formula: V = -GM/r.
  • Scalar quantity, unit: J/kg.
  • Related to potential energy: U = mV.

🎙️ Gravitational potential is like an 'energy landscape' created by a massive body. It tells us the potential energy per unit mass at any point in the field, making it independent of the test mass.

Escape Velocity: Breaking Free from Gravity

  • Minimum velocity required for an object to escape a planet's gravitational field.
  • Formula: v_e = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR).
  • For Earth, v_e ≈ 11.2 km/s.
  • Independent of the mass or direction of projection of the escaping object.

🎙️ To escape Earth's gravity, a rocket needs to achieve a very high speed. This concept is vital for space missions and understanding planetary atmospheres.

Worked Example: Calculating Escape Velocity

  • Problem: Calculate the escape velocity from a planet with mass 4M_earth and radius 2R_earth.
  • Given: M_earth, R_earth, v_e_earth = 11.2 km/s.
  • Solution steps: Use v_e = √(2GM/R), substitute new M and R in terms of Earth's values.

🎙️ This problem helps you understand how escape velocity scales with the mass and radius of a celestial body. It's a good way to practice proportional reasoning.

Kepler's Laws: Unveiling Planetary Orbits

  • 1st Law (Law of Orbits): Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.
  • 2nd Law (Law of Areas): The line joining the planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time.
  • 3rd Law (Law of Periods): The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (T² ∝ a³).

🎙️ Kepler's empirical laws precisely describe how planets move around the Sun. Newton later showed that these laws are a direct consequence of his Law of Universal Gravitation.

Satellites: Orbits and Orbital Velocity

  • Natural (Moon) or artificial objects orbiting a planet.
  • Orbital velocity: v_o = √(GM/r).
  • For a stable orbit, gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force.
  • Orbital period: T = 2πr / v_o.

🎙️ Satellites are crucial for communication, weather forecasting, and navigation. Their orbital velocity depends on the mass of the central body and the radius of their orbit.

Special Orbits: Geostationary and Polar Satellites

  • Geostationary: Appears stationary relative to Earth, period 24h, high altitude (36,000 km), equatorial orbit.
  • Polar: Orbits over poles, low altitude, covers entire Earth surface over time.
  • Different applications based on their unique orbital characteristics.

🎙️ Geostationary satellites are ideal for communication and broadcasting, while polar satellites are used for remote sensing, weather, and surveillance, providing global coverage.

Weightlessness: The Feeling of No Weight

  • Occurs when there is no normal force supporting an object.
  • Not due to zero gravity, but due to free fall.
  • Astronauts in orbit are continuously falling towards Earth.
  • Apparent weight is zero in a freely falling elevator.

🎙️ Weightlessness is a common misconception. It doesn't mean there's no gravity; it means the sensation of weight is absent because you're in a state of continuous free fall, like in an orbiting satellite.
