What is a Fraction?

  • A fraction is a part of a whole thing.
  • When we divide something into equal parts, each part is a fraction.

🎙️ A fraction simply means a 'part' of something 'whole'. Imagine a whole waffle. If you cut it into pieces, each piece becomes a fraction of the whole waffle. Remember, all parts must be equal!

🧇
A whole waffle

The Top and Bottom Numbers

  • Fractions have two numbers.
  • The top number is the 'Numerator'.
  • The bottom number is the 'Denominator'.

🎙️ Every fraction has two special numbers. The number on top tells us 'how many parts we have'. We call it the Numerator. The number at the bottom tells us 'how many total equal parts the whole thing was divided into'. This is the Denominator.

Unit Fractions: One Special Piece

  • A unit fraction has '1' as its numerator.
  • It means we are talking about just one equal part.

🎙️ Sometimes, we talk about just one single part of a whole. When the top number, the numerator, is '1', we call it a Unit Fraction. Like 1/2, 1/3, 1/5. It means one part out of the total equal parts.

Proper Fractions: Less Than a Whole

  • In a proper fraction, the numerator is smaller than the denominator.
  • It means you have less than one whole thing.

🎙️ Imagine you have a chocolate bar with 5 squares. If you eat 2 squares, you have eaten 2/5 of the bar. Here, 2 is smaller than 5. This is a Proper Fraction. It always shows a part that is less than a full whole.

Improper Fractions: More Than a Whole!

  • In an improper fraction, the numerator is equal to or bigger than the denominator.
  • It means you have one whole thing or even more!

🎙️ Now, what if the top number is bigger than or equal to the bottom number? Like 7/4. This is an Improper Fraction. It means you have enough parts to make one whole thing, or even more than one whole thing!

Mixed Fractions: Whole and a Part

  • A mixed fraction has a whole number and a proper fraction together.
  • Like having 2 full cakes and 1/2 of another cake.

🎙️ Sometimes, we have whole things and also parts of another thing. Like if you have 2 whole cookies and half of another cookie. We write this as 2 1/2. This is called a Mixed Fraction – a mix of a whole number and a proper fraction.

🍪🍪🍪

Example: Mixed Fractions

  • 2 whole apples and 1/2 an apple is 2 1/2.
  • 3 full glasses and 1/4 of another glass is 3 1/4.

🎙️ If you have 2 whole cookies and then one more cookie that is cut in half, and you take only one half, you have 2 and a half cookies. This is written as 2 1/2. The '2' is the whole part, and '1/2' is the fractional part.

Equivalent Fractions: Same Value, Different Look

  • Equivalent fractions are fractions that look different but have the same value.
  • Like cutting a cake into 2 halves or 4 quarters – it's still the same amount!

🎙️ Imagine you have a cake. If you cut it into 2 equal pieces and take 1 piece, you have 1/2. If you cut the *same* cake into 4 equal pieces and take 2 pieces, you have 2/4. Both 1/2 and 2/4 mean the same amount of cake! They are equivalent fractions.

How to Find Equivalent Fractions

  • Multiply the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • Example: 1/2 = (1x2)/(2x2) = 2/4.

🎙️ To find an equivalent fraction, you can multiply both the top and bottom numbers by the same number. For example, if you have 1/2, multiply both 1 and 2 by 2. You get 2/4. Both fractions are equal!

Comparing Fractions: Who Has More?

  • When denominators are the same, compare the numerators.
  • The fraction with the bigger numerator is the bigger fraction.

🎙️ Let's say you and your friend both have a chocolate bar cut into 5 equal pieces. You have 3 pieces (3/5) and your friend has 2 pieces (2/5). Since the pieces are the same size, the person with more pieces has the bigger fraction. So, 3/5 is bigger than 2/5.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing numerator and denominator.
  • Not dividing into equal parts.
  • Trying to compare fractions with different denominators (without converting).

🎙️ Sometimes, children make small mistakes. Always remember which number is the numerator and which is the denominator. Also, fractions are only about EQUAL parts. And don't compare fractions with different bottom numbers directly – that's for a higher class!
