What are Fractions?

  • When we share something equally, each part is a fraction.
  • It means a 'part of a whole'.

🎙️ Imagine you have a whole pizza. When you cut it into pieces to share, each piece is a fraction of the whole pizza. Fractions help us talk about these parts.

A pizza cut into slices shows fractions — each slice is a part of the whole!

Sharing Equally is Key!

  • Fractions are only when parts are the same size.
  • No unfair sharing! All parts must be equal.

🎙️ This is super important! For something to be a fraction, all the parts must be exactly the same size. If the pieces are not equal, we cannot call them fractions of the whole.

Two Special Numbers: Numerator

  • The top number is the Numerator.
  • It tells us 'how many parts we have' or 'are taken'.

🎙️ Every fraction has two numbers, one on top and one at the bottom. The number on top is called the Numerator. It counts the number of pieces we are talking about.

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

Denominator: The Total Parts

  • The bottom number is the Denominator.
  • It tells us 'how many equal parts are in the whole thing'.

🎙️ The number at the bottom is called the Denominator. It tells us the total number of equal parts the whole thing was divided into. So, if a pizza has 4 slices, 4 is the denominator.

Pizza Party Fraction!

  • Imagine a pizza cut into 4 equal slices.
  • If you eat 1 slice, you ate 1/4 of the pizza.

🎙️ Here's our pizza! It's cut into 4 equal parts. If you take one part, the numerator is 1. The total parts are 4, so the denominator is 4. You have 1/4 of the pizza!

Chocolate Bar Fun!

  • If you eat 2 pieces, you ate 2/8 of the chocolate.

🎙️ Another yummy example! A chocolate bar has 8 small squares. If you break off and eat 2 of them, you have eaten 2 out of 8 parts. So, you ate 2/8 of the chocolate bar.

How to Write Fractions

  • We write the Numerator on top.
  • We draw a line in the middle.
  • We write the Denominator at the bottom.

🎙️ This is how we put it all together! First, write the number of parts you are talking about (Numerator). Then, draw a small line. Below the line, write the total number of equal parts (Denominator).

Proper Fractions: A Small Piece

  • In proper fractions, the Numerator is smaller than the Denominator.
  • It always represents less than one whole thing.

🎙️ Proper fractions are like taking just a few slices from a pizza. The number of slices you take (numerator) is less than the total number of slices (denominator). For example, 2/5.

Denominator (5)

Improper Fractions: More Than One Whole!

  • In improper fractions, the Numerator is bigger than or equal to the Denominator.
  • It means we have one or more full things, plus maybe some extra parts.

🎙️ Sometimes, you might have more than a whole! Imagine you have 7 slices of pizza, but each whole pizza has only 3 slices. This means you have more than two whole pizzas! This is an improper fraction, like 7/3.

Numerator (7)
Denominator (3)

Mixed Fractions: Whole & Part Together

  • A mixed fraction has a whole number and a proper fraction.
  • It shows whole items and a part of another item, like '2 and a half' cakes.

🎙️ Mixed fractions are a neat way to write improper fractions. They show how many full things you have, and then what part of another thing you have. For example, 2 and 1/3 means two whole things and one-third of another.

Whole Number + Fraction

Improper to Mixed: Sharing Leftovers

  • We can change an improper fraction into a mixed fraction.
  • It's like finding how many whole pizzas you can make, and what slices are left.

🎙️ To change an improper fraction like 7/3, we divide 7 by 3. We get 2 with a remainder of 1. So, it becomes 2 whole and 1/3. Easy, right?

Mixed to Improper: Back to all Parts

  • We can also change a mixed fraction back to an improper fraction.
  • This means counting all the small pieces again.

🎙️ Now, let's go the other way! To change 2 and 1/3 back, we multiply the whole number (2) by the denominator (3), which is 6. Then we add the numerator (1), making it 7. So, it's 7/3!

11. Multiply Whole Number by Denominator (2 x 3 = 6)
22. Add the Numerator (6 + 1 = 7)
33. This is your new Numerator (7)
44. Denominator (3) stays the same (7/3)

Equivalent Fractions: Same Value, Different Look

  • Equivalent fractions look different but mean the same amount.
  • Like 1/2 of a cake is the same as 2/4 of a cake.

🎙️ Imagine you have a cake. If you cut it into 2 pieces and take 1, or cut it into 4 pieces and take 2, you still have the same amount of cake! These are called equivalent fractions.

2/4

How to Find Equivalent Fractions

  • To find equivalent fractions, multiply or divide.
  • Always multiply or divide BOTH Numerator and Denominator by the SAME number.

🎙️ To make equivalent fractions, you must be fair! Whatever you do to the top number, you must do to the bottom number. For example, to get 2/4 from 1/2, you multiply both 1 and 2 by 2.

Comparing Fractions: Same Denominator

  • If denominators are the same, just compare the Numerators.
  • The fraction with the bigger Numerator is the bigger fraction.

🎙️ If two friends are sharing a cake cut into 5 pieces, and one friend gets 2 pieces (2/5) and another gets 3 pieces (3/5), who got more? The one with 3 pieces, because 3 is bigger than 2.

Comparing Fractions: Different Denominators (Simple)

  • When Numerators are the same, the smaller Denominator means a bigger fraction.
  • Example: 1/2 is bigger than 1/4.

🎙️ Imagine you have one chocolate bar. If you share it with 2 friends (1/2 each), each friend gets a bigger piece than if you share it with 4 friends (1/4 each). So, 1/2 is bigger than 1/4.
