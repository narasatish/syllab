What is Weight?

  • Weight tells us how heavy or light something is.
  • Imagine lifting a big stone and a small feather.

🎙️ Weight helps us know if something is heavy, like a big stone, or light, like a tiny feather. It's about how much 'stuff' is in something.

  • We use Kilograms (kg) for heavier things.

🎙️ For things that are quite heavy, we use Kilograms, or 'kg' for short. Think about buying vegetables or carrying your heavy school bag.

Changing Kilograms to Grams

  • 1 Kilogram (kg) is equal to 1000 Grams (g).
  • To change kg to g, we multiply by 1000.

🎙️ Did you know that 1 kilogram is the same as 1000 grams? If you want to know how many grams are in 2 kg, you just multiply 2 by 1000.

Example: Kg to g Conversion

  • How many grams are in 3 kg of apples?
  • 3 kg = 3 x 1000 g = 3000 g.

🎙️ Let's try an example. If you have 3 kilograms of apples, how many grams is that? We multiply 3 by 1000, which gives us 3000 grams.

Adding Weights Together

  • We can add weights just like regular numbers.
  • Make sure units are the same (kg with kg, g with g).

🎙️ We can add weights together! If you buy 2 kg of potatoes and 1 kg of onions, you can add them to find the total weight. Just make sure both are in kg or both in g.

Subtracting Capacities

  • We can subtract capacities too.
  • Always keep units the same (L from L, mL from mL).

🎙️ We can also subtract capacities. If a bottle has 1 Litre of milk and you drink 500 mL, you can subtract to find out how much is left. Remember to keep the units the same.

Recap: What We Learned!

  • Weight: How heavy or light (kg, g).
  • Capacity: How much liquid (L, mL).
  • 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 L = 1000 mL.

🎙️ Let's quickly remember what we learned today. Weight tells us how heavy something is, and we use kg and g. Capacity tells us how much liquid a container holds, using L and mL. And don't forget the magic number 1000 for conversions!
