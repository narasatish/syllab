What is Weight? - Heavy or Light?

  • Weight tells us how heavy or light something is.
  • Think about lifting a feather versus a big rock.
  • Which one is easier to lift?

🎙️ Weight is simply how much 'stuff' is in something, making it heavy or light. A tiny feather is very light, but a big rock is very heavy. We use weight to compare them.

  • We use a weighing scale to find out weight.
  • A scale helps us compare the heaviness of things.
  • The side that goes down is heavier!

🎙️ Just like we use a ruler to measure length, we use a weighing scale to measure weight. When you put things on a balance scale, the heavier item pulls its side down.

The Big Secret: Kilograms and Grams

  • 1 Kilogram is equal to 1000 Grams!
  • This is a very important number to remember.
  • So, 1 kg = 1000 g.

🎙️ This is a super important rule! Imagine 1000 tiny gram weights balancing out one big kilogram weight. Always remember, when you have 1000 grams, you have exactly 1 kilogram.

🔢
1 kg = 1000 g

Converting Kilograms to Grams

  • To change kg to g, we multiply by 1000.
  • Example: 2 kg = ? g
  • 2 x 1000 = 2000 g

🎙️ When you want to know how many grams are in a certain number of kilograms, you simply multiply that number by 1000. It's like having 2 groups, and each group has 1000 members.

Converting Grams to Kilograms

  • To change g to kg, we divide by 1000.
  • Example: 3000 g = ? kg
  • 3000 ÷ 1000 = 3 kg

🎙️ And if you want to go the other way, from grams to kilograms, you do the opposite: you divide by 1000. This helps you figure out how many full kilograms you have from a big number of grams.

2Divide by 1000

Adding Weights: Buying More Fruits

  • Mama bought 1 kg of apples and 500 g of grapes.
  • How much fruit did she buy in total?
  • First, convert 1 kg to 1000 g. Then add: 1000g + 500g = 1500g.

🎙️ When you add weights, make sure they are in the same unit first! It's like adding apples to apples, not apples to oranges. Convert kilograms to grams before adding to grams.

Subtracting Weights: Using Flour

  • You had 2 kg of flour. You used 500 g for a cake.
  • How much flour is left?
  • Convert 2 kg to 2000 g. Then subtract: 2000g - 500g = 1500g.

🎙️ Similarly, when you subtract weights, ensure both quantities are in the same unit. This makes the calculation simple and correct. Always convert to the smaller unit (grams) if different units are involved.

  • 1 Litre is equal to 1000 Millilitres!
  • This is just like kilograms and grams.

🎙️ Here's another important secret! The relationship between litres and millilitres is exactly the same as kilograms and grams. One litre holds 1000 millilitres. Remember this magic number!

Converting Litres to Millilitres

  • To change L to mL, we multiply by 1000.
  • Example: 3 L = ? mL
  • 3 x 1000 = 3000 mL

🎙️ Converting litres to millilitres is just like converting kilograms to grams. You take the number of litres and multiply it by 1000. This tells you how many small millilitre units are in your large litre unit.

Converting Millilitres to Litres

  • To change mL to L, we divide by 1000.
  • Example: 5000 mL = ? L
  • 5000 ÷ 1000 = 5 L

🎙️ And for converting millilitres back to litres, you do the opposite: divide by 1000. This helps you group 1000 millilitres together to form one litre.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing kg with L, or g with mL.
  • Forgetting to multiply/divide by 1000 during conversion.
  • Not converting units before adding or subtracting.

🎙️ Many students make small mistakes like mixing up weight and capacity units, or forgetting the all-important 1000 in conversions. Always double-check your units and your calculations!

Exam Tips and Recap!

  • Weight: how heavy/light. Units: kg, g (1 kg = 1000 g).
  • Capacity: how much liquid. Units: L, mL (1 L = 1000 mL).
  • Always convert to the same unit before adding or subtracting.
  • Practice conversions daily!

🎙️ In exams, you'll get questions on conversions and word problems involving adding or subtracting weights and capacities. Remember the key relationships: 1000 grams in a kilogram, and 1000 millilitres in a litre. Practice makes perfect!
