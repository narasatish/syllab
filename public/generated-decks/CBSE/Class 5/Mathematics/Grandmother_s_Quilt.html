What is Perimeter?

  • Imagine putting a border around your quilt.
  • Perimeter is the total length around a shape.
  • It's like walking along the edge!

🎙️ Think about walking around a park or putting a fence around your garden. The total distance you walk, or the length of the fence, is called the Perimeter. It's the 'outline' of a shape.

Perimeter of a Square Quilt Piece

  • A square has 4 equal sides.
  • To find its perimeter, add all four sides.
  • Formula: Side + Side + Side + Side = 4 x Side

🎙️ Grandma has a square piece of fabric. Since all sides of a square are the same length, we can simply add the length of each side. Or, even faster, multiply one side's length by 4!

Let's Calculate! (Square Perimeter)

  • A square piece has a side of 5 cm.
  • Perimeter = 4 x 5 cm
  • Perimeter = 20 cm

🎙️ If one side of Grandma's square fabric is 5 cm long, how much lace does she need to go around it? We multiply 5 by 4, and we get 20 cm. Easy, right?

1Side = 5 cm
2Formula: 4 x Side

Perimeter of a Rectangular Quilt Piece

  • A rectangle has 4 sides, with opposite sides equal.
  • Add all four sides: Length + Breadth + Length + Breadth.
  • Formula: 2 x (Length + Breadth)

🎙️ Now, what if Grandma has a rectangular piece? Rectangles have two long sides (length) and two short sides (breadth). You can add all four, or use a shortcut: add length and breadth, then multiply by 2!

🟫

Let's Calculate! (Rectangle Perimeter)

  • A rectangular piece is 6 cm long and 4 cm wide.
  • Perimeter = 2 x (6 cm + 4 cm)
  • Perimeter = 2 x 10 cm = 20 cm

🎙️ For a rectangular piece that is 6 cm long and 4 cm wide, we add 6 and 4 to get 10. Then, we multiply by 2, which gives us 20 cm. So, the perimeter is 20 cm.

1Length=6, Breadth=4
2Formula: 2(L+B)
32 x (6+4) = 20 cm

What is Area?

  • Area is the space inside a flat shape.
  • It tells us how much surface the shape covers.
  • Think of how much fabric a quilt piece needs!

🎙️ Now, let's talk about Area. If Perimeter is the boundary, Area is everything inside that boundary. It's the amount of fabric Grandma needs to make the whole piece, not just the edge.

Measuring Area

  • We measure area in 'square units'.
  • Like square centimetres (sq cm) or square metres (sq m).
  • Imagine counting tiny squares that fit inside!

🎙️ When we measure area, we don't just use cm or m. We use 'square' units! Think of it like covering a surface with tiny square tiles. We count how many of those squares fit inside.

Area of a Square Quilt Piece

  • To find the area of a square, multiply its side by itself.
  • Formula: Side x Side
  • Units will be square cm or square m.

🎙️ For a square piece of fabric, to find out how much fabric it is, we multiply the length of one side by itself. Remember, the unit will always be 'square' units!

Let's Calculate! (Square Area)

  • A square piece has a side of 5 cm.
  • Area = 5 cm x 5 cm
  • Area = 25 square cm (or 25 cm²)

🎙️ If Grandma's square fabric piece has a side of 5 cm, its area will be 5 multiplied by 5, which is 25. And don't forget the unit: 25 square centimetres!

1Side = 5 cm
2Formula: Side x Side
35 x 5 = 25 cm²

Area of a Rectangular Quilt Piece

  • To find the area of a rectangle, multiply its length by its breadth.
  • Formula: Length x Breadth
  • Units will be square cm or square m.

🎙️ For a rectangular piece, finding the area is also simple. We just multiply its length by its breadth. This tells us how much fabric is inside the entire rectangle.

Let's Calculate! (Rectangle Area)

  • A rectangular piece is 6 cm long and 4 cm wide.
  • Area = 6 cm x 4 cm
  • Area = 24 square cm (or 24 cm²)

🎙️ If our rectangular fabric piece is 6 cm long and 4 cm wide, its area is 6 multiplied by 4, which equals 24. So, it covers 24 square centimetres of space.

1Length=6, Breadth=4
2Formula: L x B

Perimeter vs. Area: What's the Difference?

  • Perimeter is the boundary, like a fence or border.
  • Area is the space inside, like the grass in a park or the fabric of the quilt.
  • They measure different things!

🎙️ It's important not to confuse Perimeter and Area. Perimeter is just the line around the outside, while Area is the entire flat surface covered by the shape. One is about length, the other about space.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Perimeter and Area formulas.
  • Forgetting to write the correct units (cm vs cm²).
  • Simple calculation errors.

🎙️ Sometimes, students mix up the formulas for perimeter and area, or forget to write 'square cm' for area. Always double-check your work and remember the correct units to avoid these small errors!

Exam Tips for Perimeter & Area
