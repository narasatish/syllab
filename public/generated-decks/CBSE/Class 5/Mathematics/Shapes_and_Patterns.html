What are Shapes?

  • Shapes are how things look.
  • They have outlines and boundaries.
  • Shapes help us understand objects.

🎙️ Think about your favourite toy or a biscuit. It has a special form, right? That form is its shape! Shapes are all around us, from the sun to your lunchbox. They give things their identity.

Shapes are all around us.

Flat Shapes (2D)

  • These shapes are flat, like a drawing.
  • They have length and width.
  • You can draw them on paper.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a picture on your notebook. The shapes you draw, like a square or a circle, are flat. We call them 2D shapes because they only have two dimensions: how long they are and how wide they are.

Meet the Square!

  • A square has 4 straight sides.
  • All its sides are of the same length.
  • It has 4 corners.

🎙️ Look at this happy red square! Can you count its sides? One, two, three, four! And guess what? All its sides are exactly the same size. Think of a carrom board or a slice of bread.

🟥
A red square

Hello, Rectangle!

  • A rectangle also has 4 straight sides.
  • Its opposite sides are equal in length.
  • It has 4 corners.

🎙️ Next, we have the blue rectangle! It also has four sides and four corners, just like a square. But for a rectangle, only the sides opposite to each other are equal. Think of your classroom door or a chocolate bar.

🟦
A blue rectangle

The Tricky Triangle!

  • A triangle has 3 straight sides.
  • It has 3 corners.
  • Tri means three!

🎙️ Here comes the triangle! The word 'tri' means three, so a triangle has three sides and three corners. Can you spot triangles on a party hat or a slice of pizza? They are everywhere!

🔼
A green triangle

Round and Round, the Circle!

  • A circle has no straight sides.
  • It has no corners.
  • It is perfectly round.

🎙️ The circle is special! It has no straight sides and no corners. It's perfectly round, like a bangle or a wheel. Can you draw a perfect circle with your finger in the air?

Solid Shapes (3D)

  • These shapes are not flat, they are 'fat'!
  • They have length, width, and height.
  • You can hold them in your hand.

🎙️ Now, let's talk about shapes you can hold! These are called solid or 3D shapes. They are not flat like drawings; they take up space. Think of a ball or a brick. You can feel their thickness or height.

📦
A solid box

Meet the Cube!

  • A cube is like a 3D square.
  • It has 6 flat faces.
  • All its faces are squares.

🎙️ The cube is a very common 3D shape! Imagine a dice or an ice cube. All its sides, called faces, are perfect squares. It has six faces, just like a gift box.

🧊
A cool cube

Hello, Cuboid!

  • A cuboid is like a 3D rectangle.
  • It also has 6 flat faces.
  • Its faces are rectangles (or squares).

🎙️ The cuboid is a cousin of the cube! Think of a brick or a shoebox. Its faces are usually rectangles, but some can be squares too. It also has six faces, just like a cube.

🧱
A strong cuboid

The Rolling Cylinder!

  • A cylinder has two flat circular faces.
  • It has one curved surface.
  • It can roll easily!

🎙️ Look at the cylinder! It has a round top and a round bottom, like a tin can or a water bottle. The middle part is curved, which is why it can roll so easily! Try rolling a can of juice.

🥫
A tin can

The Bouncing Sphere!

  • A sphere is perfectly round.
  • It has no flat faces or corners.
  • It can roll in any direction!

🎙️ The sphere is the roundest of all! Think of a football or a globe. It has no flat parts at all, which means it can roll in any direction you push it. It's a truly amazing shape!

A round ball

What are Patterns?

  • Patterns are things that repeat.
  • They follow a rule.
  • They can be with shapes, colours, or numbers.

🎙️ Now, let's talk about patterns! Imagine a beautiful design on your mother's saree or a row of flowers in a garden. Patterns are like a secret code where things repeat in an organised way. They make things look beautiful and interesting.

✨
A sparkling pattern

Repeating Patterns

  • Something repeats again and again.
  • Example: Red, Blue, Red, Blue...
  • Example: Square, Circle, Square, Circle...

🎙️ A repeating pattern is like a song that plays the same chorus over and over. Once you know the rule, you can guess what comes next! For example, if you see a pattern of 'star, moon, star, moon,' what would come next?

🔁
A repeating arrow
17 / 20

Growing Patterns

  • Something changes or grows each time.
  • Example: 1, 2, 3, 4...
  • Example: One apple, two apples, three apples...

🎙️ Growing patterns are a bit different! They don't just repeat; they change or grow according to a rule. Like counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4... Each number is one more than the previous one. Can you think of other growing patterns?
