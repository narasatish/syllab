What is a Jump?

  • A jump is when an animal moves from one spot to another.
  • It lifts its body off the ground and lands somewhere else.
  • The path it covers is called 'distance'.

🎙️ Think about when you jump! You start at one point and land at another. That space you cover, from where you started to where you landed, is what we call a jump's distance.

How Do We Measure Jumps?

  • To know how long a jump is, we measure its 'distance'.
  • Distance is how far something travels from its start to end.
  • We use special tools like a measuring tape or ruler.

🎙️ Just like we measure the length of your pencil or the height of a table, we can measure how far an animal jumps. We need a way to put a number to that length.

Units for Measuring Jumps: Metre and Centimetre

  • For small jumps, we use 'centimetres' (cm).
  • For bigger jumps, we use 'metres' (m).
  • Remember, 1 metre is equal to 100 centimetres!

🎙️ Imagine a tiny ant's jump – we'd use centimetres. But for a kangaroo's giant leap, metres are much better! A metre is like 100 small centimetre steps put together.

Comparing Jumps: Who Jumps Farther?

  • We can compare the distances different animals jump.
  • The animal with the bigger number jumps 'farther' or 'longer'.
  • The animal with the smaller number jumps 'shorter' or 'less'.

🎙️ If a frog jumps 10 cm and a rabbit jumps 50 cm, we can easily see who jumped more. Comparing helps us understand which jump is the biggest or smallest.

Adding Jumps: What is the Total Distance?

  • Sometimes an animal makes more than one jump.
  • To find the 'total distance', we add all the jump lengths together.
  • We use the plus sign (+) for adding.

🎙️ Imagine a squirrel jumping from one branch to another, and then to a third branch. To know how far it travelled in total, we just add the length of each jump.

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

Example 2: Kangaroo's Two Jumps

  • A kangaroo jumps 5 metres, then another 3 metres.
  • What is the total distance it jumped?
  • Step 1: Write the jumps. Step 2: Add them!

🎙️ The kangaroo made two separate jumps. To find out the complete distance it covered, we simply add the length of the first jump to the length of the second jump. So, 5 + 3 equals 8 metres.

1First jump: 5 m
2Second jump: 3 m
3Total jump: 5 m + 3 m = 8 m

Multiplying Jumps: Many Same Jumps!

2Jumps backward: 2 m

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Forgetting units: Always write 'cm' or 'm' with your answer.
  • Mixing up operations: Read carefully if you need to add, subtract, or multiply.
  • Not reading the question fully: Understand what the question is asking.

🎙️ These are small things that can make a big difference in your answers. Always double-check your units and make sure you're doing the correct math operation for the problem.
