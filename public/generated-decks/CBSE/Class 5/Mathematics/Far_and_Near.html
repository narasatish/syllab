What does "Far" mean?

  • 'Far' means a long distance away.
  • It takes a lot of time to reach.
  • Like your home to another city.

🎙️ Imagine your friend lives in another city. To visit them, you might need to take a train or a bus for many hours. That's what we call 'far'.

What does "Near" mean?

  • 'Near' means a short distance away.
  • It takes less time to reach.
  • Like your home to the shop next door.

🎙️ Now, think about the small shop just outside your house. You can walk there in just a few minutes. That's 'near'. It's very close by.

Comparing Distances

  • We compare how long the path is.
  • A longer path means 'far'.
  • A shorter path means 'near'.

🎙️ Sometimes, we just look at two things and compare. If one path looks much longer than the other, we know it's farther away. It's like comparing two different roads.

When "Far" and "Near" are not enough

  • Just saying 'far' or 'near' is not always exact.
  • We need to know *how* far.
  • This is where measurement helps us!

🎙️ What if you need to buy a curtain for your window? You can't just say 'it's a near size'. You need an exact number! That's why we measure.

Why Body Parts Don't Work

  • Everyone's hands and feet are different sizes.
  • My hand is smaller than your dad's hand!
  • We need a standard way to measure.

🎙️ If I measure a table with my hand and you measure it with your hand, we might get different numbers! This is confusing. We need everyone to agree on one size.

For Smaller Things: Centimetre

  • For very small lengths, we use 'centimetre'.
  • We write 'centimetre' as 'cm'.
  • Like measuring a pencil or your finger.

🎙️ What if you want to measure something tiny, like your eraser or a pencil? A metre is too big! For these, we use a smaller unit called a centimetre.

A clock helps us read hours and minutes.

Metre and Centimetre Link

  • There are 100 centimetres in 1 metre!
  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • Imagine 100 small 'cm' pieces making one big 'm'.

🎙️ This is very important to remember! If you line up 100 small centimetre rulers, end to end, they would make one long metre. So, 1 metre is equal to 100 centimetres.

Kilometre and Metre Link

  • There are 1000 metres in 1 kilometre!
  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • A kilometre is like a thousand steps of a metre!

🎙️ Just like 100 cm make 1 m, a very large number of metres make a kilometre. You need 1000 metres to make just one kilometre! So, 1 km is a really long distance.

Watch Out for These Mistakes!

  • Forgetting 1 m = 100 cm
  • Forgetting 1 km = 1000 m
  • Using the wrong unit for a measurement

🎙️ Many students sometimes mix up how many centimetres are in a metre, or how many metres are in a kilometre. Always remember these key conversion numbers. Also, don't use cm for a distance that should be in km!

How to Score Well in Exams

  • Read questions carefully: 'far/near' or exact length?
  • Practice converting units (m to cm, km to m).
  • Always write the correct unit with your answer!

🎙️ In exams, pay close attention to what the question asks. Does it want you to just say 'far' or 'near', or does it ask for an exact measurement? And always, always write 'cm', 'm', or 'km' with your number!

Let's Recap What We Learned!

  • 'Far' is a long distance, 'Near' is a short distance.
  • We use standard units for exact measurement.
  • Metre (m), Centimetre (cm), Kilometre (km) are units.
  • 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1000 m.

🎙️ We covered a lot today! Remember, 'far' and 'near' tell us about distance. And for exact distances, we use our special units: cm for small, m for medium, and km for very long distances. And don't forget their magical connections!
