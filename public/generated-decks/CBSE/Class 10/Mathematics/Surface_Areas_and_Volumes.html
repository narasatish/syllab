🎙️ Volume, on the other hand, deals with three-dimensional objects. It tells us how much 'stuff' can fit inside an object, like water in a bottle or air in a balloon. We use cubic units as we are dealing with length, width, and height.

Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • A cuboid has 6 rectangular faces.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl)
  • Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 2(l+b)h (Area of 4 walls)

🎙️ A cuboid is like a brick or a shoebox. To find its total surface area, we add the areas of all six faces. The lateral surface area is the area of just the four side walls, excluding the top and bottom.

Worked Example: Cuboid TSA

  • Find TSA of a cuboid: L=10cm, B=5cm, H=3cm.
  • TSA = 2(10×5 + 5×3 + 3×10)
  • TSA = 2(50 + 15 + 30) = 2(95) = 190 cm²

🎙️ Let's apply the formula to a simple example. Always remember to substitute the values carefully and follow the order of operations. The final answer must include the correct square units.

Surface Area of a Cube

  • A cube is a special cuboid with all sides equal (s).
  • All 6 faces are identical squares.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6s²
  • Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 4s²

🎙️ A cube is a very symmetrical shape, like a dice. Since all its faces are identical squares, calculating its surface area becomes simpler. We just find the area of one face and multiply by six for TSA, or four for LSA.

Worked Example: Cube TSA

  • Find TSA of a cube with side length 7 cm.
  • TSA = 6 × (side)²
  • TSA = 6 × (7)² = 6 × 49 = 294 cm²

🎙️ Here's a quick calculation for a cube. Notice how the formula simplifies the process compared to a cuboid. Remember to square the side length before multiplying by 6.

Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • Has two circular bases and a curved surface.
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r)

🎙️ A cylinder resembles a can or a pipe. Its curved surface area is like unrolling the label of a can into a rectangle. The total surface area includes this curved part plus the areas of the two circular bases.

Worked Example: Cylinder TSA

  • Find TSA of a cylinder: r=7cm, h=10cm (Use π=22/7).
  • TSA = 2πr(h+r) = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (10+7)
  • TSA = 2 × 22 × 17 = 44 × 17 = 748 cm²

🎙️ Let's calculate the total surface area for a cylinder. Notice how 'r' cancels out with the '7' in 22/7, simplifying the calculation. Always remember to include the units in your final answer.

Volume of a Cuboid

  • Volume is the product of its length, breadth, and height.
  • Volume (V) = length × breadth × height = l × b × h
  • Measured in cubic units.

🎙️ Now let's move on to volume. For a cuboid, finding its volume is as simple as multiplying its three dimensions. This tells us how much space it occupies or how much it can hold.

Volume of a Cylinder

  • Volume (V) = Area of base × height
  • Area of circular base = πr²
  • Volume (V) = πr²h

🎙️ For a cylinder, the volume is found by multiplying the area of its circular base by its height. This formula is widely used for containers like bottles, pipes, and drums to calculate their capacity.
