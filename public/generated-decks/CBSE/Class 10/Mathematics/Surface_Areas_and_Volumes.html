Understanding Surface Area

  • The total area of all the faces (surfaces) of a 3D object.
  • Imagine painting the outside of a box.
  • Measured in square units (cm², m²).

🎙️ Surface area is like wrapping a gift or painting a wall. It's the total space covered by the outer layer of any three-dimensional object. We measure it in square units because it's essentially a sum of 2D areas.

Understanding Volume

  • The amount of space a 3D object occupies.
  • Imagine filling a box with water or sand.
  • Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³).

🎙️ Volume, on the other hand, tells us how much 'stuff' a 3D object can hold or how much space it takes up. Think of a water bottle – its volume tells us how much water it can contain. We measure volume in cubic units.

Surface Area of a Cuboid

  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = sum of areas of all 6 faces.
  • Faces: 2(l x b), 2(b x h), 2(l x h).
  • TSA = 2(lb + bh + lh) square units.

🎙️ To find the total surface area of a cuboid, we simply add up the areas of all its six rectangular faces. Since opposite faces are identical, we have two pairs of (length x breadth), two pairs of (breadth x height), and two pairs of (length x height). This gives us the formula 2(lb + bh + lh).

Example: Cuboid Surface Area

  • A box is 10cm long, 5cm wide, 3cm high.
  • TSA = 2(10x5 + 5x3 + 10x3)

Surface Area of a Cube

  • All 6 faces are squares with side 'a'.
  • Area of one face = a².
  • TSA = 6a² square units.

🎙️ Since a cube is a special cuboid where all sides are equal, calculating its surface area becomes simpler. Each of its six faces is a square with side 'a', so the area of one face is a². Therefore, the total surface area is simply 6 times a².

Surface Area of a Cylinder

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh.
  • Area of two circular bases = 2πr².
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = CSA + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r) square units.

🎙️ A cylinder has two parts for its surface area: the curved side and the two circular bases. The curved surface area is like unrolling a label from a can, which forms a rectangle with length 2πr (circumference) and height h. Adding the areas of the two circular bases (πr² each) gives us the total surface area formula.

Example: Cylinder Surface Area

  • A cylinder has radius 7 cm, height 10 cm.
  • CSA = 2π(7)(10) = 140π cm².
  • TSA = 2π(7)(10+7) = 14π(17) = 238π cm².

Volume of a Cuboid

  • Volume = Area of base x height.
  • Base area = length x breadth (lb).
  • Volume = l x b x h cubic units.

🎙️ Now, let's move to volume. For a cuboid, its volume is found by multiplying its length, breadth, and height. Imagine the base as a rectangle, and then stacking layers of that rectangle up to its height. Each layer contributes to the total space occupied.

Volume of a Cube

  • All dimensions are equal (a).
  • Volume = a x a x a.
  • Volume = a³ cubic units.

🎙️ For a cube, since all its sides are equal, calculating the volume is even simpler. You just multiply the side length by itself three times, or 'a cubed'. This gives us the total space enclosed by the cube.

Volume of a Cylinder

  • Volume = Area of base x height.
  • Base is a circle: Area = πr².
  • Volume = πr²h cubic units.

🎙️ Similar to a cuboid, the volume of a cylinder is the area of its base multiplied by its height. Since the base is a circle, its area is πr². Therefore, the volume of a cylinder is simply πr²h. Imagine stacking circular discs to form the cylinder.
