Revisiting Circle Basics

  • A circle is a set of all points equidistant from a central point.
  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on the circle.
  • Diameter (d): Distance across the circle through the center (d = 2r).
  • Circumference: The perimeter or distance around the circle (C = 2πr).

🎙️ Before we dive into new concepts, let's quickly refresh our memory on the fundamental terms related to a circle. Remember, these terms are crucial for understanding everything ahead.

Understanding Area of a Circle

  • The space occupied by the circle in a two-dimensional plane.
  • Formula: Area (A) = πr² square units.
  • π (pi) is a constant, approximately 22/7 or 3.14.

🎙️ The area of a circle tells us how much surface it covers. Imagine painting the inside of a circular disk; the amount of paint needed depends on its area. The formula πr² is fundamental.

Exploring Sectors of a Circle

  • A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • Imagine cutting a slice of cake or pizza – that's a sector!
  • Minor Sector: The smaller part. Major Sector: The larger part.

🎙️ Now, let's look at parts of a circle. A sector is like a 'slice' of the circle. It's defined by two radii and the curved edge between them. We differentiate between the smaller and larger slices.

Calculating Area of a Sector

  • The area of a sector depends on the angle it subtends at the center (θ).
  • Formula: Area of Sector = (θ / 360°) × πr² square units.
  • This formula is a fraction of the total circle's area.

🎙️ The area of a sector is a fraction of the total circle's area. This fraction is determined by the central angle, θ, out of the full 360 degrees. If the angle is 90 degrees, it's a quarter of the circle's area.

Measuring Length of an Arc

  • An arc is a continuous portion of the circumference of a circle.
  • The length of an arc depends on the central angle (θ) and the radius (r).
  • Formula: Length of Arc = (θ / 360°) × 2πr units.

🎙️ Just as we can find the area of a sector, we can also find the length of its curved boundary, which is called an arc. This length is also a fraction of the total circumference, determined by the central angle.

Worked Example: Sector & Arc

  • A circle has a radius of 6 cm. Find the area of the sector and the length of the arc formed by a central angle of 60°.
  • Area = (60/360) * π * 6² = 6π cm².
  • Arc Length = (60/360) * 2 * π * 6 = 2π cm.

Unveiling Segments of a Circle

  • A segment is a region of a circle bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • Imagine cutting a straight line across a pizza slice – the piece remaining is a segment.
  • Minor Segment: The smaller region. Major Segment: The larger region.

🎙️ Moving on from sectors, let's understand segments. A segment is formed when you draw a straight line, called a chord, across the circle. It's different from a sector because it doesn't involve the center.

Calculating Area of a Segment

  • Area of Minor Segment = Area of Minor Sector - Area of Triangle formed by radii and chord.
  • Area of Triangle = (1/2) * r² * sin(θ) (for angle θ in degrees).
  • Area of Major Segment = Area of Circle - Area of Minor Segment.

🎙️ The key to finding the area of a segment is to realize it's the area of the sector minus the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord. Remember your trigonometry for the triangle area!

Worked Example: Segment Area

  • Find the area of the minor segment of a circle with radius 14 cm and central angle 90°. (π=22/7)
  • Area of Sector = (90/360) * (22/7) * 14² = 154 cm².
  • Area of Triangle = (1/2) * 14 * 14 = 98 cm².
  • Area of Segment = 154 - 98 = 56 cm².

Worked Example: Square & Circle

  • A circle is inscribed in a square of side 14 cm. Find the area of the unshaded region.
  • Side of square = 14 cm. Area of Square = 14² = 196 cm².
  • Diameter of circle = 14 cm, so radius = 7 cm. Area of Circle = π * 7² = 154 cm².

Worked Example: Touching Circles

  • Two circles with radii 3.5 cm and 7 cm touch externally. Find the sum of their areas.
  • Area 1 = π * (3.5)² = 12.25π cm².

Area of a Circular Path or Ring

  • A circular path is formed between two concentric circles (circles with the same center).
  • This shape is also called an annulus or a ring.
  • Formula: Area of Path = Area of Outer Circle - Area of Inner Circle = π(R² - r²).

🎙️ Imagine a running track or a circular garden path. These are examples of a circular path or a ring. To find its area, we simply subtract the area of the inner circle from the area of the outer circle.
