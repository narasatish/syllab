Revisiting Circles: The Basics

  • A circle is a set of points equidistant from a central point.
  • The distance from the center to any point on the circle is the radius (r).
  • The distance across the circle through its center is the diameter (d = 2r).

🎙️ Before diving into new concepts, let's quickly revise what we already know about circles. Remember, the radius is key to all our calculations, and the diameter is simply twice the radius.

Area of a Circle: The Magic Formula

  • The area is the space enclosed by the circle.
  • Formula: Area (A) = πr²
  • π (pi) is a constant, approximately 22/7 or 3.14.

🎙️ The area of a circle tells us how much surface it covers. This formula, A = πr², is fundamental and you will use it repeatedly. Always remember the approximate values of pi for calculations.

Circumference of a Circle: The Boundary Line

  • Circumference is the distance around the circle.
  • It's like the perimeter of a polygon.
  • Formula: Circumference (C) = 2πr or πd.

🎙️ While area measures the space inside, circumference measures the length of the boundary of the circle. This formula is crucial when we need to calculate distances travelled along a circular path.

Area of a Sector: The Formula Unpacked

  • The area of a sector is a fraction of the total circle's area.
  • The fraction is determined by the central angle (θ).
  • Formula: Area of Sector = (θ/360°) × πr²

🎙️ Since a sector is just a part of the whole circle, its area is a proportional part of the circle's total area. The ratio 'theta over 360 degrees' represents this proportion, multiplied by the full circle's area.

Worked Example: Finding Sector Area

  • Given: Radius (r) = 7 cm, Central Angle (θ) = 60°
  • Apply formula: Area = (60/360) × (22/7) × 7²
  • Calculation: (1/6) × 22 × 7 = 25.67 cm² (approx)

Understanding Segments: A Part Apart

  • A segment is a region of a circle bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • It's like the crustless part of a pizza slice.
  • There can be a minor segment and a major segment.

🎙️ Now, let's explore segments. Unlike a sector, which includes the center, a segment is cut off by a chord. Think of it as the area remaining after you remove a triangular part from a sector.

💧
Circle Segment

Area of a Segment: Sector minus Triangle

  • Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle formed by radii and chord.
  • Area of Triangle = (1/2)r² sinθ (for angle θ in degrees).
  • This applies for minor segments.

🎙️ To find the area of a segment, we visualize it as a sector from which a triangle has been removed. The area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord is subtracted from the area of the corresponding sector.

Worked Example: Area of a Segment

  • Given: r = 10 cm, θ = 90°
  • Area of Sector = (90/360) × π(10)² = (1/4) × 3.14 × 100 = 78.5 cm²
  • Area of Triangle = (1/2) × 10 × 10 = 50 cm²
  • Area of Segment = 78.5 - 50 = 28.5 cm²

🎙️ Here's a step-by-step example for finding the area of a segment. Notice how we first calculate the area of the entire sector, then the area of the triangle within it, and finally subtract to find the segment's area.

Arc Length: Measuring the Curve

  • The arc length is the length of the curved boundary of a sector.
  • It's a fraction of the total circumference of the circle.
  • Formula: Arc Length (L) = (θ/360°) × 2πr

🎙️ Just as we found the area of a sector, we can also find the length of its curved edge, which is called the arc length. This formula is very similar to the area of a sector, but it uses the circumference instead of the area of the circle.
