Degree Matters: Linear vs. Quadratic

  • Linear: Highest power is 1.
  • Quadratic: Highest power is 2.

🎙️ The 'degree' of a polynomial is its highest power. If the highest power of the variable is 1, it's a linear polynomial. But if the highest power is 2, it's a quadratic polynomial, which is our focus today!

What is a Quadratic Equation?

  • An equation of degree 2.
  • Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0.
  • 'a' cannot be zero.

🎙️ A quadratic equation is simply a quadratic polynomial set equal to zero. The 'a' coefficient is crucial; if 'a' were zero, the x² term would vanish, and it would no longer be quadratic.

Finding Roots by Factorisation

  • Split the middle term 'bx'.
  • Group terms to find common factors.
  • Set each factor to zero to find roots.

🎙️ Factorisation is a common method to solve quadratic equations. We try to rewrite the middle term 'bx' in such a way that we can factor out common terms from the expression.

1Standard Form
2Split Middle Term
3Factor by Grouping
4Set Factors to Zero
10 / 25

Example: x² + 5x + 6 = 0

  • Split 5x into 2x + 3x.
  • Factor (x+2)(x+3)=0.
  • Roots are x = -2, x = -3.

🎙️ Here, we need two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5. These are 2 and 3. Once we split the middle term, we can factor by grouping to get our two factors, and then easily find the roots.

1x²+2x+3x+6=0
2x(x+2)+3(x+2)=0
3(x+2)(x+3)=0
4x=-2 or x=-3
11 / 25

Example: 2x² - 5x + 3 = 0

  • Product ac = 6. Sum b = -5.
  • Split -5x into -2x - 3x.
  • Roots are x = 1, x = 3/2.

🎙️ In this example, 'a' is not 1. We look for two numbers whose product is (a*c) = 6 and whose sum is 'b' = -5. These numbers are -2 and -3. Follow the same steps to find the roots.

12x²-2x-3x+3=0
22x(x-1)-3(x-1)=0
3(2x-3)(x-1)=0
4x=3/2 or x=1

Completing the Square Method

  • Transform into (x+k)² = d form.
  • Add (b/2a)² to both sides.
  • Take square root to solve.

🎙️ This method is a bit more involved but is very powerful. The goal is to manipulate the equation so that one side becomes a perfect square trinomial, making it easier to isolate 'x'.

1Move 'c' term
2Divide by 'a'
3Add (b/2)² to both sides
4Factor left side
5Take square root
14 / 25

Example: x² + 4x - 5 = 0

  • x² + 4x = 5.
  • Add (4/2)² = 4 to both sides.
  • (x+2)² = 9.
  • Roots are x = 1, x = -5.

🎙️ Here, 'a' is 1, which simplifies things. We move the constant term, then add the square of half the coefficient of 'x' to both sides to complete the square. Then, we take the square root.

1x²+4x = 5
2x²+4x+4 = 5+4
3(x+2)² = 9
4x+2 = ±3
5x=1 or x=-5

Introducing the Quadratic Formula

  • A universal formula to find roots.
  • Works for ALL quadratic equations.
  • Derived from completing the square.

🎙️ This is perhaps the most famous method! The quadratic formula is a direct way to find the roots of any quadratic equation, no matter how complex it seems. It's a lifesaver when factorisation or completing the square are difficult.

➗
Universal Solution

The Mighty Quadratic Formula!

  • x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a.
  • 'a', 'b', 'c' are coefficients from ax² + bx + c = 0.

🎙️ Memorise this formula! It gives you the two roots of a quadratic equation directly. Just identify the values of 'a', 'b', and 'c' from your equation, plug them into the formula, and calculate.

✨
The Magic Formula

Understanding the Nature of Roots

  • The term b² - 4ac is called the Discriminant (D).
  • D tells us about the type of roots.

🎙️ The part under the square root in the quadratic formula, b² - 4ac, is very important! It's called the Discriminant, and its value tells us whether the roots are real or not, and if they are distinct or equal.

🤔
What Kind of Roots?
21 / 25

Two Distinct Real Roots (D > 0)

  • If D is positive (D > 0).
  • Two different real numbers as roots.
  • The parabola intersects x-axis at two points.

🎙️ When the discriminant is greater than zero, it means we have a positive number under the square root. This results in two distinct, or different, real number solutions for 'x'.

✌️✌️
Two Different Solutions
22 / 25

Two Equal Real Roots (D = 0)

  • If D is zero (D = 0).
  • Both roots are the same real number.
  • The parabola touches x-axis at one point.

🎙️ If the discriminant is exactly zero, the square root term vanishes. This means both parts of the formula (with + and -) yield the same result, giving us two equal real roots.

🤝🤝
Two Identical Solutions
23 / 25

No Real Roots (D < 0)

  • If D is negative (D < 0).
  • No real number solutions.
  • The parabola does not intersect x-axis.

🎙️ When the discriminant is less than zero, we have a negative number under the square root. In real numbers, we cannot take the square root of a negative number, so there are no real roots in this case.

🚫
No Real Solutions
