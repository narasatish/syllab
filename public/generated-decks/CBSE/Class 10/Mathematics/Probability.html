What is Probability?

  • Measures how likely an event is to occur.
  • Expressed as a fraction, decimal, or percentage.
  • Ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).

🎙️ Probability is all about understanding chance. It gives us a numerical way to describe how likely something is to happen, like whether it will rain tomorrow or if you'll win a game.

  • Can be repeated under similar conditions.
  • Examples: Tossing a coin, rolling a die.

🎙️ In probability, an 'experiment' is any process where we observe something. It's like a scientific experiment, but here we are interested in the different possible results.

Key Terms: Sample Space

  • The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.
  • Represented by 'S' or 'Ω'.
  • Example: For a coin toss, S = {Heads, Tails}.

🎙️ The 'sample space' is like a complete list of everything that could possibly happen in an experiment. It's crucial to identify all possible outcomes before calculating probability.

Key Terms: Event

  • One or more outcomes of an experiment.
  • A subset of the sample space.
  • Example: Rolling an even number on a die (2, 4, 6).

🎙️ An 'event' is what we are interested in. It could be a single outcome, or a group of outcomes. For instance, if you want to know the probability of rolling an even number, that's your event.

The Probability Formula

  • P(E) = (Number of Favorable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Outcomes)
  • P(E) is the probability of event E.
  • Favorable outcomes are those that satisfy the event.

🎙️ This formula is the heart of probability! It's simple yet powerful. To find the probability of any event, you just need to count how many ways it can happen and divide by the total number of things that could happen.

The Probability Scale

  • Probability values are always between 0 and 1.
  • 0 means the event is impossible.
  • 1 means the event is certain to happen.

🎙️ Think of probability as a scale from 0 to 1. An event that can never happen has a probability of 0. An event that will definitely happen has a probability of 1. Most events fall somewhere in between.

Impossible Event

  • An event that cannot happen.
  • Its probability is 0.
  • Example: Rolling a 7 on a standard six-sided die.

🎙️ Some things just can't happen. Like trying to get a score of 7 on a single roll of a standard die – it's impossible, so its probability is zero. There are no favorable outcomes for such an event.

Certain Event

  • An event that is sure to happen.
  • Its probability is 1.
  • Example: The sun rising tomorrow.

🎙️ On the other hand, some events are guaranteed. The sun will rise tomorrow – that's a certain event. In such cases, all possible outcomes are favorable outcomes, making the probability 1.

Complementary Events

  • Two events are complementary if one happens if and only if the other does not.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • P(not E) is also written as P(E').

🎙️ Complementary events are like two sides of a coin: if one happens, the other can't, and vice-versa. The sum of their probabilities is always 1, because between them, one is sure to happen.

🔄
Opposite

Example: Not Getting a 6

  • Experiment: Rolling a die.
  • Event E: Getting a 6. P(E) = 1/6.
  • Event E': Not getting a 6. P(E') = 1 - P(E).

🎙️ If the probability of rolling a 6 is 1/6, then the probability of NOT rolling a 6 is simply 1 minus 1/6. This makes calculations easier, especially when 'not E' has many favorable outcomes.

Example: Not Drawing a King

  • Using complementary events for drawing a King:
  • P(King) = 4/52 = 1/13.
  • P(Not a King) = 1 - P(King) = 1 - 1/13 = 12/13.

🎙️ This is where complementary events are very useful. Instead of counting all the cards that are NOT Kings (which is 48), we can simply subtract the probability of drawing a King from 1.
