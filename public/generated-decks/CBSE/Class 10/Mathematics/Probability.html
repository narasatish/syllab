The Probability Formula: Quantifying Chance

  • P(E) = (Number of Favourable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Possible Outcomes)
  • Outcomes must be equally likely
  • P(E) is the probability of event E

🎙️ The classical probability of an event E is calculated by dividing the number of outcomes that are favourable to E by the total number of possible outcomes of the experiment. This formula assumes all outcomes are equally likely.

Probability Calculation

Example: Tossing a Coin

  • Experiment: Tossing a fair coin
  • Total possible outcomes: Head, Tail (2)
  • Favourable outcome (for 'Head'): Head (1)
  • P(Head) = 1/2

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. When you toss a fair coin, there are two possible outcomes: Head or Tail. If we want to find the probability of getting a Head, there's only one favourable outcome. So, P(Head) is 1 divided by 2.

Example: Rolling a Die

  • Experiment: Rolling a standard die
  • Total possible outcomes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (6)
  • Favourable outcomes (for 'even number'): 2, 4, 6 (3)
  • P(Even number) = 3/6 = 1/2

🎙️ Now, consider rolling a standard six-sided die. What are all the possible outcomes? There are 6. If we're interested in getting an even number, our favourable outcomes are 2, 4, and 6. So, the probability is 3 favourable outcomes out of 6 total outcomes.

Sure Event: It Will Definitely Happen!

  • An event that is certain to occur
  • Its probability is always 1
  • Example: Getting a number less than 7 on a die roll

🎙️ A sure event, also called a certain event, is an event that will always happen. Its probability is always 1. For example, when you roll a standard die, getting a number less than 7 is a sure event because all possible outcomes (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) are less than 7.

Impossible Event: No Chance at All!

  • An event that can never occur
  • Its probability is always 0
  • Example: Getting an 8 on a standard die roll

🎙️ An impossible event is an event that can never happen. Its probability is always 0. For example, if you roll a standard six-sided die, the probability of getting an 8 is 0, because 8 is not a possible outcome.

Probability Range: From 0 to 1

  • Probability of any event E is P(E)
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1
  • Cannot be negative, cannot be greater than 1

🎙️ The probability of any event E always lies between 0 and 1, inclusive. P(E) = 0 for an impossible event, and P(E) = 1 for a sure event. If you calculate a probability outside this range, you've made a mistake!

Complementary Events: What Doesn't Happen

  • E' (E-bar) is the event that E does NOT happen
  • P(E) + P(E') = 1
  • If it rains, it doesn't rain (one or the other)

🎙️ The complement of an event E, often denoted as E-bar or E', is the event that E does not happen. The sum of the probability of an event and its complement is always 1. This relationship is very useful for solving problems.

Opposite events

Example: Not Drawing an Ace

  • Total cards: 52
  • Aces: 4
  • P(Drawing an Ace) = 4/52 = 1/13
  • P(Not Drawing an Ace) = 1 - P(Drawing an Ace) = 1 - 1/13 = 12/13

🎙️ If you draw a card from a standard deck, what is the probability of not drawing an Ace? First, find the probability of drawing an Ace. Then, use the complementary event formula to find the probability of not drawing an Ace.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Incorrectly counting total outcomes
  • Missing favourable outcomes (e.g., in card problems)
  • Calculating probability outside 0-1 range
  • Confusing 'at least' with 'exactly'

🎙️ Students often make mistakes like incorrectly counting total or favourable outcomes, especially with cards or multiple dice. Always double-check your counts. Also, remember that probability cannot be less than 0 or greater than 1, so if your answer is outside this range, something is wrong!
