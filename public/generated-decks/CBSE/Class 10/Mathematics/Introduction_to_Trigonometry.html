What is Trigonometry?

The Foundation: Right-Angled Triangles

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees
  • The side opposite the 90° angle is the Hypotenuse
  • All basic trigonometry starts here

🎙️ The entire world of basic trigonometry revolves around a very special shape: the right-angled triangle. Remember, it's a triangle with one angle that measures exactly 90 degrees.

Naming the Sides

  • Hypotenuse: Always opposite the 90° angle
  • Opposite: Side opposite to the chosen reference angle (θ)
  • Adjacent: Side next to the chosen reference angle (θ) (not the hypotenuse)

🎙️ In a right triangle, we name the sides based on their position relative to the 90-degree angle AND a chosen reference angle. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle.

Introducing Trigonometric Ratios

  • Special ratios of side lengths of a right triangle
  • These ratios relate angles to side lengths
  • There are three primary ratios: Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

🎙️ Now, imagine we take the lengths of these sides and divide them. These special divisions create what we call 'trigonometric ratios.' These ratios connect the angles of a right triangle to the lengths of its sides.

Remembering the Ratios: SOH CAH TOA

  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ To make remembering these ratios easy, we use a fun mnemonic: SOH CAH TOA! This simple phrase helps you recall how each ratio is formed. Let's break down each one individually.

Sine (sin θ) Ratio

  • Defined as: Length of Opposite side / Length of Hypotenuse
  • Abbreviated as 'sin'
  • sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse

🎙️ The Sine of an angle (written as sin θ, where θ is our reference angle) is the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the length of the hypotenuse. It tells us how 'tall' the opposite side is compared to the hypotenuse.

Cosine (cos θ) Ratio

  • Defined as: Length of Adjacent side / Length of Hypotenuse
  • Abbreviated as 'cos'
  • cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

🎙️ The Cosine of an angle (cos θ) is the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to the angle to the length of the hypotenuse. Think of it as how 'wide' the adjacent side is compared to the hypotenuse.

🎙️ The Tangent of an angle (tan θ) is the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the length of the side adjacent to the angle. This ratio is useful when we don't know the hypotenuse.

The Reciprocal Ratios

  • There are three more trigonometric ratios
  • These are simply the inverse (flipped) of the main three
  • Cosecant, Secant, and Cotangent

🎙️ Just like we have reciprocals in normal numbers (like 1/2 is the reciprocal of 2), trigonometric ratios also have reciprocals. These are Cosecant, Secant, and Cotangent, and they are simply the 'flipped' versions of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent.

Cosecant (csc θ) Ratio

  • Reciprocal of Sine (1/sin θ)
  • Defined as: Hypotenuse / Opposite side
  • Abbreviated as 'csc'

🎙️ Cosecant (csc θ) is the reciprocal of Sine. So, if sin θ is Opposite/Hypotenuse, then csc θ is Hypotenuse/Opposite. It's like looking at the ratio from a different perspective.

Secant (sec θ) Ratio

  • Reciprocal of Cosine (1/cos θ)
  • Defined as: Hypotenuse / Adjacent side
  • Abbreviated as 'sec'

🎙️ Secant (sec θ) is the reciprocal of Cosine. If cos θ is Adjacent/Hypotenuse, then sec θ is Hypotenuse/Adjacent. Notice how these ratios always involve the hypotenuse.

Cotangent (cot θ) Ratio

🎙️ Finally, Cotangent (cot θ) is the reciprocal of Tangent. So, if tan θ is Opposite/Adjacent, then cot θ is Adjacent/Opposite. It's the last of our six fundamental trigonometric ratios.

Basic Trigonometric Identities

  • Equations that are true for all valid angles
  • The most fundamental is the Pythagorean Identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • Identities help simplify expressions and solve equations

🎙️ Trigonometric identities are like special formulas that are always true, no matter what valid angle you put in. The most fundamental one, derived from the Pythagorean theorem, is sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.
