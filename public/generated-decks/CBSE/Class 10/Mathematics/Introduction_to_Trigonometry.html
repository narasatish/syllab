What is Trigonometry?

  • Derived from Greek words: 'Trigon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).
  • It's the study of relationships between the sides and angles of a triangle.
  • Primarily focuses on right-angled triangles.

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's all about how the lengths of a triangle's sides are connected to its angles. We'll start by focusing on a special type of triangle: the right-angled triangle.

The Foundation: Right-Angled Triangle

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • This 90-degree angle is crucial for trigonometry.
  • The other two angles are always acute (less than 90 degrees).

🎙️ Our entire study of basic trigonometry revolves around the right-angled triangle. Remember, a right angle is like the corner of a square or a book. We'll always look for this 90-degree angle.

Naming the Sides of a Right Triangle

  • Hypotenuse: The longest side, opposite the 90-degree angle.
  • Opposite Side: The side directly across from the angle we are considering.
  • Adjacent Side: The side next to the angle we are considering (not the hypotenuse).

🎙️ It's very important to correctly identify the sides relative to a specific acute angle in the triangle. The hypotenuse is always the same, but 'opposite' and 'adjacent' change depending on which acute angle you're focusing on.

Let's Identify the Sides! (Example)

  • Consider angle A in a right triangle ABC (right-angled at B).
  • Hypotenuse: AC (always opposite 90°).
  • Opposite to angle A: BC.
  • Adjacent to angle A: AB.

🎙️ Look at the triangle on the slide. If we are looking at angle A, then side BC is directly opposite it. Side AB is next to it, sharing the angle, and AC is the longest side, the hypotenuse. Practice this, it's key!

Our Tools: Trigonometric Ratios

  • These are ratios of the lengths of the sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.
  • They relate an angle to the sides of the triangle.

🎙️ Think of these ratios as special 'tools' that help us connect angles and side lengths. For any given angle in a right triangle, these ratios will always be the same, regardless of the triangle's size. Let's learn the first three!

🛠️
Trigonometry Tools
8 / 25

Sine (sin) Ratio

  • The sine of an angle (often written as 'sin') is the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the length of the hypotenuse.
  • Formula: sin(angle) = Opposite / Hypotenuse.
  • Example: sin A = BC / AC.

🎙️ The sine ratio is one of the most fundamental trigonometric ratios. Remember, 'opposite' and 'hypotenuse' are specific sides we just discussed. This ratio helps us find an unknown side if we know an angle and another side.

➗
Opposite / Hypotenuse
9 / 25

Cosine (cos) Ratio

  • The cosine of an angle (often written as 'cos') is the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to the angle to the length of the hypotenuse.
  • Formula: cos(angle) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.
  • Example: cos A = AB / AC.

🎙️ Next up is the cosine ratio. Similar to sine, it's also a ratio involving the hypotenuse, but this time it's with the adjacent side. These ratios are always for an acute angle in the right triangle.

➗
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
10 / 25

Tangent (tan) Ratio

  • The tangent of an angle (often written as 'tan') is the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the length of the side adjacent to the angle.
  • Formula: tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • Example: tan A = BC / AB.

🎙️ The tangent ratio connects the two 'legs' of the right triangle, the opposite and adjacent sides, without involving the hypotenuse. An interesting fact is that tan A can also be written as sin A / cos A.

➗
Opposite / Adjacent

Remembering the Ratios: SOH CAH TOA

  • A helpful mnemonic to recall the three main ratios.
  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ This little phrase, SOH CAH TOA, is super useful for remembering the definitions of sine, cosine, and tangent. Say it out loud a few times, and it will stick in your mind, making it easier to solve problems.

🧠

The Reciprocal Ratios

  • In addition to sin, cos, and tan, there are three more ratios.
  • These are the reciprocals (or inverses) of the first three.
  • They are Cosecant, Secant, and Cotangent.

🎙️ Just like how 2 and 1/2 are reciprocals, our trigonometric ratios also have their reciprocal pairs. Understanding these will complete our set of six fundamental ratios.

🔄
Flipping Ratios
13 / 25

Cosecant (cosec) Ratio

  • The cosecant of an angle (cosec) is the reciprocal of the sine ratio.
  • Formula: cosec(angle) = 1 / sin(angle) = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • Example: cosec A = AC / BC.

🎙️ Cosecant is simply the hypotenuse divided by the opposite side. If you know the sine of an angle, you can easily find its cosecant by just flipping the fraction!

⬆️⬇️
Flipped Sine
14 / 25

Secant (sec) Ratio

  • The secant of an angle (sec) is the reciprocal of the cosine ratio.
  • Formula: sec(angle) = 1 / cos(angle) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.
  • Example: sec A = AC / AB.

🎙️ Similarly, secant is the reciprocal of cosine. It's the hypotenuse divided by the adjacent side. Knowing these reciprocal relationships makes remembering all six ratios much easier.

⬆️⬇️
Flipped Cosine
15 / 25

Cotangent (cot) Ratio

  • The cotangent of an angle (cot) is the reciprocal of the tangent ratio.
  • Formula: cot(angle) = 1 / tan(angle) = Adjacent / Opposite.
  • Example: cot A = AB / BC.

🎙️ Finally, cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent. It's the adjacent side divided by the opposite side. With these six ratios, we have a complete set of tools to analyze right-angled triangles!

⬆️⬇️
Flipped Tangent

Ratios for 45-degree Angle

  • Consider an isosceles right-angled triangle (angles 45°, 45°, 90°).
  • If opposite and adjacent sides are 'a', hypotenuse is 'a√2'.
  • sin 45° = 1/√2, cos 45° = 1/√2, tan 45° = 1.

🎙️ By taking a simple isosceles right triangle, where the two shorter sides are equal, we can use the Pythagorean theorem to find the hypotenuse and then easily calculate the ratios for 45 degrees.

Ratios for 30-degree and 60-degree Angles

  • Start with an equilateral triangle (all angles 60°).
  • Draw an altitude to split it into two 30°-60°-90° triangles.
  • Use side relationships to find ratios for 30° and 60°.

🎙️ For 30 and 60 degrees, we use a clever trick: start with an equilateral triangle and cut it in half. This creates two perfect 30-60-90 triangles, allowing us to find all the side ratios and thus the trigonometric values.

Ratios for 0-degree and 90-degree Angles

  • These are limiting cases, where one side shrinks or becomes equal to hypotenuse.
  • sin 0° = 0, cos 0° = 1, tan 0° = 0.
  • sin 90° = 1, cos 90° = 0, tan 90° = Undefined.

🎙️ These angles are special because they represent triangles that are 'flattened' or 'stretched'. Imagine an angle getting smaller and smaller towards 0 degrees, or larger and larger towards 90 degrees. The side lengths change in specific ways.
