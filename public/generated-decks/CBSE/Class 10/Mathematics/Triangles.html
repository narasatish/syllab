What Exactly is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides
  • Three vertices and three angles
  • Sum of angles is 180°

🎙️ A triangle is one of the most basic yet powerful shapes in geometry. It's defined by having three straight sides that connect at three points called vertices, forming three internal angles. Remember, a key property is that these three angles always add up to 180 degrees.

Classifying Triangles: By Sides

  • Equilateral: All 3 sides equal
  • Isosceles: 2 sides equal
  • Scalene: No sides equal

🎙️ We can classify triangles based on the lengths of their sides. An equilateral triangle has all three sides equal, making all its angles equal too. An isosceles triangle has two sides equal, and the angles opposite those sides are also equal. If no sides are equal, it's a scalene triangle.

Classifying Triangles: By Angles

  • Acute: All angles < 90°
  • Right: One angle = 90°
  • Obtuse: One angle > 90°

🎙️ Another way to classify triangles is by their angles. An acute triangle has all three angles less than 90 degrees. A right-angled triangle, as the name suggests, has exactly one angle that is 90 degrees. If one angle is greater than 90 degrees, it's an obtuse triangle.

What are Congruent Figures?

  • Exactly the same shape
  • Exactly the same size
  • Can perfectly overlap each other

🎙️ When two figures are congruent, it means they are identical in every way. They have the same shape and the same size. If you were to place one on top of the other, they would perfectly overlap. Think of two identical coins or two pages from the same book.

Understanding Similar Figures

  • Same shape, but different size
  • Proportional corresponding parts
  • One is an enlargement/reduction of the other

🎙️ Similar figures, on the other hand, share the same shape but can have different sizes. Imagine a photograph and its smaller print – they look alike but are not the same size. Their corresponding angles are equal, and their corresponding sides are in proportion.

Congruent vs. Similar: A Key Distinction

  • Congruent: Same shape AND size
  • Similar: Same shape, DIFFERENT size

🎙️ This is a crucial difference in geometry. Congruent figures are exact duplicates, while similar figures are like scaled versions of each other. Think of it this way: all congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent. Size is the key differentiator here.

How Do We Prove Triangles are Similar?

  • We need specific rules or criteria
  • Three main criteria: AAA, SSS, SAS
  • These help us identify similar triangles easily

🎙️ Just like we have rules for congruence, we also have specific criteria to determine if two triangles are similar. We don't need to check every single angle and side. There are three powerful criteria that simplify this process: Angle-Angle-Angle, Side-Side-Side, and Side-Angle-Side.

AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) Similarity

  • If all three corresponding angles are equal
  • Then the two triangles are similar
  • Sides will automatically be proportional

🎙️ The AAA criterion states that if the corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the triangles are similar. This is a very intuitive rule – if the angles are the same, the shape must be the same, even if the size differs. The proportionality of sides follows automatically from this.

1Angle A = Angle D
2Angle B = Angle E
3Angle C = Angle F
11 / 25

Measuring Height with AAA Similarity (Shadows)

  • Sun's rays create similar triangles
  • Angle of elevation is the same for both objects
  • Use shadows to find unknown heights

🎙️ Imagine you want to find the height of a tall tree without climbing it. You can use AAA similarity! At any given time, the angle of elevation of the sun is the same for all objects. This creates two similar right-angled triangles: one formed by you and your shadow, and another by the tree and its shadow. By measuring lengths, you can find the tree's height.

Worked Example: AAA Similarity

  • Given ΔABC and ΔPQR
  • ∠A = ∠P = 60°, ∠B = ∠Q = 80°, ∠C = ∠R = 40°
  • Are they similar? Yes, by AAA.

🎙️ Let's apply the AAA criterion. If we have two triangles, ΔABC and ΔPQR, and we know that their corresponding angles are equal – for instance, ∠A equals ∠P, ∠B equals ∠Q, and ∠C equals ∠R – then we can confidently say that ΔABC is similar to ΔPQR. This is a direct application of the rule.

13 / 25

AA (Angle-Angle) Similarity

  • If two corresponding angles are equal
  • The third angle must also be equal (180° rule)
  • Therefore, AA is sufficient for similarity

🎙️ A powerful shortcut to AAA is the AA criterion. If just two corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then the third angles must also be equal because the sum of angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees. So, if two angles match, the triangles are similar. This saves us from checking all three angles.

1Angle A = Angle D
2Angle B = Angle E
14 / 25

Worked Example: AA Similarity

  • In ΔABC, ∠A = 70°, ∠B = 50°
  • In ΔDEF, ∠D = 70°, ∠E = 50°
  • Are they similar? Yes, by AA.

🎙️ Consider ΔABC with angles 70° and 50°, and ΔDEF with angles 70° and 50°. Since ∠A = ∠D and ∠B = ∠E, by the AA similarity criterion, ΔABC is similar to ΔDEF. We don't even need to find the third angle, as it will automatically be 60° in both cases.

15 / 25

SSS (Side-Side-Side) Similarity

  • If the ratios of all three corresponding sides are equal
  • Then the two triangles are similar
  • The corresponding angles will also be equal

🎙️ The SSS criterion states that if the corresponding sides of two triangles are in the same proportion, then the triangles are similar. This means if you divide the length of one side of the first triangle by the corresponding side of the second triangle, you'll get the same ratio for all three pairs of sides. The angles will then automatically match.

1AB/DE = k
2BC/EF = k
3CA/FD = k
16 / 25

SSS Similarity in Design and Models

  • Scale models of cars, planes, buildings
  • Miniature replicas have proportional sides
  • Maintaining shape while changing size

🎙️ Think about scale models of cars or airplanes. These models are designed to be similar to the real objects. Every part of the model is scaled down by the same proportion. If the real car is 10 times larger than the model, then every side length on the real car is 10 times the corresponding side length on the model, demonstrating SSS similarity.

Worked Example: SSS Similarity

  • ΔABC sides: 3, 4, 5
  • ΔPQR sides: 6, 8, 10
  • Check ratios: 3/6=1/2, 4/8=1/2, 5/10=1/2. Yes, by SSS.

🎙️ Let's take ΔABC with sides 3, 4, and 5 units, and ΔPQR with sides 6, 8, and 10 units. If we check the ratios: 3/6 = 1/2, 4/8 = 1/2, and 5/10 = 1/2. Since all corresponding side ratios are equal (to 1/2), by the SSS similarity criterion, ΔABC is similar to ΔPQR.

18 / 25

SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Similarity

  • If two sides are proportional
  • AND the included angle between them is equal
  • Then the two triangles are similar

🎙️ The SAS criterion is a combination of sides and angles. It states that if two sides of one triangle are proportional to two corresponding sides of another triangle, and the included angles (the angles between those two sides) are equal, then the triangles are similar. This criterion is very useful when you don't have all three sides or all three angles.

1AB/DE = k
2Angle B = Angle E
3BC/EF = k
19 / 25

Worked Example: SAS Similarity

  • ΔABC: AB=4, BC=6, ∠B=50°
  • ΔPQR: PQ=8, QR=12, ∠Q=50°
  • Check ratios AB/PQ=1/2, BC/QR=1/2. Yes, by SAS.

🎙️ Consider ΔABC with sides AB=4, BC=6, and the included angle ∠B=50°. For ΔPQR, sides PQ=8, QR=12, and included angle ∠Q=50°. The ratio AB/PQ = 4/8 = 1/2. The ratio BC/QR = 6/12 = 1/2. Since the ratios are equal and the included angles (∠B and ∠Q) are equal, by SAS similarity, ΔABC is similar to ΔPQR.

Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

  • The ratio of areas of two similar triangles
  • Is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides
  • Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔPQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)² = (CA/RP)²

🎙️ This is a very important theorem. It tells us that if two triangles are similar, the relationship between their areas is not just linear, but quadratic. The ratio of their areas is equal to the square of the ratio of any pair of their corresponding sides. This means if sides are doubled, the area becomes four times larger!

Pythagoras Theorem: A Special Triangle

  • Only applies to right-angled triangles
  • a² + b² = c² (hypotenuse)
  • Often used in problems involving similar right triangles

🎙️ While not a similarity criterion itself, the Pythagoras Theorem is crucial when dealing with right-angled triangles, which often appear in similarity problems. Remember, it states that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides. We can use it to find unknown side lengths within similar right triangles.
