The Thales Theorem: A Powerful Tool

  • Introduced by Greek mathematician Thales.
  • Helps find unknown lengths in triangles.
  • Also known as Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT).

🎙️ This theorem is a cornerstone of triangle similarity. It's often called Thales Theorem, named after the ancient Greek mathematician. It provides a simple yet powerful way to relate the sides of a triangle when a line parallel to one of its sides is drawn.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT)

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle...
  • ...it divides the other two sides proportionally.

🎙️ So, if you have a triangle ABC and you draw a line DE parallel to BC, intersecting AB at D and AC at E, then the theorem states that AD/DB will be equal to AE/EC. This ratio is constant!

Applying BPT: Finding Unknown Sides

  • Given triangle ABC, DE || BC.
  • AD = 3cm, DB = 6cm, AE = 2cm.
  • Find EC.

🎙️ Let's solve a problem using BPT. If AD/DB = AE/EC, then 3/6 = 2/EC. By cross-multiplication, 3 * EC = 12, so EC = 4cm. It's a straightforward application once you understand the theorem.

Converse of BPT: Proving Parallel Lines

  • If a line divides two sides of a triangle proportionally...
  • ...then the line is parallel to the third side.

🎙️ The converse theorem works in reverse. If you find that a line segment divides the other two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, you can then conclude that this line segment must be parallel to the third side. This is useful for proving lines are parallel.

AAA Similarity Criterion: All Angles Match!

  • If all three corresponding angles are equal...
  • ...then the triangles are similar.

🎙️ This is one of the most intuitive criteria. If you have two triangles and you can show that all three pairs of corresponding angles are equal, then the triangles are definitely similar. Their shapes are identical, even if their sizes differ.

AAA in Action: When Angles Decide

  • Triangle ABC has angles 60°, 70°, 50°.
  • Triangle PQR has angles 60°, 70°, 50°.
  • Therefore, triangle ABC ~ triangle PQR.

🎙️ Consider two triangles. If one has angles 60°, 70°, and 50°, and another triangle also has angles 60°, 70°, and 50°, then they are similar. The sizes might be different, but their shapes are exactly the same.

  • ...then the triangles are similar.

🎙️ This criterion focuses on the side lengths. If you can show that the ratios of all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal, then the triangles are similar. This means their shapes are identical, and one is just a scaled version of the other.

SSS in Action: Proportional Sides

  • Triangle ABC sides: 2, 3, 4.
  • Triangle PQR sides: 4, 6, 8.
  • Ratios: 2/4 = 1/2, 3/6 = 1/2, 4/8 = 1/2.
  • Therefore, triangle ABC ~ triangle PQR.

🎙️ If triangle ABC has sides 2, 3, 4 and triangle PQR has sides 4, 6, 8, then the ratio of corresponding sides is 2/4 = 1/2, 3/6 = 1/2, and 4/8 = 1/2. Since all ratios are equal, the triangles are similar.

SAS Similarity Criterion: Two Sides and Included Angle

  • If two corresponding sides are in proportion...
  • ...AND the included angle is equal...
  • ...then the triangles are similar.

🎙️ This criterion is a mix of sides and angles. If you have two triangles where two pairs of corresponding sides are proportional, and the angles *between* those sides (the included angles) are equal, then the triangles are similar.

Area Relationship: How Areas Scale

  • If two triangles are similar...
  • ...the ratio of their areas is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

🎙️ This theorem tells us how the areas of similar triangles are related. It's not just a linear relationship! If the sides are twice as long, the area isn't just twice, it's four times! This is a common point of confusion.

💡
Area Scaling Rule
24 / 30

Calculating Area Ratios

  • Triangle ABC ~ Triangle PQR.
  • AB/PQ = 1/2.
  • Area(ABC) / Area(PQR) = (AB/PQ)^2 = (1/2)^2 = 1/4.

🎙️ If two similar triangles have a side ratio of 1:2, it means the larger triangle has sides twice as long. But its area will be (1/2)^2, which is 1/4th of the larger triangle's area. Or, the larger triangle's area is 4 times the smaller one.

  • Hypotenuse² = Perpendicular² + Base².
  • A fundamental theorem in geometry.

Converse of Pythagoras: Proving Right Angles

  • If the square of one side equals the sum of squares of other two sides...
  • ...then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.

🎙️ Just like with BPT, Pythagoras Theorem also has a converse. If you know the lengths of all three sides of a triangle and you find that a² + b² = c² holds true, then you can confidently say that the angle opposite side 'c' is a right angle (90 degrees).
