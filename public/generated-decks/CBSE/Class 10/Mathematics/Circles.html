What is a Circle? - A Quick Recap

  • A set of all points in a plane.
  • Equidistant from a fixed central point.
  • This fixed point is called the Centre.

🎙️ Remember from Class 9, a circle is defined by all points that are the same distance from a central point. Think of drawing a perfect circle with a compass – the pin stays at the centre, and the pencil traces the circle.

Key Terms: Radius and Diameter

  • Radius (r): Distance from centre to any point on the circle.
  • Diameter (d): A chord passing through the centre.
  • Diameter is twice the radius (d = 2r).

🎙️ The radius is like the arm of your compass, setting the size of the circle. The diameter is the longest distance across the circle, always passing through the centre, and it's always double the radius.

Key Terms: Chord and Arc

  • Chord: A line segment joining any two points on the circle.
  • Arc: A continuous piece or part of a circle.

🎙️ Imagine cutting a slice off a pizza – the straight edge of the slice is like a chord. The curved crust of that slice is an arc. A chord connects two points, while an arc is the path along the circle's boundary between those points.

Key Terms: Segment and Sector

  • Segment: Region between a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • Sector: Region between two radii and their corresponding arc.

🎙️ A segment is like a slice of pizza cut by a straight line, not necessarily through the center. A sector is a more traditional pizza slice, cut from the center outwards. Both are regions within the circle.

Introducing Tangents: What are they?

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • This point is called the 'point of contact'.
  • It never enters the interior of the circle.

🎙️ A tangent is a special kind of line. Imagine a car wheel moving on a straight road. The road 'touches' the wheel at just one point at any given instant. That's exactly what a tangent does to a circle.

👆
Touching Point

Secant vs. Tangent: What's the Difference?

  • Secant: A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.
  • Tangent: A line that intersects the circle at exactly one point.

🎙️ The key difference is the number of intersection points. A secant 'cuts through' the circle, touching it twice. A tangent just 'grazes' the circle, touching it only once. Keep this distinction clear!

Key Property 1: Radius Perpendicular to Tangent

  • The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius.
  • This radius passes through the point of contact.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle.

🎙️ This is a fundamental property! Wherever a tangent touches a circle, if you draw a line from the centre to that point of contact (which is the radius), they will always meet at a perfect right angle. This property is very useful for solving problems.

📐
Right Angle

Visualizing Perpendicularity (Property 1)

  • Draw radius OP from the centre O to P.
  • Angle OPL will always be 90 degrees.

🎙️ This step-by-step visualization helps solidify the concept. No matter where you draw the tangent on the circle, this perpendicular relationship between the radius and the tangent at the point of contact will always hold true. It's a cornerstone of circle geometry.

Worked Example 1: Applying Perpendicularity

  • A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at a point Q so that OQ = 12 cm. Find the length of PQ.

🎙️ Here, we have a right-angled triangle OPQ, where OP is the radius and PQ is the tangent. Since OP is perpendicular to PQ, we can use the Pythagorean theorem to find the unknown length. Remember, OP = 5 cm and OQ = 12 cm.

📏
Solving for Length
13 / 26

Solution: Example 1

  • In ΔOPQ, ∠OPQ = 90° (Radius ⊥ Tangent).
  • By Pythagoras Theorem: OQ² = OP² + PQ².
  • 12² = 5² + PQ² => 144 = 25 + PQ².
  • PQ² = 119 => PQ = √119 cm.

🎙️ So, PQ = √119 cm. This example clearly demonstrates how the property of perpendicularity between radius and tangent allows us to form a right-angled triangle and apply the Pythagorean theorem to find unknown lengths. Always draw a diagram for such problems!

✔️
Pythagoras in Action

Key Property 2: Equal Tangent Lengths

  • The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
  • If P is an external point, and PA, PB are tangents to a circle.
  • Then, PA = PB.

🎙️ This is another very important property. Imagine standing outside a circular pond and trying to touch it with two sticks. If both sticks just graze the pond at one point each, and you hold them from the same spot, their lengths from your hand to the pond will be equal.

Visualizing Equal Tangent Lengths (Property 2)

  • From an external point P, draw two tangents PA and PB.
  • Connect centre O to P, A, and B.
  • Triangles OAP and OBP are congruent (RHS congruence rule).

🎙️ When we connect the centre to the point of contact and the external point, we form two right-angled triangles. These triangles are congruent, which proves that the tangent lengths PA and PB must be equal. This congruence is a powerful tool.

Worked Example 2: Applying Equal Tangents

  • From a point Q, the length of the tangent to a circle is 24 cm.
  • The distance of Q from the centre is 25 cm.
  • Find the radius of the circle.

🎙️ This problem combines both properties! First, use Property 1 (radius is perpendicular to tangent) to form a right-angled triangle. Then, use the given lengths and Pythagoras theorem to find the missing radius. Draw a diagram to help you visualize!

🔍
Finding Radius
18 / 26

Solution: Example 2

  • Let O be the centre and P be the point of contact.
  • In ΔOPQ, ∠OPQ = 90° (Radius ⊥ Tangent).
  • By Pythagoras Theorem: OQ² = OP² + PQ².
  • 25² = OP² + 24² => 625 = OP² + 576.
  • OP² = 625 - 576 = 49 => OP = 7 cm. (Radius)

🎙️ The radius of the circle is 7 cm. Notice how we used the first property (perpendicularity) to set up the right triangle and then applied Pythagoras. These two properties are often used together in problems, so master them!

✅
Radius is 7 cm

How Many Tangents Can We Draw?

  • Point inside the circle: Zero tangents.
  • Point on the circle: Exactly one tangent.
  • Point outside the circle: Exactly two tangents.

🎙️ The position of the point relative to the circle determines how many tangents can be drawn. If a point is inside, any line through it will be a secant, not a tangent. If it's on the circle, only one line can just 'touch' it. From outside, you can always draw two distinct tangents.

Inside (0)
On (1)
Outside (2)

Advanced Application: Circumscribing Quadrilaterals

  • If a quadrilateral ABCD is drawn to circumscribe a circle.
  • Then the sum of opposite sides are equal: AB + CD = AD + BC.
  • This uses the property of equal tangent lengths.

🎙️ This is an interesting application of Property 2. When a quadrilateral circumscribes a circle, each side is a tangent to the circle. By applying the property that tangents from an external point are equal, we can prove this relationship between the sums of opposite sides.

Solution: Example 3 & Chapter Recap

  • From Property: AB + CD = AD + BC.
  • 6 + 4 = AD + 7.
  • 10 = AD + 7 => AD = 3 cm.
  • Recap: Circle basics, Tangent definition, Two key tangent properties.
