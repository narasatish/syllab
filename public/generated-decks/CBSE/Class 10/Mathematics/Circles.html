What is a Circle?

  • A set of all points in a plane equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.
  • The constant distance is called the 'radius'.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a point on a paper and then, keeping your compass fixed at that point, drawing a curve. That curve is a circle! Every single point on that curve is the exact same distance from the center point.

Key Terms: Radius and Diameter

  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on the circle.
  • Diameter (d): A line segment passing through the center with endpoints on the circle.
  • Diameter is twice the radius (d = 2r).

🎙️ The radius is like the arm of your compass, defining the size of the circle. The diameter is the longest chord in a circle, cutting it right through the middle, and it's always twice the length of the radius.

📏
Measuring Distance

Key Terms: Chord and Arc

  • Chord: A line segment joining any two points on the circle.
  • Arc: A continuous piece of a circle.
  • Major arc is longer, Minor arc is shorter.

🎙️ Think of a chord as a straight line cutting across the circle, but not necessarily through the center. An arc is simply a part of the circle's boundary, like a curved path along its edge.

🔗
Connecting Points

Key Terms: Sector and Segment

  • Sector: Region enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • Segment: Region enclosed by a chord and an arc.
  • Both can be major or minor.

🎙️ Imagine a pizza slice; that's a sector! It's like a pie-shaped part of the circle. A segment, on the other hand, is the area cut off by a chord, like a crusty piece of pizza cut straight across.

Tangent to a Circle

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • The point where it touches is called the 'point of contact'.
  • It never enters the interior of the circle.

🎙️ A tangent is a special kind of line. It's like a fleeting touch, just grazing the edge of the circle without crossing into its space. This single point of contact is very important.

🎙️ Think about a bicycle chain. It touches the gears at a single point as it moves, demonstrating the concept of a tangent. Similarly, a car tire touches the road tangentially at any given moment.

Tangent-Radius Theorem

  • The radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • This means the angle between the radius and tangent is 90 degrees.
  • Fundamental property for solving problems.

🎙️ This is a very important theorem! It tells us that if you draw a line from the center to where the tangent touches the circle, that line (the radius) will always form a perfect right angle with the tangent. This property is often used in problem-solving.

📐
Right Angle

Tangents from a Point

  • Point inside the circle: Zero tangents.
  • Point on the circle: Exactly one tangent.
  • Point outside the circle: Exactly two tangents.

🎙️ The number of tangents you can draw to a circle depends entirely on where the point is located. If it's inside, you can't draw any. If it's on the circle, only one is possible. But from an external point, you can always draw two distinct tangents.

External Tangents Theorem

  • The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
  • If PA and PB are tangents from point P, then PA = PB.
  • This creates congruent triangles and useful properties.

🎙️ This is another incredibly useful theorem! It tells us that if you stand outside a circle and draw two lines that just touch the circle, the lengths of those two lines from your point to the circle will always be exactly the same. This symmetry is very powerful.

Secant to a Circle

  • A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.
  • It passes through the interior of the circle.
  • Different from a tangent, which only touches one point.

🎙️ While a tangent just brushes the circle, a secant is a line that actually cuts through it, crossing the boundary at two different places. Think of it as a line that 'secures' two points on the circle.

Properties of Chords

  • A perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects the chord.
  • The converse is also true: the line joining the center to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
  • Equal chords are equidistant from the center.

🎙️ Chords have some very neat properties. If you drop a perpendicular line from the center of the circle to any chord, it will always cut that chord into two equal halves. This is extremely useful in constructions and calculations.

➡️

Concentric Circles

  • Circles with the same center but different radii.
  • They share a common central point.
  • Examples: archery targets, ripples in water.

🎙️ Concentric circles are like Russian nesting dolls for circles – they all share the exact same center point, but each one has a different size. Think of the rings of an archery target or the ripples spreading out from a stone dropped in water.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

  • A quadrilateral whose all four vertices lie on a circle.
  • The sum of opposite angles is 180 degrees (supplementary).
  • Example: a square or rectangle inscribed in a circle.

🎙️ When all four corners of a four-sided shape touch the circumference of a circle, we call it a cyclic quadrilateral. A special property of these shapes is that their opposite angles always add up to 180 degrees.

Recap: Circles in a Nutshell

  • Basic terms: radius, diameter, chord, arc, sector, segment.
  • Tangent: touches at one point, perpendicular to radius.
  • Two tangents from an external point are equal in length.
  • Secant: cuts at two points.
  • Chord property: perpendicular from center bisects chord.
  • Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles sum to 180°.

🎙️ We've covered a lot today! From the basic definitions of a circle's parts to important theorems about tangents and chords, and even special quadrilaterals. These concepts form the foundation for more advanced geometry.
