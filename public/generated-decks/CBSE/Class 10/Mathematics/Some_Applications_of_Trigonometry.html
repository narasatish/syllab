Line of Sight: Where are your eyes looking?

  • The imaginary line from observer's eye to the object.
  • It's your direct visual path.
  • Think of it as the path light travels to your eyes.

🎙️ When you look at something, your eye creates an invisible line directly to that object. That's what we call the Line of Sight.

Looking Up: The Angle of Elevation

  • Angle formed when you look UP at an object.
  • Between the Line of Sight and the horizontal line.
  • Observer is BELOW the object.

🎙️ If you're looking up at a kite flying high in the sky, the angle your line of sight makes with the ground is the Angle of Elevation.

Looking Down: The Angle of Depression

  • Angle formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • Between the Line of Sight and the horizontal line.
  • Observer is ABOVE the object.

🎙️ Now, imagine you're on a tall building, looking down at a car on the road. The angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal is the Angle of Depression.

Example: Tower Height (Two Points)

  • From two points 4m and 9m from the base of a tower, on the same side.
  • Angles of elevation are complementary.
  • Find the height of the tower.

🎙️ Complementary angles mean their sum is 90°. If one is θ, the other is (90°-θ). This often simplifies calculations using tan θ and tan (90°-θ) = cot θ.

Tackling Multi-Triangle Problems

  • 1. Draw a single comprehensive diagram.

  • 3. Formulate equations for each triangle.
  • 4. Solve the system of equations.

🎙️ It's like solving a puzzle with multiple pieces. Each triangle gives you an equation, and you combine them to find the final answer.

Types of Problems You'll Encounter

  • Single object, single observer (elevation/depression).
  • Single object, two observers (same/opposite sides).
  • Two objects, single observer (e.g., flagpole on building).
  • Motion problems (e.g., car approaching tower).

🎙️ Most problems fall into these categories. Once you identify the type, you can apply the appropriate strategy.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing Angle of Elevation with Depression.
  • Incorrectly drawing the horizontal line.
  • Calculation errors with square roots or decimals.
  • Not considering observer's height (if given).

🎙️ Pay close attention to the wording of the problem. A small error in the diagram or choice of ratio can lead to a completely wrong answer.
