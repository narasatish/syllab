The Power of Trigonometric Ratios

  • Sine (sin), Cosine (cos), and Tangent (tan).
  • These are ratios of sides in a right triangle.
  • Used to relate angles and side lengths.

🎙️ Our main tools in this chapter are the trigonometric ratios: sine, cosine, and tangent. These ratios connect the angles of a right triangle to the lengths of its sides. 'SOH CAH TOA' is a handy mnemonic to recall them.

1SOH: Sin = Opp/Hyp
2CAH: Cos = Adj/Hyp
3TOA: Tan = Opp/Adj

What is the Line of Sight?

  • The imaginary line from observer's eye to object.
  • It's the path your gaze follows.
  • Forms the basis for elevation and depression angles.

🎙️ Imagine you are looking at an object. The straight line connecting your eye to that object is called the 'line of sight'. This concept is fundamental to understanding the angles we'll discuss next.

Understanding Angle of Elevation

  • Formed when you look UP at an object.
  • Angle between line of sight and horizontal level.
  • Always measured from the horizontal.

🎙️ When you look upwards at an object, the angle formed between your line of sight and the horizontal line (usually the ground) is called the 'angle of elevation'. Think of it as 'elevating' your gaze.

Understanding Angle of Depression

  • Formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • Angle between line of sight and horizontal level (from eye).
  • Measured from the horizontal line at the observer's eye level.

🎙️ Now, if you are looking downwards at an object, the angle formed between your line of sight and the horizontal line (drawn from your eye level) is called the 'angle of depression'. You are 'depressing' your gaze.

Worked Example 1: Finding Height

  • Problem: A pole 15m high casts a shadow 15√3 m long.
  • Find the angle of elevation of the sun.
  • Solution: tan θ = Opposite/Adjacent = 15 / (15√3) = 1/√3. So, θ = 30°.

🎙️ Let's try an example. Here, we know the height of the pole and the length of its shadow. By forming a right-angled triangle and using the tangent ratio, we can easily find the angle of elevation of the sun.

Worked Example 2: Finding Distance

  • Problem: From a point on the ground 30m from a tower's foot,
  • the angle of elevation of the tower top is 30°.
  • Find the height of the tower. Solution: tan 30° = h/30. So, h = 30/√3 = 10√3 m.

🎙️ In this problem, we are given the distance from the tower and the angle of elevation. We need to find the height of the tower. Again, the tangent ratio proves very useful here to relate the angle to the sides.
