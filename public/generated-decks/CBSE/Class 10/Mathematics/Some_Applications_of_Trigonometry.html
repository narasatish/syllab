Key Term 1: Line of Sight

  • An imaginary straight line.
  • Connects the observer's eye to the object being viewed.
  • Forms a side of our right-angled triangle.

🎙️ Our first key term is the 'Line of Sight'. Imagine a straight line from your eye directly to whatever you are looking at. This line is crucial for forming our trigonometric triangles.

Key Term 2: Angle of Elevation

  • Angle formed when an observer looks UP.
  • Between the horizontal level and the line of sight.
  • The object is above the horizontal level of the eye.

🎙️ Next, the 'Angle of Elevation'. When you look up at something, like a bird in the sky, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal ground is called the angle of elevation.

Key Term 3: Angle of Depression

  • Angle formed when an observer looks DOWN.
  • Between the horizontal level and the line of sight.
  • The object is below the horizontal level of the eye.

🎙️ Now, the 'Angle of Depression'. This is the opposite! When you're at a higher point and look down at something below you, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal is the angle of depression.

Worked Example 1: Height of a Tower

  • Problem: A tower stands vertically on the ground.
  • From a point 15m away, angle of elevation to top is 60°.
  • Find the height of the tower.

🎙️ Let's apply our knowledge to a problem. We need to find the height of a tower given an angle of elevation and a distance from its base. This is a classic application.

🗼
Tall Tower

Solution 1: Drawing the Diagram

  • Draw a right-angled triangle ABC.
  • AB is the tower (height 'h').
  • BC is the distance from foot (15m).
  • Angle ACB is the angle of elevation (60°).

🎙️ First, visualize and draw. The tower is the perpendicular side (AB), the distance from the foot is the base (BC), and the line of sight is the hypotenuse (AC). Label the known values carefully.

✏️
Sketching
13 / 25

Solution 1: Calculation

  • In ΔABC, we have opposite (h) and adjacent (15m).
  • Use tan ratio: tan(60°) = AB/BC = h/15.
  • Since tan(60°) = √3, then h = 15√3 meters.

🎙️ Since we know the base and need the height, which are opposite and adjacent to the angle, the tangent ratio is perfect here. Remember the value of tan 60 degrees, which is root 3.

  • Problem: From the top of a 75m high lighthouse,
  • The angle of depression of a ship is 30°.
  • Find the distance of the ship from the lighthouse.

🎙️ Now, let's work with an angle of depression. We are at a height and looking down. We need to find the horizontal distance to the object.

Solution 2: Drawing the Diagram

  • Let AB be the lighthouse (75m). C is the ship.
  • Draw horizontal line AX parallel to BC.
  • Angle XAC (depression) = 30°. So, angle ACB = 30° (alternate interior angles).

🎙️ For the angle of depression, draw the horizontal line at the observer's eye. The angle inside the triangle, at the ship's position (angle ACB), will be equal to the angle of depression due to parallel lines.

📏
Measuring
17 / 25

Solution 2: Calculation

  • In right ΔABC, we have opposite (AB=75m) and need adjacent (BC).
  • Use tan ratio: tan(30°) = AB/BC = 75/BC.
  • Since tan(30°) = 1/√3, then BC = 75√3 meters.

🎙️ Again, the tangent ratio is suitable as we have the height (opposite) and need the distance (adjacent). Substitute the value of tan 30 degrees and solve for the distance.

Worked Example 3: Multiple Angles

  • Problem: A 1.2m tall girl spots a balloon at a height of 88.2m.
  • Angle of elevation from her eyes is 60°. After some time, it becomes 30°.
  • Find the distance traveled by the balloon during the interval.

🎙️ Sometimes, problems involve two different angles or positions. Here, the balloon changes its position, leading to two different angles of elevation from the same observer. We need to find the horizontal distance it covered.

🎈
Moving Balloon
20 / 25

Solution 3: Drawing the Diagram

  • Draw two right-angled triangles, one for each balloon position.
  • Remember to subtract the girl's height (1.2m) from the balloon's total height (88.2m) to get the height above her eye level (87m).
  • Let the initial horizontal distance be x and the final be y. The distance traveled is y-x.

🎙️ This problem requires two triangles. Remember that the angles are from her eye level, so the effective height of the balloon above her eye level needs to be calculated first. This will be the perpendicular for both triangles.

📐
Geometric Setup
21 / 25

Solution 3: Calculation

  • For 60°: tan(60°) = 87/x => x = 87/√3 = 29√3 m.
  • For 30°: tan(30°) = 87/y => y = 87√3 m.
  • Distance traveled = y - x = 87√3 - 29√3 = 58√3 meters.

🎙️ Solve for the horizontal distance for both positions of the balloon using tangent. The difference between these two distances will give us how far the balloon has traveled horizontally. Rationalize denominators for final answers.
