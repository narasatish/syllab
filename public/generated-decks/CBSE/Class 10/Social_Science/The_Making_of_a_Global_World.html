What is 'Globalisation'?

  • Analogy: A family living in different cities
  • Connected by phone calls, visits, shared traditions
  • Globalisation: Interconnectedness of nations

🎙️ Just like a family spread across different cities stays connected, globalisation describes how countries become increasingly linked through trade, migration, and the exchange of ideas. It's a process, not just an event.

Pre-Modern World: The Silk Routes

  • Ancient trade networks connecting Asia, Europe, and Africa
  • Existed from before the Christian era
  • More than just silk: pottery, spices, textiles travelled

🎙️ Long before modern transport, the Silk Routes were like ancient superhighways. They were crucial for the movement of goods, people, and knowledge, demonstrating early forms of global interaction. Think of it as the internet of the ancient world!

📜
Ancient Trade

Beyond Goods: Ideas, Disease, and Culture

  • Buddhism spread from India to China via these routes
  • Early Christian missionaries and Muslim preachers travelled
  • Germs and diseases also spread along these paths

🎙️ The Silk Routes were not just about economic exchange; they were vibrant channels for cultural diffusion. Philosophies, religions, and even technologies moved across continents, shaping diverse societies. Unfortunately, pathogens also found easy passage.

🤝
Cultural Exchange

Food Travels: A Global Kitchen

  • Many common foods originated elsewhere
  • Potatoes, maize, tomatoes, chillies, groundnuts, sweet potatoes
  • Introduced to Asia and Europe after Columbus discovered America

🎙️ Imagine Italian food without tomatoes, or Indian cuisine without chillies! These staples, essential to global diets today, were unknown in many parts of the world just a few centuries ago. Their introduction dramatically altered food habits and life expectancy.

The Age of Discovery and Conquest

  • 16th century: European sailors find sea routes to America
  • Vasco da Gama reached India, Columbus found America
  • New lands, new resources, new markets opened up

🎙️ This era marked a massive shift. The discovery of sea routes to America and Asia by European explorers fundamentally changed global power dynamics. It led to vast expansion of trade and, unfortunately, conquest.

🚢
New Sea Routes

Disease: The Silent Conqueror in America

  • European conquest of America was not just military
  • Smallpox, a European disease, devastated native populations
  • Native Americans had no immunity to these diseases

🎙️ The most powerful weapon the Spanish conquerors had was not their firearms, but germs. Smallpox decimated indigenous communities, making it easier for Europeans to establish control over vast territories. This highlights how interconnectedness can have unforeseen, devastating consequences.

The 19th Century (1815-1914): A World Transformed

  • Rapid economic, political, social, cultural changes
  • Three key 'flows': trade, labour, capital
  • Industrial Revolution and European colonialism intensified globalisation

🎙️ The 19th century was a period of unprecedented transformation. It saw the emergence of a truly global economy driven by industrialisation and imperialism, leading to massive movements of goods, people, and money across continents.

🏭
Industrial Age
12 / 30

Flow 1: Trade – The Corn Laws

  • British government restricted import of corn (grains)
  • Kept food prices high, benefiting landlords
  • Abolition led to cheaper food, industrial growth, but agricultural decline

🎙️ The Corn Laws are a classic example of how domestic policies can impact global trade. Their abolition in Britain, driven by industrial interests, led to a huge influx of cheaper food, impacting British farmers but also fostering global agricultural trade.

🌾
Grain Trade
13 / 30

Flow 2: Labour – Indentured Migration from India

  • Analogy: Modern-day migrant workers seeking jobs abroad
  • Indians migrated under contract for work in plantations and mines
  • Often called 'new system of slavery'

🎙️ During the 19th century, millions of Indians migrated as indentured labourers, particularly from Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Tamil Nadu. They were seeking better opportunities but often faced harsh conditions, highlighting the human cost of this 'globalisation of labour'.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦
Migrant Workers

Destinations and Conditions of Indentured Labour

  • Caribbean islands (Trinidad, Guyana), Fiji, Mauritius, Ceylon, Malaya
  • Recruitment by agents, often deceptive promises
  • Harsh living and working conditions; limited legal rights

🎙️ These Indian labourers were instrumental in building economies in various British colonies. However, their experiences were often traumatic, marked by long voyages, exploitative contracts, and cultural displacement. Their descendants form significant diaspora communities today.

🗺️
Global Journeys
15 / 30

Abolition of Indentured Labour

  • Grew into a system of abuse and exploitation
  • Protests and activism, including by Mahatma Gandhi
  • Abolished in 1921 by the British government

🎙️ The 'new system of slavery' faced increasing criticism both in India and abroad. Leaders like Mahatma Gandhi, who first experienced racial discrimination in South Africa, played a key role in raising awareness and advocating for its end, leading to its eventual abolition.

⚖️
Justice Prevails

Flow 3: Capital – European Imperialism

  • European powers invested capital in colonies
  • Built railways, mines, plantations to extract resources
  • India became a major market for British manufactured goods

🎙️ European capital flowed into colonies not for their development, but for exploitation. Investments in infrastructure like railways facilitated the extraction of raw materials and penetration of colonial markets, further integrating them into the global capitalist system.

The First World War (1914-1918): A Global Conflict

  • Fought primarily in Europe but with global impact
  • Allies (Britain, France, Russia, US) vs. Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary)
  • Massive destruction, death, and economic disruption

🎙️ The First World War was a devastating event that fundamentally reshaped the global order. It involved colonial armies, including Indian soldiers, fighting far from home, demonstrating the deep interconnectedness of the imperial world.

Global Conflict

Post-War Recovery and Economic Instability

  • War debt, reparations, and economic boom-bust cycles
  • Agricultural overproduction led to falling prices
  • US emerged as a major economic power and creditor

🎙️ The war left Europe in ruins and heavily indebted. The post-war period was marked by attempts at recovery, but underlying economic weaknesses, particularly in agriculture, set the stage for a much larger crisis.

The Great Depression (1929-1930s): Causes

  • Agricultural overproduction and falling prices
  • US withdrawal of loans to European countries
  • Stock market crash in 1929 in the US

🎙️ The Great Depression was a global economic catastrophe. It wasn't just a US problem; its causes were interconnected, showing how a crisis in one part of the world could quickly spread due to global financial linkages.

The Great Depression: Impact on India

  • Indian exports and imports almost halved (1928-1934)
  • Agricultural prices fell sharply, but colonial taxes remained high
  • Peasants and farmers suffered immensely, leading to unrest

🎙️ India, as a colonial economy, was severely hit. The sharp fall in agricultural prices devastated farmers, who found it impossible to sell their produce and pay taxes. This period fueled nationalist sentiments against British rule.

Rebuilding a Global Economy: Bretton Woods

  • Post-WW2 conference (1944) in Bretton Woods, USA
  • Aimed to ensure global economic stability and full employment
  • Established the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank

🎙️ After two devastating world wars and the Great Depression, leaders realised the need for a stable international economic order. The Bretton Woods institutions were designed to prevent future economic crises and foster global cooperation.

🏛️
New Institutions

Bretton Woods Institutions: IMF and World Bank

  • IMF: To deal with external surpluses and deficits of member nations
  • World Bank: To finance post-war reconstruction and development
  • Both institutions began operations in 1947

🎙️ The IMF and World Bank became the pillars of the post-war global financial system. They provided loans and guidance, aiming to stabilise currencies and fund development projects, particularly in developing countries.

Decolonisation and Globalisation

  • Many countries gained independence after WW2 (e.g., India in 1947)
  • These new nations faced challenges of poverty and lack of resources
  • They needed assistance for economic development

🎙️ The post-war era also saw a wave of decolonisation, as India and many other nations gained freedom. These newly independent countries, often poor and underdeveloped due to colonial exploitation, sought to catch up with the industrialised world.

The G-77: Demand for a NIEO

  • Group of 77 developing countries formed for collective bargaining
  • Demanded a New International Economic Order (NIEO)
  • Aimed for fairer prices for raw materials, better access to markets

🎙️ The G-77 represented the collective voice of developing nations, seeking to reform the global economic system that they felt was biased towards the rich industrialised countries. They pushed for a more equitable distribution of global resources and opportunities.
