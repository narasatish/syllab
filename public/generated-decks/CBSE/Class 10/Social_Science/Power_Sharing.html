What is Power Sharing? 🤝

  • Distribution of power among different organs of government.
  • Sharing power across various levels of government.
  • Ensuring representation for diverse social groups.

🎙️ At its core, power sharing means that power isn't held by just one person or one group. It's carefully divided and shared. Think of it like a family where everyone has a role in making decisions, from daily chores to bigger plans. This prevents any single entity from becoming too powerful.

Why is Power Sharing Desirable? (Prudential Reasons) 🛡️

  • Reduces the possibility of conflict between social groups.
  • Ensures political stability and order.
  • A practical calculation based on gains and losses.

🎙️ One major reason to share power is purely practical – it helps avoid trouble. If one group dominates, others feel alienated, leading to tension and conflict. Sharing power, even if it means compromises, prevents such instability and ensures a smoother functioning society. It's about smart governance.

Why is Power Sharing Desirable? (Moral Reasons) ✊

  • The very spirit of democracy.
  • Citizens have a right to be consulted on how they are governed.
  • Legitimate government is one where citizens participate.

🎙️ Beyond practical benefits, power sharing is morally right. Democracy is 'of the people, by the people, for the people.' This means citizens should have a say. A government that shares power is more legitimate because it reflects the will and participation of its diverse population. It upholds the dignity of every citizen.

Belgium's Power Sharing Arrangement: The 'Belgian Model' 🇪🇺

  • Constitution amended four times (1970-1993) to work out an arrangement.
  • Equal number of Dutch and French ministers in Central Government.
  • Community government elected by people belonging to one language group.

🎙️ Belgium chose a unique path to resolve its ethnic tensions. They didn't just impose rules; they continuously worked on their constitution. Key features included ensuring equal representation in the federal government and creating 'community governments' that dealt with cultural and educational matters for specific language groups. This was a true effort in accommodation.

This demographic structure, combined with religious differences, set the stage for a very different outcome compared to Belgium.

Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka (1948-2009) 💔

  • Sri Lanka gained independence in 1948.
  • Sinhala leaders sought to establish Sinhala supremacy.
  • Act in 1956: Sinhala declared the only official language.
  • State protected and promoted Buddhism; Tamils felt alienated.

🎙️ Unlike Belgium, Sri Lanka adopted a policy of majoritarianism after independence. This meant the majority community, the Sinhala speakers, imposed their will, disregarding the interests of the Tamil minority. The 1956 Act was a turning point, making Sinhala the sole official language and promoting Buddhism. This created deep resentment among the Tamils.

Outcome of Majoritarianism: Civil War 💔

Forms of Power Sharing: Horizontal Distribution 🏛️

  • Power shared among different organs of government.
  • Legislature (Parliament), Executive (Government), Judiciary (Courts).
  • Places different organs at the same level.
  • System of 'checks and balances'.

🎙️ Now, let's explore how power is shared within a government. Horizontal power sharing means dividing power among different branches at the same level. Think of it like a three-legged stool: the Legislature makes laws, the Executive implements them, and the Judiciary interprets them. Each branch has its own powers but also checks the others.

🏛️

Horizontal Power Sharing in India: Checks and Balances ⚖️

  • Parliament (Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha) makes laws.
  • Executive (PM & Council of Ministers) implements laws.
  • Judiciary (Supreme Court, High Courts) interprets laws and ensures constitutionality.
  • Example: Judiciary can strike down laws passed by Parliament if unconstitutional.

🎙️ In India, the horizontal distribution of power is a cornerstone of our democracy. Our Parliament, Executive, and Judiciary operate with a system of checks and balances. For instance, while Parliament makes laws, the Judiciary can review them. This prevents any single branch from becoming authoritarian and protects citizens' rights.

Forms of Power Sharing: Vertical Distribution 🇮🇳

  • Power shared among governments at different levels.
  • Union (Central) Government, State Governments, Local Governments.
  • Known as Federalism.
  • Different levels have their own jurisdiction.

🎙️ Vertical power sharing means distributing power across different levels of government, from the national to the local. Imagine a layered cake: the Union government is the top layer, state governments are the middle, and local bodies are the base. Each layer has specific responsibilities, ensuring governance reaches everyone.

Vertical Power Sharing in India: Federal Structure 🗺️

  • Union List (Defence, Railways, Banking) – only Central govt. can legislate.
  • State List (Police, Agriculture, Health) – only State govt. can legislate.
  • Concurrent List (Education, Forests, Trade Unions) – both can legislate.
  • Panchayati Raj and Municipalities (Local Self-Government) at grassroots.

🎙️ India is a classic example of a federal system. Our Constitution clearly defines the powers of the Union and State governments through various lists. Furthermore, the 73rd and 74th Amendments introduced Panchayati Raj and Municipalities, decentralizing power to the grassroots level, empowering local communities to make their own decisions.

Power Sharing for Social Groups in India: Reserved Constituencies ⚖️

  • Reserved constituencies in Assemblies and Parliament for SCs and STs.
  • Ensures fair representation for historically disadvantaged groups.
  • Example: Women's reservation in Panchayats and Municipalities (33%).
  • Community government in Belgium is another example.

🎙️ India has implemented power sharing for social groups through reserved constituencies. This ensures that Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, who might otherwise struggle to get elected, have a voice in our legislative bodies. Similarly, women's reservation in local bodies empowers them to participate directly in governance, promoting inclusive democracy.

Forms of Power Sharing: Among Political Parties & Pressure Groups 🗳️

  • Power shared among different political parties competing for power.
  • Competition ensures that power does not remain in one hand.
  • Coalition governments when no single party secures a majority.
  • Pressure groups and movements also influence power.

🎙️ In a democracy, power is also shared dynamically through political competition. Different parties contend for power, and if no single party wins a majority, they form coalition governments, sharing power. Additionally, pressure groups and social movements often influence government decisions, ensuring that public concerns are addressed.

Power Sharing via Parties/Groups in India: Coalitions & Influence 📈

  • Example: National Democratic Alliance (NDA) and United Progressive Alliance (UPA) – coalitions.

🎙️ India has seen many coalition governments at both the Centre and states, like the NDA and UPA, where different parties share power based on common programmes. Beyond elections, groups like farmers' unions or environmental activists exert pressure on the government, ensuring that their specific concerns are addressed in policymaking. Key Terms to Remember 📚

  • Majoritarianism: Belief that majority community should rule.
  • Prudential: Based on careful calculation of gains and losses.
  • Moral: Based on ethical considerations, the 'spirit' of democracy.
  • Federalism: System of government where power is divided between central and regional authorities.
  • Coalition Government: Government formed by two or more political parties.

🎙️ These are some of the essential terms you'll encounter in this chapter. Understanding them clearly will help you grasp the nuances of power sharing and articulate your answers effectively in exams. Make sure to differentiate between prudential and moral reasons, as well as the different forms of power sharing.
