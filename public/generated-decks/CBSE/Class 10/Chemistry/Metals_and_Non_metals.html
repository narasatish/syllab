What are Elements?

  • Pure substances made of only one type of atom.
  • Cannot be broken down into simpler substances.
  • The basic building blocks of all matter.

🎙️ Before we dive into metals and non-metals, let's remember what an element is. It's the simplest form of matter, like gold or oxygen, made up of identical atoms and can't be chemically broken down further.

Classifying Elements

  • Based on their properties, elements are grouped.
  • Main categories: Metals, Non-metals.
  • A special group: Metalloids (show properties of both).

🎙️ Scientists classify elements to make them easier to study. The two major groups are metals and non-metals, which we'll focus on today. There's also a smaller group called metalloids that share properties of both.

What are Metals?

  • Usually solid at room temperature (except Mercury).
  • Have a shiny appearance (luster).
  • Good conductors of heat and electricity.

🎙️ Metals are generally hard, shiny substances that we use every day. Think of iron, copper, or gold. They have distinct physical and chemical properties that set them apart.

✨
Shiny & Strong
6 / 25

Metallic Luster & Hardness

  • Luster: Metals have a shiny surface when polished.
  • Example: Gold ornaments, polished silver.
  • Hardness: Most metals are hard and strong.
  • Example: Iron used in construction.

🎙️ The characteristic shine of metals is called metallic luster. This is why gold and silver are popular for jewelry. Most metals are also very hard, making them useful for building structures and tools.

Shiny & Solid

Malleability & Ductility

  • Malleability: Can be hammered into thin sheets.
  • Example: Aluminium foil for food wrapping.
  • Ductility: Can be drawn into thin wires.
  • Example: Copper wires for electrical wiring.

🎙️ Malleability means you can flatten a metal without it breaking, like making thin sheets of aluminum. Ductility means you can stretch it into a wire, which is why copper is used for electrical cables.

🔨
Shape-shifting Metals

Good Conductors

  • Thermal Conductivity: Allow heat to pass through easily.
  • Example: Metal cooking utensils.
  • Electrical Conductivity: Allow electricity to flow.
  • Example: Copper wires in appliances.

🎙️ Metals are excellent conductors of both heat and electricity. This property makes them indispensable for cooking pots and pans, and for all electrical circuits and wiring in our homes.

Heat & Electric Flow

Sonorous & High MP/BP

  • Sonority: Produce a ringing sound when struck.
  • Example: School bell, temple bells.
  • High MP/BP: Melt and boil at high temperatures.
  • Example: Iron melts at 1538°C.

🎙️ Metals are sonorous, meaning they make a distinct sound when hit, like a bell. They also have very high melting and boiling points, requiring a lot of energy to change their state, which makes them durable.

What are Non-metals?

  • Often gases or brittle solids at room temperature.
  • Generally dull in appearance (no luster).
  • Poor conductors of heat and electricity (insulators).

🎙️ Non-metals are quite different from metals. They include common elements like oxygen, nitrogen, and carbon. They usually lack the shiny appearance and conductivity that metals possess.

☁️
Dull & Brittle
11 / 25

No Luster & Brittle

  • Luster: Generally dull and non-lustrous.
  • Exception: Iodine has a metallic luster.
  • Hardness: Usually soft or brittle solids.
  • Example: Sulphur is brittle and breaks easily.

🎙️ Unlike metals, non-metals typically don't shine. Most non-metals are soft or brittle. For example, if you hit a piece of sulfur, it would break into powder, unlike a metal that would just dent.

💔
Dull & Fragile
12 / 25

Non-Malleable & Non-Ductile

  • Malleability: Cannot be hammered into sheets.
  • Ductility: Cannot be drawn into wires.
  • They are brittle and will break instead of bending.

🎙️ Non-metals are neither malleable nor ductile. If you try to hammer a non-metal, it will crumble. If you try to stretch it, it will break. This is a key difference from metals.

✖️
Cannot be Shaped
13 / 25

Poor Conductors (Insulators)

  • Thermal Conductivity: Poor conductors of heat.
  • Electrical Conductivity: Poor conductors of electricity.
  • Exception: Graphite (a form of carbon) conducts electricity.

🎙️ Non-metals are generally good insulators, meaning they don't allow heat or electricity to pass through easily. This is why plastic, a non-metal derivative, is used to cover electrical wires.

🚫
Blocks Heat & Electricity

Non-Sonorous & Low MP/BP

  • Sonority: Do not produce a ringing sound.
  • High MP/BP: Generally have low melting and boiling points.
  • Many are gases at room temperature (e.g., Oxygen).

🎙️ Non-metals are non-sonorous; they don't ring when struck. They also tend to have much lower melting and boiling points compared to metals, with many existing as gases at room temperature.

Metals React with Oxygen

  • Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides.
  • Metal oxides are generally basic in nature.
  • Example: Magnesium burns in air to form Magnesium Oxide (MgO).

🎙️ When metals are exposed to air, they often react with oxygen. This reaction forms metal oxides. For example, the rusting of iron is a slow reaction with oxygen and moisture.

1Metal + Oxygen
2Heat/Ignition
3Metal Oxide

Metals React with Water

  • Metals react with water to form metal oxides/hydroxides and hydrogen gas.
  • Different metals react differently (cold, hot, or steam).
  • Example: Sodium reacts vigorously with cold water.

🎙️ The reaction of metals with water varies greatly. Some, like sodium, are extremely reactive even with cold water, while others, like iron, only react with steam at high temperatures.

1Metal + Water
2Metal Hydroxide + H₂
3(or Metal Oxide + H₂)

Metals React with Acids

  • Metals react with dilute acids to form salt and hydrogen gas.
  • More reactive metals react more vigorously.
  • Example: Zinc reacts with dilute HCl to produce Zinc Chloride and H₂.

🎙️ When metals react with dilute acids, they displace hydrogen from the acid, forming a salt and releasing hydrogen gas. The speed of this reaction depends on the metal's reactivity.

1Metal + Dilute Acid
2Salt + Hydrogen Gas

Non-metals React with Oxygen

  • Non-metals react with oxygen to form non-metal oxides.
  • Non-metal oxides are generally acidic or neutral in nature.
  • Example: Carbon burns in air to form Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).

🎙️ Similar to metals, non-metals also react with oxygen, but they form non-metal oxides. These oxides are typically acidic, like carbon dioxide, which forms carbonic acid in water, or neutral, like carbon monoxide.

1Non-metal + Oxygen
2Non-metal Oxide

The Reactivity Series

  • A list of metals arranged in decreasing order of their reactivity.
  • Helps predict if a metal can displace another.
  • More reactive metals are at the top, less reactive at the bottom.

🎙️ The reactivity series is a very important tool in chemistry. It tells us which metals are more reactive than others, which is crucial for understanding displacement reactions and how metals corrode.

Displacement Reactions

  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
  • Example: Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.
  • Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

🎙️ Displacement reactions are a direct application of the reactivity series. If a metal is higher on the series, it can 'kick out' a lower metal from its compound, taking its place.

1Reactive Metal + Salt Solution (Less Reactive Metal)
2Salt Solution (Reactive Metal) + Less Reactive Metal

Everyday Uses

  • Metals: Construction, electrical wiring, jewelry, tools, vehicles.
  • Non-metals: Air (O₂, N₂), fertilizers, disinfectants, fuels, plastics.
  • Both are vital for our modern world.

🎙️ Metals are used everywhere, from the smallest electronic components to massive bridges. Non-metals are equally important, forming the air we breathe, the fuels we burn, and many essential chemicals.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Elements are classified into Metals and Non-metals.
  • Metals are generally shiny, malleable, ductile, conductive, sonorous.
  • Non-metals are generally dull, brittle, non-conductive, non-sonorous.
  • Chemical reactions vary based on reactivity.
