  • Corrosive nature, can burn skin.
  • Release H+ ions in water.

🎙️ While some acids are safe to consume in food, concentrated acids are highly corrosive and can cause severe burns. This is due to their ability to release hydrogen ions when dissolved in water.

Properties of Bases: The Caustic Touch

  • Bitter taste (don't taste in lab!).
  • Soapy/slippery to touch.
  • Release OH- ions in water.

🎙️ Just as acids release H+ ions, bases release hydroxide (OH-) ions when dissolved in water. Strong bases can also be corrosive, so handling them with care is essential, just like strong acids.

Neutralisation: The Balance Act!

  • Acid reacts with a base.
  • Forms salt and water.
  • The acidic and basic properties are cancelled out.

🎙️ Neutralisation is a fundamental reaction where an acid and a base react to form a neutral substance, a salt, and water. It's like balancing the scales – the strong properties of both acid and base are cancelled out.

What are Salts? The Product of Neutralisation

  • Formed from the reaction of an acid and a base.
  • Composed of a cation from a base and an anion from an acid.
  • Example: Sodium Chloride (common salt).

🎙️ Salts are ionic compounds formed when the hydrogen ion of an acid is replaced by a metal ion or an ammonium ion. They are the 'product' of the neutralisation reaction, and many are essential for life.

🧂

Families of Salts: Acidic, Basic, Neutral

  • Strong Acid + Strong Base → Neutral Salt (e.g., NaCl)
  • Strong Acid + Weak Base → Acidic Salt (e.g., NH4Cl)
  • Weak Acid + Strong Base → Basic Salt (e.g., Na2CO3)

🎙️ Salts aren't always neutral. Their nature depends on the strength of the acid and base from which they are formed. A strong acid and strong base give a neutral salt, while a strong acid with a weak base gives an acidic salt, and so on.

The pH Scale: Measuring Strength

  • Measures the strength of acids and bases.
  • Ranges from 0 to 14.
  • pH 7 is neutral; below 7 is acidic; above 7 is basic.

🎙️ The pH scale is a fantastic tool that tells us exactly how acidic or basic a substance is. It's like a scientific ruler for acidity and alkalinity, with 7 being perfectly neutral, like pure water.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing acid/base taste with lab identification (NEVER taste!).
  • Misremembering litmus paper colour changes (Acid: Blue to Red; Base: Red to Blue).
  • Forgetting the 'pop' test for hydrogen gas and limewater test for CO2.

🎙️ Students often make a few common errors. Always remember safety first – never taste chemicals! And practice those indicator colour changes and gas tests; they are frequently asked in exams.
