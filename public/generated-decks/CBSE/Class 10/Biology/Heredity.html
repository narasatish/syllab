What is Heredity?

  • The passing of traits from parents to offspring.
  • Why offspring resemble their parents.
  • Think of it as a family recipe being passed down.

🎙️ Heredity is simply the process by which features or characteristics are passed from one generation to the next. It's why a mango tree always produces mangoes, and not apples!

What is Variation?

  • Differences among individuals of the same species.
  • Why you are unique, even from your siblings.
  • No two individuals are exactly alike (except identical twins).

🎙️ While heredity explains resemblances, variation explains differences. Even within the same family, siblings look different. This diversity is crucial for evolution and survival.

Why Mendel Chose Pea Plants

  • Easy to grow and maintain.
  • Short life cycle, allowing many generations quickly.
  • Clearly distinguishable traits (e.g., tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled seeds).

🎙️ Mendel's choice of pea plants was brilliant. They offered several advantages, making them ideal subjects for studying inheritance patterns. Imagine trying to do this with elephants!

Mendel's Monohybrid Cross: One Trait

  • Crossing plants differing in only one trait (e.g., tall vs. dwarf).
  • Parental generation (P): Pure tall x Pure dwarf.
  • First Filial generation (F1): All offspring were tall.

🎙️ Mendel started with a monohybrid cross, focusing on a single characteristic, like plant height. He crossed pure tall plants with pure dwarf plants and observed the first generation of offspring.

Monohybrid Cross: F1 to F2 Generation

  • F1 generation plants were self-pollinated.
  • Second Filial generation (F2): Both tall and dwarf plants appeared.
  • Ratio of tall to dwarf in F2 was approximately 3:1.

🎙️ The surprising result was in the F2 generation. When the F1 tall plants were self-pollinated, both tall and dwarf plants reappeared, but in a specific ratio. This was a crucial discovery.

Mendel's Laws: Law of Dominance

  • In a pair of contrasting traits, one trait masks the other.
  • The trait that expresses itself is 'dominant'.
  • The masked trait is 'recessive'.

🎙️ From his monohybrid crosses, Mendel proposed the Law of Dominance. It explains why all F1 plants were tall – the tall trait dominated over the dwarf trait.

💪
Stronger Trait
16 / 30

Mendel's Laws: Law of Segregation

  • Alleles for a trait separate during gamete formation.
  • Each gamete receives only one allele.
  • Ensures genetic variation in offspring.

🎙️ The Law of Segregation explains how the recessive trait reappeared in the F2 generation. It states that during the formation of reproductive cells, the two alleles for a trait separate from each other.

Genes and Alleles: The Blueprint Details

  • Genes: Units of heredity, located on chromosomes.
  • Alleles: Different forms of a gene (e.g., tall allele, dwarf allele).
  • Each individual has two alleles for each gene (one from each parent).

🎙️ Now let's introduce the modern terms. Mendel didn't know about genes, but his 'factors' are what we now call genes. Alleles are the different versions of these genes.

Genotype vs. Phenotype

  • Genotype: Genetic makeup of an individual (e.g., TT, Tt, tt).
  • Phenotype: Observable physical characteristics (e.g., Tall, Dwarf).
  • Genotype determines phenotype.

🎙️ It's important to distinguish between genotype, which is the internal genetic code, and phenotype, which is what we actually see. Two individuals can have the same phenotype but different genotypes.

Dihybrid Cross: Inheriting Two Traits

  • Crossing plants differing in two traits (e.g., seed shape AND seed color).

  • Example: Round-Yellow x Wrinkled-Green.

🎙️ Mendel didn't stop at one trait. He also studied dihybrid crosses, looking at two traits simultaneously. This led to another important law.

Law of Independent Assortment

  • Alleles for different traits assort independently during gamete formation.
  • The inheritance of one trait does not influence another.
  • Increases genetic variation in offspring.

🎙️ This law states that the inheritance of one characteristic, like seed color, does not affect the inheritance of another characteristic, like seed shape. They are passed on independently of each other.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Confusing genotype and phenotype.

  • Mixing up Mendel's laws (e.g., segregation vs. independent assortment).

🎙️ It's easy to get confused with new terms. Remember, genotype is the genetic code, phenotype is what you see. Dominant doesn't mean more frequent, just that it masks the recessive.
