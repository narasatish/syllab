What is Heredity?

  • The process of passing characteristics from parents to offspring.
  • Ensures continuity and similarities within a species.
  • Also known as 'inheritance'.

🎙️ Heredity is like a secret recipe passed down through generations. It dictates why a human baby always grows into a human, and not, say, a cat! It's the reason you might have your mother's eyes or your father's smile.

Why aren't we identical?

  • Differences among individuals of the same species are called variation.
  • Arise during reproduction, especially sexual reproduction.
  • Crucial for survival and evolution of species.

🎙️ Even though you inherit traits, you're not an exact clone of your parents or siblings. These small differences are called variations. Think about siblings in a family – they share parents, but each one is unique!

How Variations Add Up

  • Variations occur in each new generation.
  • These variations are passed on to the next generation.
  • Over many generations, significant variations accumulate.

🎙️ Imagine a small change in one generation, like a slightly different shade of hair. If this trait is passed on and further modified over many generations, it can lead to noticeable differences within a population. This accumulation is key to how species change over time.

Gregor Mendel: Father of Genetics

  • Austrian monk who experimented with pea plants.
  • Proposed fundamental laws of inheritance.
  • His work laid the foundation for modern genetics.

🎙️ Gregor Mendel, an Austrian monk, is considered the 'Father of Genetics'. He meticulously studied pea plants in his monastery garden, carefully observing how traits like height and seed color were passed down. His groundbreaking work, though initially overlooked, revolutionized our understanding of heredity.

Monohybrid Cross: Studying One Trait

  • A cross involving parents differing in only one contrasting character.
  • Helps understand inheritance patterns for a single gene.
  • Example: Crossing tall pea plants with dwarf pea plants.

🎙️ To simplify his experiments, Mendel first focused on just one trait at a time, like plant height. This type of study is called a monohybrid cross. It allows us to clearly see how a single characteristic is inherited from one generation to the next.

🌿
Focusing on one feature

Monohybrid Cross: Tall vs. Dwarf

  • Parents: Pure Tall (TT) x Pure Dwarf (tt).
  • F1 Generation: All Tall (Tt).
  • F2 Generation: Tall (TT, Tt) and Dwarf (tt) in 3:1 ratio.

🎙️ When Mendel crossed pure tall pea plants with pure dwarf pea plants, all the offspring in the first generation (F1) were tall! But when he self-pollinated these F1 plants, the next generation (F2) showed both tall and dwarf plants in a 3:1 ratio. This was a crucial observation!

Dominant vs. Recessive Traits

  • Dominant: Trait expressed even with one copy (e.g., Tallness).
  • Recessive: Trait expressed only with two copies (e.g., Dwarfness).

🎙️ Mendel realized that some traits, like tallness, 'dominated' over others, like dwarfness. We call these dominant traits. Recessive traits only appear when the dominant trait is completely absent. Think of it like a strong voice (dominant) and a soft voice (recessive) – you only hear the soft voice if the strong one is silent.

Genes and Alleles: Units of Heredity

  • Gene: A segment of DNA coding for a specific trait.
  • Allele: Different forms of the same gene (e.g., for tallness or dwarfness).
  • We inherit one allele from each parent for every gene.

🎙️ Think of a gene as a specific instruction in our body's manual, for example, an instruction for 'eye color'. Alleles are the different versions of that instruction – like 'blue eye color' or 'brown eye color'. We get one copy of each instruction, or allele, from each of our parents.

Genetic instructions

Genotype vs. Phenotype

  • Genotype: The genetic makeup of an individual (e.g., TT, Tt, tt).
  • Phenotype: The observable physical characteristics (e.g., Tall, Dwarf).
  • Genotype determines phenotype.

🎙️ Genotype is like the hidden code or blueprint inside you – the actual combination of alleles you possess. Phenotype is what you can actually see – your physical appearance. For example, a pea plant with genotype 'Tt' has a phenotype of 'Tall'.

Dihybrid Cross: Studying Two Traits

  • A cross involving parents differing in two pairs of contrasting characters.
  • Helps understand independent inheritance of different genes.
  • Example: Seed shape (round/wrinkled) and seed color (yellow/green).

🎙️ After mastering one trait, Mendel moved on to studying two traits simultaneously, like seed shape and seed color. This is called a dihybrid cross. It helped him discover that different traits are inherited independently of each other.

🌼

Dihybrid Cross: Round Yellow x Wrinkled Green

  • Parents: Pure Round Yellow (RRYY) x Pure Wrinkled Green (rryy).
  • F1 Generation: All Round Yellow (RrYy).
  • F2 Generation: Shows 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio.

🎙️ When Mendel crossed a pure round yellow pea plant with a pure wrinkled green one, all F1 plants produced round yellow seeds. But in the F2 generation, he observed all four combinations, including new ones like round green and wrinkled yellow, in a specific 9:3:3:1 ratio. This confirmed independent assortment.

From Genes to Traits: The Protein Connection

  • Genes contain information for making proteins.
  • Proteins perform specific functions in the body.
  • These functions determine our traits (e.g., pigment production).

🎙️ How does a gene actually make you tall or short? It's all about proteins! Genes are like blueprints for building specific proteins. These proteins then carry out various jobs in your body, ultimately leading to the expression of a particular trait, like producing the pigment for your eye color.

Sex Determination: How Gender is Decided

  • The process by which the sex of an offspring is determined.
  • In many species, it involves specific sex chromosomes.
  • Can be influenced by genetic or environmental factors.

🎙️ Have you ever wondered how it's decided whether a baby will be a boy or a girl? This process is called sex determination. In humans and many other animals, special chromosomes play a key role in this decision.

Human Sex Determination: XX and XY

  • Females have two X chromosomes (XX).
  • Males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY).
  • The father's sperm determines the sex of the baby.

🎙️ In humans, females have two X chromosomes, while males have one X and one Y chromosome. When a mother produces eggs, they all carry an X chromosome. But a father produces sperm that can carry either an X or a Y. So, it's the father's sperm that ultimately determines if the baby will be a boy (XY) or a girl (XX).

Acquired vs. Inherited Traits

  • Acquired Traits: Developed during an individual's lifetime, not passed on.
  • Inherited Traits: Passed from parents to offspring through genes.
  • Only inherited traits contribute to heredity.

🎙️ It's important to distinguish between traits we are born with and those we gain during our lives. Acquired traits, like a scar or a skill, are not passed down genetically. Only inherited traits, coded in our genes, are part of the hereditary process.

Acquired Trait Example: Learning to Swim

  • Learning to swim is a skill developed through practice.
  • It does not change your genetic makeup.
  • Your children will not automatically know how to swim.

🎙️ If you spend years learning to swim and become an expert, that's an amazing acquired trait! However, this skill won't be passed on to your children genetically. They will have to learn to swim themselves, just like you did.

Inherited Trait Example: Eye Colour

  • Eye colour is determined by genes inherited from parents.
  • It is present from birth and does not change with learning.
  • Your children may inherit your eye colour genes.

🎙️ On the other hand, your eye colour is an inherited trait. It's determined by the genes you received from your parents and is present from birth. You can't learn to change your eye colour, and it's a characteristic that can be passed on to your offspring.

Heredity Drives Evolution

  • Variations passed through heredity are the raw material for evolution.
  • Natural selection acts on these variations.
  • Over generations, populations adapt and evolve.

🎙️ Heredity is not just about individuals, it's also fundamental to the bigger picture of evolution. The variations that are passed down from generation to generation are what natural selection acts upon, leading to the gradual adaptation and development of new species over vast periods of time.

Beyond Natural Heredity: Genetic Engineering

  • Technology to directly modify an organism's genes.
  • Used in medicine, agriculture, and research.
  • Raises important ethical considerations.

🎙️ While heredity is a natural process, advancements in science have led to genetic engineering. This technology allows scientists to directly manipulate genes, opening up possibilities like curing genetic diseases or creating improved crops, but it also comes with important ethical discussions.
