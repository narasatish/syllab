How Nerve Impulses Travel: The Synapse

  • Electrical signal travels along the axon.
  • At the nerve ending, chemicals (neurotransmitters) are released.
  • These chemicals cross the synapse and bind to the next neuron.

🎙️ Nerve impulses are like a relay race. The electrical signal rushes down the axon. When it reaches the end, it doesn't jump to the next neuron directly. Instead, it releases chemical messengers called neurotransmitters across a tiny gap called a synapse, which then pass the message on.

The Reflex Arc Pathway: A Shortcut for Speed

  • Stimulus detected by receptors.
  • Sensory neuron carries signal to spinal cord.
  • Interneuron connects sensory to motor neuron.
  • Motor neuron sends signal to effector (muscle/gland).

🎙️ The reflex arc is a direct route that bypasses the brain for super-fast reactions. The signal goes from the receptor, through a sensory neuron, directly to the spinal cord, then immediately back out via a motor neuron to the muscle. The brain gets the message later, which is why you feel the pain *after* you've reacted.

Endocrine Glands: Hormone Factories

  • Ductless glands that secrete hormones directly into the blood.
  • Unlike exocrine glands, which have ducts (e.g., sweat glands).

🎙️ Endocrine glands are special factories that produce and release hormones. The key difference is they don't have ducts; they release their products directly into the bloodstream to travel to their targets. We have several important endocrine glands scattered throughout our body.

Feedback Mechanism: Keeping Hormones in Check

  • Levels of hormones are constantly monitored.

Plant Movements: Tropic vs. Nastic

  • Tropic Movements: Directional, growth-related (e.g., towards light).
  • Nastic Movements: Non-directional, independent of stimulus direction (e.g., touch-me-not plant).
  • Plants respond to stimuli too!

🎙️ Plants might not move like animals, but they certainly respond to their environment! Tropic movements are like a plant 'reaching' for something, like sunlight. Nastic movements are more like a sudden 'flinch', such as the 'touch-me-not' plant closing its leaves when touched. Both are forms of coordination.

Phototropism & Geotropism: Plant Responses to Light & Gravity

  • Phototropism: Plant growth towards light (stems positive, roots negative).
  • Geotropism (Gravitropism): Plant growth in response to gravity (roots positive, stems negative).
  • Crucial for survival and proper orientation.

🎙️ These are classic examples of tropic movements. Phototropism explains why plants grow towards a window – they need light! Geotropism explains why roots always grow downwards into the soil, anchoring the plant and seeking water, while stems grow upwards against gravity.
