What are Life Processes?

  • Basic functions performed by living organisms.
  • Maintain life and ensure survival.
  • Examples: Nutrition, Respiration, Transportation, Excretion.

🎙️ Life processes are the basic functions that all living things carry out to stay alive. Think of them as the maintenance work that keeps a house running smoothly. Without these processes, life simply cannot continue.

How do Organisms Get Food?

  • Autotrophic: Make their own food.
  • Heterotrophic: Depend on others for food.

🎙️ There are two main ways organisms obtain their food. Some, like plants, are 'self-feeders' or autotrophs, while others, like animals and humans, are 'other-feeders' or heterotrophs, relying on autotrophs or other heterotrophs.

Steps in Photosynthesis

🎙️ Photosynthesis isn't a single step but a series of reactions. First, chlorophyll captures sunlight. This energy is then used to split water and convert carbon dioxide into glucose, the plant's food.

Heterotrophic Nutrition: Diverse Ways

  • Holozoic: Ingests solid food (e.g., humans).
  • Saprophytic: Feeds on dead matter (e.g., fungi).
  • Parasitic: Lives on/in host (e.g., tapeworm).

🎙️ Heterotrophs have evolved different strategies to get their food. Holozoic organisms actively eat, saprophytes break down dead material, and parasites live off another living organism, sometimes harming it.

🍔
Eating Food

Holozoic Nutrition: 5 Key Steps

  • Ingestion: Taking food in.
  • Digestion: Breaking down food.
  • Absorption: Nutrients enter blood.
  • Assimilation: Using absorbed nutrients.
  • Egestion: Removing undigested food.

🎙️ Holozoic nutrition, common in animals, involves a series of steps from eating the food to utilizing its nutrients and finally expelling waste. Each step is crucial for the organism's survival.

Respiration: Releasing Energy

  • Process of releasing energy from food.
  • Aerobic: With oxygen.
  • Anaerobic: Without oxygen.

🎙️ Respiration is often confused with breathing, but it's much more! It's the chemical process where our cells break down glucose to release energy, which powers all our activities. This can happen with or without oxygen.

Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Respiration

  • Aerobic: More energy, CO2 + H2O products.
  • Anaerobic: Less energy, alcohol/lactic acid products.

🎙️ The key difference is oxygen. Aerobic respiration is very efficient, producing a lot of energy, while anaerobic respiration is less efficient, yielding less energy and different byproducts.

Aerobic Respiration in Humans

  • Occurs in mitochondria.
  • Glucose + Oxygen -> Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy.
  • Main energy source for most body functions.

🎙️ In our bodies, aerobic respiration primarily takes place in the mitochondria, often called the 'powerhouses' of the cell. This is where glucose is fully broken down using oxygen to produce a large amount of ATP energy.

Anaerobic Respiration: Without Oxygen

  • Occurs in cytoplasm.
  • Glucose -> Alcohol + CO2 + Energy (in yeast).
  • Glucose -> Lactic Acid + Energy (in muscles).

🎙️ When oxygen is scarce, cells can switch to anaerobic respiration. This happens in our muscle cells during intense exercise, leading to lactic acid build-up and muscle fatigue, or in organisms like yeast.

Transportation in Plants: Xylem and Phloem

  • Phloem: Transports food from leaves to other parts of the plant.

🎙️ Plants also have their own transport systems. Xylem acts like a pipeline for water and minerals, moving them upwards, while phloem distributes the food made in leaves to all parts of the plant, including roots.

Excretion: Removing Waste
