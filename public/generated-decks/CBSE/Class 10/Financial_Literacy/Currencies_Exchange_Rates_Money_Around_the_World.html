Understanding Exchange Rate

  • Price of one currency in terms of another.
  • It tells you how much foreign currency you get for your local money.
  • Example: 1 USD = 83 INR.

🎙️ An exchange rate is crucial for international transactions. It's like a conversion factor, telling you the value of one currency when you want to trade it for another. This rate changes constantly.

How Exchange Rates Work: A Simple Example

Types of Exchange Rate Systems

  • Fixed Exchange Rate System
  • Flexible (Floating) Exchange Rate System

🎙️ Countries adopt different systems to manage their currency's value. The two main types are fixed and flexible exchange rate systems, each with its own advantages and disadvantages.

Fixed Rate
Flexible Rate
12 / 25

Fixed Exchange Rate System

  • Government or central bank sets the rate.
  • Rate is pegged to another major currency or gold.
  • Requires active intervention to maintain.

🎙️ In a fixed exchange rate system, the government or central bank decides the value of its currency relative to another currency or a commodity like gold. They then intervene in the market to keep it at that level.

🔒
Stable Value
13 / 25

Flexible (Floating) Exchange Rate System

  • Determined by market forces (demand & supply).
  • No direct government intervention.
  • Rate fluctuates continuously.

🎙️ Most major economies today use a flexible exchange rate system. Here, the value of the currency is determined purely by the forces of demand and supply in the foreign exchange market, leading to constant fluctuations.

🌊
Market Driven

Factors Affecting Exchange Rates: Demand & Supply

  • High demand for a currency raises its value.
  • High supply of a currency lowers its value.
  • Influenced by trade, investment, and tourism.

🎙️ Just like any other good or service, the price of a currency is heavily influenced by demand and supply. If many people want to buy US dollars, its value against other currencies will likely increase.

⚖️
Market Balance

Other Factors Affecting Exchange Rates

  • Interest Rates: Higher rates attract foreign investment.
  • Inflation: High inflation can devalue a currency.
  • Political Stability: Stable countries attract more investment.

🎙️ Beyond basic demand and supply, several other economic and political factors play a significant role. Higher interest rates, for instance, can make a country's bonds more attractive, increasing demand for its currency.

Worked Example 1: Converting INR to USD

  • You have ₹10,000. Exchange rate: 1 USD = ₹82.
  • Amount in USD = ₹10,000 / ₹82 per USD.
  • You will get approximately 121.95 USD.

🎙️ Let's try a calculation. If you have ten thousand rupees and the dollar is trading at 82 rupees, you simply divide your rupees by the exchange rate to find out how many dollars you will receive.

Worked Example 2: Converting USD to INR

  • You have 200 USD. Exchange rate: 1 USD = ₹83.
  • Amount in INR = 200 USD * ₹83 per USD.
  • You will get ₹16,600.

🎙️ Now, let's reverse the conversion. If you are returning from the US with 200 dollars and the rate is 83 rupees per dollar, you multiply the dollars by the rate to find out how many rupees you will get back.

Benefits of Stable Exchange Rates

  • Predictability for businesses and investors.
  • Reduced risk in international trade.
  • Encourages long-term foreign investment.

🎙️ Stable exchange rates are generally good for an economy. They provide certainty, allowing businesses to plan imports and exports without fear of sudden currency value shifts, which encourages more international trade and investment.

✅
Economic Certainty

Risks of Volatile Exchange Rates

  • Uncertainty for businesses and travelers.
  • Increased risk for international transactions.
  • Can lead to economic instability.

🎙️ On the flip side, highly volatile exchange rates can be problematic. They create uncertainty, making it difficult for businesses to predict costs and revenues, potentially discouraging international trade and investment due to higher risks.
