What are Commodities?

  • Basic goods used in commerce.
  • Interchangeable with other goods of the same type.
  • Raw materials for industries and daily life.

🎙️ Think of commodities as the fundamental building blocks of our economy. They are raw materials or agricultural products that are essential for making other things or for our daily consumption. One kilogram of wheat is generally the same, no matter where it comes from.

Why Do People Trade Commodities?

  • For consumption (e.g., fuel, food).
  • For production (e.g., metals for manufacturing).
  • As an investment to make profit.
  • To hedge against future price changes.

🎙️ People trade commodities for various reasons. Farmers sell their produce, industries buy raw materials, and investors try to profit from price movements. It's all about meeting needs and managing risks.

Types of Commodities: Hard vs. Soft

  • Hard commodities: Mined from the earth (e.g., gold, oil).
  • Soft commodities: Grown or raised (e.g., wheat, coffee, livestock).

🎙️ Commodities are broadly categorized into 'hard' and 'soft'. Hard commodities are finite resources extracted from the ground, while soft commodities are renewable resources that are cultivated or farmed. Both are vital for our world.

How Commodity Prices are Determined

  • Primarily by supply and demand.
  • Global economic conditions.
  • Geopolitical events and weather patterns.
  • Technological advancements.

🎙️ Commodity prices are dynamic and influenced by a multitude of factors. A poor monsoon can drastically affect crop yields and push up food prices, while global conflicts can disrupt oil supplies and send prices soaring.

Supply and Demand: The Basic Rule

  • High demand + Low supply = Higher prices.
  • Low demand + High supply = Lower prices.
  • This balance drives market prices for all commodities.

🎙️ Imagine if everyone wants to buy mangoes, but there are very few available – the price will go up! Conversely, if there are too many mangoes and not enough buyers, prices will fall. This simple principle governs commodity prices.

Futures Contracts: A Way to Trade

  • An agreement to buy or sell a commodity at a predetermined price on a future date.
  • Used by producers to lock in prices and by investors to speculate.
  • Standardized and traded on exchanges.

🎙️ Futures contracts are powerful tools used in commodity markets. Imagine a farmer agreeing today to sell his harvest next year at a fixed price, protecting him from price drops. This is how futures work.

🤝
Future Agreement

Understanding Futures: An Example

  • Farmer expects 1000 kg of wheat in 3 months.
  • Worries price might fall, so sells a future contract today at ₹25/kg.
  • Buyer agrees to pay ₹25/kg in 3 months, securing supply.
  • Both manage their risk and certainty.

🎙️ Let's say a farmer knows he'll have wheat ready in three months. To avoid uncertainty, he can sell a 'futures contract' today. This locks in a price, giving him peace of mind, and assuring the buyer of a future supply.

Risks in Commodity Trading

  • Price Volatility: Prices can change rapidly.
  • Storage Costs: For physical commodities.
  • Geopolitical Risks: Conflicts can disrupt supply.
  • Weather Risks: Affects agricultural commodities.

🎙️ While commodity trading can be profitable, it's not without risks. Prices can swing wildly, especially for things like oil. Natural disasters or political instability can also suddenly impact supplies and prices.
