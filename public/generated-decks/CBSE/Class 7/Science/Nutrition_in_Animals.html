  • For energy to perform daily activities.
  • For growth and development.
  • For repair of damaged body parts.
  • To protect against diseases.

🎙️ Just like us, all animals need food to survive. Food provides the essential fuel and building blocks for every function their bodies perform, from running to repairing a tiny cell.

Two Main Modes of Nutrition

  • Autotrophic: Organisms make their own food (e.g., plants).
  • Heterotrophic: Organisms obtain food from others (e.g., animals).

🎙️ You've learned about plants making their own food. Animals, however, are different. They cannot make their own food and must rely on other organisms, making them heterotrophs.

Animals: Always Heterotrophs

  • Animals depend directly or indirectly on plants for food.
  • They consume other organisms or their products.
  • This makes them part of a food chain.

🎙️ Whether an animal eats plants directly or eats another animal that ate plants, the ultimate source of energy traces back to plants. This fundamental dependency defines heterotrophic nutrition.

Herbivores: Plant Eaters

  • Animals that feed solely on plants and plant products.
  • Examples: Cow, goat, deer, rabbit.
  • Have specialized teeth and digestive systems for plant matter.

🎙️ Cows grazing in a field are a perfect example of herbivores. Their flat, strong teeth are ideal for grinding tough plant fibers, and their digestive systems are adapted to break down cellulose.

Carnivores: Meat Eaters

  • Animals that feed only on other animals.
  • Examples: Lion, tiger, snake, eagle.
  • Possess sharp teeth or claws for hunting and tearing flesh.

🎙️ Lions are powerful carnivores. Their sharp canines and strong jaws are perfectly designed for catching and tearing meat from their prey. They play a crucial role in controlling animal populations.

Omnivores: Both Plant & Meat Eaters

  • Animals that consume both plants and animals.
  • Examples: Humans, bear, crow, dog.
  • Have a more versatile digestive system.

🎙️ Humans are a great example of omnivores. We enjoy both fruits and vegetables, as well as meat. Our teeth and digestive system are adapted to process a wide variety of foods.

What is Digestion?

  • The process of breaking down complex food substances.
  • Into simpler, absorbable forms.
  • A physical and chemical process.

🎙️ When we eat, our body can't directly use the complex molecules in food. Digestion is like a factory process that breaks these big molecules into tiny ones that our body cells can actually absorb and use.

  • 2. Digestion

  • 4. Assimilation
  • 5. Egestion

🎙️ Nutrition in animals is a continuous process involving these five distinct steps. We will now look at each of these steps in more detail to understand how food is processed in the body.

Step 1: Ingestion - Taking Food In

  • The process of taking food into the body.
  • Different animals have different methods.
  • Examples: Human uses hands, Amoeba uses pseudopodia.

🎙️ Ingestion is simply putting food into your mouth. But for other animals, it can be very different, like a snake swallowing its prey whole or a butterfly sipping nectar with its proboscis.

👄
Entry point

Step 2: Digestion - Breaking Down Food

  • Complex food molecules are broken down into simpler ones.
  • Involves mechanical and chemical breakdown.
  • Enzymes play a key role in chemical digestion.

🎙️ Once ingested, food undergoes digestion. Mechanical digestion involves chewing, while chemical digestion uses digestive juices and enzymes to chemically alter the food molecules.

Step 3: Absorption - Into the Blood

  • The process where digested food passes into the blood vessels.
  • Mainly occurs in the small intestine in humans.
  • Nutrients are now available for the body.

🎙️ After food is broken down, the small, simple nutrient molecules are ready to be absorbed. They pass through the walls of the digestive tract and enter the bloodstream to be transported throughout the body.

🩸
Nutrients enter blood
14 / 25

Step 4: Assimilation - Using the Nutrients

  • The utilization of absorbed nutrients by body cells.
  • For energy, growth, and repair.
  • Building new complex substances from simple ones.

🎙️ Assimilation is when your body actually puts the absorbed nutrients to work. Cells use glucose for energy, amino acids to build proteins for growth, and fatty acids for energy storage and cell membranes.

💪
Body uses food

Step 5: Egestion - Removing Waste

  • The process of eliminating undigested food material from the body.
  • This waste is called faeces.
  • An essential step to remove unwanted substances.

🎙️ Not all food we eat can be digested and absorbed. The remaining undigested material needs to be removed from the body, and this final step is called egestion, ensuring no harmful waste builds up.

Nutrition in Amoeba: A Simple Case

  • Amoeba is a single-celled organism.
  • Uses pseudopodia to engulf food (phagocytosis).
  • Food is digested in a food vacuole.
  • Absorption and egestion also occur.

🎙️ Amoeba provides a great example of all five steps of nutrition in a very simple form. It literally 'eats' by surrounding its food with its false feet, forming a food vacuole for digestion.

Human Digestive System: An Overview

  • A long tube (alimentary canal) and associated glands.
  • Starts from the mouth and ends at the anus.
  • Main organs: Mouth, Oesophagus, Stomach, Small Intestine, Large Intestine.

🎙️ The human digestive system is much more complex than Amoeba's, but it follows the same fundamental steps. It's a fascinating journey food takes through various organs, each with a specific role.

Digestion in Ruminants (Cows, Buffaloes)

  • Ruminants are grass-eating animals.
  • They have a special stomach with four chambers.
  • The first chamber is called the rumen.

🎙️ Cows and buffaloes are fascinating because they eat a lot of grass, which is hard to digest. They have a unique digestive system that allows them to extract maximum nutrients from their fibrous diet.

Special stomach

Ruminant Digestion: Chewing the Cud

  • Partially digested food (cud) is brought back to the mouth.
  • Animal chews it again thoroughly.
  • This process is called rumination.

🎙️ Have you ever seen a cow chewing even when it's not eating? That's rumination! They chew the cud to further break down the tough plant material, making it easier for complete digestion in later stomach chambers.

Special Adaptations for Nutrition

  • Animals have evolved unique features for feeding.
  • Examples: Long neck of giraffe for high leaves.
  • Sharp beaks of birds for cracking seeds or tearing prey.

🎙️ Nature is full of incredible adaptations! A giraffe's long neck allows it to reach leaves high up, while a woodpecker's strong beak helps it find insects in tree bark. These adaptations ensure they can get the food they need.

Chapter Recap: Nutrition in Animals

  • Animals are heterotrophs (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores).
  • Nutrition involves Ingestion, Digestion, Absorption, Assimilation, Egestion.
  • Digestion breaks down complex food into simple forms.
  • Ruminants have a special digestive process (rumination).
