  • Happens in all living organisms.
  • Essential for survival and all life activities.

  • Food (glucose) is broken down.
  • Energy is released in the form of ATP.

🎙️ When we talk about respiration, we are often referring to cellular respiration. This is the microscopic process happening within each and every cell of our body. It's where the actual energy release from food occurs.

The Respiration Equation

  • Food + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy
  • Glucose is the main food source.
  • Oxygen helps in breaking down glucose.

🎙️ This is the simplified equation for respiration. You can see that food, usually glucose, combines with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, water, and most importantly, energy. Remember this fundamental process.

1Food (Glucose)
2+ Oxygen
3→ Carbon Dioxide
4+ Water
5+ Energy

  • Aerobic Respiration: Needs oxygen.
  • Anaerobic Respiration: No oxygen needed.
  • Both release energy, but differently.

🎙️ There are two main types of respiration, depending on whether oxygen is available or not. Aerobic respiration is what usually happens in our bodies, but anaerobic respiration can also occur in specific situations.

🎙️ Aerobic respiration is very efficient. When there's plenty of oxygen, our cells can fully break down glucose, releasing a significant amount of energy. This is the primary way our bodies get energy for sustained activities.

Anaerobic Respiration

  • Occurs without oxygen.
  • Releases much less energy.
  • Produces different by-products, like lactic acid or alcohol.

🎙️ Sometimes, our cells don't get enough oxygen, especially during intense exercise. In such cases, anaerobic respiration kicks in. It's a quick way to get some energy, but it's less efficient and produces different waste products.

Anaerobic Respiration: Muscles

  • During heavy exercise, muscles may lack oxygen.
  • Glucose breaks down to lactic acid + energy.
  • Lactic acid causes muscle cramps.

🎙️ Have you ever felt a burning sensation or cramps in your muscles after a very intense workout? That's often due to lactic acid build-up from anaerobic respiration. Your muscles were working so hard they couldn't get enough oxygen!

Breathing vs. Respiration

  • Breathing: Physical process of taking in and letting out air.
  • Respiration: Chemical process of energy release inside cells.
  • Breathing is a part of the overall respiratory process.

🎙️ It's common to confuse breathing and respiration, but they are different! Breathing is the physical act of inhaling and exhaling air. Respiration is the cellular process of breaking down food for energy, and breathing helps supply the oxygen needed for it.

How We Breathe In (Inhalation)

  • Diaphragm moves down.
  • Rib cage moves up and out.
  • Space in chest cavity increases, air rushes in.

🎙️ When we inhale, our diaphragm contracts and moves downwards, and our rib cage expands. This increases the space in our chest cavity, creating lower pressure, which draws air into our lungs.

1Diaphragm Down
2Ribs Up/Out
3Chest Space ⬆️
4Air In!
17 / 25

How We Breathe Out (Exhalation)

  • Diaphragm moves up.
  • Rib cage moves down and in.
  • Space in chest cavity decreases, air pushes out.

🎙️ Exhalation is the opposite. Our diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards, and the rib cage moves inwards. This reduces the chest cavity space, increasing pressure and forcing carbon dioxide-rich air out of our lungs.

1Diaphragm Up
2Ribs Down/In
3Chest Space ⬇️
4Air Out!

Respiration: Fish

  • Breathe using specialized organs called gills.
  • Gills extract oxygen dissolved in water.
  • Water passes over gills, oxygen absorbed into blood.

🎙️ Fish have a specialized respiratory organ called gills. They take in water through their mouths, and as it passes over their gills, the gills absorb the dissolved oxygen from the water. It's a very efficient system for aquatic life.

Respiration: Cockroach

  • Breathe through small openings called spiracles.
  • Air enters a network of tubes called tracheae.
  • Oxygen reaches cells directly through these tubes.

🎙️ Insects like cockroaches have a unique respiratory system. They don't have lungs or gills. Instead, they have tiny holes on their bodies called spiracles, which lead to a network of air tubes called tracheae that carry oxygen directly to their cells.

Respiration in Plants

  • Plants also respire 24/7.
  • Take in oxygen, give out carbon dioxide.
  • Occurs through stomata (leaves) and lenticels (stems).

🎙️ Many people think plants only perform photosynthesis, but they also respire! They take in oxygen and release carbon dioxide, just like us. This happens continuously, day and night, through tiny pores on their leaves and stems.
