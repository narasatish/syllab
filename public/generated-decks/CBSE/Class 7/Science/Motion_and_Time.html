What is Motion?

  • Change in position of an object.
  • Observed over a period of time.
  • Relative to a fixed reference point.

🎙️ Formally, motion is defined as the change in the position of an object with respect to its surroundings in a given interval of time. If an object's position changes compared to a stationary object nearby, we say it is in motion. Think of a car moving past a parked tree.

  • Example: Earth revolving around the Sun.

What is Speed?

  • The distance covered by an object.
  • In a unit amount of time.
  • It tells us 'how fast' something is moving.

🎙️ Speed is a measure of how quickly an object is moving. It quantifies the rate at which an object changes its position. A higher speed means an object covers more distance in the same amount of time, or covers the same distance in less time.

The Speed Formula

  • Speed = Distance / Time
  • This is the fundamental relationship.
  • Remember this formula well!

🎙️ To calculate speed, we simply divide the total distance covered by the object by the total time taken to cover that distance. This formula is crucial for solving problems related to motion. Make sure you understand what each component represents.

Units of Speed

  • Standard unit: metres per second (m/s).
  • Common unit: kilometres per hour (km/h).

🎙️ Just like we measure length in metres or kilometres, and time in seconds or hours, speed also has specific units. The SI unit for speed is metres per second (m/s). However, for longer distances and times, kilometres per hour (km/h) is very commonly used, especially for vehicles.

Worked Example - Speed Calculation

  • Distance (D) = 120 km
  • Time (T) = 2 hours
  • Speed (S) = D / T = 120 km / 2 h = 60 km/h.

🎙️ So, by dividing 120 km by 2 hours, we find that the car's speed is 60 kilometres per hour. This means that, on average, the car covers 60 kilometres every hour. This is a very typical calculation you'll encounter.

Uniform Motion

  • An object covers equal distances.
  • In equal intervals of time.
  • Moves at a constant speed.

🎙️ When an object travels equal distances in equal intervals of time, no matter how small those intervals are, it is said to be in uniform motion. This means its speed remains constant throughout the journey. A train moving at a steady speed on a straight track is a good example.

Non-Uniform Motion

  • An object covers unequal distances.
  • In equal intervals of time.
  • Its speed is changing.

🎙️ Conversely, if an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time, its motion is called non-uniform motion. This implies that the object's speed is not constant; it might be speeding up or slowing down. A bus stopping and starting at bus stops is a perfect example.

Standard Units of Time

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing distance with displacement.
  • Incorrect unit conversions (km/h to m/s).
  • Calculating average speed vs. instantaneous speed.

🎙️ Students often make errors in unit conversions, like forgetting to convert hours to seconds when calculating speed in m/s. Another common mistake is to confuse average speed (total distance/total time) with instantaneous speed (speed at a particular moment). Always double-check your units and understand the context of the problem.
