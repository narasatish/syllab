What is Wastewater?

  • Water that has been used
  • Contains dissolved and suspended impurities
  • Also called dirty water or sewage

🎙️ Wastewater is simply any water that has been used and now contains impurities. It's the water that goes down our drains after we've used it for various purposes. This water is no longer clean and safe to drink.

Sources of Wastewater

  • Homes (domestic sewage)
  • Industries (industrial effluent)
  • Hospitals, offices, schools

🎙️ Wastewater comes from many places. The biggest source is our homes, from bathing, washing clothes, and cooking. Industries, hospitals, and even schools also produce significant amounts of wastewater.

Understanding Sewage

  • Liquid waste from homes, industries, etc.
  • Contains water, human waste, chemicals, oils
  • A complex mixture of pollutants

🎙️ Sewage is a specific type of wastewater, primarily liquid waste containing human excreta, chemicals, oils, and other impurities. It's a highly contaminated mixture that needs proper handling.

Dangers of Untreated Sewage

  • Spreads diseases (cholera, typhoid)
  • Pollutes water bodies and soil
  • Harms aquatic life

🎙️ If sewage is released directly into rivers or soil without treatment, it causes serious problems. It contaminates our water sources, making them unsafe to drink, and can lead to outbreaks of dangerous diseases.

🦠

Impact on Rivers

  • Untreated sewage makes rivers black
  • Foul smell and unhealthy environment
  • Fish and other aquatic animals die

🎙️ Many of India's rivers suffer from pollution due to untreated sewage. You can often see the difference: a clean river is vibrant, while a polluted one looks dark, smells bad, and has very little life.

Wastewater and Water Pollution

  • Wastewater is a major cause of pollution
  • Makes water unfit for use
  • Affects human health and ecosystems

  • Network of pipes for transporting sewage
  • Includes sewers, manholes, pumping stations
  • Carries wastewater to treatment plants

🎙️ To manage sewage effectively, cities have a sewerage system. This is a complex underground infrastructure designed to collect all the wastewater and transport it safely to a treatment facility.

Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP)

  • Where wastewater is cleaned
  • Removes pollutants physically, chemically, biologically
  • Converts dirty water into safer water

🎙️ The Wastewater Treatment Plant, or WWTP, is the hero of our story. This is where the magic happens – where dirty, contaminated wastewater is transformed into water that is much cleaner and safer to release back into the environment.

Wastewater from homes and industries is treated in multiple stages to remove contaminants
14 / 25

Primary Treatment: Physical Removal

  • Bar screens remove large objects
  • Grit and sand settle down
  • Sedimentation removes suspended solids

🎙️ The first stage of treatment is primary treatment, which is mainly physical. Large debris is filtered out, then heavier solids like grit and sand settle, and finally, smaller suspended solids settle in large tanks.

1Bar Screens
2Grit Chamber
3Sedimentation Tank
15 / 25

Secondary Treatment: Biological Process

  • Aeration tanks introduce air
  • Beneficial bacteria consume organic matter
  • Removes dissolved impurities

🎙️ Next comes secondary treatment, a biological process. Air is pumped into the water, encouraging 'good' bacteria to grow. These bacteria feed on the organic impurities, cleaning the water further.

🦠🦠🦠🦠🦠🦠🦠
Bacteria at Work
16 / 25

Tertiary Treatment (Advanced Cleaning)

  • Further purification for specific uses
  • Filtration, disinfection (chlorine/UV)
  • Removes remaining pathogens and chemicals

🎙️ Sometimes, especially if the treated water needs to be very clean or reused, a tertiary treatment stage is added. This involves advanced filtration and disinfection methods to remove almost all remaining impurities and disease-causing germs.

🔬
Final Polish

A Working Treatment Plant

  • Complex infrastructure with many tanks
  • Continuous monitoring and testing
  • Ensures safe discharge of treated water

🎙️ A wastewater treatment plant is a sophisticated facility. It operates 24/7, with various tanks, pumps, and monitoring systems working together to ensure that the water released back into our rivers is as clean as possible.

By-products: Sludge and Biogas

  • Sludge: solid waste from treatment
  • Can be used as fertilizer after processing
  • Biogas: produced from sludge, used as fuel

🎙️ Even the waste from wastewater treatment can be useful! The solid waste, called sludge, can be treated and used as a fertilizer. Also, during sludge digestion, biogas is produced, which is a good source of energy.

Sanitation and Our Health

  • Proper disposal of waste is crucial
  • Poor sanitation causes many diseases
  • Safe water and hygiene prevent illness

🎙️ Good sanitation practices are fundamental for public health. When waste is not disposed of properly, it creates breeding grounds for germs, leading to widespread diseases like cholera, dysentery, and jaundice.

The Problem of Open Defecation

  • Practiced in areas lacking toilets
  • Contaminates soil and water sources
  • Major cause of waterborne diseases

🎙️ In many rural areas, open defecation is still a challenge due to lack of proper toilets. This directly contaminates fields, water bodies, and leads to serious health issues, especially for children.

Better Housekeeping at Home

  • Do not throw cooking oil/fats down the drain
  • Do not flush chemicals, paints, medicines
  • Use dustbins for solid waste

🎙️ We all have a role to play! Simple habits at home can make a big difference. Never pour oils or fats down the drain as they can block pipes. Also, chemicals and medicines should not be flushed.
