What Exactly is a Tunnel?

  • An underground passage.
  • Created through mountains or under water.
  • Connects two points, often for transport.

🎙️ A tunnel is essentially a pathway that goes through something solid, like a mountain, or even under a river or a sea. Its main purpose is to create a shortcut or a safe route.

🚇
Underground Passage
3 / 25

Why Do We Build Tunnels?

  • To shorten travel distances.
  • To avoid difficult terrain (mountains, rivers).
  • For safe passage of trains, cars, or water.

🎙️ Tunnels are incredibly useful. They help us cut down long routes, bypass high mountains, and provide a protected pathway for transportation, making travel faster and safer.

🛣️
Connecting Places

Different Types of Tunnels

  • Road Tunnels (for vehicles)
  • Railway Tunnels (for trains)
  • Water Tunnels (for aqueducts/pipes)
  • Utility Tunnels (for cables/services)

🎙️ From the tunnels we drive through to the ones carrying water to our homes, tunnels serve many purposes. Each type is designed specifically for its function.

Literary Device: Imagery

  • Using descriptive language to create mental pictures.
  • Appeals to our five senses (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste).
  • Helps us visualize and experience the scene vividly.

🎙️ Authors use imagery to create vivid pictures in our minds. It's like painting a scene with words, making us feel, see, hear, smell, or taste what's happening in the story.

🎨
Painting with Words

Example: Imagery in the Story

  • "The air was thick with dust and the smell of damp earth." (Smell, sight)
  • "The rhythmic chipping of pickaxes against rock." (Sound)
  • "Tiny beads of sweat glistened on their foreheads." (Sight)

🎙️ In 'The Tunnel', Ruskin Bond uses imagery beautifully. Phrases like 'thick with dust' or 'rhythmic chipping' help us imagine the tunnel environment and the workers' efforts.
