About the Author: Helen Keller

  • Born in 1880, became deaf and blind at 19 months.
  • Learned to communicate with the help of Anne Sullivan.
  • An author, activist, and lecturer who inspired millions.

🎙️ Helen Keller's story is one of incredible resilience. Despite losing her sight and hearing at a very young age, she broke through barriers to become a brilliant scholar and a powerful advocate for people with disabilities. Her life teaches us that limitations can be overcome with courage and perseverance.

The Essay's Big Idea: Three Days of Sight

  • Helen Keller imagines regaining her sight for just 72 hours.
  • She plans every moment to absorb the world visually.
  • A thought experiment to appreciate the gift of sight.

🎙️ The core idea of this essay is a beautiful thought experiment. Helen asks herself: if I could see for just three days, what would I do? This isn't just about seeing; it's about deeply experiencing and appreciating the world around us, something we often take for granted.

Literary Devices: Comparing Ideas (Simile & Metaphor)

  • Simile: Comparing two unlike things using 'like' or 'as'.
  • Metaphor: Directly comparing two unlike things without 'like' or 'as'.
  • These devices add depth and understanding to the text.

🎙️ Helen Keller also uses similes and metaphors to enrich her writing. For example, she might compare the bustling city to a 'human anthill' (metaphor) or describe a sound 'like a distant bell' (simile). These comparisons help us understand complex ideas by relating them to something familiar.
