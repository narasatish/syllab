What Does 'Right Thing' Mean?

  • Speaking truthfully and kindly.
  • Choosing words carefully.
  • Considering others' feelings.

  • Showing regard for others' opinions.

Key Concept 3: Empathy

  • Understanding and sharing others' feelings.
  • Putting yourself in someone else's shoes.
  • Responding with care and understanding.

🎙️ Empathy is about truly understanding what someone else is feeling. It's like stepping into their situation for a moment to see things from their perspective, and then responding with kindness.

❤️‍🩹
Understanding Feelings
9 / 25

Example: Showing Empathy

  • Your friend is sad about a low test score.
  • You say: 'I understand you're upset. It's okay to feel that way.'
  • You offer comfort, not just advice.

🎙️ If a friend is sad about a test, instead of saying 'Don't worry about it!', you can say 'I know you studied hard, and it's okay to feel disappointed.' This shows you understand their pain.

Key Concept 4: Constructive Feedback

  • Giving helpful suggestions for improvement.
  • Focusing on the task, not the person.
  • Delivering feedback kindly and clearly.

🎙️ Constructive feedback is about helping someone get better. It's not criticism, but rather thoughtful suggestions given in a way that encourages growth and learning.

🛠️
Building Improvements
11 / 25

Example: Constructive Feedback

  • Your friend drew a picture for a project.
  • Instead of 'It's bad!', you say:
  • 'The colours are great! Maybe try adding more detail here?'

🎙️ If a friend asks for your opinion on their drawing, instead of just saying it's bad, you can point out what you like and then suggest specific ways they could improve it. This is much more helpful.

Key Concept 5: Avoiding Gossip

  • Not spreading rumours or unverified information.
  • Protecting others' privacy and reputation.
  • Focusing on positive or neutral topics.

🎙️ Avoiding gossip is crucial for maintaining good relationships. It means not talking about others behind their backs or spreading information that might not be true or could harm them.

Example: Avoiding Gossip

🎙️ If friends start gossiping, you can politely steer the conversation away. For example, by saying, 'Let's not talk about that, how about we discuss something else?' This helps stop the spread of harmful talk.

Key Concept 6: Apologies

  • Saying 'sorry' sincerely when you've made a mistake.
  • Acknowledging your action and its impact.
  • Taking responsibility for your words or actions.

🎙️ Making a sincere apology is a powerful way to 'say the right thing' after you've made a mistake. It shows you understand the harm caused and are taking responsibility for it.

😔
Saying Sorry
17 / 25

Example: Sincere Apology

  • You accidentally bumped into someone and made them drop their books.
  • You say: 'Oh no, I am so sorry! Are you okay? Let me help you pick those up.'
  • Your apology includes action and concern.

🎙️ If you accidentally cause someone trouble, a simple 'I'm sorry' isn't enough. Add sincerity and action, like 'I'm so sorry, are you okay? Let me help you.' This shows genuine regret.

Worked Example: A Difficult Conversation

  • Scenario: Your friend is always late.
  • Wrong: 'You're always late! You don't care about my time!'
  • Right: 'I feel upset when you're late because it makes me wait. Can we try to meet on time?'

🎙️ Let's work through an example. If a friend is often late, instead of accusing them, express how their actions make you feel. This is a more constructive way to address the issue.
