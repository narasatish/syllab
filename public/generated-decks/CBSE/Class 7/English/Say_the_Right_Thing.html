Principle 1: Always Speak the Truth

  • Be honest in your words
  • Avoid exaggeration or lies
  • Even small untruths can damage trust

🎙️ The foundation of good communication is honesty. Always tell the truth, even if it's difficult. It builds credibility and shows others that you can be trusted.

✅
Honesty

Example: Being Truthful

  • Scenario: You accidentally broke a vase at home.
  • Right thing: 'Mom, I'm sorry, I accidentally broke the vase.'
  • Wrong thing: Blaming the cat or hiding it.

🎙️ Imagine you've broken something by mistake. The right thing to do is to admit it honestly, even if you're scared of the consequences. This shows responsibility and earns respect.

Principle 2: Use Respectful Language

  • Address elders politely
  • Avoid name-calling or insults
  • Show consideration for others' feelings

🎙️ Respectful language means speaking to everyone with courtesy, regardless of their age or position. It involves using polite words and avoiding any language that might offend or demean someone.

🙏
Politeness
7 / 25

Example: Showing Respect

  • Scenario: Asking a teacher for help.
  • Right way: 'Excuse me, Sir/Ma'am, could you please explain this again?'
  • Wrong way: 'Hey, explain this!'

🎙️ Think about how you ask for things. A polite request, using 'please' and 'thank you', makes a big difference. It shows respect for the other person's time and effort.

Principle 3: Speak with Empathy and Kindness

  • Try to understand others' feelings
  • Offer support and encouragement
  • Avoid harsh or critical words

🎙️ Empathy is about putting yourself in someone else's shoes. When you speak with empathy and kindness, you show that you care about their feelings, making them feel understood and valued.

❤️
Kindness

Example: Being Kind

  • Scenario: Your friend is sad about a low test score.
  • Right thing: 'It's okay, you tried your best. Let's study together next time!'
  • Wrong thing: 'You should have studied more.'

🎙️ When a friend is feeling down, choose words that comfort and encourage them, rather than criticize. A little kindness can go a long way in cheering someone up.

Principle 4: Use an Appropriate Tone

  • Match your tone to the situation
  • Avoid shouting or whispering unnecessarily
  • A calm tone helps resolve conflicts

🎙️ Your tone of voice can change the meaning of your words. A calm, clear tone is usually best, especially when discussing serious matters or trying to resolve disagreements.

  • Right tone: Calm and persuasive.
  • Wrong tone: Whining or demanding.

Principle 5: Listen Actively Before Speaking

  • Pay full attention to the speaker
  • Don't interrupt before they finish
  • Understand their message completely

🎙️ Good communication is a two-way street. Before you speak, make sure you've truly listened to what the other person is saying. This shows respect and helps you respond thoughtfully.

👂
Listening
13 / 25

Example: Practicing Active Listening

  • Steps: 1. Look at the speaker. 2. Nod occasionally. 3. Ask clarifying questions. 4. Summarize what you heard.

🎙️ When someone is talking, make eye contact, show you're engaged with small gestures, and ask questions if something is unclear. This ensures you grasp their message fully before you reply.

Avoid Gossip and Spreading Rumors

  • Talking about others behind their back
  • Spreading unverified information
  • Can hurt reputations and create conflict

🎙️ Gossip can be very damaging. It's best not to talk about others when they're not present, especially if the information isn't confirmed or is negative. It erodes trust.

🤫

Avoid Interrupting Others

  • Shows disrespect to the speaker
  • Breaks the flow of conversation
  • Prevents full understanding of the message

🎙️ When someone is speaking, let them finish their thought. Interrupting shows impatience and disrespect, and you might miss important details of what they are trying to say.

Avoid Impolite or Demanding Requests

  • Saying 'Give me that!' instead of 'Could you please pass that?'
  • Demanding rather than asking politely
  • Comes across as rude or entitled

🎙️ Always remember your manners. A polite request is much more likely to be met with cooperation than a demand. It reflects well on you and fosters positive interactions.

  • Scenario: A friend says your drawing isn't good.

🎙️ It's hard to hear criticism, but it's an opportunity to learn. The right way to respond is to listen without getting angry, ask what you can improve, and thank them for their honesty.
