Context: Who, Where, When?

  • Context means the situation around you.
  • It includes who you're talking to, where you are, and when you're speaking.
  • The context helps you decide the best way to say something.

🎙️ Context is like the background story for your words. Is it your best friend, your teacher, or a stranger? Are you in a classroom, at home, or in a public place? All these factors change how you should speak.

Speaking at School vs. Home

  • At school (formal): 'May I please borrow your pen, sir?'
  • At home (informal): 'Can I have your pen, Papa?'
  • The message is the same, but the words change with context.

🎙️ Think about how you talk to your principal versus your younger sibling. The words you use are probably very different, even if you're asking for the same thing. That's context in action!

Formal vs. Informal Language

  • Formal language is used in serious situations (school, office, strangers).
  • Informal language is used with close friends and family.
  • Choosing between them depends on your relationship and the setting.

🎙️ Think of it like this: you wouldn't tell your teacher 'What's up, ma'am?' right? That's informal. In formal settings, we use more structured and polite language, while with friends, we can be more relaxed.

Clarity: Being Understood

  • Clarity means making sure your message is easy to understand.
  • Avoid vague words or complicated sentences.
  • Speak directly and simply, so there's no confusion.

🎙️ Clarity is about removing any fog from your message. If you say, 'I need that thing over there,' it's not clear. But if you say, 'I need the red book on the top shelf,' that's clear. Be specific!

🗣️
Say what you mean, mean what you say

Conciseness: Short & Sweet

  • It saves time and keeps your listener engaged.

🎙️ Imagine reading a very long story that takes ages to get to the main idea. It can be boring! Conciseness is about being efficient with your words. Say what needs to be said, and then stop.

Using 'I' Statements

  • When you're upset, it's tempting to say 'You always...' or 'You never...'
  • Instead, use 'I' statements: 'I feel [emotion] when you [action] because [reason].'
  • This focuses on your feelings, not blaming the other person.

🎙️ This is a powerful technique for expressing feelings without making the other person defensive. Instead of accusing, you're simply stating your experience. It helps open up a conversation rather than shutting it down.

🧍
Focus on your feelings
19 / 30

'I-Statements' in Action

  • Instead of: 'You always interrupt me! You're so rude!'
  • Try: 'I feel unheard when I'm speaking and you interrupt me, because I want to finish my thought.'
  • This shows respect and invites understanding.

🎙️ See the difference? The first statement is accusatory and likely to cause an argument. The second one explains your feelings and the impact of the action, making it easier for the other person to listen and respond positively.

Slang: Informal & Specific

  • Slang consists of very informal words or phrases.
  • It's often specific to a certain group, age, or region.
  • Examples: 'Wassup?', 'Lit', 'Chill out'.

🎙️ Slang changes quickly and is meant for casual conversation. While it can make your speech sound cool among friends, it's generally not appropriate for school assignments, talking to elders, or formal presentations.

💬
Words only some understand

Jargon: Professional Talk

  • Jargon refers to special words or expressions used by a particular profession or group.
  • Examples: 'RAM', 'CPU' (computer science); 'diagnosis' (medicine).
  • It can confuse people outside that specific field.

🎙️ Jargon is different from slang because it's usually technical or professional. Doctors use medical jargon, and computer scientists use tech jargon. It's useful within their fields, but not for general conversation if you want everyone to understand.

Using Clear Language for Projects

  • Your goal is to inform, not to confuse!

🎙️ Imagine you're presenting a science project. If you use too many complex scientific terms without explaining them, your classmates might get lost. Always aim for clarity and accessibility in your formal presentations.

Oops! Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using informal language in formal situations.
  • Not thinking before speaking, especially when emotional.
  • Being vague or unclear in your explanations.
  • Forgetting to use polite words like 'please' and 'thank you'.

🎙️ It's okay to make mistakes, but it's even better to learn from them! These are some common pitfalls when it comes to communication. Being aware of them helps you consciously improve your speech.
