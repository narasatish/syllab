Understanding Personification

  • Giving human qualities or actions to inanimate objects or animals.
  • Makes descriptions more vivid and engaging for the reader.
  • Helps us connect emotionally with non-human elements in a story.

🎙️ This brings us to a very important literary device called personification. It's when an author makes something non-human act or feel like a human. This technique makes stories much more imaginative and relatable.

✨
Human Touch

Personification in Action: Examples

  • 'The wind whispered secrets through the trees.' (Wind cannot whisper).
  • In our story: 'The river groaned in pain.'
  • Or 'The river wept tears of pollution and sorrow.'

🎙️ Let's look at an example. When we say 'the wind whispered secrets', we know wind doesn't have a mouth to whisper. But it helps us imagine the soft sound of the wind. Similarly, the river in our story is personified when it 'speaks' or shows emotions.
