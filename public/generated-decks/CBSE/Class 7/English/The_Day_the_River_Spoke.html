When Objects Come Alive

  • Giving human qualities or actions to inanimate objects or animals.
  • Makes descriptions more vivid and relatable.
  • The river 'speaks' or 'feels'.

🎙️ Have you ever read a story where the wind 'whispered secrets' or the sun 'smiled'? This is a literary trick called personification. It helps authors make non-human things feel more real and emotional to us.

🗣️
Human Voice
15 / 30

The River's Voice in Action

  • "The river groaned with pain." (Groaning is a human action).
  • "Its currents wept tears of sorrow." (Weeping is a human emotion).
  • "The water begged for help." (Begging is a human plea).

🎙️ In our story, the author uses personification to show the river's suffering. When the river 'groaned' or 'wept', it helps us understand its pain as if it were a person, making us feel more connected to its plight.

Pitfalls to Avoid!

  • Misinterpreting personification as literal speech.
  • Not connecting character actions to themes.
  • Forgetting key vocabulary terms.

🎙️ Students sometimes confuse personification with actual talking, or miss the deeper meaning behind the characters' choices. Remember to look for the symbolic meaning and pay attention to specific vocabulary used in the text.
