Literary Device: Personification

  • Giving human qualities or actions to inanimate objects or animals.
  • Example: 'clouds are sailing' – clouds don't literally sail!
  • Makes abstract ideas more relatable and vivid.

🎙️ Personification is a fantastic tool poets use to bring things to life. When Tagore says 'clouds are sailing', he's giving the clouds a human action, making the sky feel more active and connected to the child's boating game.

🗣️
Making it Human
16 / 30

Literary Device: Imagery

  • Using descriptive language to create vivid mental pictures for the reader.
  • Appeals to our five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch).
  • Example: 'little streams of the rainy day', 'shiuli flowers'.

🎙️ Imagery is like painting with words. Tagore uses words that help us clearly see the rainy day, the small streams, and the delicate shiuli flowers. We can almost smell the rain and feel the dampness, thanks to his descriptive power.

Structure of the Poem: The Flow of the Poem

  • The poem is written in free verse, meaning no strict rhyme scheme or meter.
  • It flows naturally, much like the stream carrying the boats.
  • Each stanza builds on the child's actions and evolving thoughts.

🎙️ Unlike some traditional poems with strict patterns, 'Paper Boats' uses free verse. This allows the poem to feel more like a stream of consciousness, mirroring the child's uninhibited imagination and the natural flow of the water.
