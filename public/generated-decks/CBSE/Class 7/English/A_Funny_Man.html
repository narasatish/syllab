Slide 6: Literary Device: Rhyme Scheme

  • The pattern of rhymes at the end of each line of a poem.
  • Creates musicality and rhythm.
  • Often marked with letters (AABB, ABAB).

🎙️ If 'A Funny Man' is a poem, then rhyme scheme is crucial. It's how the poem sounds musical to our ears. Knowing the pattern helps us appreciate the poet's craft.

🎶
Poetic Harmony
7 / 25

Slide 7: Rhyme Scheme Example

  • He wore his hat upon his feet (A)
  • He walked on his hands down the street (A)
  • He loved to eat soup with a fork (B)
  • And never said a single word or talk (B)

🎙️ Here, 'feet' rhymes with 'street', so they are both 'A'. 'Fork' rhymes with 'talk', so they are both 'B'. This gives us an AABB rhyme scheme, which is common in many simple and humorous poems.

Slide 10: Literary Device: Alliteration

  • Repetition of the same initial consonant sound.
  • Used for emphasis and to create a musical effect.
  • Makes phrases memorable and catchy.

🎙️ Alliteration is another fun literary device. It's all about the sound! When you hear the same beginning sound repeated, it often makes the line more impactful and enjoyable to read aloud.

🗣️
Sound Play

Slide 11: Alliteration Example

  • He **w**ore his hat upon his **w**rong way.
  • The **f**unny **f**ellow **f**orgot his fear.
  • He **p**ainted **p**urple **p**atches.

🎙️ Look at these examples: 'wore' and 'wrong way' both start with 'w'. 'Funny fellow forgot' repeats the 'f' sound. 'Painted purple patches' repeats 'p'. See how it adds a rhythm?
