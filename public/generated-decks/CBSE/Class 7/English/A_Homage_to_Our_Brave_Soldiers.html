Literary Devices: Simile

  • Comparing two unlike things
  • Using 'like' or 'as'
  • Adds vividness to descriptions

🎙️ When we talk about brave soldiers, authors often use literary devices to make their descriptions more powerful. A simile compares two different things using 'like' or 'as'.

↔️
Comparison
16 / 25

Example of Simile: "Brave as a Lion"

  • Soldiers fought *as* bravely *as* lions
  • Comparing soldiers' courage to a lion's
  • Highlights their fearless nature

🎙️ For instance, 'The soldier fought as bravely as a lion.' Here, the soldier's bravery is compared to a lion's, emphasizing their immense courage and strength.

🦁🦁
Two Brave Lions
17 / 25

Literary Devices: Metaphor

  • Direct comparison
  • States one thing *is* another
  • Creates a stronger image

🎙️ A metaphor is similar to a simile but makes a direct comparison without using 'like' or 'as'. It says one thing *is* another, creating a more impactful image.

🎭
Transformation
18 / 25

Example of Metaphor: "He Was a Rock"

  • The commander *was* a rock
  • Comparing commander to a strong rock
  • Implies strength, stability, and resilience

🎙️ Consider, 'The commander was a rock in the face of adversity.' This doesn't mean he literally turned into a rock, but it vividly conveys his unwavering strength and stability.
