Making Comparisons: Simile

  • Compares two different things using 'like' or 'as'.
  • Example: 'Brave as a lion' or 'He fought like a tiger'.
  • It makes descriptions more vivid and relatable.

🎙️ English often uses special ways to describe things. A 'simile' is a literary device that compares two unlike things using the words 'like' or 'as'. When we say 'brave as a lion', we are comparing a person's bravery to a lion's, making the description stronger. Soldiers are often described using similes.

↔️
Comparing with 'Like'/'As'

Speaking in Pictures: Metaphor

  • Directly states one thing is another, without 'like' or 'as'.
  • Example: 'The soldier was a rock, unmoving.'
  • It creates a strong image by equating two things.

🎙️ Similar to simile is 'metaphor'. A metaphor also compares two unlike things, but it does so by directly stating that one thing *is* another. Instead of 'brave like a lion', a metaphor would say 'He is a lion in battle'. It paints a very clear and powerful picture in your mind.

🖼️
Direct Comparison

Painting with Words: Imagery

  • Using descriptive language to create mental images.
  • Appeals to our five senses (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste).
  • Example: 'The crisp mountain air, the glint of steel, the distant rumble.'

🎙️ Another powerful tool is 'Imagery'. This is when an author uses words that appeal to our senses, helping us to see, hear, smell, taste, or feel what they are describing. For example, describing 'the crisp mountain air' or 'the glint of steel' helps us imagine the scene where a soldier might be stationed.

👁️
