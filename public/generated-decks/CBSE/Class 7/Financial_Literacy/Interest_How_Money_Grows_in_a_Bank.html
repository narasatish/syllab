Key Term 1: Principal (P)

  • The original amount of money you deposit.
  • The starting point of your investment.

🎙️ Principal is simply the initial sum of money you put into the bank. If you deposit ₹1000, then ₹1000 is your principal amount. It's the base on which interest will be calculated.

💵
Original Deposit
8 / 25

Key Term 2: Rate of Interest (R)

  • The percentage at which interest is calculated.
  • Usually given 'per annum' (per year).
  • Example: 5% per year.

🎙️ The rate of interest tells you how much interest you will earn, expressed as a percentage. If the rate is 5% per annum, it means for every ₹100 you deposit, you'll earn ₹5 in interest each year.

📈
Percentage Growth
9 / 25

Key Term 3: Time (T)

  • The duration for which the money is kept in the bank.
  • Always expressed in years for calculation.
  • Example: 1 year, 2 years, 6 months (0.5 year).

🎙️ Time refers to how long your principal amount stays in the bank. It's crucial for calculating interest, as interest is usually earned over a period. Remember, for calculations, time is always converted to years.

Duration

The Simple Interest Formula

  • Simple Interest (SI) = (P × R × T) / 100
  • P = Principal, R = Rate, T = Time.

🎙️ This formula helps us easily calculate the simple interest earned. We just need to multiply the principal, rate, and time together, and then divide by 100 because the rate is a percentage.

Example: Calculating Simple Interest

  • P = ₹1000, R = 5% per annum, T = 1 year.
  • SI = (1000 × 5 × 1) / 100
  • SI = ₹50

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. If you deposit ₹1000 at a 5% interest rate for 1 year, your simple interest will be ₹50. This means you earn an extra ₹50 just by keeping your money in the bank.

What is the Total Amount?

  • The total money you get back at the end.
  • Amount = Principal + Simple Interest.

🎙️ When you withdraw your money from the bank after a period, you don't just get your principal back. You get your original principal plus the interest you've earned. This total sum is called the Amount.

➕
Principal + Interest
13 / 25

Example: Calculating Total Amount

  • Principal (P) = ₹1000
  • Simple Interest (SI) = ₹50 (from previous example)
  • Total Amount = P + SI = ₹1000 + ₹50 = ₹1050

🎙️ Using our previous example, after one year, your initial ₹1000 has grown to ₹1050. This ₹1050 is the total amount you would receive from the bank. Your money has truly grown!

Real Life: Savings Account

  • Most common type of bank account.
  • You can deposit and withdraw money easily.
  • Earns a small rate of interest (e.g., 2-4% p.a.).

🎙️ A savings account is where most people keep their everyday money. It's very flexible, allowing you to access your funds whenever you need them, and it still earns you a little interest, helping your money grow gradually.

Real Life: Fixed Deposit (FD)

  • You deposit money for a fixed period (e.g., 1-5 years).
  • Cannot withdraw easily before the period ends.
  • Earns a higher rate of interest (e.g., 5-7% p.a.).

🎙️ A Fixed Deposit is for money you don't need for a while. You 'lock' your money away for a set time, and in return, the bank offers you a higher interest rate. It's a great way to grow your savings faster for long-term goals.

🔒
Locked for Growth

Savings Account vs. Fixed Deposit

  • Savings: Lower interest, high flexibility.
  • FD: Higher interest, lower flexibility.

🎙️ The main difference lies in the interest earned and how easily you can access your money. If you need regular access, a savings account is better. For bigger, long-term goals, an FD can offer better returns.
