Calculating Simple Interest (SI)

  • Simple Interest is calculated only on the original Principal amount.
  • Formula: SI = (P × R × T) / 100
  • Where P = Principal, R = Rate, T = Time (in years).

🎙️ This is the most straightforward way to calculate interest. It's called 'simple' because it doesn't get complicated by adding interest to the principal for the next calculation.

Total Money Back: Amount (A)

  • The total money returned at the end of the period.
  • It includes both the Principal and the earned Simple Interest.
  • Formula: Amount (A) = Principal (P) + Simple Interest (SI).

🎙️ The Amount is what you actually get back from the bank – your original money plus all the interest it has earned. It's the grand total!

Example: Calculating the Total Amount

  • From our previous example: Principal = ₹2,000, SI = ₹100.
  • Amount (A) = P + SI
  • A = ₹2,000 + ₹100 = ₹2,100.

🎙️ So, if you deposited ₹2,000 and earned ₹100 in interest, you would withdraw a total of ₹2,100 from the bank. Your money grew!

Full Example: SI and Amount

  • Problem: Find the Amount for a Principal of ₹5,000 at 4% p.a. for 2 years.
  • Step 1: Calculate SI = (P × R × T) / 100 = (5000 × 4 × 2) / 100 = ₹400.
  • Step 2: Calculate Amount = P + SI = ₹5,000 + ₹400 = ₹5,400.

🎙️ This example combines both calculations. First, find the interest, and then add it to the principal to get the final amount. It's a two-step process.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting to divide by 100 in the SI formula.
  • Not converting Time into years if given in months or days.
  • Confusing Simple Interest with the final Amount.
  • Incorrectly substituting values into the formula.

🎙️ These are typical errors students make. Always double-check your formula, ensure units are consistent, and understand the difference between SI and Amount.
