What are Expressions?

  • A combination of numbers, variables, and operation symbols.
  • Does NOT contain an equals sign (=).
  • It represents a value or a quantity.

🎙️ Think of an expression as a mathematical phrase or sentence that isn't complete yet. It combines different elements using operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Crucially, it doesn't have an equals sign, so we're not solving for anything, just representing a value.

Variables: The Changing Values

  • Symbols (usually letters) that represent unknown values.
  • Their value can change.
  • Commonly used: x, y, a, b.

🎙️ Imagine a box where you can put different numbers. That's what a variable is! It's a letter like 'x' or 'y' that stands for a number whose value can change or is not yet known. Variables are what make expressions flexible and powerful.

Constants: The Fixed Values

  • Numbers in an expression whose value is fixed.
  • They do NOT change.
  • Example: In 'x + 5', '5' is a constant.

🎙️ In contrast to variables, constants are numbers that always stay the same. If you see a '7' in an expression, it always means seven. Constants provide the stable, known parts of our mathematical statements.

Arithmetic vs. Algebraic Expressions

  • Arithmetic: Only numbers and operations (e.g., 5 + 3).
  • Algebraic: Includes variables, numbers, and operations (e.g., x + 5).

🎙️ This is a key distinction. Arithmetic expressions are what you've mostly worked with so far, just numbers. Algebraic expressions introduce variables, making them more versatile for representing real-world situations where values might be unknown or changing.

Terms of an Expression

  • Parts of an expression separated by '+' or '-' signs.
  • Each term can be a number, a variable, or a product of both.
  • Example: In '3x + 5y - 2', the terms are 3x, 5y, and -2.

🎙️ Think of terms as the individual 'pieces' or 'chunks' that make up an expression. They are clearly separated by addition or subtraction signs. Understanding terms is crucial for simplifying expressions later on.

Like Terms: Friends Together

  • Terms that have the exact same variable part.
  • They can be combined (added or subtracted).
  • Example: 3x and 7x are like terms.

🎙️ Like terms are similar to items you can group together. If you have 3 apples and 7 apples, you have 10 apples. Similarly, if terms have the same variable part, you can add or subtract their numerical coefficients.

Unlike Terms: Different Paths

  • Terms that have different variable parts.
  • They CANNOT be combined.
  • Example: 3x and 7y are unlike terms.

🎙️ Unlike terms are like trying to add apples and oranges – it doesn't make sense to combine them into a single type. You cannot add 3x and 7y to get 10xy or 10x or 10y; they must remain separate.

Coefficients: The Multipliers

  • The numerical factor of a term with a variable.
  • It tells you how many of that variable you have.
  • Example: In '5x', '5' is the coefficient.

🎙️ The coefficient is the number that sits right in front of a variable. It indicates multiplication. If you see '4y', it means 4 multiplied by y, and '4' is the coefficient. If there's no number, the coefficient is 1 (e.g., 'x' means '1x').

Order of Operations: BODMAS/PEMDAS

  • A rule to ensure consistent results when evaluating expressions.
  • It dictates the sequence of performing operations.
  • Bracket, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.

🎙️ When an expression has multiple operations, we need a standard order to solve it, otherwise, everyone might get a different answer! BODMAS, or PEMDAS in some regions, provides this universal sequence, ensuring we all arrive at the correct result.

1Brackets
2Orders (Powers/Roots)
3Division & Multiplication (L to R)
4Addition & Subtraction (L to R)
15 / 25

BODMAS Explained

  • B: Brackets (Parentheses) - Solve inside first.
  • O: Orders (Exponents) - Powers and roots.
  • DM: Division and Multiplication - From left to right.
  • AS: Addition and Subtraction - From left to right.

🎙️ It's important to remember that Division and Multiplication have equal priority, as do Addition and Subtraction. When you encounter them together, you work from left to right. This ensures every step is performed correctly.

Worked Example: Evaluating with BODMAS

  • Evaluate: 10 + 4 × (6 - 2)
  • Step 1 (Brackets): 10 + 4 × 4
  • Step 2 (Multiplication): 10 + 16
  • Step 3 (Addition): 26

🎙️ Let's apply BODMAS to a numerical expression. Notice how we tackle the operation inside the brackets first, then multiplication, and finally addition. Following these steps systematically prevents errors and leads us to the correct answer.

Worked Example: Evaluating with Variables

  • Evaluate 2x + 5 for x = 3.
  • Step 1 (Substitute): 2(3) + 5
  • Step 2 (Multiply): 6 + 5
  • Step 3 (Add): 11

🎙️ When we have an algebraic expression and are given a value for the variable, we substitute that value into the expression. Then, we use BODMAS to evaluate the resulting arithmetic expression. This shows how variables allow expressions to represent different values.

Simplifying Expressions: Combining Like Terms

  • Make an expression easier to understand and use.
  • Combine (add or subtract) all like terms.
  • Example: 3x + 5 + 2x -> 5x + 5.

🎙️ Simplifying an expression means writing it in its most compact and understandable form. The main way to do this is by combining any like terms. Remember, you can only combine terms that have the exact same variable part.

1Identify Like Terms
2Group Them
3Combine Coefficients
19 / 25

Worked Example: Simplifying Algebraic Expression

  • Simplify: 7y + 3x - 2y + 5
  • Step 1 (Identify like terms): (7y - 2y) and (3x)
  • Step 2 (Combine 'y' terms): 5y + 3x + 5
  • Final simplified expression: 3x + 5y + 5

🎙️ Here, we first identified the terms with 'y' as their variable part and grouped them. Then, we performed the subtraction of their coefficients. The '3x' and '5' are unlike terms, so they remain separate in the final simplified expression.

2(7y - 2y) + 3x + 5
