Two Main Types: Numerical vs. Algebraic Expressions

  • Expressions can be broadly categorized based on their components.
  • Numerical expressions contain only numbers and operations.
  • Algebraic expressions include variables, numbers, and operations.

🎙️ It's important to distinguish between these two types. Numerical expressions give us a definite answer once calculated, while algebraic expressions often represent a general rule or a value that depends on the variable.

Terms: The Ingredients of an Expression

  • Terms are the individual parts of an expression, separated by addition (+) or subtraction (-) signs.
  • A term can be a single number, a single variable, or a product of numbers and variables.
  • Example: In 5x + 3y - 2, the terms are 5x, 3y, and -2.

🎙️ Think of an expression as a dish, and terms are its individual ingredients. Each piece separated by a plus or minus sign is a term. Understanding terms is crucial for simplifying and working with expressions.

Like & Unlike Terms: Sorting Our Ingredients!

  • Like Terms: Terms that have the same variables raised to the same power.
  • Example: 3x and 7x are like terms. 5y² and -2y² are like terms.
  • Unlike Terms: Terms that have different variables or different powers of the same variable.
  • Example: 3x and 7y are unlike terms. 5x and 5x² are unlike terms.

🎙️ This concept is like sorting fruits. You group all the apples together and all the bananas together. Similarly, in expressions, 'like terms' are those that are identical in their variable part, including the exponent. We can only combine like terms.

Spotting Like Terms (Example 5)

  • From the list: 2a, 5b, -a, 3a², 4b
  • Like terms are: (2a, -a) and (5b, 4b)
  • Why? Because '2a' and '-a' both have the variable 'a' raised to the power of 1.
  • And '5b' and '4b' both have the variable 'b' raised to the power of 1.

🎙️ Let's practice identifying like terms. Remember, the numerical part (coefficient) doesn't matter for determining if terms are 'like'. Only the variable part and its exponent must be identical. So, 2a and -a are like terms because both have 'a' as the variable.

Combining Like Terms: Simplifying Expressions

  • We can add or subtract only like terms.
  • To combine, we add or subtract their coefficients (the numbers in front of the variables).
  • The variable part remains unchanged.
  • This process makes expressions shorter and easier to work with.

🎙️ Combining like terms is like saying '2 apples + 3 apples = 5 apples'. You add the counts, but the 'apple' (the variable) remains the same. This simplification makes expressions much more manageable.

2Group Them Together
3Add/Subtract Coefficients

Let's Simplify! (Example 6)

  • Expression: 3x + 2y + 5x - y
  • Step 1: Identify like terms: (3x, 5x) and (2y, -y)
  • Step 2: Group them: (3x + 5x) + (2y - y)
  • Step 3: Combine coefficients: (3+5)x + (2-1)y = 8x + 1y
  • Simplified expression: 8x + y

🎙️ Here's a step-by-step example. First, we carefully find all the 'x' terms and all the 'y' terms. Then, we perform the addition or subtraction only on their numerical coefficients, keeping the variable part as it is. Remember, 'y' is the same as '1y'.

Evaluating Expressions: Finding the Final Value!

  • Evaluating an expression means finding its numerical value.
  • We do this by substituting specific values for the variables.
  • Then, we perform the arithmetic operations according to the order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS).

🎙️ Evaluating an expression is like solving a puzzle. If we know what numbers the 'mystery boxes' (variables) hold, we can then perform all the operations and find a single numerical answer. This is where BODMAS or PEMDAS rules become very important.

Numerical Evaluation (Example 7)

  • Expression: 10 + 4 × 2 - 6 ÷ 3
  • Step 1: Perform Multiplication and Division from left to right: 10 + 8 - 2
  • Step 2: Perform Addition and Subtraction from left to right: 18 - 2 = 16
  • The value of the expression is 16.

🎙️ For numerical expressions, we simply apply the order of operations: Brackets, Orders (powers/roots), Division and Multiplication (from left to right), Addition and Subtraction (from left to right). Remember, multiplication and division have equal priority, as do addition and subtraction.

Algebraic Evaluation (Example 8)

  • Step 1: Substitute the value of x into the expression: 5(4) + 3
  • Step 2: Perform multiplication: 20 + 3

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Ignoring Order of Operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS) when evaluating.
  • Trying to combine unlike terms (e.g., adding 3x and 2y).
  • Incorrectly handling negative signs during addition/subtraction.
  • Forgetting that a variable without a number has a coefficient of 1 (e.g., x is 1x).

🎙️ These are the pitfalls where students often stumble. Always double-check your order of operations. Remember, you cannot add apples and oranges! And be extra careful with negative numbers, as they can easily change your final answer.
