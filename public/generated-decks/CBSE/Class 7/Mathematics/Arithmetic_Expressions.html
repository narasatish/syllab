What are Arithmetic Expressions?

  • A combination of numbers and arithmetic operations.
  • No equality sign (=) unlike equations.
  • Examples: 5 + 3, 12 - 4 * 2.

🎙️ An arithmetic expression is like a mathematical phrase. It uses numbers and operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, but it doesn't have an equals sign. It represents a single value.

Components of Expressions: Numbers

  • These are fixed values, also called constants.
  • They can be positive, negative, fractions, or decimals.
  • Examples: 7, -3, 1/2, 4.5.

🎙️ The building blocks of any expression are numbers. These numbers are constants, meaning their value never changes. They are the quantities we work with in our calculations.

Components of Expressions: Operators

  • Symbols that tell us what operation to perform.
  • Four basic arithmetic operators: Addition (+), Subtraction (-), Multiplication (*), Division (/).
  • They connect the numbers in an expression.

🎙️ Operators are the action verbs of our expressions. They tell us exactly what to do with the numbers. Mastering these four basic operations is key to understanding expressions.

Understanding Terms in an Expression

  • Parts of an expression separated by '+' or '-' signs.
  • Each term can be a number or a product/quotient of numbers.
  • Example: In 5 + 3 * 2, '5' and '3 * 2' are terms.

🎙️ Think of terms as the individual pieces of a puzzle that make up the whole expression. They are distinct parts that we combine or separate using addition and subtraction.

Evaluating Arithmetic Expressions

  • Finding the single numerical value of an expression.
  • This means performing all the operations.
  • Example: Evaluating 5 + 3 * 2 gives a single number.

🎙️ Evaluating an expression means calculating its final answer. It's like solving the puzzle to find out what number the entire phrase represents. This is where we apply our calculation skills.

Example: Evaluating 15 - 4 + 7

  • Expression: 15 - 4 + 7
  • Step 1: 15 - 4 = 11
  • Step 2: 11 + 7 = 18
  • Value of the expression is 18.

🎙️ When we have only addition and subtraction, or only multiplication and division, we usually work from left to right. Here, we first subtract, then add, to get our final value.

The Importance of Order: BODMAS

  • Ensures everyone gets the same answer for an expression.
  • BODMAS stands for Brackets, Of, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.
  • It's a set of rules for the sequence of operations.

🎙️ Imagine if everyone solved expressions in a different order – we'd get chaos! BODMAS is our universal rulebook, ensuring consistency and correctness in our calculations. It's a fundamental rule in mathematics.

🚧
Rulebook

BODMAS: Step 1 - Brackets ( ) { } [ ]

  • Operations inside brackets must be performed first.
  • Types: Parentheses ( ), Curly Brackets { }, Square Brackets [ ].
  • Solve innermost brackets first, then work outwards.

🎙️ Brackets act like a shield, grouping operations that need to be done before anything else outside them. Always look for brackets first and simplify whatever is inside them.

BODMAS: Step 2 - Of / Orders

  • 'Of' usually refers to multiplication, especially with fractions (e.g., 'half of 10').
  • 'Orders' refers to powers or roots (e.g., 2^3).
  • Perform these operations after brackets are solved.

🎙️ After brackets, we look for 'Of' or 'Orders'. In Class 7, 'Of' is often seen with fractions. 'Orders' involves exponents like squares or cubes, which you'll learn more about soon. For now, treat 'Of' as multiplication.

BODMAS: Step 3 - Division (÷) & Multiplication (×)

  • Perform division and multiplication from left to right.
  • They have equal priority; solve whichever comes first.
  • Example: 10 ÷ 2 * 3. First 10 ÷ 2 = 5, then 5 * 3 = 15.

🎙️ Once brackets and 'Of' are dealt with, we move to division and multiplication. Remember, they are equally important! Just scan from left to right and do the operation you encounter first.

BODMAS: Step 4 - Addition (+) & Subtraction (-)

  • Perform addition and subtraction from left to right.
  • They also have equal priority; solve whichever comes first.
  • Example: 7 + 3 - 2. First 7 + 3 = 10, then 10 - 2 = 8.

🎙️ Finally, we tackle addition and subtraction. Just like division and multiplication, they are solved from left to right. This is the last step to simplify your expression to a single value.

  • Original: 18 ÷ 3 + 5 × 2

Common Mistakes in Evaluating Expressions

  • Ignoring the order of operations (BODMAS).
  • Solving operations from left to right always, without priority.
  • Making calculation errors with basic arithmetic.

🎙️ It's easy to make mistakes if we rush. The biggest pitfall is not following BODMAS properly. Always take your time, apply the rules step-by-step, and double-check your basic calculations.

Real-life Application: Calculating Travel Time

  • You travel 60 km at 20 km/h, then stop for 1 hour.
  • Expression for total time (in hours): 60 / 20 + 1.
  • This evaluates to 3 + 1 = 4 hours.

🎙️ Expressions help us calculate things like travel time. Dividing distance by speed gives us travel time, and then we can add any stops or breaks to find the total duration of our journey.

Translating Words into Arithmetic Expressions

  • Look for keywords: 'sum' (+), 'difference' (-), 'product' (*), 'quotient' (/)
  • 'More than', 'increased by' mean addition.
  • 'Less than', 'decreased by' mean subtraction.

🎙️ Mathematics often involves converting real-world scenarios or sentences into expressions. Identifying these keywords is like finding clues that tell you which arithmetic operation to use.
