Understanding the Parts of a Fraction

  • A fraction has two main parts: Numerator and Denominator.
  • Numerator: The number of parts taken or considered (top number).
  • Denominator: The total number of equal parts the whole is divided into (bottom number).

🎙️ For example, in the fraction 3/4, '3' is the numerator, meaning we have 3 parts, and '4' is the denominator, meaning the whole was divided into 4 equal parts. The line in between represents division.

➗
Numerator / Denominator

Proper Fractions: Less Than a Whole

  • Numerator is smaller than the Denominator.
  • Represents a value less than 1.
  • Example: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8.

🎙️ Imagine a cake cut into 4 slices. If you take 3 slices, you have 3/4 of the cake. Here, 3 is less than 4, so it's a proper fraction. It clearly shows a part of the whole.

Improper Fractions: More Than a Whole

  • Numerator is greater than or equal to the Denominator.
  • Represents a value equal to or more than 1.
  • Example: 5/4, 7/3, 4/4.

🎙️ What if you have 5 quarters of a pizza? That's more than one whole pizza! So, 5/4 is an improper fraction. The numerator, 5, is greater than the denominator, 4.

Mixed Fractions: Combining Wholes and Parts

  • A combination of a whole number and a proper fraction.
  • Another way to represent improper fractions.
  • Example: 1 ½, 2 ¾.

🎙️ When you have more than one whole, like one full cake and half of another, you can write it as a mixed fraction: 1 ½. It makes the quantity easier to visualize than an improper fraction like 3/2.

🍰
1 whole cake + 1/2 cake

From Improper to Mixed: A Step-by-Step Guide

  • Divide the numerator by the denominator.
  • The quotient is the whole number part.
  • The remainder is the new numerator, with the original denominator.

🎙️ Let's convert 7/3 to a mixed fraction. 7 divided by 3 gives a quotient of 2 and a remainder of 1. So, 7/3 becomes 2 1/3. It's like having two full pizzas and one-third of another.

1Divide Numerator by Denominator
2Quotient = Whole Number
3Remainder = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same
10 / 30

From Mixed to Improper: Reversing the Process

  • Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
  • Add the numerator to the product.
  • This sum becomes the new numerator, with the original denominator.

🎙️ To convert 2 1/3 back to an improper fraction: (2 x 3) + 1 = 7. So, the improper fraction is 7/3. This conversion is often needed when you perform operations like addition or subtraction.

1Whole x Denominator
2+ Numerator
3Result = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same

Same Amount, Different Look: Equivalent Fractions

  • Fractions that represent the same value.
  • They look different but mean the same thing.
  • Example: 1/2 is the same as 2/4.

🎙️ Imagine cutting a cake into 2 equal halves. If you take one half, that's 1/2. Now, if you cut the same cake into 4 equal quarters, and take two quarters, that's 2/4. Both represent the same amount of cake!

🧮🧮
1/2 and 2/4 are equal

Finding Equivalent Fractions

  • Multiply the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • Divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • This maintains the fraction's value.

🎙️ To find equivalent fractions, whatever you do to the top (numerator), you must do to the bottom (denominator). If you multiply 1/2 by 2/2, you get 2/4. Both are equivalent and represent the same quantity.

1Start with a fraction (e.g., 1/2)
2Multiply Numerator & Denominator by 2
3Result: 2/4 (Equivalent)
4Can also divide by common factors

Simplifying Fractions: Making Them Easier

  • Writing a fraction in its simplest (or lowest) form.
  • Numerator and denominator have no common factors other than 1.
  • Divide both by their Highest Common Factor (HCF).

🎙️ Simplifying a fraction is like cleaning up a messy room – you want to make it as neat and compact as possible. For example, 6/9 can be simplified by dividing both by 3, resulting in 2/3. This is its simplest form.

1Find HCF of Numerator & Denominator
2Divide Numerator by HCF
3Divide Denominator by HCF
4Result: Simplest Form

Comparing Fractions: Same Denominators

  • Example: 3/5 is greater than 2/5.

Comparing Fractions: Same Numerators

  • When numerators are the same, compare the denominators.
  • The fraction with the smaller denominator is the larger fraction.
  • Example: 1/2 is greater than 1/4.

🎙️ Think of it this way: if you divide a cake into 2 parts, each part is bigger than if you divide the same cake into 4 parts. So, 1/2 is larger than 1/4.

1/2
1/4
18 / 30

Comparing Fractions: Different Denominators

  • Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the common denominator.
  • Then compare their numerators.

🎙️ This is the most common scenario. For example, to compare 2/3 and 3/4, the LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. So, 2/3 becomes 8/12, and 3/4 becomes 9/12. Now it's easy to see that 9/12 (or 3/4) is larger.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Compare New Numerators

Adding Fractions: Same Denominators

  • Simply add the numerators.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Always simplify the result if possible.

🎙️ If you have 1/5 of an apple and your friend gives you another 2/5 of an apple, you now have (1+2)/5 = 3/5 of an apple. The 'size' of the parts (denominator) doesn't change, only the 'number' of parts (numerator).

🍎🍎🍎🍎🍎
1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5
20 / 30

Adding Fractions: Different Denominators

  • Find the LCM of the denominators.
  • Convert to equivalent fractions with the common denominator.
  • Add the numerators and keep the common denominator.

🎙️ This is a crucial step for addition and subtraction. For example, to add 1/2 and 1/3, the LCM of 2 and 3 is 6. Convert them to 3/6 and 2/6, then add to get 5/6.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Add Numerators
4Keep Common Denominator

Adding Mixed Fractions: Two Ways

  • Method 1: Add whole numbers and fractional parts separately.
  • Method 2: Convert mixed fractions to improper fractions, then add.
  • Convert back to mixed fraction if the answer is improper.

🎙️ Both methods work, choose the one you find easier! For example, 1 1/2 + 2 1/3 can be done by adding 1+2=3, and 1/2+1/3=5/6, giving 3 5/6. Or convert to 3/2 + 7/3, which is 9/6 + 14/6 = 23/6, then convert to 3 5/6.

1Separate Whole & Fraction
2Add Whole Parts
3Add Fractional Parts (using LCM)
4Combine and Simplify

Subtracting Fractions: Same Denominators

  • Subtract the numerators.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Simplify the result if possible.

🎙️ If you have 4/5 of a packet of biscuits and you eat 1/5, you are left with (4-1)/5 = 3/5 of the packet. The principle is the same as addition, but we subtract the numerators.

🍪🍪🍪🍪🍪
4/5 - 1/5 = 3/5

Subtracting Fractions: Different Denominators

  • Find the LCM of the denominators.
  • Convert to equivalent fractions with the common denominator.
  • Subtract the numerators and keep the common denominator.

🎙️ Just like addition, for subtraction with different denominators, we need a common ground. Find the LCM, convert the fractions, and then perform the subtraction. For example, 3/4 - 1/2 becomes 3/4 - 2/4 = 1/4.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Subtract Numerators
4Keep Common Denominator

Subtracting Mixed Fractions: Careful Steps

  • Convert to improper fractions first (often easiest).
  • Find common denominator and subtract.
  • Alternatively, subtract whole numbers and fractional parts, borrowing if needed.

🎙️ Subtracting mixed fractions can sometimes be tricky, especially if the first fraction's proper part is smaller than the second's. Converting to improper fractions first often simplifies the process and avoids borrowing complications.

1Convert to Improper Fractions
2Find LCM of Denominators
3Subtract Numerators
4Convert back to Mixed if needed

Multiplying Fractions: Whole Number by Fraction

  • Multiply the whole number by the numerator.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Example: 3 × 1/4 = 3/4.

🎙️ Multiplying a whole number by a fraction is like having multiple copies of that fraction. If you have 3 groups, and each group is 1/4 of something, you have a total of 3/4. Think of 3 as 3/1 and then proceed to multiply fractions.

⭐⭐⭐
3 groups of 1/4

Multiplying Fractions: Fraction by Fraction

  • Multiply the numerators together.
  • Multiply the denominators together.
  • Simplify the resulting fraction if possible.

🎙️ This is the most straightforward operation! For example, 1/2 × 1/3. Multiply 1 by 1 (numerator) to get 1, and 2 by 3 (denominator) to get 6. So the answer is 1/6. It's like finding a 'fraction of a fraction'.

✖️
Numerator x Numerator / Denominator x Denominator

Dividing Fractions: The 'Keep, Change, Flip' Rule

  • Keep the first fraction as it is.
  • Change the division sign to multiplication.
  • Flip the second fraction (find its reciprocal).

🎙️ Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. The reciprocal of a fraction is simply flipping its numerator and denominator. This 'Keep, Change, Flip' rule makes division easy!

➗
a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c

Avoiding Common Mistakes with Fractions

  • Adding/subtracting denominators: Only numerators are added/subtracted.
  • Forgetting common denominators: Essential for addition and subtraction.
  • Not simplifying answers: Always give fractions in their simplest form.

🎙️ Many students make these common errors. Remember, denominators tell you the 'size' of the parts, which doesn't change when you add or subtract. Always look for the HCF to simplify your final answers.
