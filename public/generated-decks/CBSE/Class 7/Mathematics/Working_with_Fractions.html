What are Fractions?

  • A fraction represents a part of a whole.
  • It shows how many parts of a certain size there are.
  • Expressed as a ratio of two numbers.

🎙️ Imagine sharing a pizza with friends. If the pizza is cut into 4 equal slices and you take one, you've taken one-fourth of the pizza. This 'one-fourth' is a fraction, representing a part of the whole pizza.

🍕🍕🍕🍕
1 out of 4 parts is coloured

Components of a Fraction

  • Numerator: The number on top (parts taken/considered).
  • Denominator: The number below (total equal parts of the whole).
  • The line separating them is called the fraction bar.

🎙️ Every fraction has two main parts: the numerator and the denominator. The numerator tells us how many pieces we have or are looking at, while the denominator tells us how many equal pieces the whole was divided into.

Types of Fractions: Proper Fractions

  • Numerator is smaller than the Denominator.
  • Value is always less than 1.
  • Example: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8.

🎙️ Proper fractions represent less than a whole. Imagine cutting a cake into 5 pieces and taking 2; you have 2/5 of the cake, which is less than the whole cake. The numerator is always smaller than the denominator.

Denominator (5)

Types of Fractions: Improper Fractions

  • Numerator is greater than or equal to the Denominator.
  • Value is always 1 or greater than 1.
  • Example: 5/4, 7/3, 9/9.

🎙️ Improper fractions represent a whole or more than a whole. If you have 5 quarters of a pizza, you actually have more than one whole pizza. Here, the numerator is either equal to or larger than the denominator.

Numerator (5)
Denominator (4)

Types of Fractions: Mixed Fractions

  • Combination of a whole number and a proper fraction.
  • Represent values greater than 1.
  • Example: 1 ½, 2 ¾, 3 ⅕.

🎙️ Mixed fractions are another way to express improper fractions. They clearly show the whole number part and the fractional part. For instance, if you have one whole apple and half an apple, you have 1 ½ apples.

🍎🍎
1 whole and 1/2 apple

Converting Improper to Mixed Fractions

  • Divide the numerator by the denominator.
  • The quotient is the whole number part.
  • The remainder is the new numerator.
  • The denominator remains the same.

🎙️ To convert an improper fraction like 7/3 into a mixed fraction, you perform division. The result of the division gives you the whole number and the remainder helps form the new proper fraction. For 7/3, 7 divided by 3 is 2 with a remainder of 1, so it becomes 2 1/3.

1Divide Numerator by Denominator
2Quotient = Whole Number
3Remainder = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same

Converting Mixed to Improper Fractions

  • Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
  • Add the numerator to the result.
  • This sum becomes the new numerator.
  • The denominator remains the same.

🎙️ To convert a mixed fraction like 2 1/3 back into an improper fraction, you reverse the process. Multiply the whole number by the denominator and then add the original numerator. This gives you the new numerator, while the denominator stays the same.

1Whole x Denominator
2Add Numerator
3Result = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same

Equivalent Fractions

  • Fractions that represent the same value.
  • They look different but are equal in magnitude.
  • Example: 1/2, 2/4, 3/6 are all equivalent.

🎙️ Equivalent fractions are like different names for the same amount. Imagine two pizzas, one cut into 2 halves and another into 4 quarters. One half of the first pizza is the same amount as two quarters of the second pizza.

Equal Value

Finding Equivalent Fractions

  • Multiply the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • Divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
  • This maintains the fraction's value.

🎙️ To find equivalent fractions, you must always perform the same operation on both the numerator and the denominator. This ensures that the proportion remains unchanged, giving you a fraction that represents the same value.

✖️
Multiply or Divide

Simplest Form of a Fraction

  • A fraction is in its simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factors other than 1.
  • Also called 'lowest terms'.
  • To simplify, divide N and D by their HCF.

🎙️ Simplifying a fraction means writing it in its most reduced form. For example, 2/4 can be simplified to 1/2 because both 2 and 4 can be divided by 2. This makes the fraction easier to understand and work with.

Reduce to Smallest

Comparing Fractions: Like Denominators

  • If fractions have the same denominator, compare their numerators.
  • The fraction with the larger numerator is the greater fraction.
  • Example: 3/5 < 4/5.

🎙️ When the denominators are the same, comparing fractions is straightforward. It's like comparing how many slices you have from the same-sized pizza. More slices mean a larger fraction.

Comparing Fractions: Unlike Denominators

  • Find the LCM (Least Common Multiple) of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the LCM as the new denominator.
  • Compare the new numerators.

🎙️ Comparing fractions with different denominators requires a bit more work. We need to make their denominators the same first, by finding their LCM. Once they have common denominators, we can easily compare their numerators.

2Convert to Equivalent Fractions

Addition of Fractions: Like Denominators

  • Add the numerators directly.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Simplify the resulting fraction if possible.
  • Example: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.

🎙️ Adding fractions with like denominators is very simple. Just add the top numbers together and keep the bottom number the same. Think of it as combining pieces of the same size.

Addition of Fractions: Unlike Denominators

  • Find the LCM of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the LCM.
  • Add the new numerators.
  • Keep the common denominator.

🎙️ When denominators are different, we cannot add them directly. First, we need to find a common ground by converting them to equivalent fractions with the same denominator, using their LCM. Then, we can add them as usual.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Add New Numerators
4Keep Common Denominator

Worked Example: Addition

  • Add: 1/2 + 1/3
  • LCM of 2 and 3 is 6.
  • 1/2 = 3/6, 1/3 = 2/6
  • 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

🎙️ Let's try an example together. To add 1/2 and 1/3, we find the LCM of 2 and 3, which is 6. Then we convert 1/2 to 3/6 and 1/3 to 2/6. Now, simply add the numerators: 3 + 2 = 5, keeping the denominator 6. So, the answer is 5/6.

Subtraction of Fractions: Like Denominators

  • Subtract the numerators directly.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Simplify the resulting fraction if possible.
  • Example: 3/4 - 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.

🎙️ Subtracting fractions with like denominators is just like adding them, but you subtract the numerators instead. Imagine taking away some slices from a pizza, where all slices are the same size.

Subtraction of Fractions: Unlike Denominators

  • Find the LCM of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the LCM.
  • Subtract the new numerators.
  • Keep the common denominator.

🎙️ Similar to addition, when denominators are different for subtraction, you must first find the LCM and convert the fractions. Once they have a common denominator, you can subtract the numerators. Always remember to simplify your final answer.

Worked Example: Subtraction

  • Subtract: 3/4 - 1/2
  • LCM of 4 and 2 is 4.
  • 3/4 remains 3/4, 1/2 = 2/4
  • 3/4 - 2/4 = 1/4.

🎙️ Let's solve another example. To subtract 1/2 from 3/4, the LCM of 4 and 2 is 4. So, 1/2 becomes 2/4. Now we have 3/4 minus 2/4, which gives us 1/4. Always ensure your answer is in its simplest form.
