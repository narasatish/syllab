What is a Fraction?

  • A part of a whole.
  • Represents a division of a whole into equal parts.
  • Example: one piece out of four equal pieces of a cake.

🎙️ A fraction is simply a way to represent a part of a whole. Imagine a cake cut into equal pieces. If you take one piece, that's a fraction of the whole cake. It tells us how many parts we have out of the total number of equal parts.

Numerator and Denominator

  • Numerator: The top number, tells how many parts are taken.
  • Denominator: The bottom number, tells the total number of equal parts.
  • Example: In 3/4, 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator.

🎙️ Every fraction has two main parts: the numerator and the denominator. The numerator, on top, counts the number of parts we are interested in. The denominator, at the bottom, tells us the total number of equal parts the whole is divided into. It's crucial to remember which is which!

Types of Fractions: Proper Fractions

  • Numerator is smaller than the denominator.
  • Represents a value less than one whole.
  • Examples: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8.

🎙️ A proper fraction is when the numerator is smaller than the denominator. This means the fraction represents a quantity that is less than one whole. Think of it as having a part of something, but not the whole thing yet.

Types of Fractions: Improper Fractions

  • Numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator.
  • Represents a value equal to or greater than one whole.
  • Examples: 5/4, 7/3, 9/9.

🎙️ An improper fraction is the opposite of a proper fraction. Here, the numerator is either greater than or equal to the denominator. This indicates that the fraction represents one whole or even more than one whole. We often convert these to mixed fractions for easier understanding.

Types of Fractions: Mixed Fractions

  • A combination of a whole number and a proper fraction.
  • Represents a value greater than one whole.
  • Examples: 1 1/2, 2 3/4, 5 1/3.

🎙️ A mixed fraction combines a whole number with a proper fraction. It's another way to express values greater than one whole, often derived from improper fractions. For example, if you have one whole pizza and half of another, that's 1 and 1/2 pizzas.

Converting Improper to Mixed Fractions

  • Divide the numerator by the denominator.
  • The quotient is the whole number part.
  • The remainder is the new numerator.
  • The denominator remains the same.

🎙️ To convert an improper fraction to a mixed fraction, we perform division. The number of times the denominator goes into the numerator gives us the whole number. Any leftover, the remainder, becomes the new numerator, while the original denominator stays the same.

1Divide Numerator by Denominator
2Quotient = Whole Number
3Remainder = New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same
9 / 25

Worked Example: Improper to Mixed

  • Convert 7/3 to a mixed fraction.
  • Divide 7 by 3: 7 ÷ 3 = 2 with a remainder of 1.
  • Whole number = 2, Numerator = 1, Denominator = 3.
  • So, 7/3 = 2 1/3.

🎙️ Let's try an example. To convert 7/3, we divide 7 by 3. We get a quotient of 2 and a remainder of 1. This means our mixed fraction is 2 and 1/3. It's like having two full cakes and one-third of another cake.

Converting Mixed to Improper Fractions

  • Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
  • Add the numerator to the product.
  • This sum becomes the new numerator.
  • The denominator remains the same.

🎙️ Now, let's learn how to go the other way: converting a mixed fraction back to an improper fraction. You multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the existing numerator. This result becomes your new numerator, and the denominator remains unchanged.

1Whole Number × Denominator
2+ Numerator
3= New Numerator
4Denominator Stays Same

Worked Example: Mixed to Improper

  • Convert 2 1/3 to an improper fraction.
  • Multiply whole number (2) by denominator (3): 2 × 3 = 6.
  • Add numerator (1) to the product: 6 + 1 = 7.
  • New numerator = 7, Denominator = 3.
  • So, 2 1/3 = 7/3.

🎙️ Using our previous example, let's convert 2 1/3 back. Multiply 2 by 3 to get 6, then add 1 to get 7. So, the new numerator is 7, and the denominator remains 3. This gives us 7/3, confirming our earlier conversion.

Equivalent Fractions

  • Fractions that represent the same value.
  • Look different but are equal in amount.
  • Obtained by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

🎙️ Equivalent fractions are like different names for the same amount. For example, 1/2 is the same as 2/4 or 3/6. You can find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero number. This is a very useful concept.

⚖️
Equal Value
13 / 25

Worked Example: Finding Equivalent Fractions

  • Find an equivalent fraction for 1/2.
  • Multiply numerator and denominator by 2: (1 × 2) / (2 × 2) = 2/4.
  • Multiply numerator and denominator by 3: (1 × 3) / (2 × 3) = 3/6.
  • So, 1/2, 2/4, and 3/6 are equivalent fractions.

🎙️ Let's find equivalent fractions for 1/2. If we multiply both top and bottom by 2, we get 2/4. If we multiply both by 3, we get 3/6. All these fractions represent the exact same portion of a whole, even though their numbers are different.

Simplest Form of a Fraction

  • A fraction is in its simplest form when the only common factor of its numerator and denominator is 1.
  • Also known as 'lowest terms'.
  • To simplify, divide both by their Highest Common Factor (HCF).

🎙️ The simplest form of a fraction means it cannot be reduced any further. The numerator and denominator share no common factors other than 1. To simplify a fraction, you find the HCF of the numerator and denominator and divide both by it.

✂️
Cut to Smallest
15 / 25

Worked Example: Simplifying Fractions

  • Simplify the fraction 6/9.
  • Factors of 6: 1, 2, 3, 6.
  • Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9.
  • HCF of 6 and 9 is 3.
  • Divide both by 3: (6 ÷ 3) / (9 ÷ 3) = 2/3.
  • The simplest form of 6/9 is 2/3.

🎙️ Let's simplify 6/9. The factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6. The factors of 9 are 1, 3, 9. The highest common factor is 3. Dividing both 6 and 9 by 3 gives us 2/3. So, 2/3 is the simplest form of 6/9.

Comparing Fractions: Same Denominator

  • If denominators are the same, compare the numerators.
  • The fraction with the larger numerator is greater.
  • Example: 3/5 is greater than 2/5.

🎙️ Comparing fractions is easy when they have the same denominator. You just look at the numerators. The fraction with the bigger numerator is the larger fraction because it means you have more parts of the same sized whole. Imagine having 2 slices versus 3 slices from the same 5-slice pizza.

Comparing Fractions: Different Denominators

  • Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the LCM as the new denominator.
  • Compare the new numerators (as in 'same denominator' case).

🎙️ When denominators are different, we can't compare them directly. We first need to make them 'alike' by finding a common denominator, usually the LCM. Once both fractions have the same denominator, we can then compare their numerators just like before.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Compare New Numerators
18 / 25

Worked Example: Comparing Fractions

  • Compare 1/2 and 2/3.
  • LCM of 2 and 3 is 6.
  • Convert 1/2: (1×3)/(2×3) = 3/6.
  • Convert 2/3: (2×2)/(3×2) = 4/6.
  • Since 4/6 > 3/6, then 2/3 > 1/2.

🎙️ Let's compare 1/2 and 2/3. The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6. We convert 1/2 to 3/6 and 2/3 to 4/6. Now comparing 3/6 and 4/6, it's clear that 4/6 is greater. Therefore, 2/3 is greater than 1/2.

Addition of Fractions: Same Denominator

  • Add the numerators directly.
  • Keep the denominator the same.
  • Simplify the result if possible.
  • Example: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.

🎙️ Adding fractions with the same denominator is straightforward. You simply add the numerators together and keep the denominator as it is. It's like adding 1 apple and 2 apples to get 3 apples; the 'type' of fruit, or the denominator, stays the same.

🎙️ Consider adding 3/8 and 2/8. Since the denominators are the same, we just add the numerators: 3 plus 2 equals 5. The denominator stays 8. So the sum is 5/8. This fraction is already in its simplest form.

Addition of Fractions: Different Denominators

  • Find the LCM of the denominators.
  • Convert both fractions to equivalent fractions with the LCM as the new denominator.
  • Add the new numerators, keeping the common denominator.
  • Simplify the result.

🎙️ When adding fractions with different denominators, we can't just add them directly. First, find the LCM of the denominators. Then, convert both fractions into equivalent fractions using this LCM. Once they have the same denominator, you can add their numerators and simplify.

1Find LCM of Denominators
2Convert to Equivalent Fractions
3Add New Numerators
4Simplify if Needed

Worked Example: Adding Different Denominators

  • Add 1/3 and 1/4.
  • LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.
  • Convert 1/3: (1×4)/(3×4) = 4/12.
  • Convert 1/4: (1×3)/(4×3) = 3/12.
  • Add: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. (Already in simplest form).

🎙️ Let's add 1/3 and 1/4. The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. So, 1/3 becomes 4/12, and 1/4 becomes 3/12. Now, add the numerators: 4 + 3 equals 7. The denominator remains 12. So, the sum is 7/12, which is already in its simplest form.
