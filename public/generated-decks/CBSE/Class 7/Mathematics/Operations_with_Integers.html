What are Integers?

  • Integers are whole numbers (positive, negative, and zero).
  • They don't have fractional or decimal parts.
  • Examples: -3, 0, 5, -100, 25.

🎙️ Think of integers as a big family of numbers! It includes all the counting numbers you know (1, 2, 3...), their negative counterparts (-1, -2, -3...), and the number zero. They are always 'whole' – no halves or quarters!

Adding Integers: Same Signs

  • Analogy: Collecting money or accumulating debt.
  • When signs are the same, add the absolute values.
  • Keep the common sign in the answer.

🎙️ Imagine you're collecting money. If you get ₹5 and then ₹3, you add them up to get ₹8. If you have a debt of ₹5 and then another debt of ₹3, your total debt is ₹8. The principle is the same: add the amounts and keep the sign!

Adding Integers: Different Signs

  • Analogy: A tug-of-war! Positive pulls one way, negative the other.
  • Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger one.
  • The answer takes the sign of the integer with the larger absolute value.

🎙️ Think of a tug-of-war! If one team pulls with strength 10 and the other with strength 7, the stronger team wins, and the net pull is 3 in their direction. Similarly, with different signs, the 'stronger' number (larger absolute value) dictates the sign of the result.

Properties of Integer Operations (Part 1)

  • Commutative Property (Addition): a + b = b + a (Order doesn't matter).
  • Associative Property (Addition): (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) (Grouping doesn't matter).
  • Additive Identity: a + 0 = a (Zero is the additive identity).

🎙️ Just like whole numbers, integers follow certain rules or 'properties'. The commutative property means you can swap numbers when adding. Associative means you can group them differently. And adding zero doesn't change the number, making zero the additive identity.

Multiplying Integers: Same Signs

  • Analogy: Repeated actions in the same direction.
  • Multiply the absolute values of the integers.
  • If both signs are positive or both are negative, the product is POSITIVE.

🎙️ When you multiply two integers with the same sign, the answer is always positive! Think of it as 'a friend of a friend is a friend' (positive * positive = positive) or 'an enemy of an enemy is a friend' (negative * negative = positive). The result is always good!

Properties of Integer Operations (Part 2)

  • Commutative Property: a × b = b × a
  • Associative Property: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
  • Distributive Property: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
  • Multiplicative Identity: a × 1 = a
  • Zero Property: a × 0 = 0

🎙️ Multiplication also has its own set of properties. Commutative and Associative work similarly to addition. The Distributive property is very useful for expanding expressions. Multiplying by 1 doesn't change the number, and multiplying by 0 always results in 0.

Dividing Integers: The Inverse Operation

  • Division is the inverse of multiplication.
  • The sign rules are the SAME as multiplication!
  • Same signs (++, --) give a POSITIVE quotient.
  • Different signs (+-, -+) give a NEGATIVE quotient.

🎙️ Division is like multiplication's opposite twin! The good news is that the sign rules for division are exactly the same as for multiplication. If the signs are the same, the answer is positive. If the signs are different, the answer is negative.

Beware! Common Integer Mistakes

  • Sign errors in addition/subtraction (e.g., -5 - 2 is -7, not -3).
  • Confusing subtraction with 'add the opposite' rule.
  • Forgetting that negative × negative = positive.

🎙️ Even experts make mistakes sometimes! The most common errors are getting the signs wrong, especially in subtraction. Always remember to convert subtraction to 'add the opposite'. And don't forget the 'two negatives make a positive' rule for multiplication and division!
