Recap: What are Integers?

  • Whole numbers including positive numbers (1, 2, 3...), negative numbers (-1, -2, -3...), and zero (0).
  • No fractional or decimal parts.
  • Positive integers are greater than zero, negative integers are less than zero.

🎙️ Remember, integers are like whole numbers but they also include their negative counterparts. Zero is special, it's neither positive nor negative.

Adding Integers with the Same Sign

  • Add their absolute values.
  • Keep the common sign.
  • Example: 3 + 5 = 8
  • Example: (-3) + (-5) = -8

🎙️ When both integers are positive, it's just like regular addition. If both are negative, you still add the numbers, but the result remains negative, showing a combined decrease.

Adding Positives

  • Morning temperature: 5°C
  • Temperature rises by 3°C during the day.
  • New temperature: 5°C + 3°C = 8°C

🎙️ Imagine the temperature rising. If it's already positive and goes up further, the final temperature will be a higher positive value. This is a clear example of adding same-signed integers.

Worked Example: Same Sign Addition

  • Problem: Add (-7) + (-4)
  • Step 1: Identify signs (Both negative).
  • Step 2: Add absolute values (7 + 4 = 11).
  • Step 3: Keep the common sign (Negative).
  • Answer: (-7) + (-4) = -11

🎙️ Follow these steps carefully. When both numbers are negative, think of it as accumulating debt; the total debt becomes larger. The process is straightforward once you remember to keep the sign.

Adding Integers with Different Signs

  • Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value.
  • Keep the sign of the integer with the larger absolute value.
  • Example: 7 + (-3) = 4
  • Example: (-7) + 3 = -4

🎙️ When signs are different, one number 'cancels out' part of the other. The result depends on which number has a greater 'strength' or absolute value. Think of it as a tug-of-war.

Worked Example: Different Sign Addition

  • Problem: Add (-12) + 8
  • Step 1: Identify signs (Different signs).
  • Step 2: Subtract absolute values (12 - 8 = 4).
  • Step 3: Keep sign of larger absolute value (12 is larger, its sign is negative).
  • Answer: (-12) + 8 = -4

🎙️ Here, the negative number is 'stronger' than the positive one. So, even after the positive 8 'cancels out' part of the negative 12, the result remains negative. Practice these steps carefully.

Properties of Addition: Commutative Property

  • The order of integers in addition does not change the sum.
  • For any integers 'a' and 'b', a + b = b + a.
  • Example: 3 + (-5) = -2 and (-5) + 3 = -2.

🎙️ This property is very useful! It means you can swap the positions of numbers when adding, and you'll still get the same answer. It simplifies calculations sometimes.

🔄
Order doesn't matter

Properties of Addition: Associative Property

  • The way integers are grouped in addition does not change the sum.
  • For any integers 'a', 'b', and 'c', (a + b) + c = a + (b + c).
  • Example: (2 + 3) + (-4) = 5 + (-4) = 1.
  • Also: 2 + (3 + (-4)) = 2 + (-1) = 1.

🎙️ This property tells us that when adding three or more integers, we can group them in any way we want without affecting the final sum. It helps in organizing complex additions.

🤝
Grouping doesn't matter

Properties of Addition: Additive Identity

  • Zero (0) is the additive identity for integers.
  • Adding zero to any integer does not change its value.
  • For any integer 'a', a + 0 = a and 0 + a = a.
  • Example: (-9) + 0 = -9.

🎙️ Zero acts as a special 'neutral' number in addition. It doesn't add or subtract any value, simply leaving the original integer unchanged. This property is quite intuitive.

Subtracting Integers

  • To subtract an integer, add its additive inverse (opposite).
  • a - b = a + (-b)
  • Example: 5 - 3 = 5 + (-3) = 2
  • Example: 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8

🎙️ Subtraction of integers is often thought of as 'adding the opposite'. This rule transforms every subtraction problem into an addition problem, which you already know how to solve!

  • Difference: 10 - (-3) = 10 + 3 = 13 meters.

🎙️ Calculating the vertical distance between two points, one above and one below a reference point, is a perfect example of integer subtraction. The 'minus a minus' becomes a 'plus'.

Properties of Subtraction

  • Subtraction of integers is NOT commutative: a - b ≠ b - a.
  • Example: 5 - 3 = 2, but 3 - 5 = -2.
  • Subtraction of integers is NOT associative: (a - b) - c ≠ a - (b - c).
  • Example: (10 - 5) - 2 = 3, but 10 - (5 - 2) = 7.

🎙️ It's important to note that subtraction does not follow the commutative or associative properties. The order and grouping absolutely matter when you are subtracting integers. Be careful!

Multiplying Integers with Same Signs

  • The product of two integers with the same sign is always positive.
  • (+) × (+) = (+)
  • (-) × (-) = (+)
  • Example: 4 × 3 = 12
  • Example: (-4) × (-3) = 12

🎙️ This is a key rule for multiplication. When the signs are the same, whether both positive or both negative, the answer will always be positive. Remember: 'a friend of a friend is a friend'.

✖️
Positive Result

Multiplying Integers with Different Signs

  • The product of two integers with different signs is always negative.
  • (+) × (-) = (-)
  • (-) × (+) = (-)
  • Example: 4 × (-3) = -12
  • Example: (-4) × 3 = -12

🎙️ If one integer is positive and the other is negative, their product will always be negative. Think of it as: 'a friend of an enemy is an enemy', or 'an enemy of a friend is an enemy'.

Worked Example: Multiplication

  • Step 1: Identify signs (One negative, one positive).

Recap: Operations with Integers

  • Addition: Same signs (add & keep sign), Different signs (subtract & keep sign of larger absolute value).
  • Subtraction: Add the opposite (a - b = a + (-b)).
  • Multiplication: Same signs (positive product), Different signs (negative product).
  • Properties: Commutative/Associative for Addition, not for Subtraction.
