Constructing a Line Segment of a Given Length

  • Step 1: Draw a line 'l' and mark a point 'A' on it.
  • Step 2: Open your compass to the desired length (e.g., 5 cm) using a ruler.
  • Step 3: With 'A' as the center, draw an arc intersecting line 'l' at 'B'.
  • The segment AB is the required length.

🎙️ This is the most basic construction. It's like measuring out a specific length of cloth with a measuring tape before cutting it.

Perpendicular Bisector: Cutting a Line in Half at 90°

  • A perpendicular bisector is a line that cuts another line segment exactly in half.
  • AND it crosses the line segment at a perfect 90-degree angle.
  • Think of it as slicing a sandwich perfectly down the middle.

🎙️ This construction is incredibly useful. Imagine needing to find the exact center of a wall to hang a painting, and you want it perfectly straight.

Steps to Construct a Perpendicular Bisector

  • Step 1: Draw a line segment AB.
  • Step 2: With A as center, draw arcs above and below AB (radius > half AB).
  • Step 3: With B as center, draw arcs of the same radius intersecting the first arcs.
  • Step 4: Join the two intersection points. This line is the perpendicular bisector.

🎙️ Remember, the compass opening must be more than half the length of the segment, otherwise, the arcs won't intersect!

2Arc from A
3Arc from B
4Join Points

Example: Bisecting a 7 cm Line Segment

  • Draw a line segment AB of 7 cm.
  • Open your compass to more than 3.5 cm (half of 7 cm).
  • Place the compass at A, draw arcs above and below.
  • Place the compass at B, draw arcs intersecting the first ones.
  • Connect the intersection points to get the perpendicular bisector.

Steps to Construct an Angle Bisector

  • Step 1: Draw an angle ABC.
  • Step 2: With B as center, draw an arc intersecting BA at D and BC at E.
  • Step 3: With D and E as centers, draw two arcs of the same radius intersecting each other at F.
  • Step 4: Join B to F. Ray BF is the angle bisector.

🎙️ Make sure your compass radius is consistent for the arcs from D and E. This ensures the point F is exactly in the middle.

Constructing a 60° Angle (The Basic Building Block)

  • Step 1: Draw a ray OA.
  • Step 2: With O as center, draw an arc of any radius intersecting OA at B.
  • Step 3: With B as center and the SAME radius, draw an arc intersecting the first arc at C.
  • Step 4: Join OC. Angle AOC is 60°.

🎙️ This 60° angle is super important! Many other angles, like 30°, 90°, and 120°, can be derived from it.

Constructing a 90° Angle (Perpendicular Line)

  • Step 1: Draw a ray OA.
  • Step 2: With O as center, draw a semicircle intersecting OA at P and Q.
  • Step 3: With P and Q as centers, draw arcs of radius greater than half PQ intersecting at R.
  • Step 4: Join OR. Angle AOR is 90°.

🎙️ This is essentially constructing a perpendicular line to a ray at its endpoint. It's a very common and useful construction.

Constructing a Parallel Line Through a Given Point

  • Step 1: Draw a line 'l' and a point 'P' not on 'l'.
  • Step 2: Choose a point 'Q' on 'l' and join PQ.
  • Step 3: At Q, construct an angle equal to the angle PQl (e.g., using alternate interior angles concept).
  • Step 4: Extend the arm of the new angle through P. This is the parallel line.

🎙️ This method relies on the property that if alternate interior angles (or corresponding angles) are equal, then the lines are parallel.

The Golden Rule for Tiling: Angles at a Vertex

  • For shapes to tile a flat surface, the sum of the angles around any common vertex (point) must be exactly 360°.
  • If the sum is less than 360°, there will be a gap.
  • If the sum is more than 360°, the shapes will overlap.

🎙️ Imagine placing pieces of a pie around a central point. To make a full pie, all the slices must add up to a complete circle, which is 360 degrees.

Regular Polygons That Can Tile a Plane

  • Equilateral Triangle (60° per angle): 6 x 60° = 360°
  • Square (90° per angle): 4 x 90° = 360°
  • Regular Hexagon (120° per angle): 3 x 120° = 360°

🎙️ These three regular polygons are the only ones that can tile a surface perfectly on their own without using other shapes. They are true tiling champions!

Why Other Regular Polygons Don't Tile

  • Regular Pentagon (108° per angle): 3 x 108° = 324° (gap), 4 x 108° = 432° (overlap).
  • Regular Octagon (135° per angle): 2 x 135° = 270° (gap), 3 x 135° = 405° (overlap).
  • Their interior angles don't divide 360° perfectly.

🎙️ Even though pentagons and octagons are beautiful shapes, they just can't form a perfect, gap-free, overlap-free tiling on their own. They need other shapes to help!

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Constructions

  • Inaccurate Compass Opening: Always ensure the compass maintains the correct radius.
  • Faint Construction Lines: Don't erase construction lines; they show your work.
  • Not Labeling Points: Clear labels (A, B, C) help track steps.
  • Using Ruler for Angles: Only use ruler for straight lines, not for measuring angles in constructions.

🎙️ Precision is paramount in constructions. A slight error in compass opening can lead to completely wrong results. Always be careful and neat.
