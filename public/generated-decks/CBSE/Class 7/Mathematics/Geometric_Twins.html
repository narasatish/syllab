Defining Congruence

  • Two figures are congruent if they have the same shape and the same size.
  • They can be perfectly superimposed on each other.
  • The symbol for congruence is '≅'.

🎙️ Congruence is the mathematical term for 'geometric twins'. If you can place one figure exactly over another, and they match perfectly, then they are congruent. Remember the symbol '≅'!

Congruence of Line Segments: Definition

  • Two line segments are congruent if and only if they have the same length.
  • If AB = 5 cm and CD = 5 cm, then AB ≅ CD.

🎙️ It's straightforward! To check if two line segments are congruent, all you need to do is measure their lengths. If the measurements are equal, they are congruent.

Congruence of Angles: Analogy

  • Imagine a clock showing 3 o'clock.
  • The angle formed by the hands is a right angle (90°).
  • Now imagine another clock also showing 3 o'clock. Both angles are identical!

🎙️ The 'openness' between the hands of the clock is the angle. If two clocks show the same time, the angles formed by their hands will be exactly the same. This applies to geometric angles too.

Congruence of Angles: Definition

  • Two angles are congruent if and only if they have the same measure.
  • If ∠A = 60° and ∠B = 60°, then ∠A ≅ ∠B.

🎙️ Similar to line segments, for angles, we compare their measures in degrees. If two angles have identical degree measures, they are congruent, regardless of the length of their arms.

🎙️ Plane figures are 2D shapes like squares, circles, or polygons. If you can pick up one figure and place it perfectly on top of another, such that all edges and vertices align, they are congruent.

Congruence of Plane Figures: Definition

  • Two plane figures are congruent if they have exactly the same shape and size.
  • They can be perfectly superimposed on each other.
  • Example: Two identical circles with the same radius.

🎙️ For any plane figure, congruence means a perfect match. Every corresponding part – sides, angles, curves – must be identical. If one is a scaled-up or scaled-down version, they are not congruent.

🎙️ Congruence of triangles is a big part of this chapter. Triangles are unique because they are rigid – their shape is fixed once their sides are determined. This makes their congruence criteria special.

What Makes Two Triangles Congruent?

  • Two triangles are congruent if all their corresponding parts are equal.
  • This means: 3 corresponding sides are equal.
  • And: 3 corresponding angles are equal.

🎙️ For a complete definition of triangle congruence, we need all six corresponding parts to be equal: three sides and three angles. However, checking all six can be tedious. Luckily, there are shortcuts!

Criteria for Congruence of Triangles

  • Do we always need to check all 6 parts?
  • No! There are special conditions (criteria) that guarantee congruence.
  • These are our 'shortcuts' to prove triangles are twins!

🎙️ These criteria are powerful tools. They allow us to prove two triangles are congruent by checking only three specific corresponding parts, saving us a lot of effort. Let's explore them one by one.

Criterion 1: SSS (Side-Side-Side) Analogy

  • Imagine you have exactly 3 sticks of specific lengths.
  • Can you build two different triangles using these same 3 sticks?
  • No, you can only build one unique triangle!

🎙️ This analogy highlights the rigidity of triangles. If you fix the lengths of the three sides, the shape and size of the triangle are also fixed. This is the essence of the SSS criterion.

🪵🪵🪵
Building with 3 sticks

SSS Criterion: Definition & Example

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent (SSS Congruence Rule).

🎙️ So, if you know that all three corresponding sides are equal in length, you can confidently say the triangles are congruent without checking the angles. This is a very common criterion.

1Side 1 = Side 1
2Side 2 = Side 2
3Side 3 = Side 3

Criterion 2: SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Analogy

  • Imagine two slices of pizza from the same large pizza.
  • If their crust lengths (sides) are the same, and the angle at the tip (included angle) is the same,
  • Then the slices are identical!

🎙️ Here, the angle is 'included' between the two sides. This means the angle is formed by the two sides whose lengths we know. This specific arrangement is crucial for SAS congruence.

🍕🍕

SAS Criterion: Definition & Example

  • If two sides and the *included* angle of one triangle are equal to the two corresponding sides and the included angle of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent (SAS Congruence Rule).

🎙️ The term 'included angle' is very important here. It must be the angle *between* the two sides you are comparing. If it's any other angle, SAS does not apply.

1Side 1 = Side 1
2Included Angle = Included Angle
3Side 2 = Side 2

Criterion 3: ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Analogy

  • Picture two identical road signs, perhaps triangular 'Yield' signs.
  • If two of their angles are the same, and the side *between* those angles is also the same,
  • Then the signs are identical in shape and size!

🎙️ Similar to SAS, ASA also requires an 'included' element – in this case, the side. The side must be common to both the angles you are comparing for this criterion to be valid.

🛑

ASA Criterion: Definition & Example

  • If two angles and the *included* side of one triangle are equal to the two corresponding angles and the included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent (ASA Congruence Rule).

🎙️ Again, the word 'included' is key. The side must be the one connecting the vertices of the two angles you are using for comparison. If the side is not included, this rule doesn't work.

1Angle 1 = Angle 1
2Included Side = Included Side
3Angle 2 = Angle 2

Criterion 4: RHS (Right-angle Hypotenuse Side) Analogy

  • Think of two identical right-angled set squares.
  • They both have a 90° angle, and if their hypotenuse and one other side are equal,
  • Then they are perfect copies!

🎙️ This criterion is specific to right-angled triangles. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle. This special case helps us prove congruence for these triangles.

RHS Criterion: Definition & Example

  • If in two right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle, then the two triangles are congruent (RHS Congruence Rule).

🎙️ Remember, RHS only applies to right-angled triangles. You must have the right angle, the hypotenuse, and one other corresponding side equal for this rule to be valid. It's a powerful shortcut!

1Right Angle = Right Angle
2Hypotenuse = Hypotenuse
3Side = Side

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing congruence with similarity (same shape, different size).
  • Assuming AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) proves congruence (it only proves similarity).
  • Not using the 'included' side or angle correctly in SAS/ASA.

🎙️ These are crucial points. Many students make these mistakes. Always remember that for congruence, both shape and size must be identical. AAA is a big trap – it's for similarity, not congruence!

Exam Tips for Congruence Questions

  • Always draw and label the triangles clearly.
  • Identify the corresponding parts carefully.
