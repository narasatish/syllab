What Does "Congruent" Mean?

  • Exact copies of each other
  • Same size and same shape
  • Symbol: ≅

🎙️ When two figures are congruent, it means they are identical in every way. Imagine two identical twins; they are congruent! They match perfectly when one is placed over the other.

Congruent Line Segments

  • Two line segments are congruent if they have the same length.

🎙️ If you have two rulers and measure two line segments, and both measure exactly 5 cm, then those two line segments are congruent. Length is the only factor we consider here.

Congruent Angles

  • Two angles are congruent if they have the same measure.

🎙️ Similarly, for angles, if two angles both measure 60 degrees, then they are congruent. The length of their arms doesn't matter, only the opening between them.

Congruent Plane Figures

  • Two plane figures are congruent if one can be exactly superimposed on the other.

🎙️ This is the general idea for any 2D shape. If you can pick up one shape and place it directly on top of another, and they match perfectly, then they are congruent.

Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (CPCTC)

  • If two triangles are congruent, then their corresponding parts are equal.

🎙️ This is a crucial concept. It means if you know two triangles are congruent, you automatically know that all their matching sides and matching angles are also equal. We call this CPCTC.

The Need for Congruence Criteria

  • Tracing is not always practical
  • We need rules to prove congruence without tracing
  • These rules are called congruence criteria

🎙️ While tracing is good for understanding, it's not always possible or precise. Imagine proving two huge triangles are congruent! We need mathematical rules, or criteria, to do this logically.

✅
Rules for Proof
11 / 25

Criterion 1: SSS (Side-Side-Side) Rule

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ This rule is powerful! If you can measure all three sides of two triangles and find that they are exactly the same lengths, you don't need to check angles – the triangles are congruent.

📏📏📏
Sides Match
12 / 25

SSS Worked Example

  • Triangle ABC and Triangle PQR
  • AB=PQ, BC=QR, CA=RP
  • Therefore, ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR (by SSS)

🎙️ Let's see this in action. If we have two triangles, and we know that side AB equals side PQ, side BC equals side QR, and side CA equals side RP, then we can confidently say the triangles are congruent by the SSS rule.

✍️
Solve It
13 / 25

Criterion 2: SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Rule

  • If two sides and the *included* angle of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and the included angle of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ Here, the order matters! The angle MUST be between the two sides. If you have two sides and the angle formed by them matching in both triangles, they are congruent.

📏📐📏
Side Angle Side
14 / 25

SAS Worked Example

  • Triangle LMN and Triangle XYZ
  • LM=XY, ∠M=∠Y, MN=YZ
  • Therefore, ΔLMN ≅ ΔXYZ (by SAS)

🎙️ Imagine two triangles where you know two sides and the angle between them. If these three parts match perfectly in both triangles, then they are congruent by the SAS rule. Remember, the angle must be the *included* angle.

Criterion 3: ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Rule

  • If two angles and the *included* side of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and the included side of another triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ Similar to SAS, the side here MUST be between the two angles. If you have two angles and the side connecting their vertices matching in both triangles, they are congruent.

📐📏📐
Angle Side Angle
17 / 25

ASA Worked Example

  • Triangle DEF and Triangle GHI
  • ∠E=∠H, EF=HI, ∠F=∠I
  • Therefore, ΔDEF ≅ ΔGHI (by ASA)

🎙️ If we know two angles and the side that connects them in one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and included side in another, then the ASA rule proves their congruence.

Criterion 4: RHS (Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side) Rule

  • For two right-angled triangles, if the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one corresponding side of the other triangle, then the triangles are congruent.

🎙️ This rule is specifically for right-angled triangles. You need to identify the right angle, the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle), and one other corresponding side.

📐📈📏
Right Hypotenuse Side
19 / 25

RHS Worked Example

  • Triangle PQR and Triangle STU (both right-angled at Q and T)
  • Hypotenuse PR = Hypotenuse SU
  • Side PQ = Side ST
  • Therefore, ΔPQR ≅ ΔSTU (by RHS)

🎙️ Let's apply RHS. If you have two right-angled triangles, and their hypotenuses are equal, and one pair of corresponding legs are also equal, then the triangles are congruent.

Common Mistakes & Misconceptions

  • AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) does NOT prove congruence
  • SSA (Side-Side-Angle) does NOT prove congruence

🎙️ Be careful! Just because angles are equal (AAA) doesn't mean triangles are congruent (they could be similar). Also, SSA is not a valid congruence rule because it can lead to two different triangles.
