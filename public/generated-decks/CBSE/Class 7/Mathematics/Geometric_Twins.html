What are "Geometric Twins"?

  • Shapes that are exactly the same in size and shape
  • They can be placed one over the other perfectly
  • Think of two identical copies of a photo

🎙️ When we talk about 'geometric twins', we mean figures that are an exact match. Imagine you have two identical coins. If you place one on top of the other, they cover each other perfectly, right? That's the essence of congruence.

Understanding Congruence

  • Derived from Latin: 'con' (together) and 'gruere' (to agree)
  • Means 'to agree together' or 'to coincide'
  • Two figures are congruent if they have the same size and the same shape

🎙️ The word 'congruence' itself tells us a lot. It comes from Latin, meaning 'to agree together'. In geometry, it means two figures perfectly match. If you can superimpose one figure exactly onto another, they are congruent.

Congruence of Line Segments

  • Two line segments are congruent if they have the same length
  • The symbol for congruence is '≅'
  • If segment AB is congruent to segment CD, we write AB ≅ CD

🎙️ Let's start with the simplest geometric figure: a line segment. Two line segments are congruent if, and only if, they have the exact same length. It doesn't matter how they are oriented; if their measures are equal, they are twins.

Congruence of Angles

  • Two angles are congruent if they have the same measure
  • The length of the arms does not affect congruence
  • If angle PQR is congruent to angle XYZ, we write ∠PQR ≅ ∠XYZ

🎙️ Next, let's look at angles. For two angles to be congruent, their degree measures must be identical. Remember, the length of the lines forming the angle doesn't change its measure; only how 'open' or 'closed' it is matters.

Congruence of Plane Figures

  • Two plane figures are congruent if they have the same shape and the same size
  • One can be exactly superimposed on the other
  • This applies to squares, circles, triangles, and any other 2D shape

🎙️ Extending this idea, any two-dimensional shape – be it a square, a circle, or an irregular polygon – is congruent if one can be perfectly placed over the other. They are exact replicas, sharing identical dimensions and form.

Why Triangles are Special

  • They are rigid shapes, unlike squares or rectangles
  • Understanding triangle congruence is key to many geometric proofs

🎙️ Triangles are incredibly important in geometry. They are the simplest closed figures and possess a unique rigidity. This makes their congruence particularly useful in engineering and architectural designs, as well as in proving other geometric properties.

Congruence of Triangles

  • Two triangles are congruent if their corresponding parts (sides and angles) are equal
  • We don't need to check all 6 parts (3 sides, 3 angles)
  • Specific criteria help us determine congruence more easily

🎙️ For two triangles to be congruent, every corresponding side must be equal, and every corresponding angle must be equal. However, we have some clever shortcuts, called criteria, that allow us to prove congruence by checking fewer parts.

SSS Congruence Criterion

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle
  • Then the two triangles are congruent
  • This means all angles will also be equal automatically

🎙️ The SSS criterion is straightforward: if you can show that all three sides of one triangle are exactly the same length as the three corresponding sides of another triangle, then those triangles are congruent. You don't even need to check the angles!

📏
All Sides Match

SSS Worked Example

  • Given ΔABC and ΔPQR
  • AB = PQ = 4 cm, BC = QR = 5 cm, CA = RP = 6 cm
  • By SSS criterion, ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR

🎙️ Let's say we have two triangles. If we measure their sides and find that AB matches PQ, BC matches QR, and CA matches RP, then we can confidently declare them congruent using the SSS rule. It's like having two identical triangular pieces of cardboard.

SAS Congruence Criterion

  • If two sides and the *included angle* of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and the *included angle* of another triangle
  • Then the two triangles are congruent
  • The angle must be between the two sides

🎙️ The SAS criterion is powerful: if two sides and the angle *between* them (the included angle) in one triangle are equal to the corresponding parts in another, the triangles are congruent. The order here is crucial; the angle must be 'sandwiched' between the two sides.

SAS Worked Example

  • XY = DE = 5 cm, ∠Y = ∠E = 70°, YZ = EF = 6 cm
  • By SAS criterion, ΔXYZ ≅ ΔDEF

🎙️ Consider two triangles where we know two sides and the angle between them. If XY equals DE, angle Y equals angle E, and YZ equals EF, then by SAS, these triangles are congruent. Always ensure the angle is indeed the one between the two known sides.

ASA Congruence Criterion

  • If two angles and the *included side* of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two angles and the *included side* of another triangle
  • Then the two triangles are congruent
  • The side must be between the two angles

🎙️ Similar to SAS, ASA focuses on two angles and the side *between* them (the included side). If these three corresponding parts match in two triangles, they are congruent. Again, the 'included' part is vital for this criterion.

ASA Worked Example

  • ∠L = ∠S = 60°, LM = ST = 7 cm, ∠M = ∠T = 80°
  • By ASA criterion, ΔLMN ≅ ΔSTU

🎙️ If we have two triangles where angle L matches angle S, the side LM matches ST, and angle M matches angle T, then we can use ASA to prove congruence. Notice how the side LM is exactly between angles L and M.

RHS Congruence Criterion

  • Specific for *right-angled triangles* only
  • If the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and corresponding side of another right-angled triangle
  • Then the two triangles are congruent

🎙️ RHS is a special criterion just for right-angled triangles. If both triangles have a right angle, and their hypotenuses are equal, and one pair of corresponding legs (sides) are also equal, then the triangles are congruent. Remember, this only applies to right triangles!

RHS Worked Example

  • Given right-angled ΔPQR and ΔXYZ (right-angled at Q and Y)
  • Hypotenuse PR = XZ = 10 cm, Side QR = YZ = 6 cm
  • By RHS criterion, ΔPQR ≅ ΔXYZ

🎙️ In two right triangles, if the hypotenuse of one matches the hypotenuse of the other, and one leg of the first matches a leg of the second, then they are congruent by RHS. This is a powerful shortcut for right triangles.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Congruent figures are exact copies (same size, same shape)
  • Line segments are congruent if they have equal length
  • Angles are congruent if they have equal measure
  • Four criteria for triangle congruence: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS
