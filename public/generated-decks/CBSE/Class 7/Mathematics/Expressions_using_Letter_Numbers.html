What are Algebraic Expressions?

  • Combinations of variables, constants, and mathematical operations.
  • They do NOT have an equality sign (=).

🎙️ An algebraic expression is like a mathematical phrase. It combines numbers, letters (variables), and operations like addition or multiplication, but it doesn't state that something is equal to something else.

  • Numbers have fixed values.
  • Variables can take different values.

🎙️ Think of numbers as known quantities, always staying the same. Variables, represented by letters, are like placeholders for values that can change or are unknown.

Understanding Constants

  • A symbol with a fixed numerical value.
  • Its value does not change.
  • Examples: 5, -10, 3/4.

🎙️ Constants are the simple numbers you've always worked with. They have a definite value that never changes, no matter what else happens in the problem.

Introducing Variables

  • A symbol (usually a letter) that represents a quantity that can change.
  • Its value is not fixed.
  • Examples: x, y, a, b, p.

🎙️ Variables are the exciting new part! They are letters that stand for numbers whose values might change or are currently unknown. They make expressions flexible.

How to Form Expressions

  • Combine variables and constants.
  • Use mathematical operations (+, -, ×, ÷).

🎙️ To build an expression, you simply take variables and constants and connect them using the basic mathematical operations we all know. It's like building a sentence with words and punctuation.

1Start with a variable (x)
2Add a constant (+5)
3Result: x + 5

Real-Life Example: Age

  • If your current age is 'y' years.
  • What will be your age after 5 years?

🎙️ Imagine your friend's age is unknown, so we call it 'y'. If you want to find their age five years from now, you simply add 5 to 'y'. So, the expression is y + 5.

Expressions with Addition & Subtraction

  • 'Sum of x and 7' becomes x + 7.
  • '5 less than y' becomes y - 5.
  • 'p increased by 10' becomes p + 10.

🎙️ When you see words like 'sum', 'more than', 'increased by', think addition. For 'difference', 'less than', 'decreased by', think subtraction. These words translate directly into mathematical operations.

🎙️ If you start with an amount of money, 'm', and then spend 50 rupees, the money remaining is found by subtracting 50 from 'm'. The expression is m - 50.

Expressions with Multiplication & Division

  • 'Product of 3 and x' becomes 3x.
  • 'y divided by 4' becomes y/4.
  • 'Twice p' becomes 2p.

🎙️ For multiplication, words like 'product', 'times', 'twice', 'thrice' are clues. For division, look for 'divided by', 'quotient', 'half'. Remember, 3x means 3 multiplied by x.

Understanding Terms of an Expression

  • Parts of an expression separated by '+' or '-' signs.
  • Each term is a product of factors.
  • Example: In 3x + 5, '3x' and '5' are terms.

🎙️ Think of terms as the individual building blocks of an expression. They are the pieces that are added or subtracted together. Each term itself can be a combination of numbers and variables multiplied together.

🧩
Building Blocks

Worked Example: Identifying Terms

  • Expression: 4a - 7b + 2
  • Terms are: 4a, -7b, 2

🎙️ In this expression, we see three distinct parts separated by plus or minus signs. So, 4a is one term, -7b is another term (remember to include the sign!), and 2 is the third term.

Like and Unlike Terms

  • Like terms have the same variables with the same powers.
  • Unlike terms have different variables or different powers.
  • Example: 2x and 5x are like terms; 2x and 5y are unlike terms.

🎙️ This is crucial for simplifying expressions! Like terms are like identical twins – they have the exact same variable parts. Unlike terms are different, like apples and oranges, and cannot be directly combined.

Worked Example: Grouping Like Terms

  • Expression: 3a + 4b - a + 2b
  • Like terms: (3a, -a) and (4b, 2b)
  • Simplified: 2a + 6b

🎙️ To simplify, we group the terms that are 'alike'. Here, 3a and -a are like terms, and 4b and 2b are like terms. We combine them separately to get the simplified expression.

Understanding Coefficients

  • The numerical factor of a term.
  • It is the number multiplied by the variable part.
  • Example: In 5x, '5' is the coefficient.

🎙️ The coefficient is simply the number that stands in front of a variable in a term. It tells you how many times that variable is being counted. If there's no number, the coefficient is usually 1 (like in 'x', the coefficient is 1).

Multiplier

Worked Example: Identifying Coefficients

  • Expression: 7p - q + 3r
  • Coefficient of p is 7.
  • Coefficient of q is -1.
  • Coefficient of r is 3.

🎙️ Be careful with the signs! For -q, the coefficient is -1, as it's equivalent to -1 * q. For terms like 'p', the coefficient is 1, even if not explicitly written.

Expressions vs. Equations

  • Expressions: No equality sign (=). Example: 3x + 5
  • Equations: Have an equality sign (=). Example: 3x + 5 = 11

🎙️ This is a key difference. Expressions are just phrases, while equations are complete sentences that state two expressions are equal to each other. We will learn more about equations later.

Why are Expressions Useful?

  • Represent real-world situations simply.
  • Solve problems where values are unknown or change.
  • Foundation for higher mathematics.

🎙️ Expressions help us generalize problems. Instead of solving for one specific number, we can create a formula that works for any number, making math much more powerful and efficient.

Chapter Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Expressions combine variables, constants, and operations.

  • Terms are parts separated by '+' or '-'.
