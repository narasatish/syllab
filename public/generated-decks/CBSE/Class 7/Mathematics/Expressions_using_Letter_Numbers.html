What are Algebraic Expressions?

  • Combinations of variables, constants, and operations.
  • No equality sign (=).
  • They represent a value.

🎙️ An algebraic expression is like a mathematical phrase. It uses numbers, letters, and operations like addition or subtraction, but it doesn't state that something is equal to something else.

Variables: Our Changing Friends (a, b, x, y)

  • Letters that represent unknown values.
  • Their value can change.
  • Examples: x, y, a, b, m, p.

🎙️ Think of variables as placeholders for numbers. Their value isn't fixed; it can vary depending on the situation. We use letters like 'x' or 'y' to denote them.

Constants: The Unchanging Numbers

  • Numbers with fixed values.
  • Their value never changes.
  • Examples: 5, -7, 100, 3/4.

🎙️ Unlike variables, constants are just regular numbers. Their value is always the same, no matter what. They provide the fixed part of our expressions.

Terms: The Building Blocks

  • Parts of an expression separated by + or -.
  • Each term can be a variable, a constant, or a product.
  • Example: In 3x + 5y - 7, the terms are 3x, 5y, and -7.

🎙️ Think of terms as the individual pieces that make up an expression. They are separated by the addition or subtraction signs. Identifying terms is crucial for understanding expressions.

Coefficients: The Multiplier of a Variable

  • The numerical factor of a term.
  • It tells us how many times the variable is multiplied.
  • Example: In 5x, 5 is the coefficient of x.

🎙️ When a number is placed directly before a variable, it's called the coefficient. It literally 'co-operates' with the variable, multiplying its value. If there's no number, the coefficient is 1.

Like Terms vs. Unlike Terms

  • Like terms have the same variables with the same powers.
  • Unlike terms have different variables or different powers.
  • Example: 2x and 5x are like terms; 2x and 5y are unlike terms.

🎙️ This is a very important concept! Like terms are similar because they have the exact same variable part. Unlike terms are different and cannot be directly combined through addition or subtraction.

Why Do Like Terms Matter?

  • Only like terms can be added or subtracted.
  • Simplifies expressions.
  • Makes calculations easier and more organized.

🎙️ Just like you can add 2 apples and 3 apples to get 5 apples, you can add 2x and 3x to get 5x. But you cannot add 2 apples and 3 bananas directly. This rule is fundamental in algebra.

Adding & Subtracting Like Terms: The Rule

  • Add/subtract their coefficients.
  • Keep the variable part the same.
  • Example: 3x + 4x = (3+4)x = 7x.

🎙️ The process is straightforward: focus on the numbers in front of the variables. Perform the addition or subtraction on these coefficients, and the variable part simply tags along.

1Identify Like Terms
2Add/Subtract Coefficients
3Keep Variable Part Same

Worked Example: Adding Like Terms

  • Simplify: 5a + 3b + 2a + 4b
  • Group like terms: (5a + 2a) + (3b + 4b)
  • Add coefficients: (5+2)a + (3+4)b
  • Result: 7a + 7b

🎙️ Let's work through an example. First, find terms that have the same variable part. Then, combine them by adding their coefficients. Notice how 'a' terms combine with 'a' terms, and 'b' terms with 'b' terms.

Worked Example: Subtracting Like Terms

  • Simplify: 8x - 3y + 2x - y
  • Group like terms: (8x + 2x) + (-3y - y)
  • Subtract coefficients: (8+2)x + (-3-1)y
  • Result: 10x - 4y

🎙️ Subtraction works similarly. Remember that if a variable has no coefficient, it's understood to be 1. Pay close attention to the signs before each term when combining them.

Multiplying Expressions: Simple Cases

  • Multiply constants with constants.
  • Multiply variables with variables (add powers).
  • Example: 3x * 2y = (3*2) * (x*y) = 6xy.

🎙️ When multiplying, we can multiply numbers together and variables together. If the variables are the same, we add their powers, but for now, we'll focus on simpler cases where variables are different or just one variable exists.

Dividing Expressions: Simple Cases

  • Divide constants by constants.
  • Divide variables by variables (subtract powers).
  • Example: 6x / 2 = (6/2) * x = 3x.

🎙️ Division also follows a similar pattern. Divide the numerical parts and the variable parts separately. This helps simplify complex expressions into a more manageable form.

Evaluating Expressions: Finding the Value

  • Substitute numerical values for variables.
  • Perform the operations.
  • Find the final numerical value of the expression.

🎙️ Evaluating an expression means finding its actual numerical value. We do this by replacing each variable with a given number and then calculating the result using the order of operations.

➡️
Substitution

Worked Example: Evaluating an Expression

  • Evaluate 2x + 5 when x = 3.
  • Step 1: Substitute x = 3 into the expression.
  • 2(3) + 5
  • Step 2: Perform multiplication.
  • 6 + 5
  • Step 3: Perform addition.
  • Result: 11

🎙️ Let's see this in action. If we are given an expression and a value for the variable, we simply plug in that value and calculate the answer step-by-step.
