Variables: The Changing Values

🎙️ In mathematics, we use letters like 'x', 'y', 'a', or 'b' to stand for these unknown or changing quantities. That's why they are called 'variables' - their value can vary!

The Number Partner: Coefficients

  • The numerical factor of a term.
  • It's the number multiplying the variable(s).
  • It tells us 'how many' of the variable.

🎙️ Every variable term has a number attached to it, called the coefficient. It literally 'co-operates' with the variable, telling us how many times the variable is being counted.

Finding the Coefficients

  • Expression: 5x - 7y + z
  • Coefficient of x is 5.
  • Coefficient of y is -7.
  • Coefficient of z is 1 (since z is 1z).

🎙️ In 5x, the coefficient is 5. In -7y, it's -7. If a variable stands alone, like 'z', its coefficient is understood to be 1, because z is the same as 1z.

Grouping Terms: Like vs. Unlike

  • Terms with the same variable part are 'like terms'.
  • Terms with different variable parts are 'unlike terms'.
  • Think of sorting fruits!

🎙️ Imagine you have a basket of fruits. You can easily group all the apples together and all the bananas together. Similarly, 'like terms' can be grouped because they represent the same kind of quantity.

Spot the Difference!

  • Like Terms: 4a, -2a, a (all have 'a' as variable part).
  • Unlike Terms: 3x, 5y, 7 (different variable parts or no variable).
  • Like Terms: 2xy, -xy, 5xy (all have 'xy' as variable part).

🎙️ The key is to look at the variable part of the term. If it's exactly the same, including the powers of variables (which we'll learn later), then they are like terms.

How to Evaluate: Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Write down the expression.
  • Step 2: Substitute the given value for each variable.
  • Step 3: Perform the operations using BODMAS/PEMDAS rule.
  • Step 4: Write down the final numerical value.

🎙️ This systematic approach helps avoid errors. Always substitute first, then follow the order of operations carefully.

Let's Evaluate an Expression!

  • Evaluate 2x + 3 when x = 4.
  • Step 1: Expression is 2x + 3.
  • Step 2: Substitute x = 4: 2(4) + 3.
  • Step 3: Calculate: 8 + 3 = 11.
  • The value is 11.

🎙️ Remember that 2x means 2 multiplied by x. So, when x is 4, it becomes 2 times 4, which is 8. Then, we add 3 to get 11.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing 'x' as a variable with '×' as multiplication sign.
  • Incorrectly identifying like terms (e.g., 2x and 2xy).
  • Ignoring the sign of a term or coefficient.
  • Errors in order of operations during evaluation.

🎙️ Many students mix up the variable 'x' with the multiplication symbol. Always be clear about what you're writing. Also, pay close attention to the variable parts when identifying like terms.

Today's Takeaways: A Quick Recap!

  • Variables are letters for unknown values; Constants are fixed numbers.
  • Expressions combine variables, constants, and operations.
  • Terms are parts of expressions separated by + or -.
  • Coefficients are numerical factors of terms.
  • Like terms have identical variable parts.
  • Evaluate by substituting values and simplifying.
