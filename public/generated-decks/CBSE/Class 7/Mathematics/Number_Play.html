Numbers & Their Places

  • Every digit in a number has a value.
  • Value depends on its position (place).
  • Example: In 345, '3' is hundreds, '4' is tens, '5' is units.

🎙️ Remember how important a digit's position is? The same digit can mean different things based on whether it's in the units, tens, or hundreds place. This concept is fundamental to number play.

Building 2-Digit Numbers

  • A 2-digit number 'ab' means (10 × a) + b.
  • 'a' is the tens digit, 'b' is the units digit.
  • Example: 47 = (10 × 4) + 7.

🎙️ Think of it like building blocks! A 2-digit number is made up of 'a' tens blocks and 'b' unit blocks. So, a number like '47' is actually 4 groups of ten and 7 single units.

Building 3-Digit Numbers

  • A 3-digit number 'abc' means (100 × a) + (10 × b) + c.
  • 'a' is hundreds, 'b' is tens, 'c' is units.
  • Example: 529 = (100 × 5) + (10 × 2) + 9.

🎙️ Similarly, for 3-digit numbers, we add a hundreds place. So, a number like 'abc' is 'a' hundreds, 'b' tens, and 'c' units. It's like building a bigger house!

Puzzle Time!

  • Original number = 10a + b.
  • Reversed number = 10b + a.

Rules of the Game

  • Each letter (A, B, C, etc.) stands for only one digit (0-9).
  • Different letters must stand for different digits.
  • The first digit of a number cannot be zero (e.g., A cannot be 0 in AB).

🎙️ To play this game fairly, we have some clear rules. Remember, if 'A' is 3, it cannot be any other digit in the same puzzle. And just like real numbers, the leading digit can't be zero.

Solve the Addition Puzzle

  • A B
  • + 3 7
  • ------
  • 1 0 C
  • Clue: B + 7 ends in C. A + 3 + (carry if any) = 10. If B=3, then B+7 = 10, C=0, carry 1. A+3+1 = 10, so A=6. Thus, A=6, B=3, C=0.

🎙️ Let's try an addition puzzle. We look at each column, starting from the units place, and use logical deduction. Remember to account for any carry-overs between columns.

  • Helps in simplifying fractions and finding factors.

Is it Divisible by 10?

  • A number is divisible by 10 if its units digit is 0.
  • Example: 120, 500, 90 are divisible by 10.
  • 125 is not divisible by 10 (units digit is 5).

🎙️ This is one of the easiest rules! If a number ends with a zero, it's definitely divisible by 10. Think about why this makes sense – numbers ending in zero are multiples of 10.

Is it Divisible by 5?

  • A number is divisible by 5 if its units digit is 0 or 5.
  • Example: 35, 100, 785 are divisible by 5.
  • 23 is not divisible by 5 (units digit is 3).

🎙️ Similar to 10, but a bit broader. If a number ends in a 0 or a 5, it's a multiple of 5. This is because every multiple of 5 will have either 0 or 5 in its units place.

Is it Divisible by 2?

  • A number is divisible by 2 if its units digit is an even number (0, 2, 4, 6, 8).
  • Example: 48, 122, 576 are divisible by 2.
  • 37 is not divisible by 2 (units digit is 7, odd).

🎙️ This is the rule for even numbers! If the last digit of a number is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8, then the entire number can be divided by 2 without any remainder.

Is it Divisible by 3?

  • A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3.
  • Example: For 123, sum = 1+2+3 = 6. 6 is divisible by 3, so 123 is too.
  • For 451, sum = 4+5+1 = 10. 10 is not divisible by 3, so 451 is not.

🎙️ This rule is a bit different. You need to add up all the digits of the number. If that sum is a multiple of 3, then the original number is also divisible by 3. It's a neat trick!

Is it Divisible by 9?

  • A number is divisible by 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9.
  • Example: For 549, sum = 5+4+9 = 18. 18 is divisible by 9, so 549 is too.
  • For 123, sum = 1+2+3 = 6. 6 is not divisible by 9, so 123 is not.

🎙️ The rule for 9 is very similar to the rule for 3. Again, you sum up the digits. If that sum is a multiple of 9, then the number is divisible by 9. All numbers divisible by 9 are also divisible by 3.

Is it Divisible by 6?

  • A number is divisible by 6 if it is divisible by BOTH 2 and 3.
  • Example: For 126: Ends in 6 (divisible by 2). Sum = 1+2+6 = 9 (divisible by 3). So 126 is divisible by 6.
  • For 207: Sum = 9 (divisible by 3), but ends in 7 (not divisible by 2). So 207 is not divisible by 6.

🎙️ To check for divisibility by 6, you need to apply two rules! First, check if it's an even number (divisible by 2). Second, check if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. Both must be true!

Is it Divisible by 4?

  • A number is divisible by 4 if the number formed by its last two digits is divisible by 4.
  • Example: For 716, last two digits form 16. 16 is divisible by 4, so 716 is too.
  • For 323, last two digits form 23. 23 is not divisible by 4, so 323 is not.

🎙️ For divisibility by 4, you don't need to look at the whole number, just the last two digits! If the number formed by the tens and units digit is divisible by 4, then the whole number is.

Is it Divisible by 8?

  • A number is divisible by 8 if the number formed by its last three digits is divisible by 8.
  • Example: For 1216, last three digits form 216. 216 is divisible by 8 (216 ÷ 8 = 27), so 1216 is too.
  • For 5123, last three digits form 123. 123 is not divisible by 8, so 5123 is not.

🎙️ Similar to the rule for 4, but for 8, we look at the last *three* digits. If the number formed by the hundreds, tens, and units digit is divisible by 8, the whole number is.

Is it Divisible by 11?

  • Find the difference between the sum of digits at odd places and the sum of digits at even places.
  • If this difference is 0 or divisible by 11, the number is divisible by 11.
  • Example: For 1331: (1+3) - (3+1) = 4 - 4 = 0. So 1331 is divisible by 11.

🎙️ This rule is a bit unique. You alternate adding and subtracting digits. Take the sum of digits at odd places (from the right) and subtract the sum of digits at even places. If the result is 0 or a multiple of 11, it works!

Watch Out for These!

  • Confusing rules: Sum of digits for 3/9, last digits for 2/4/5/8/10.
  • For 6, forgetting to check BOTH divisibility by 2 AND 3.
  • In letter puzzles, allowing A=0 for a number like AB.

🎙️ It's easy to get confused with so many rules! Remember to distinguish between rules that use the sum of digits and those that look at the last few digits. Also, always double-check both conditions for divisibility by 6.
