Let's Recall: What is a Point?

  • A point indicates an exact location.
  • It has no size, only position.
  • Represented by a dot and a capital letter.

🎙️ Remember, a point is like a pinpoint on a map – it tells you exactly where something is, without having any thickness or width itself. We usually name it with a capital letter, like point A or point B.

Exact Location

Connecting Points: The Line Segment

  • A part of a line with two distinct endpoints.
  • It has a definite length.
  • Example: The edge of your ruler.

🎙️ When you connect two points directly, you get a line segment. It's like measuring a specific distance between two places, say from your home to the school. It has a clear start and end.

Fixed Length

One Direction: The Ray

  • Starts at one point and extends infinitely in one direction.
  • It has one endpoint and no definite length.
  • Example: A beam of light from a torch.

🎙️ Imagine a ray of sunlight – it starts from the sun and travels outwards endlessly. A ray in geometry is similar; it has a starting point but continues forever in one direction.

💡
Light Beam

Endless Path: The Line

  • Extends infinitely in both directions.
  • It has no endpoints and no definite length.
  • Example: The horizon where the sky meets the sea.

🎙️ A line is like an infinitely long, perfectly straight road that never ends in either direction. It doesn't have a beginning or an end, just a continuous path.

Goes On Forever

Where Paths Cross: Intersecting Lines

  • Two lines that meet at a common point.
  • The common point is called the point of intersection.
  • They share exactly one point.

🎙️ Just like two roads can cross each other, two lines can meet at a single point. This meeting point is very important in many geometric problems.

✖️
Cross Roads

Never Meeting: Parallel Lines

  • Two lines that never intersect, no matter how far they extend.
  • They always maintain the same distance apart.
  • Example: Railway tracks.

🎙️ Parallel lines are like the two rails of a railway track – they run side-by-side, always the same distance apart, and will never meet. They are very common in architecture and design.

⏸️
Always Apart

The Turn: What is an Angle?

  • Formed when two rays share a common endpoint.
  • The common endpoint is called the vertex.
  • The two rays are called the arms of the angle.

🎙️ An angle measures the 'turn' or 'opening' between two rays that start from the same point. Think of opening a door – the door and its frame form an angle.

📐
Measuring Turn

A Perfect Corner: Right Angle

  • Measures exactly 90 degrees.
  • Looks like an 'L' shape.
  • Example: The corner of a square table.

🎙️ A right angle is very common; it's the perfect corner you see in many buildings, books, and rooms. It's a fundamental angle that measures exactly 90 degrees.

∟
90 Degrees

Small and Sharp: Acute Angle

  • Measures less than 90 degrees.
  • Looks 'sharp' or 'pointed'.
  • Example: The tip of a pizza slice.

🎙️ Acute angles are the 'sharp' angles, smaller than a right angle. Think of the tip of a sharpened pencil or the hands of a clock at 1 o'clock.

↗️
< 90 Degrees
14 / 25

Wide Open: Obtuse Angle

  • Measures greater than 90 degrees but less than 180 degrees.
  • Looks 'wide open'.
  • Example: The angle formed by an open laptop screen.

🎙️ An obtuse angle is wider than a right angle, but not completely flat. Imagine opening a book really wide, but not flattening it completely – that's an obtuse angle.

↖️
> 90 Degrees

Flat Line: Straight Angle

  • Measures exactly 180 degrees.
  • Forms a straight line.
  • Example: A perfectly flat road.

🎙️ When two rays point in exactly opposite directions from a common vertex, they form a straight angle. It's essentially a straight line, measuring 180 degrees.

➖
180 Degrees

Two to Make 90: Complementary Angles

  • Two angles whose sum is 90 degrees.
  • They 'complement' each other to form a right angle.
  • They don't have to be adjacent.

🎙️ When two angles add up to exactly 90 degrees, we call them complementary angles. Think of it as two pieces that perfectly fit together to form a right-angle corner.

Finding the Complement

  • If angle A = 30°, what is its complement?
  • Let the complement be X.
  • 30° + X = 90°
  • X = 60°

Two to Make 180: Supplementary Angles

  • Two angles whose sum is 180 degrees.
  • They 'supplement' each other to form a straight angle.
  • They don't have to be adjacent.

🎙️ Similarly, if two angles add up to 180 degrees, they are called supplementary angles. They complete each other to form a straight line.

Finding the Supplement

  • If angle P = 110°, what is its supplement?
  • Let the supplement be Y.
  • 110° + Y = 180°
  • Y = 70°

Side-by-Side: Adjacent Angles

  • Angles that share a common vertex and a common arm.
  • They do not overlap.
  • They are 'next to' each other.

🎙️ Adjacent angles are like two friends sitting next to each other, sharing an armrest. They have a common starting point and a common side, but they don't sit on top of each other.

🤝
Sharing a Side

Straight Line Pair: Linear Pair

  • A pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line.
  • Their non-common arms are opposite rays.
  • The sum of angles in a linear pair is 180 degrees.

🎙️ A linear pair is a special case of adjacent angles. When two adjacent angles combine to form a straight line, their sum is always 180 degrees. This is a very important property.
