What are Factors?

  • Numbers that divide another number exactly.
  • They leave no remainder.
  • Think of them as 'building blocks' of a number.

🎙️ Let's start with factors. A factor of a number is simply a number that divides it completely, leaving no remainder. You can think of factors as the 'building blocks' from which a larger number can be formed by multiplication.

Building blocks of a number

Example: Factors of 12

🎙️ To find the factors of 12, we look for pairs of numbers that multiply to give 12. We can start from 1 and go up. So, 1 and 12, 2 and 6, 3 and 4 are all pairs. Therefore, the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.

What are Common Factors?

  • Factors that are shared by two or more numbers.
  • Like common interests between friends.
  • They appear in the factor list of all numbers.

🎙️ Just like friends might have common interests, numbers can have common factors. These are the factors that appear in the list of factors for all the numbers we are considering. They are the 'shared' building blocks.

Example: Common Factors of 12 and 18

🎙️ Let's find the common factors of 12 and 18. We list all factors for each number. Then, we identify the numbers that appear in both lists. Here, 1, 2, 3, and 6 are present in both, making them the common factors.

Highest Common Factor (HCF)

  • The largest of all common factors.
  • Also known as Greatest Common Divisor (GCD).
  • It's the biggest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.

🎙️ Among all the common factors we just found, the largest one is called the Highest Common Factor, or HCF. It's like finding the 'king' of the common factors. The HCF is the biggest number that can divide both (or more) numbers without leaving any remainder.

👑
The largest shared factor
9 / 30

Method 1: HCF by Listing Factors

🎙️ The simplest way to find HCF is by listing all factors. First, list factors for each number. Second, circle the ones common to all lists. Finally, pick the largest circled number. For 12 and 18, the common factors are 1, 2, 3, 6, so the HCF is 6.

1Step 1: List all factors of each number.
2Step 2: Identify the common factors.
3Step 3: The largest among the common factors is the HCF.
4Example: HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.

What are Prime Numbers?

  • Numbers greater than 1.
  • Have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves.
  • Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11...

🎙️ Before we learn another method for HCF, let's understand prime numbers. A prime number is a special number, greater than 1, that can only be divided by 1 and itself. Think of them as the 'atoms' of numbers, indivisible into smaller whole number factors.

Unique numbers with only two factors

What are Composite Numbers?

  • Numbers greater than 1.
  • Have more than two factors.
  • Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12...

🎙️ On the other hand, composite numbers are also greater than 1, but they have more than two factors. They are 'composed' of smaller prime numbers multiplied together. For example, 4 has factors 1, 2, and 4.

What is Prime Factorization?

  • Expressing a number as a product of its prime factors.
  • Like breaking a number down to its prime building blocks.
  • Uses a factor tree or division method.

🎙️ Prime factorization is a powerful technique where we break down a number into its prime number components. It's like finding the fundamental prime numbers that, when multiplied together, give you the original number. We can use a factor tree or repeated division for this.

🌳
Breaking numbers into prime factors
14 / 30

Method 2: HCF by Prime Factorization

🎙️ This method is more efficient for larger numbers. First, perform prime factorization for all numbers. Then, list all prime factors that are common to every number. For each common prime factor, take the lowest power it appears in any of the factorizations and multiply them together to get the HCF.

1Step 1: Find the prime factorization of each number.
2Step 2: Identify common prime factors.
3Step 3: Multiply the common prime factors (taking the lowest power).
4Example: HCF of 36 (2²x3²) and 48 (2⁴x3) is 2²x3 = 12.

What are Multiples?

  • Numbers obtained by multiplying a number by an integer.
  • They are like 'skipping' counts of a number.
  • Every number has infinite multiples.

🎙️ Now, let's switch gears and talk about multiples. A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply that number by any whole number. Think of it as skip-counting. For example, multiples of 2 are 2, 4, 6, 8, and so on. Unlike factors, multiples go on infinitely.

Numbers growing by a factor

Example: Multiples of 4

🎙️ To find the multiples of 4, we simply multiply 4 by 1, then by 2, then by 3, and so on. This gives us 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, and the list continues indefinitely. Remember, there's no end to the multiples of a number.

Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)

  • The smallest of all common multiples.
  • It's the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
  • Useful for adding fractions with different denominators.

🎙️ Since common multiples go on infinitely, we focus on the smallest one, which is called the Lowest Common Multiple, or LCM. The LCM is the smallest non-zero number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. It's very useful when we need to add or subtract fractions!

🏁
The first shared multiple
22 / 30

Method 1: LCM by Listing Multiples

🎙️ To find the LCM by listing, write down the multiples of each number until you find the first common multiple. For 4 and 6, the multiples are 4, 8, 12, 16... and 6, 12, 18.... The first number they share is 12, so the LCM is 12.

1Step 1: List the first few multiples of each number.
2Step 2: Identify the common multiples.
3Step 3: The smallest among the common multiples is the LCM.
4Example: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

Method 2: LCM by Prime Factorization

🎙️ For LCM using prime factorization, first factorize both numbers into their primes. Then, list all unique prime factors that appear in any of the factorizations. For each prime factor, take the highest power it appears with and multiply them all together. This gives you the LCM.

1Step 1: Find the prime factorization of each number.
2Step 2: List all prime factors from both numbers.
3Step 3: Multiply each factor with its highest power.
4Example: LCM of 12 (2²x3) and 18 (2x3²) is 2²x3² = 36.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing HCF and LCM definitions.
  • Incorrectly identifying prime factors.
  • Missing common factors/multiples in listing method.
  • Forgetting to take highest/lowest powers in prime factorization.

🎙️ Students often confuse HCF and LCM, or make errors in prime factorization. Remember, HCF deals with the highest shared 'divisor', while LCM is the lowest shared 'multiple'. Always double-check your prime factors and ensure you're using the correct power for each method.

Relationship Between HCF and LCM

  • For any two positive integers 'a' and 'b':
  • Product of numbers = HCF (a, b) x LCM (a, b)
  • This formula is very useful for checking answers or finding one if others are known.

🎙️ There's a beautiful relationship between HCF and LCM for any two numbers. If you multiply the two numbers, the result is always equal to the product of their HCF and LCM. This formula is a fantastic tool for verifying your calculations or finding a missing value if the others are given.
