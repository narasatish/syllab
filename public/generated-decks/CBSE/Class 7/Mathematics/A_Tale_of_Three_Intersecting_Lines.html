Linear Pair: Angles on a Straight Line

  • Analogy: A straight path divided into two sections.
  • Definition: Two adjacent angles whose non-common arms form a straight line.
  • Their sum is always 180 degrees.

🎙️ Imagine a perfectly straight road. If you divide it into two parts, the angles formed by those parts will always add up to 180 degrees. That's a linear pair! They are adjacent angles that together make a straight line.

🎙️ When two lines cross, the angles that are directly opposite each other are called vertically opposite angles. They are like mirror images! And here's the cool part: they are always equal in size, a very useful property!

Consecutive Interior Angles: Property with Parallel Lines

  • If a transversal intersects two PARALLEL lines...
  • Then, each pair of consecutive interior angles is supplementary (sum to 180°).
  • This is a sum property, not an equality!

🎙️ Unlike the other angle pairs, consecutive interior angles don't have to be equal. Instead, when the lines are parallel, they are supplementary, meaning they add up to 180 degrees. This is a key distinction to remember!

Proving Lines Parallel: Working Backwards

  • If corresponding angles are equal, then lines are parallel.
  • If alternate interior angles are equal, then lines are parallel.
  • If consecutive interior angles sum to 180°, then lines are parallel.

🎙️ We've learned that if lines are parallel, then certain angle properties hold true. But what if we don't know if the lines are parallel? We can use these same properties in reverse! If any of these conditions are met, then we can conclude the lines MUST be parallel.
