Understanding Integers: What Are They?

  • Whole numbers, including zero, and their negative counterparts.
  • They do NOT include fractions or decimals.
  • Examples: ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

🎙️ Integers are simply all the whole numbers, positive and negative, plus zero. Think of them as a complete set of numbers without any parts or pieces. This concept is fundamental to many areas of mathematics you'll learn later.

Comparing Integers: Who is Bigger?

  • On the number line, the integer further to the right is always greater.
  • Positive integers are always greater than negative integers.
  • For negative integers, the one closer to zero is greater (e.g., -2 > -5).

🎙️ Comparing integers can sometimes be tricky, especially with negative numbers. Always imagine them on the number line. The further right a number is, the larger its value. So, -2 is greater than -5 because it's closer to zero.

Adding Integers: Same Signs

  • If both integers have the same sign, add their absolute values.
  • Keep the common sign in the result.
  • Example: (+3) + (+5) = +8; (-3) + (-5) = -8.

🎙️ Adding integers with the same sign is quite straightforward. It's like combining two groups of positives or two groups of negatives. You just sum up their magnitudes and keep the original sign.

Adding Integers: Different Signs

  • If integers have different signs, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value.
  • The result takes the sign of the integer with the larger absolute value.
  • Example: (+7) + (-4) = +3; (-7) + (+4) = -3.

🎙️ When adding integers with different signs, think of it as a tug-of-war. The stronger side (the number with the larger absolute value) determines the direction and magnitude of the result. We subtract because the positive and negative values cancel each other out partly.

Let's Practice: Integer Addition

  • Problem: 7 + (-12)
  • Step 1: Identify signs (Positive and Negative).
  • Step 2: Subtract absolute values (12 - 7 = 5).
  • Step 3: Take the sign of the larger absolute value (12 is larger and negative). Answer: -5.

🎙️ Let's walk through an example together. When we have 7 + (-12), we see different signs. We subtract the smaller absolute value (7) from the larger (12), which gives us 5. Since 12 is negative and has a larger absolute value, our answer is -5.

Subtracting Integers: The 'Add the Opposite' Rule

  • Change the subtraction sign to an addition sign.
  • Change the sign of the second integer to its opposite.
  • Then, follow the rules for adding integers.

🎙️ Subtracting integers can be confusing, but there's a neat trick! We convert every subtraction problem into an addition problem. You change the minus sign to a plus, and then flip the sign of the number immediately after it. Then you just add as usual!

Multiplying Integers: The Sign Rules

  • If both integers have the SAME sign (both positive or both negative), the product is POSITIVE.
  • If the integers have DIFFERENT signs (one positive, one negative), the product is NEGATIVE.
  • Multiply the absolute values of the numbers.

🎙️ Multiplying integers has very clear rules for signs. The key is to look at whether the signs are the same or different. Once you determine the sign of the answer, you simply multiply the numbers as you normally would.

Dividing Integers: The Sign Rules (Again!)

  • The sign rules for division are IDENTICAL to multiplication.
  • Same signs (both positive or both negative) give a POSITIVE quotient.
  • Different signs (one positive, one negative) give a NEGATIVE quotient.
  • Divide the absolute values of the numbers.

🎙️ Good news! The sign rules you just learned for multiplication apply exactly the same way to division. This makes it easier to remember. Just like multiplication, you first determine the sign of the answer, then perform the division.

Properties of Integers: Closure

  • A set of numbers is 'closed' under an operation if performing that operation on any two numbers in the set always results in a number also in that set.
  • Integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication.
  • Integers are NOT closed under division (e.g., 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, which is not an integer).

🎙️ Closure means that when you do an operation, the answer stays within the same family of numbers. For integers, if you add, subtract, or multiply any two integers, you will always get another integer. But division can sometimes give you fractions or decimals, so integers are not closed under division.

Properties of Integers: Commutativity

  • The order of numbers does not affect the result.
  • Commutative for addition: a + b = b + a (e.g., 3 + (-5) = (-5) + 3)
  • Commutative for multiplication: a × b = b × a (e.g., 2 × (-4) = (-4) × 2)
  • NOT commutative for subtraction or division.

🎙️ Commutativity is about whether you can swap the order of numbers in an operation and still get the same answer. It works for addition and multiplication of integers, but not for subtraction or division. For example, 5 - 3 is not the same as 3 - 5.

Properties of Integers: Associativity

  • The grouping of numbers does not affect the result when performing multiple operations.
  • Associative for addition: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
  • Associative for multiplication: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
  • NOT associative for subtraction or division.

🎙️ Associativity is similar to commutativity but deals with how numbers are grouped when you have three or more. For addition and multiplication, you can group them in any way you like, and the answer will be the same. This simplifies calculations with multiple integers.

Properties of Integers: Distributivity

  • Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction.
  • a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
  • Example: 2 × (3 + (-5)) = (2 × 3) + (2 × (-5))

🎙️ The distributive property is very useful! It tells us that if you're multiplying a number by a sum or difference inside parentheses, you can 'distribute' the multiplication to each term inside. This often makes complex expressions easier to solve.

Sharing the Multiplication

Identities: Additive & Multiplicative

  • Additive Identity: Zero (0). When you add 0 to any integer, the integer remains unchanged (a + 0 = a).
  • Multiplicative Identity: One (1). When you multiply any integer by 1, the integer remains unchanged (a × 1 = a).

🎙️ Identities are special numbers that don't change the value of an integer when operated upon. Zero is the additive identity, and one is the multiplicative identity. These are very important concepts in algebra.
