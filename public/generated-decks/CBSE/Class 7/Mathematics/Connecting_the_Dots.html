  • Used for temperature, depth, money owed

Adding and Subtracting Integers

  • Same signs: add and keep the sign
  • Different signs: subtract and take the sign of the larger number
  • Subtraction is adding the opposite

🎙️ Performing operations with integers follows specific rules. Whether you're adding or subtracting, remember to pay attention to the signs. Subtraction can always be thought of as adding the opposite.

Fractions: Parts of a Whole

  • Represent parts of a whole or a collection
  • Numerator (top) and Denominator (bottom)
  • Examples: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8

🎙️ Fractions are all about dividing things into equal parts. The denominator tells us how many total parts there are, and the numerator tells us how many of those parts we're considering.

Decimals: Another Way to Show Parts

  • Based on powers of 10 (tenths, hundredths, etc.)
  • Uses a decimal point to separate whole and fractional parts
  • Examples: 0.5, 0.75, 1.25

🎙️ Decimals provide another convenient way to express parts of a whole, especially useful with our base-10 number system. Money is a perfect everyday example where decimals are used.

Introducing Rational Numbers

  • Numbers that can be written as p/q
  • Where p and q are integers, and q ≠ 0
  • Includes integers, fractions, and terminating/repeating decimals

🎙️ Now, let's connect all these dots! Rational numbers are the big family that includes all the integers, fractions, and decimals we've discussed. It's a powerful concept because it unifies many number types.

What are Equations?

  • A statement that two expressions are equal
  • Uses an equality sign (=)
  • Often contains an unknown variable (e.g., x)

🎙️ Equations are like a balanced scale. Both sides must always be equal. Our goal is usually to find the value of the unknown variable that makes the equation true.

⚖️
Balanced Scale
14 / 24

Solving Simple Equations: The Method

  • Isolate the variable on one side
  • Perform the inverse operation on both sides
  • Maintain the balance of the equation

🎙️ To solve an equation, we need to get the variable by itself. This involves using inverse operations – for example, if something is added, we subtract it from both sides to keep the equation balanced.

Worked Example: Solve for 'x'

  • Equation: x + 5 = 12
  • Subtract 5 from both sides: x + 5 - 5 = 12 - 5
  • Solution: x = 7

🎙️ Let's try one together. If x + 5 equals 12, what is x? We subtract 5 from both sides to find that x must be 7. Always check your answer by plugging it back into the original equation.
