What is Work?

  • In science, 'work' has a specific meaning.
  • Work is done when a force causes displacement.
  • Both force and displacement are crucial.

🎙️ When we talk about 'work' in everyday life, it might mean studying hard or doing chores. But in physics, work means something very precise. It's about applying a force and seeing something move because of it.

Conditions for Work Done

  • A force must act on an object.
  • The object must move (be displaced).
  • Force and displacement must be in the same direction or have a component in that direction.

🎙️ For work to be done, two main conditions must be met. First, there needs to be a force pushing or pulling. Second, that force must actually cause the object to move. If you push a wall, you apply force, but the wall doesn't move, so no work is done.

When is Work Zero?

  • No displacement (object doesn't move).
  • Force and displacement are perpendicular (at 90 degrees).
  • Example: Carrying a bag horizontally.

🎙️ There are situations where we apply force, but no work is done in the scientific sense. If an object doesn't move, or if the force is applied at a right angle to the direction of motion, then the work done is zero. Imagine carrying a heavy school bag and walking on a flat road – the force of gravity is downwards, your motion is horizontal, so gravity does no work.

Positive & Negative Work

  • Positive work: Force and displacement are in the same direction.
  • Negative work: Force and displacement are in opposite directions.
  • Example: Braking a car.

🎙️ Work can also be positive or negative. When you push a trolley forward, and it moves forward, that's positive work. But when you apply brakes to a moving car, the braking force acts opposite to the car's motion, doing negative work, which slows it down.

Units of Work

  • The SI unit of work is Joule (J).
  • 1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 meter.
  • Named after James Prescott Joule.

🎙️ Just like we measure length in meters or mass in kilograms, work has its own unit. The standard international unit for work is the Joule, named after a famous scientist. One Joule is the amount of work done when a force of one Newton moves an object by one meter.

What is Energy?

  • Energy is the capacity to do work.
  • Without energy, no work can be done.
  • It exists in many forms.

🎙️ If work is about doing something, energy is the ability to do it. You need energy to push a box, to run, or even to think! Think of energy as the fuel that makes things happen.

💡
Capacity to Work

Different Forms of Energy

  • Mechanical Energy (Kinetic + Potential)
  • Heat Energy, Light Energy, Sound Energy
  • Chemical Energy, Electrical Energy, Nuclear Energy

🎙️ Energy isn't just one thing; it comes in various disguises. We see it as the movement of objects, the warmth of a fire, the glow of a bulb, or the sound of music. All these are different forms of energy.

Kinetic Energy

  • Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
  • Formula: KE = 1/2 mv² (m=mass, v=velocity).
  • Example: A moving car, a flying bird.

🎙️ Any object that is moving has kinetic energy. The faster it moves and the heavier it is, the more kinetic energy it possesses. A speeding car has a lot of kinetic energy, which is why it can do a lot of work if it hits something.

🏃
Energy of Motion
11 / 25

Potential Energy

  • Energy stored in an object due to its position or state.
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: PE = mgh (m=mass, g=gravity, h=height).
  • Example: Water stored in a dam, a stretched rubber band.

🎙️ Potential energy is like hidden energy, waiting to be released. A ball held high above the ground has gravitational potential energy because of its position. When you let it go, this stored energy turns into kinetic energy as it falls.

Law of Conservation of Energy

  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • It can only be transformed from one form to another.
  • Total energy in an isolated system remains constant.

🎙️ This is one of the most fundamental laws in physics. It means we can't just 'make' energy out of nothing, nor can it disappear. It simply changes its form, like electrical energy becoming light and heat in a bulb.

🔄
Energy Transforms

Energy Transformations

  • Electrical to Light and Heat (bulb).
  • Chemical to Heat and Light (burning wood).
  • Potential to Kinetic (falling object).

🎙️ Energy is constantly changing forms around us. When you switch on a fan, electrical energy transforms into kinetic energy of the blades. When you eat food, the chemical energy stored in it powers your body, turning into kinetic energy for movement and heat energy to keep you warm.

What is Power?

  • Power is the rate at which work is done.
  • Power = Work / Time.
  • It tells us how fast energy is used or transferred.

🎙️ While work tells us *how much* is done, power tells us *how quickly* it's done. A strong person might lift a heavy box (doing work), but a powerful person can lift the same box much faster, meaning they have more power.

⏱️
Work per Unit Time

Units of Power

  • The SI unit of power is Watt (W).
  • 1 Watt = 1 Joule / 1 second.
  • Named after James Watt, inventor of the improved steam engine.

🎙️ The standard unit for power is the Watt, named after the famous engineer James Watt. One Watt means one Joule of work is done every second. You often see power ratings on electrical appliances like bulbs or motors.

What are Simple Machines?

  • They make work easier for us.
  • No internal moving parts, unlike complex machines.

🎙️ Now let's shift our focus to simple machines. These are fundamental tools that have been used by humans for thousands of years to make tasks easier. They don't have complex engines or electronics, just basic mechanical principles.

Why Do We Use Simple Machines?

  • To multiply force (mechanical advantage).
  • To change the direction of force.
  • To increase speed or distance moved.

🎙️ The main reason we use simple machines is to gain an advantage. They can help us lift heavy objects with less effort, or push things in a more convenient direction. They don't reduce the total work done, but they make it easier for us to do it.

Simple Machines: The Lever

  • A rigid bar that pivots around a fixed point called a fulcrum.
  • Three classes based on fulcrum, effort, and load positions.
  • Example: See-saw, crowbar, tweezers.

🎙️ The lever is one of the most common simple machines. Think of a see-saw: the pivot in the middle is the fulcrum. By applying force (effort) at one end, you can lift a load at the other end. Crowbars and even your arm are examples of levers.

Simple Machines: The Pulley

  • A wheel with a groove, often used with a rope or cable.
  • Can change direction of force or multiply force.
  • Example: Well bucket system, flagpoles, construction cranes.

🎙️ Pulleys are incredibly useful for lifting things. A single fixed pulley simply changes the direction of your pull, making it easier to pull down to lift something up. Systems with multiple pulleys can significantly reduce the effort needed to lift very heavy loads.

🏗️

Simple Machines: Inclined Plane

  • A flat surface set at an angle to the horizontal.
  • Makes it easier to move objects to a higher elevation.
  • Example: Ramp, slide, mountain road.

🎙️ An inclined plane, or a ramp, is a simple way to move objects up or down without lifting them directly. Instead of lifting a heavy box straight up, you can push it up a ramp, spreading the work over a longer distance but requiring less force at any given moment.

Other Simple Machines

  • Wheel and Axle: A wheel attached to a rod (axle) for rotation.
  • Wedge: Two inclined planes joined back-to-back (axe, knife).
  • Screw: An inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder (bolt, jar lid).

🎙️ Besides levers, pulleys, and inclined planes, there are other simple machines that are essentially variations of these. The wheel and axle helps us move things easily, a wedge helps us split things, and a screw is very effective for fastening objects together.
