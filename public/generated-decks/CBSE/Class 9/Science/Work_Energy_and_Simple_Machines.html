Work in Science

  • Work is done when a force causes displacement.
  • An object must move for work to be done.
  • Force and displacement must be in the same direction.

🎙️ Scientifically, work is done only when two things happen: a force acts on an object, AND the object moves a certain distance in the direction of that force. If there's no movement, or movement is perpendicular to the force, no work is done.

Calculating Work Done

  • Work (W) = Force (F) × Displacement (s)
  • W = F × s
  • This formula applies when force and displacement are in the same direction.

🎙️ To calculate the amount of work done, we simply multiply the force applied by the distance the object moved in the direction of the force. Remember this simple formula, W equals F times s.

✖️

Units of Work: Joule (J)

  • The SI unit of work is Joule (J).
  • 1 Joule = 1 Newton × 1 Metre (1 J = 1 N·m).
  • Named after James Prescott Joule.

🎙️ Just like we measure length in metres and mass in kilograms, work is measured in Joules. One Joule is the amount of work done when a force of one Newton moves an object by one metre. It's a small unit, so we often see kilojoules or megajoules.

Worked Example: Calculating Work

  • A force of 10 N moves a box by 2 m.

  • Displacement (s) = 2 m
  • Work (W) = F × s = 10 N × 2 m = 20 J

Kinetic Energy (KE)

  • Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
  • Faster an object moves, more KE it has.
  • Formula: KE = ½ mv² (m=mass, v=velocity)

🎙️ Kinetic energy is the energy of movement. Anything that is moving possesses kinetic energy. A rolling ball, a flying bird, or a running person all have kinetic energy. Its amount depends on both mass and speed.

🏃
Energy of motion

Potential Energy (PE)

  • Energy stored in an object due to its position or state.
  • Gravitational Potential Energy: PE = mgh (m=mass, g=gravity, h=height)
  • Elastic Potential Energy: Stored in springs/stretched bands.

🎙️ Potential energy is stored energy. It's the energy an object has because of its position, like a book on a high shelf, or its state, like a stretched rubber band. It has the 'potential' to do work.

Stored energy

Example: Water in a Dam

Law of Conservation of Energy

  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • It can only be transformed from one form to another.
  • Total energy in an isolated system remains constant.

🎙️ This is a fundamental law of physics. It states that the total amount of energy in the universe is constant. We can't create new energy or destroy existing energy; we can only change its form, like from chemical to kinetic in a car engine.

Worked Example: Energy Conversion

  • A ball is dropped from a height.
  • At the top: Max Potential Energy, Min Kinetic Energy.
  • Mid-way: PE converts to KE.
  • At the bottom (just before impact): Min PE, Max KE.

🎙️ Consider a ball dropped from a height. At the very top, it has maximum potential energy and zero kinetic energy. As it falls, its height decreases, so PE converts into KE, until just before it hits the ground, where it has maximum kinetic energy.
