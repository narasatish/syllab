What is an Atom?

  • Smallest particle of an element.
  • Retains chemical identity.
  • Building blocks of all matter.

🎙️ An atom is like the smallest Lego brick that makes up everything you see – from your desk to the air you breathe. It's so tiny, you can't see it even with the most powerful ordinary microscope.

Early Ideas: Democritus & Dalton

  • Democritus (400 BC): 'Atomos' - indivisible particles.
  • John Dalton (1808): First scientific atomic theory.

🎙️ For centuries, people wondered about the fundamental nature of matter. The ancient Greek philosopher Democritus first proposed the idea of indivisible particles. Much later, John Dalton gave us the first scientific framework for understanding atoms.

Dalton's Atomic Theory: Key Postulates

  • Matter made of indivisible atoms.
  • Atoms of same element are identical.
  • Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios.

🎙️ Dalton's theory was revolutionary. He proposed that atoms are unbreakable, all atoms of a specific element are exactly alike, and they join together in simple ways to form compounds.

Limitations of Dalton's Theory

  • Atoms are divisible (protons, neutrons, electrons).
  • Isotopes: atoms of same element can have different masses.

🎙️ While groundbreaking, Dalton's theory had some limitations. We now know that atoms are not indivisible; they have smaller subatomic particles. Also, not all atoms of the same element are identical in mass, due to the existence of isotopes.

Discovery of Electrons: Cathode Ray Experiment

  • J.J. Thomson (1897) used a cathode ray tube.
  • Observed negatively charged particles (electrons).

🎙️ The first subatomic particle to be discovered was the electron. J.J. Thomson conducted experiments with a cathode ray tube, observing streams of negatively charged particles, which he called electrons.

Properties of Electrons

  • Negative charge.
  • Very small mass.
  • Present in all atoms.

🎙️ Electrons carry a single unit of negative charge and have an incredibly small mass compared to the atom itself. They are fundamental components found in every type of atom.

J.J. Thomson's Model: Plum Pudding

  • Atom is a sphere of positive charge.
  • Electrons are embedded like plums in pudding.

🎙️ After discovering the electron, Thomson proposed his famous 'Plum Pudding' model. He imagined the atom as a sphere of uniformly distributed positive charge, with tiny negatively charged electrons scattered within it, much like plums in a pudding.

Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment: Setup

  • Alpha particles shot at a thin gold foil.
  • Detector screen observed particle deflection.

🎙️ Ernest Rutherford designed an experiment to probe the atom's structure. He fired positively charged alpha particles at an extremely thin sheet of gold foil, surrounding it with a screen to detect where the particles went.

Alpha Particle Beam

Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment: Observations

  • Most alpha particles passed straight through.
  • Some deflected at small angles.
  • Very few bounced back.

🎙️ Rutherford observed three key things: most alpha particles went straight through the foil, some were deflected slightly, and surprisingly, a very small number bounced back almost entirely.

Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment: Conclusions

  • Atom is mostly empty space.
  • Positive charge concentrated in a tiny nucleus.
  • Electrons orbit the nucleus.

🎙️ These observations led to revolutionary conclusions: atoms are mostly empty space, the positive charge and most of the mass are concentrated in a tiny central region called the nucleus, and electrons revolve around this nucleus.

Rutherford's Nuclear Model

  • Central, positively charged nucleus.
  • Electrons orbit the nucleus in circular paths.
  • Nucleus much smaller than the atom.

🎙️ Rutherford's model proposed a 'nuclear atom' where a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus is at the center, and electrons orbit around it, similar to planets orbiting the sun.

Limitations of Rutherford's Model

  • Electrons orbiting would lose energy.
  • Atom would collapse, which doesn't happen.
  • Could not explain atomic stability.

🎙️ Despite its brilliance, Rutherford's model had a major flaw. According to classical physics, an electron orbiting a nucleus should continuously lose energy and eventually spiral into the nucleus, causing the atom to collapse. This contradicts the observed stability of atoms.

🚧
Stability Problem
17 / 25

Bohr's Model of Atom: Postulates

  • Electrons orbit in fixed energy levels (shells).
  • No energy loss in these orbits.
  • Energy absorbed/emitted when changing shells.

🎙️ Niels Bohr addressed Rutherford's problem by proposing that electrons orbit only in specific, discrete energy levels or shells. They don't lose energy while in these orbits, and can only jump between shells by absorbing or emitting specific amounts of energy.

Bohr's model of the atom proposed that electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy levels

Energy Levels / Shells

  • Designated as K, L, M, N or 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • Innermost shell (K) has lowest energy.
  • Electrons fill shells from inside out.

🎙️ These energy levels are like steps on a ladder, labeled K, L, M, N, or 1, 2, 3, 4. The K-shell, closest to the nucleus, has the lowest energy. Electrons prefer to occupy the lowest possible energy levels first.

Recap: Key Models and Discoveries

  • Dalton: Indivisible atoms.
  • Thomson: Plum Pudding (electron discovery).
  • Rutherford: Nuclear Model (nucleus discovery).
  • Bohr: Energy Levels (atomic stability).

🎙️ In summary, we've traced the journey of atomic understanding from Dalton's simple spheres, through Thomson's plum pudding with electrons, Rutherford's planetary model with a nucleus, to Bohr's stable energy levels. Each model built upon the last, refining our view of the atom.
