Defining Motion and Rest

  • Motion: Position changes over time.
  • Rest: Position does not change over time.
  • Both are relative terms.

🎙️ An object is in **motion** if its position changes with respect to its surroundings over time. An object is at **rest** if its position does not change with respect to its surroundings over time. Remember, these are always relative to an observer.

Distance vs. Displacement: Key Differences

  • Distance: Scalar, always positive, path-dependent.
  • Displacement: Vector, can be zero/negative, path-independent.

🎙️ Distance is always positive and can be greater than or equal to displacement. Displacement can be positive, negative, or zero, and its magnitude is never greater than distance. This distinction is very important.

What is Uniform Motion?

  • Covers equal distances.
  • In equal intervals of time.
  • Constant speed.

🎙️ An object is said to be in **uniform motion** if it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, no matter how small these time intervals may be. This implies a constant speed and no change in direction.

Defining Speed

  • Rate of distance covered.
  • Speed = Distance / Time.
  • SI unit: meters per second (m/s).
  • Scalar quantity.

🎙️ **Speed** is the rate at which an object covers distance. It is calculated as distance divided by time. Its SI unit is meters per second (m/s) and it is a scalar quantity, so direction is not included.

Defining Velocity

  • Rate of change of displacement.
  • Speed in a specified direction.
  • SI unit: meters per second (m/s).
  • Vector quantity.

🎙️ **Velocity** is the rate of change of an object's displacement. It is speed in a specified direction. Its SI unit is also meters per second (m/s), but it's a vector quantity, meaning direction is integral.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing Distance and Displacement.
  • Forgetting Displacement can be zero or negative.
  • Not including direction for Velocity.
  • Incorrectly using units in calculations.

🎙️ Students often confuse distance and displacement, forgetting that displacement includes direction and can be zero. Also, remember that speed is scalar, but velocity is a vector! Always pay attention to units in your answers.
