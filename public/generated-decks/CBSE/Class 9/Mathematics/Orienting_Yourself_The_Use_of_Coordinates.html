Locating on a Line: One Dimension

  • On a number line, we can locate any point using a single number.
  • Positive numbers are to the right of zero, negative to the left.
  • Example: Point 'A' is at 3, Point 'B' is at -2.

🎙️ A number line helps us understand one-dimensional positioning. Here, every point has a unique numerical address, either positive or negative, relative to the origin (zero).

Introducing Two Dimensions: The Cartesian System

  • To locate points on a flat surface (a plane), we need two reference lines.
  • These lines are perpendicular to each other.
  • They form the foundation of the Cartesian Coordinate System.

🎙️ René Descartes, a French mathematician, developed this system. By using two intersecting lines, we can precisely describe the position of any point on a flat surface.

➕
Perpendicular Lines

The Origin: Our Starting Point (0,0)

  • The point where the two perpendicular lines intersect is called the Origin.
  • It is denoted by the coordinates (0, 0).
  • All measurements begin from this central point.

🎙️ Think of the origin as the 'home base' or the 'starting line' for all your coordinate journeys. It's the unique point from which all other positions are referenced.

🎙️ The X-axis helps us measure how far a point is horizontally from the origin. It's like the street number in our cinema example, determining your left-right position.

Coordinates: The Address of a Point

  • Every point on the Cartesian plane has a unique address.
  • This address is an ordered pair of numbers (x, y).
  • The first number is the x-coordinate, the second is the y-coordinate.

🎙️ Just like a house has a unique address to distinguish it, each point on the coordinate plane has its own unique (x, y) pair. The order of numbers matters!

🏡
Point's Address
13 / 30

The X-coordinate: Abscissa

  • The x-coordinate is also called the Abscissa.
  • It represents the perpendicular distance of a point from the Y-axis.
  • It tells us how far left or right a point is from the origin.

🎙️ Remember, the abscissa is all about the horizontal movement. If the abscissa is positive, move right; if negative, move left from the origin.

↔️
Horizontal Distance
14 / 30

The Y-coordinate: Ordinate

  • The y-coordinate is also called the Ordinate.
  • It represents the perpendicular distance of a point from the X-axis.
  • It tells us how far up or down a point is from the origin.

🎙️ The ordinate focuses on the vertical movement. A positive ordinate means moving up, and a negative ordinate means moving down from the origin.

↕️
Vertical Distance

Example: Identifying Abscissa and Ordinate

  • Consider the point P (5, -3).
  • The first value, 5, is the x-coordinate (Abscissa).
  • The second value, -3, is the y-coordinate (Ordinate).

🎙️ It's crucial to always remember that the x-coordinate comes first, followed by the y-coordinate. This order is fixed and cannot be swapped.

Plotting Points: Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Start at the Origin (0, 0).
  • Step 2: Move horizontally along the X-axis based on the x-coordinate.
  • Step 3: From that position, move vertically along the Y-axis based on the y-coordinate.
  • Step 4: Mark the final point.

🎙️ Always begin your journey from the origin. First horizontal, then vertical – think of it as 'walking along the street, then climbing the stairs'.

1Start at Origin (0,0)
2Move X-direction
3Move Y-direction
4Mark the point

Example: Plotting the Point (2, 3)

  • Start at (0, 0).
  • Move 2 units to the right (positive x-direction).
  • From there, move 3 units up (positive y-direction).
  • Mark the point where you land.

🎙️ Visualise this movement on a graph paper. Each unit represents a step. This systematic approach ensures accuracy when plotting any point.

Quadrants: Dividing the Plane

  • The X and Y axes divide the coordinate plane into four regions.
  • These regions are called Quadrants.
  • They are numbered I, II, III, and IV in an anti-clockwise direction.

🎙️ Understanding quadrants helps us quickly identify the general location of a point based on the signs of its coordinates. Each quadrant has a unique combination of signs.

➗
Four Divisions
20 / 30

Quadrant I: (+, +)

  • Both the x-coordinate and y-coordinate are positive.
  • Example: (4, 5) or (1, 2).
  • This is the top-right region of the plane.

🎙️ Points in Quadrant I are always found by moving right and then up from the origin. Think of it as the 'all-positive' zone.

➕➕➕➕
Positive, Positive
21 / 30

Quadrant II: (-, +)

  • The x-coordinate is negative, and the y-coordinate is positive.
  • Example: (-3, 6) or (-2, 1).
  • This is the top-left region of the plane.

🎙️ To reach Quadrant II, you move left from the origin and then up. It's where the x-values are negative, but y-values remain positive.

➖➖➖➖
Negative, Positive
22 / 30

Quadrant III: (-, -)

  • Both the x-coordinate and y-coordinate are negative.
  • Example: (-5, -2) or (-1, -4).
  • This is the bottom-left region of the plane.

🎙️ Quadrant III is where both coordinates are negative. This means you move left from the origin and then down to locate points in this region.

✖️✖️✖️✖️
Negative, Negative
23 / 30

Quadrant IV: (+, -)

  • The x-coordinate is positive, and the y-coordinate is negative.
  • Example: (7, -1) or (3, -4).
  • This is the bottom-right region of the plane.

🎙️ For Quadrant IV, you move right from the origin and then down. It's the region where x-values are positive, but y-values are negative.

Points on the Axes: Special Cases

  • If a point lies on the X-axis, its y-coordinate is always 0 (e.g., (x, 0)).
  • If a point lies on the Y-axis, its x-coordinate is always 0 (e.g., (0, y)).
  • The origin (0, 0) lies on both axes.

🎙️ These are important special cases. Points on the axes are not considered to be in any quadrant, as they lie on the boundaries between them.

Example: Plotting Points on Axes

  • To plot (4, 0): Move 4 units right from origin, no vertical movement.
  • To plot (0, -3): No horizontal movement, move 3 units down from origin.
  • These points lie directly on the X and Y axes, respectively.

🎙️ Practicing plotting these special points will solidify your understanding. Remember, a zero coordinate means no movement along that particular axis.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing the order of coordinates (always (x, y)).
  • Mixing up the X-axis and Y-axis.
  • Not starting from the origin when plotting points.
  • Incorrectly identifying quadrant signs.

🎙️ These are common pitfalls for students. Pay close attention to the order and direction of movement. Practice is key to overcoming these errors.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • The Cartesian system uses perpendicular X and Y axes meeting at the Origin (0,0).
  • Points are located using ordered pairs (x, y), where x is the abscissa and y is the ordinate.
  • The plane is divided into four Quadrants (I, II, III, IV) based on the signs of coordinates.
  • Points on axes have one coordinate as zero.
