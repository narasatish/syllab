  • Like a starting line or an origin

  • Developed by René Descartes
  • Uses perpendicular lines to locate points
  • Forms the basis of modern geometry

  • Represented by the coordinates (0, 0)

Coordinates of a Point (x, y)

  • An ordered pair (x, y)
  • 'x' tells horizontal distance from Y-axis
  • 'y' tells vertical distance from X-axis

🎙️ Every point on this plane can be uniquely identified by a pair of numbers, written as (x, y). The order matters – (2, 3) is different from (3, 2).

Abscissa and Ordinate

  • The x-coordinate is called the abscissa
  • The y-coordinate is called the ordinate
  • Together they form the coordinates

🎙️ These are fancy terms for the x and y values. So, if a point is (5, 7), then 5 is its abscissa and 7 is its ordinate. Don't let the names confuse you, they are simply the x and y values.

Plotting a Point: Steps

  • 1. Start from the Origin (0,0)
  • 2. Move horizontally (x-value)
  • 3. Move vertically (y-value)
  • 4. Mark the final spot

🎙️ Plotting a point is like following directions on a treasure map. You always start at the 'X marks the spot' (the origin) and then follow the horizontal and vertical instructions.

1Start at (0,0)
2Move X-units
3Move Y-units
4Mark the Point

Quadrants: Dividing the Plane

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions
  • These regions are called quadrants
  • Numbered I, II, III, IV counter-clockwise

🎙️ The two axes cut the entire coordinate plane into four distinct sections, much like dividing a cake into four slices. We call these sections quadrants.

Signs of Coordinates in Quadrants

  • Quadrant I: (+, +)
  • Quadrant II: (-, +)
  • Quadrant III: (-, -)
  • Quadrant IV: (+, -)

🎙️ The signs of the x and y coordinates tell us which quadrant a point lies in. This is a very important concept for quickly identifying a point's general location.

Points on the Axes

  • Points on the X-axis have y-coordinate 0 (x, 0)
  • Points on the Y-axis have x-coordinate 0 (0, y)
  • The origin is (0,0)

🎙️ What happens if a point lies exactly on one of the axes? If it's on the X-axis, its y-coordinate is zero. If it's on the Y-axis, its x-coordinate is zero.
