  • Or describing a friend's house.

What Are Coordinates?

  • A set of values that show an exact position.
  • Like an address for a point.
  • Usually written as a pair of numbers (x, y).

🎙️ Simply put, coordinates are like a unique address for any point in space. Just as your house has a specific number and street name, a point has its own (x, y) coordinates. These numbers tell us exactly where to find it.

Introducing Two Number Lines

  • One horizontal, one vertical.
  • They cross each other at their zero points.

The Cartesian System

  • Developed by René Descartes.
  • Uses two perpendicular number lines.
  • Helps locate points in a plane.

🎙️ This brilliant system, named after the French mathematician René Descartes, is what we call the Cartesian Coordinate System. It's the standard way to define points in a two-dimensional plane, forming the backbone of geometry and graphs.

The Origin: Our Starting Point

  • The point where the two axes intersect.
  • Its coordinates are always (0, 0).
  • It's the reference point for all other locations.

🎙️ The point where the horizontal and vertical number lines cross is called the Origin. Think of it as 'home' or the starting point on your coordinate map. Every other point's position is measured relative to this (0,0) spot.

Coordinates of a Point (x, y)

  • Every point is described by two numbers.
  • The first number is the x-coordinate.
  • The second number is the y-coordinate.

🎙️ When we talk about the coordinates of a point, we always write them as an ordered pair (x, y). The 'x' tells us how far left or right to go from the origin, and the 'y' tells us how far up or down.

📝
Point Notation

Abscissa and Ordinate

  • The x-coordinate is also called the Abscissa.
  • The y-coordinate is also called the Ordinate.
  • Together, they form the coordinates (Abscissa, Ordinate).

🎙️ These are just fancy names for the x and y coordinates! The abscissa tells you the horizontal distance from the y-axis, and the ordinate tells you the vertical distance from the x-axis. Remember these terms, as they are commonly used.

Plotting a Point: Step 1 (X-coordinate)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move horizontally along the X-axis.
  • Move right for positive x, left for negative x.

🎙️ To plot any point (x, y), always begin at the origin. The first step is to locate your x-position. If x is positive, move to the right; if x is negative, move to the left along the x-axis.

➡️
Horizontal Movement
12 / 25

Plotting a Point: Step 2 (Y-coordinate)

  • From your x-position, move vertically.
  • Move up for positive y, down for negative y.
  • This is your final point!

🎙️ Once you've reached your x-position, the next step is to move vertically. For a positive y-coordinate, move upwards; for a negative y-coordinate, move downwards. The spot where you stop is the exact location of your point.

⬆️
Vertical Movement

Worked Example 1: Plotting (3, 2)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 3 units right along the X-axis.
  • Then move 2 units up parallel to the Y-axis.

🎙️ Let's try plotting the point (3, 2). First, from the origin, move 3 units to the right. From there, move 2 units up. That's where you mark your point! This point lies in the first quadrant.

Worked Example 2: Plotting (-2, 4)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 2 units left along the X-axis.
  • Then move 4 units up parallel to the Y-axis.

🎙️ Now, consider (-2, 4). From the origin, we move 2 units to the left because the x-coordinate is negative. Then, from that position, move 4 units upwards, as the y-coordinate is positive. This point is in the second quadrant.

Worked Example 3: Plotting (0, -3)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • X-coordinate is 0, so no horizontal movement.
  • Move 3 units down along the Y-axis.

🎙️ What if one of the coordinates is zero? For (0, -3), we don't move left or right from the origin. We simply move 3 units down along the Y-axis. This means the point lies directly on the Y-axis.

Quadrants: Dividing the Plane

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • These regions are called Quadrants.
  • They are numbered I, II, III, IV in an anti-clockwise direction.

🎙️ The X and Y axes don't just help us locate points; they also divide the entire coordinate plane into four distinct sections. These sections are called quadrants, and understanding them helps us quickly identify the general location of a point.

🧭
Four Directions
17 / 25

Signs in Different Quadrants

  • Quadrant I: (+, +)
  • Quadrant II: (-, +)
  • Quadrant III: (-, -)
  • Quadrant IV: (+, -)

🎙️ The signs of the coordinates tell us which quadrant a point lies in. For example, a point with both positive x and y values will always be in Quadrant I. Knowing these sign patterns makes plotting and identifying points much faster.

1Quadrant I: (+x, +y)
2Quadrant II: (-x, +y)
3Quadrant III: (-x, -y)
4Quadrant IV: (+x, -y)

Points on the Axes

  • Points on the X-axis have a y-coordinate of 0 (x, 0).
  • Points on the Y-axis have an x-coordinate of 0 (0, y).
  • These points do not belong to any quadrant.

🎙️ It's important to remember that points lying directly on the X or Y axes are not considered to be in any quadrant. If a point is on the X-axis, its y-coordinate is zero, and if it's on the Y-axis, its x-coordinate is zero.
