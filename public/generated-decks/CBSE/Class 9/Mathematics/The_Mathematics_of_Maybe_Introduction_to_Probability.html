  • Denoted by 'S' or 'Ω'.
  • E.g., Tossing a coin: S = {Head, Tail}.

🎙️ The sample space is like a complete list of everything that could happen in an experiment. It's crucial to identify all possible outcomes to calculate probability correctly.

Key Terms: Event

🎙️ An event is what we are actually interested in. It could be a single outcome, like rolling a '6', or a group of outcomes, like rolling an even number.

Calculating Probability: The Formula

  • P(E) = (Number of Favourable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Possible Outcomes)
  • P(E) is the probability of an event E.

🎙️ This is the most important formula! Probability is simply a ratio: how many ways our desired event can happen, divided by all the possible things that could happen. Always remember this.

➗
Probability Ratio

Example: Tossing a Coin

  • Experiment: Tossing a coin.
  • Sample Space (S) = {Head, Tail} (Total outcomes = 2).
  • Event (E): Getting a Head (Favourable outcomes = 1).
  • P(Head) = 1/2.

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. When you toss a coin, there are two equally likely outcomes. If you want a Head, there's only one way for that to happen. So, the probability is 1 out of 2.

Example: Rolling a Die

  • Experiment: Rolling a standard six-sided die.
  • Sample Space (S) = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} (Total outcomes = 6).
  • Event (E): Getting an even number {2, 4, 6} (Favourable outcomes = 3).
  • P(Even Number) = 3/6 = 1/2.

🎙️ Now with a die. There are six possible numbers. If we want an even number, we count how many even numbers are in our sample space. Three of them! So, 3 out of 6 possibilities.

Worked Example: Drawing a Card

  • A standard deck has 52 cards.
  • Event: Drawing a 'King'.
  • Favourable outcomes: 4 Kings (K♠, K♣, K♥, K♦).

Types of Probability

  • Experimental Probability: Based on actual observations/experiments.
  • Theoretical Probability: Based on logical reasoning and possible outcomes.

🎙️ There are two main ways we look at probability: experimental and theoretical. Experimental is about what actually happens when you try something, while theoretical is about what *should* happen based on logic.

Experimental Probability

  • P(E) = (Number of times event E occurred) / (Total number of trials).
  • Based on performing an experiment multiple times.
  • Also called Empirical Probability.

🎙️ Experimental probability is all about 'doing'. You perform an experiment, count how many times your event happens, and divide by the total attempts. The more times you repeat the experiment, the closer your experimental probability usually gets to the theoretical probability.

Example: Coin Toss (Experimental)

Complementary Events

  • If E is an event, then 'not E' (or E') is its complementary event.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • This means P(not E) = 1 - P(E).

🎙️ Complementary events are like opposites. If an event happens, its complement is that it *doesn't* happen. The sum of their probabilities is always 1, because one of them *must* occur.

Example: Not Getting a 6

  • Event (E): Getting a 6 on a die roll.
  • P(6) = 1/6.
  • Complementary Event (E'): Not getting a 6.
  • P(not 6) = 1 - P(6) = 1 - 1/6 = 5/6.

🎙️ Let's use the die roll again. The probability of getting a 6 is 1/6. So, the probability of *not* getting a 6 is 1 minus 1/6, which is 5/6. You can also count the outcomes: {1,2,3,4,5} are 5 outcomes, so 5/6.
