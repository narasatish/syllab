Coefficients: The Number Partner

  • The numerical factor multiplying a variable in a term.
  • Example: In `5x`, 5 is the coefficient of `x`.
  • If no number is written, the coefficient is 1 (e.g., `x` means `1x`).

🎙️ The coefficient is simply the number that stands next to a variable and multiplies it. If you see `5x`, the number 5 is telling you how many 'x's you have. If you just see `x`, it means you have one 'x', so the coefficient is 1.

What is a Polynomial? The Special Club!

  • A special type of algebraic expression.
  • Variables must have non-negative integer powers.
  • No variables in denominators or under square roots.

🎙️ Imagine a special club for algebraic expressions. To join this club and be called a 'polynomial', an expression must follow certain rules. The most important rule is that the powers of its variables must always be whole numbers, which are non-negative integers.

🏆
Special Expressions

Powers (Exponents): The Rule for Polynomials

  • Powers must be whole numbers (0, 1, 2, 3...).
  • Examples of valid powers: `x^2`, `y^3`, `z^0` (which is 1).
  • Not allowed: `x^-1` (1/x), `x^(1/2)` (√x).

🎙️ Let's be clear about the 'non-negative integer powers' rule. This means the numbers above the variables, like the '2' in `x^2`, must be 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. You cannot have negative powers or fractional powers for an expression to be a polynomial.

Polynomial or Not? Let's Compare!

  • Polynomial: `3x^2 + 5x - 2` (powers are 2, 1, 0).
  • Not a Polynomial: `√x + 4` (power is 1/2).
  • Not a Polynomial: `1/y + 3` (power is -1).

🎙️ Here are some examples to help you distinguish. `3x^2 + 5x - 2` is a polynomial because all powers of x are non-negative integers. But `√x + 4` is not, because the power of x is 1/2. Similarly, `1/y + 3` is not a polynomial, as `1/y` means `y` to the power of -1.

Degree of a Polynomial: The Highest Power

  • The highest power of the variable in a polynomial.
  • For a constant term, the degree is 0 (e.g., degree of 5 is 0).
  • It tells us the 'rank' of the polynomial.

🎙️ The degree of a polynomial is like its highest rank or grade. It's simply the largest exponent or power of the variable present in any of its terms. For example, if you have `x^3 + x^2 + x`, the highest power is 3, so the degree is 3.

Finding the Degree: Step-by-Step

  • Step 1: Identify all terms in the polynomial.
  • Step 2: Find the power of the variable in each term.
  • Step 3: The highest power found is the degree.

🎙️ Let's walk through an example. For `4x^3 - 2x^5 + 7`, first, identify the terms: `4x^3`, `-2x^5`, `7`. Next, find the powers: 3, 5, and 0 (for the constant 7). Finally, pick the highest power, which is 5. So, the degree of this polynomial is 5.

Focus: Linear Polynomials - The Straight Path

  • A polynomial with degree 1.
  • General form: `ax + b`, where `a ≠ 0`.

🎙️ Today, our main focus is on 'Linear Polynomials'. These are the simplest type of polynomials, having a degree of exactly 1. Their general form is `ax + b`, where 'a' cannot be zero (otherwise, it wouldn't have an 'x' term and wouldn't be degree 1).

Finding the Zero of a Linear Polynomial

  • Set the polynomial equal to zero: `ax + b = 0`.
  • Solve for `x`.
  • The formula for a linear polynomial is `x = -b/a`.

🎙️ Finding the zero of a linear polynomial is straightforward. You simply set the entire expression `ax + b` equal to zero. Then, you use your basic algebraic skills to solve for `x`. This will give you the unique zero for that linear polynomial.

Worked Example: Find the Zero of `2x + 6`

  • Step 1: Set `2x + 6 = 0`.
  • Step 2: Subtract 6 from both sides: `2x = -6`.
  • Step 3: Divide by 2: `x = -6 / 2`.
  • Step 4: So, `x = -3` is the zero.

🎙️ Let's work through this example together. We have the linear polynomial `2x + 6`. To find its zero, we set it to zero: `2x + 6 = 0`. Then, we move the constant term to the other side, making it `2x = -6`. Finally, divide by 2, and we get `x = -3`. So, -3 is the zero!

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing `x` and `x^0` (degree of constant is 0).
  • Forgetting that `x` has a coefficient of 1.
  • Incorrectly identifying non-polynomials (e.g., `√x`, `1/x`).
  • Algebraic errors when finding zeros.

🎙️ Students often make a few common mistakes. Remember that a constant like '5' has a degree of 0, not 1. Also, `x` means `1x`. Be careful to identify expressions with fractional or negative powers as 'not polynomials'. And always double-check your algebra when solving for zeros.
