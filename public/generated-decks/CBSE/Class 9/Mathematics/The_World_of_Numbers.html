Rational Numbers (Q)

  • Can be written as p/q.
  • p and q are integers, q ≠ 0.
  • Includes fractions and decimals that terminate or repeat.

🎙️ Rational numbers are a big step! These are any numbers that can be expressed as a fraction, p/q, where p and q are integers, and q is not zero. This means all integers, fractions, and terminating or repeating decimals are rational.

Representing Rational Numbers

  • Divide the segment between integers.
  • Locate the fraction.
  • Example: 3/4 between 0 and 1.

🎙️ To represent a rational number like 3/4 on a number line, we first identify the two integers it lies between, which are 0 and 1. Then, we divide the segment between 0 and 1 into 'q' (here, 4) equal parts and locate the 'p-th' (here, 3rd) mark.

Irrational Numbers

🎙️ Now, for a new type of number: irrational numbers. These are numbers that *cannot* be expressed as a simple fraction p/q. Their decimal expansions go on forever without repeating any pattern. They are truly endless and non-repeating!

Locating √2 on Number Line

  • Use Pythagoras Theorem.
  • Construct a right triangle with sides 1 unit.
  • Hypotenuse length is √2.

🎙️ Representing irrational numbers like √2 on a number line requires a geometric construction. We use the Pythagorean theorem: construct a right-angled triangle with both perpendicular sides of length 1 unit. The hypotenuse will be √2 units long, which we then transfer to the number line using a compass.

Terminating Decimals

  • Decimal expansion ends after finite digits.
  • Example: 1/2 = 0.5, 3/4 = 0.75.
  • Are always rational numbers.

🎙️ Let's look at decimal expansions. Terminating decimals are those that end after a finite number of digits. For example, 1/2 is 0.5, and 3/4 is 0.75. These types of decimals always represent rational numbers.

Non-Terminating Recurring Decimals

  • Decimal expansion never ends, but repeats a pattern.
  • Example: 1/3 = 0.333..., 2/7 = 0.285714285714...

🎙️ Then we have non-terminating recurring decimals. These decimals go on infinitely, but a specific block of digits repeats itself. For instance, 1/3 is 0.333... and 2/7 is 0.285714 repeating. Importantly, these are also rational numbers!

Converting Recurring Decimals to p/q

  • Let x = the decimal.
  • Multiply by 10^n to shift repeating part.
  • Subtract equations to eliminate repeating part.

🎙️ We can convert non-terminating recurring decimals back into their p/q form. The method involves setting the decimal equal to 'x', multiplying by powers of 10 to align the repeating parts, and then subtracting the equations to eliminate the recurring part, finally solving for x.

2Multiply by 10^n

Non-Terminating Non-Recurring Decimals

  • Decimal expansion never ends and never repeats.

🎙️ Finally, non-terminating non-recurring decimals. These are the decimals that go on forever *without* any repeating pattern. This is the defining characteristic of irrational numbers. Think of √2 or Pi – their decimal expansions are truly unpredictable.

Operations on Real Numbers: Addition & Subtraction

  • Rational + Rational = Rational.
  • Rational + Irrational = Irrational.
  • Irrational + Irrational: Can be rational or irrational.

🎙️ Just like other numbers, we can perform operations on real numbers. When adding or subtracting, remember that the sum or difference of two rational numbers is always rational. However, combining rational and irrational numbers usually results in an irrational number. Two irrational numbers can surprisingly result in either a rational or irrational number.

Operations on Real Numbers: Multiplication & Division

  • Rational × Rational = Rational.
  • Rational × Irrational = Irrational (if rational ≠ 0).
  • Irrational × Irrational: Can be rational or irrational.

🎙️ Similar rules apply to multiplication and division. The product or quotient of two non-zero rational numbers is always rational. If you multiply a non-zero rational number by an irrational number, the result is always irrational. Again, multiplying or dividing two irrational numbers can lead to either a rational or an irrational result.

Identities Involving Square Roots

  • √(ab) = √a * √b
  • √(a/b) = √a / √b
  • (√a + √b)(√a - √b) = a - b

🎙️ There are some useful identities when dealing with square roots. For example, the square root of a product is the product of the square roots. Also, a very important one for rationalization is (√a + √b)(√a - √b) which simplifies to a - b, eliminating the roots.

Rationalizing the Denominator

  • Process to convert irrational denominator to rational.
  • Multiply numerator and denominator by conjugate.
  • Makes calculations easier.

🎙️ Rationalizing the denominator is a technique used to remove irrational numbers from the denominator of a fraction. We achieve this by multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by a suitable factor, often the conjugate of the denominator, to make the denominator a rational number. This simplifies expressions and makes them easier to work with.

Example: Rationalize 1/√2

  • Multiply (1/√2) by (√2/√2).
  • Result: √2 / 2.
  • Denominator is now rational.

🎙️ Let's take an example: to rationalize 1/√2, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by √2. This gives us (1 * √2) / (√2 * √2), which simplifies to √2 / 2. Now, our denominator is the rational number 2, and the expression is in a simpler form.

Laws of Exponents for Real Numbers

  • a^m * a^n = a^(m+n)
  • (a^m)^n = a^(mn)
  • a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)
  • a^0 = 1, a^(-n) = 1/a^n

🎙️ We also extend the laws of exponents to real numbers. These rules help us simplify expressions involving powers. Remember that when multiplying powers with the same base, you add the exponents, and when dividing, you subtract them. Also, any non-zero number raised to the power of zero is 1.
