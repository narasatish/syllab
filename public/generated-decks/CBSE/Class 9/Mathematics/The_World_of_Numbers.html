Rational Numbers: The Formal Definition

  • Numbers in the form p/q.
  • Where p and q are integers.
  • And q is not equal to zero (q ≠ 0).
  • Represented by 'Q'.

🎙️ Formally, any number that can be written as a fraction, where both the numerator and denominator are integers and the denominator is not zero, is a rational number. Examples include 1/2, -3/4, and even 5 (which is 5/1).

Representing √2 on the Number Line: A Step-by-Step Guide

  • Draw a number line and mark 0 and 1.
  • At 1, draw a perpendicular of length 1 unit.
  • Connect 0 to the top of the perpendicular.
  • Use a compass to mark the length on the number line.

🎙️ Let's see how to locate an irrational number like √2 on the number line. We use the Pythagorean theorem! By constructing a right-angled triangle with sides of 1 unit, the hypotenuse will be √2 units long.

2Perpendicular at 1 (1 unit)
3Connect 0 to top
4Compass arc to line

Converting 0.3̄ to p/q Form: The Magic Trick

  • Let x = 0.333...
  • Multiply by 10 (or 100, etc., depending on repeating digits).
  • Subtract the original equation.
  • Solve for x to get the fraction.

🎙️ This is a neat trick to turn a non-terminating recurring decimal back into its rational (p/q) form. The key is to multiply by a power of 10 to shift the decimal and then subtract to eliminate the repeating part.

1Let x = 0.333...
210x = 3.333...
310x - x = 3.333... - 0.333...
49x = 3 => x = 1/3

Operations on Real Numbers: Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying, Dividing

  • Rational numbers behave like usual fractions/integers.
  • Irrational numbers: √a + √a = 2√a, but √a + √b is usually not simplified.
  • Product/quotient of a non-zero rational and an irrational is irrational.
  • Sum/difference of a rational and an irrational is irrational.

🎙️ Just like with other numbers, we can add, subtract, multiply, and divide real numbers. It's important to remember how rational and irrational numbers interact, especially when combining them.

Example: Rationalize 1/√2

  • Multiply numerator and denominator by √2.
  • (1/√2) * (√2/√2)
  • = √2 / (√2 * √2)
  • = √2 / 2

🎙️ Here's how we rationalize 1/√2. We multiply both the top and bottom by √2. This doesn't change the value of the fraction, but it transforms the denominator into a rational number, 2.

1Start with 1/√2
2Multiply by √2/√2
3Simplify: √2 / (√2 * √2)
4Final: √2 / 2

Laws of Exponents: Powering Up Numbers

  • a^m * a^n = a^(m+n)
  • (a^m)^n = a^(mn)
  • a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)
  • a^m * b^m = (ab)^m

🎙️ Exponents are a shorthand for repeated multiplication. These laws help us simplify expressions involving powers, making complex calculations much simpler. These rules apply to real numbers as well.

📈
Growth & Power

  • Simplify: (3^(1/5))^4
  • Simplify: 7^(1/3) / 7^(1/5)
  • Simplify: 13^(1/5) * 17^(1/5)

🎙️ Let's put the exponent laws into action! These examples show how to combine terms with the same base, raise a power to another power, divide terms, and combine terms with the same exponent.

12^(2/3+1/3) = 2^1 = 2
23^(1/5*4) = 3^(4/5)
37^(1/3-1/5) = 7^(2/15)

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing rational and irrational numbers.
  • Incorrectly applying exponent laws (e.g., adding bases).
  • Errors in rationalizing denominators (e.g., forgetting to multiply numerator).
  • Assuming every non-terminating decimal is irrational.

🎙️ It's natural to make mistakes, but being aware of common pitfalls can help you avoid them. Pay close attention to definitions, apply rules carefully, and remember that non-terminating recurring decimals are still rational!
