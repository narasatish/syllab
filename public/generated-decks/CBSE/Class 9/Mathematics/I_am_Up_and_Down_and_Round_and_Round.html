  • A branch of mathematics.
  • Uses numbers to describe positions.
  • Helps locate points on a plane.

🎙️ Imagine you need to tell someone exactly where something is on a map. Coordinate geometry provides a powerful system using numbers to do just that. It's like giving precise instructions for a treasure hunt!

The Cartesian Plane

  • A 2D surface for plotting points.
  • Formed by two perpendicular lines.
  • Named after René Descartes.

🎙️ The Cartesian Plane is our main tool in coordinate geometry. It's a flat surface, like a piece of graph paper, that allows us to represent points using two numbers. Think of it as a grid system for our mathematical map.

📈

5 / 25

Origin and Axes

  • X-axis: Horizontal number line.
  • Y-axis: Vertical number line.
  • Origin (0,0): Where axes meet.

🎙️ The two perpendicular lines are called the X-axis and the Y-axis. They intersect at a special point called the Origin, which is represented by the coordinates (0,0). The X-axis represents 'left and right' movement, and the Y-axis represents 'up and down' movement.

Dividing the Plane: Quadrants

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • Numbered I, II, III, and IV.
  • Each quadrant has specific sign patterns.

🎙️ When the X and Y axes cross, they divide the entire plane into four sections, which we call Quadrants. They are numbered counter-clockwise, starting from the top-right. Each quadrant has a unique combination of positive or negative x and y coordinates.

Quadrant I (x+, y+)
Quadrant II (x-, y+)
Quadrant III (x-, y-)
Quadrant IV (x+, y-)

Coordinates: Your Point's Address

  • An ordered pair (x, y).
  • x-coordinate: distance from Y-axis.
  • y-coordinate: distance from X-axis.

🎙️ Every point on the Cartesian plane has a unique address, called its coordinates, written as an ordered pair (x, y). The 'x' tells you how far left or right it is from the origin, and the 'y' tells you how far up or down it is.

Plotting Points: Example (2, 3)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 2 units right (positive x).
  • Move 3 units up (positive y).

🎙️ Let's plot the point (2, 3). First, always start at the origin (0,0). Since the x-coordinate is positive 2, we move 2 units to the right. Then, since the y-coordinate is positive 3, we move 3 units upwards from there. That's where our point (2,3) is located!

Plotting Points: Example (-3, 1)

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move 3 units left (negative x).
  • Move 1 unit up (positive y).

🎙️ Now, let's try a point with a negative coordinate: (-3, 1). Again, begin at the origin. The x-coordinate is negative 3, so we move 3 units to the left. The y-coordinate is positive 1, so we move 1 unit up. This point lies in Quadrant II.

What is a Circle?

  • A set of all points.
  • Equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is the 'center'.

🎙️ A circle isn't just a drawing; it's a very precise mathematical shape. Imagine a point that moves, but always stays the same distance away from another fixed point. The path it traces is a circle. That fixed point is called the center.

⭕
Perfect Circle
15 / 25

Center and Radius

  • Center: The fixed point (O).
  • Radius: Distance from center to any point on circle.
  • Denoted by 'r'.

🎙️ The Center is the heart of the circle, from which all points on the circle are equally distant. This constant distance is known as the Radius. It's like the length of a string you'd use to draw a perfect circle with a pencil.

📏
Measuring Radius
16 / 25

Diameter and Chord

  • Chord: A line segment connecting two points on a circle.
  • Diameter: A chord passing through the center.
  • Diameter = 2 × Radius.

🎙️ Any straight line segment that connects two points on the circle is called a Chord. The longest possible chord is very special; it passes right through the center of the circle and is called the Diameter. It's always twice the length of the radius.

↔️
Connecting Points

Arc and Sector

  • Arc: A part of the circumference of a circle.
  • Sector: Region bounded by two radii and an arc.
  • Like a slice of pizza.

🎙️ An Arc is just a curved piece of the circle's boundary. If you take two radii and the arc between them, the region enclosed is called a Sector. Think of a sector as a slice of pizza or a piece of a pie chart.

🍕
Pizza Slice

Segment of a Circle

  • Region bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • Can be major or minor.
  • Different from a sector.

🎙️ Now, if you take a chord and the arc it cuts off, the region enclosed is called a Segment. It's different from a sector because it uses a straight chord instead of two radii. A chord divides the circle into two segments: a smaller 'minor' segment and a larger 'major' segment.

Real-Life: Bicycle Wheel

  • Wheel is a perfect circle.
  • Spokes are like radii.

🎙️ Look at a bicycle wheel! It's a fantastic example of a circle. The hub is the center, the spokes are like radii, and the outer rim is the circumference. When you ride, the wheel goes 'round and round', illustrating circular motion perfectly.

Circumference: How Far Around?

  • The perimeter of a circle.
  • Distance around the circle.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd.

🎙️ The Circumference is the total distance around the edge of the circle. It's like the perimeter of a square or a triangle. We calculate it using the formula C = 2πr, where 'r' is the radius, or C = πd, where 'd' is the diameter. Pi (π) is a special mathematical constant.

📏
Measuring Tape

Area: How Much Space Inside?

  • The space occupied by the circle.
  • Region enclosed by the circumference.
  • Formula: A = πr².

🎙️ While circumference tells us the distance around, the Area tells us how much flat space the circle covers. It's the region inside the boundary. The formula for the area of a circle is A = πr², where 'r' is again the radius. Remember, area is always measured in square units.

🏞️
Covered Space

Worked Example: Circle Calculations

  • Given: Radius (r) = 7 cm.
  • Circumference = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm.
  • Area = πr² = (22/7) × 7² = 154 cm².

🎙️ Let's calculate the circumference and area for a circle with a radius of 7 cm. Using π ≈ 22/7, the circumference is 2 times 22/7 times 7, which gives us 44 cm. For the area, it's 22/7 times 7 squared, resulting in 154 cm². Always include units!
