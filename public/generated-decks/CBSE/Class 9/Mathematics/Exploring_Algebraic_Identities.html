What are Algebraic Expressions?

  • Combinations of variables, constants, and operations
  • No equality sign, unlike equations
  • Examples: x + 5, 2y - 3, 3x² + 7

🎙️ Before we dive into identities, let's quickly recall what algebraic expressions are. They are like mathematical phrases built with variables, numbers, and basic operations. Remember, an expression doesn't have an equals sign, so we can't 'solve' it, only simplify or evaluate it.

Equations vs. Expressions

  • An equation states that two expressions are equal
  • Contains an equality sign (=)
  • Example: x + 5 = 10

🎙️ The key difference between an expression and an equation is the equality sign. An equation sets two expressions equal to each other, creating a balance. We can solve equations to find specific values of variables that make the statement true.

What are Algebraic Identities?

  • Equations that are true for ALL values of variables
  • Like a universal truth in algebra
  • Always holds true, no matter what!

🎙️ An algebraic identity is a special type of equation that remains true regardless of what numbers you substitute for its variables. Think of them as fundamental rules or formulas that are always reliable. They are different from conditional equations, which are only true for specific values.

Identity 1: The Square of a Sum

  • (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
  • The square of the first term
  • Plus twice the product of the terms
  • Plus the square of the second term

🎙️ Our first identity is for squaring a sum of two terms. It states that (a + b)² is equal to a² + 2ab + b². This means when you multiply (a+b) by itself, you get the square of 'a', plus twice the product of 'a' and 'b', plus the square of 'b'.

Visualizing (a + b)²

  • Imagine a large square with side (a + b)
  • It can be divided into four smaller areas
  • One square of side 'a' (a²)
  • One square of side 'b' (b²), and two rectangles of sides 'a' and 'b' (ab)

🎙️ We can geometrically understand this identity by imagining a large square. If its side length is (a+b), its total area is (a+b)². We can divide this large square into four parts: a square of area a², another square of area b², and two rectangles, each with area ab. Adding these parts gives a² + ab + ab + b², which simplifies to a² + 2ab + b².

Real-Life Use: Area of a Garden

  • A square garden has side length (10 + 2) meters
  • Find its area using the identity
  • (10 + 2)² = 10² + 2(10)(2) + 2²

🎙️ Let's say you have a square garden with each side measuring 12 meters, which can be thought of as (10+2) meters. Instead of directly calculating 12², we can use the identity. This gives us 100 + 40 + 4, which is 144 square meters, a neat way to do mental math for areas.

Worked Example: (x + 3)²

🎙️ To expand (x + 3)², we identify 'a' as 'x' and 'b' as '3'. Applying the identity (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², we get x² + 2(x)(3) + 3². This simplifies to x² + 6x + 9. See how straightforward it becomes with the identity!

Identity 2: The Square of a Difference

  • (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
  • The square of the first term
  • Minus twice the product of the terms
  • Plus the square of the second term

🎙️ Next, we have the identity for squaring a difference: (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b². Notice the only change from the previous identity is the minus sign before the '2ab' term. This is crucial to remember when expanding such expressions.

Real-Life Use: Discount Price

  • Calculate (100 - 5)² quickly
  • Imagine an item priced at Rs 100, with a Rs 5 reduction
  • Square of the new price: (100 - 5)² = 100² - 2(100)(5) + 5²

🎙️ Suppose you need to quickly calculate 95² for a discount scenario (100-5). Using the identity, it's 100² - 2(100)(5) + 5². This becomes 10000 - 1000 + 25, which is 9025. It's much faster than multiplying 95 by 95 directly.

Identity 3: Difference of Squares

  • (a + b)(a - b) = a² - b²
  • Product of a sum and a difference
  • Equals the square of the first term minus the square of the second term

🎙️ This identity is incredibly useful for factorization and quick multiplication. When you multiply two binomials where one is a sum (a+b) and the other is a difference (a-b), the result is simply the square of 'a' minus the square of 'b'. The 'ab' terms cancel each other out.

Opposite Factors

Visualizing (a² - b²)

  • Start with a square of side 'a' (area a²)
  • Cut out a smaller square of side 'b' from a corner (area b²)
  • The remaining L-shaped area can be rearranged
  • It forms a rectangle with sides (a + b) and (a - b)

🎙️ Imagine a large square with side 'a'. If you cut out a smaller square of side 'b' from one corner, the remaining area is a² - b². You can then cut this remaining L-shaped region and rearrange it to form a rectangle. This new rectangle will have dimensions (a+b) and (a-b), visually proving the identity.

Real-Life Use: Quick Multiplication

  • Calculate 52 x 48 mentally
  • Recognize as (50 + 2)(50 - 2)
  • Apply identity: 50² - 2²

🎙️ This identity is a fantastic trick for multiplying numbers close to a multiple of 10. For example, to multiply 52 by 48, you can rewrite it as (50+2)(50-2). Using the identity, this becomes 50² - 2², which is 2500 - 4 = 2496. It's much faster than traditional multiplication.

Identity 4: Product of Binomials

  • (x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
  • The square of the common term 'x'
  • Plus the sum of 'a' and 'b' multiplied by 'x'
  • Plus the product of 'a' and 'b'

🎙️ Our fourth identity deals with multiplying two binomials that have a common first term, 'x', but different second terms, 'a' and 'b'. The expansion gives us x² plus the sum of 'a' and 'b' times 'x', plus the product of 'a' and 'b'. This is a very general and useful identity.

Binomial Product

Real-Life Use: Combined Area

  • Two rectangular plots, one (x+2) by x, another (x+3) by x
  • Or a single plot (x+2) by (x+3) if we consider combined dimensions
  • Area: x² + (2+3)x + (2)(3)

🎙️ Imagine you have a rectangular field with length (x+3) meters and width (x+2) meters. To find its area, you'd multiply (x+3) by (x+2). Using our identity, the area would be x² + (3+2)x + (3)(2), which simplifies to x² + 5x + 6 square meters. This helps in quick area calculations in various fields.

Worked Example: (y + 7)(y - 3)

🎙️ Let's expand (y + 7)(y - 3). Here, 'x' is 'y', 'a' is 7, and 'b' is -3. Applying the identity x² + (a + b)x + ab, we get y² + (7 + (-3))y + (7)(-3). This simplifies to y² + 4y - 21. Remember to pay attention to the signs of 'a' and 'b'.
