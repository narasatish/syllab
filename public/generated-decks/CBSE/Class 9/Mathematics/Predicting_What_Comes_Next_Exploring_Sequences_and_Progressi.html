  • According to some specific rule

Finite vs. Infinite Sequences

  • Finite: Has a limited number of terms
  • Infinite: Continues indefinitely, no last term

🎙️ A sequence can either end or go on forever. If you can count all the terms, it's finite. If it's represented with '...', meaning it never ends, it's an infinite sequence.

Arithmetic Progression (AP) - An Analogy

  • Imagine climbing stairs, one step at a time
  • Each step adds the same height
  • Consistent increase or decrease

🎙️ Think about a ladder or saving a fixed amount of money every day. Each new amount is found by adding a constant value to the previous one. This 'constant' is the heart of an Arithmetic Progression.

Formal Definition: Arithmetic Progression (AP)

  • A sequence where the difference
  • Between consecutive terms is constant
  • This constant is called the 'common difference'

🎙️ In an AP, if you pick any term (after the first one) and subtract the term just before it, you'll always get the same number. This number is unique to that AP and is called the common difference.

➕➕➕➕➕
Adding a Constant
15 / 30

Common Difference (d)

  • Represented by 'd'
  • d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = ...
  • Can be positive, negative, or zero

🎙️ The common difference 'd' is what makes an AP special. If 'd' is positive, the terms increase. If 'd' is negative, they decrease. If 'd' is zero, all terms are the same.

➖
The Difference

General Form of an AP

  • Let 'a' be the first term
  • Let 'd' be the common difference
  • The AP is: a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, ...

🎙️ This is the blueprint for any AP. If you know the first term 'a' and the common difference 'd', you can generate any term in the sequence. It's a powerful way to represent an AP.

1First Term (a)
2Add 'd'
3Add 'd' again
17 / 30

Worked Example 2: Identifying AP and Finding 'd'

  • Is 3, 7, 11, 15, ... an AP?
  • Step 1: Find difference between 2nd and 1st term (7-3 = 4)
  • Step 2: Find difference between 3rd and 2nd term (11-7 = 4)
  • Step 3: Since differences are constant, it's an AP with d=4.

🎙️ To check if a sequence is an AP, just calculate the difference between consecutive terms. If it's the same throughout, then yes, it's an AP, and that constant difference is 'd'.

The nth Term of an AP (a_n)

  • Formula: a_n = a + (n-1)d
  • 'a' is the first term
  • 'd' is the common difference
  • 'n' is the term number you want to find

🎙️ This is one of the most important formulas for APs! It allows you to find any term in the sequence without having to list all the terms before it. Just plug in 'a', 'd', and 'n'.

Worked Example 3: Finding the 10th Term

  • Find the 10th term of the AP: 2, 5, 8, 11, ...
  • Step 1: Identify a=2, d=3, n=10
  • Step 2: Use formula a_n = a + (n-1)d
  • Step 3: a_10 = 2 + (10-1)3 = 2 + 9*3 = 2 + 27 = 29

🎙️ Following these steps, we can easily find any term. First, pull out the values for 'a', 'd', and 'n' from the problem. Then, substitute them into the formula and calculate carefully.

Formal Definition: Geometric Progression (GP)

  • A sequence where the ratio
  • Between consecutive terms is constant
  • This constant is called the 'common ratio'

🎙️ In a GP, if you divide any term by the term immediately preceding it, you'll always get the same number. This number is called the common ratio, and it's what defines a GP.

Common Ratio (r)

  • Represented by 'r'
  • r = a₂ / a₁ = a₃ / a₂ = ...
  • Can be positive or negative, fraction or whole number

🎙️ The common ratio 'r' dictates how the terms grow or shrink. If 'r' is greater than 1, terms increase. If 'r' is between 0 and 1, terms decrease. If 'r' is negative, terms alternate in sign.

➗
The Ratio

General Form of a GP

  • Let 'a' be the first term
  • Let 'r' be the common ratio
  • The GP is: a, ar, ar², ar³, ...

🎙️ Similar to AP, knowing 'a' and 'r' allows you to construct any GP. Each subsequent term is found by multiplying the previous term by the common ratio 'r'.

1First Term (a)
2Multiply by 'r'
3Multiply by 'r' again

Worked Example 4: Identifying GP and Finding 'r'

  • Is 2, 6, 18, 54, ... a GP?
  • Step 1: Find ratio of 2nd to 1st term (6/2 = 3)
  • Step 2: Find ratio of 3rd to 2nd term (18/6 = 3)
  • Step 3: Since ratios are constant, it's a GP with r=3.

🎙️ To verify a GP, perform division between consecutive terms. If the quotient is consistent, then you've found your common ratio 'r', and confirmed it's a Geometric Progression.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Mixing up AP and GP formulas
  • Incorrectly calculating 'd' or 'r'
  • Sign errors (especially with negative numbers)
  • Not identifying 'a' (first term) correctly

🎙️ It's easy to confuse the 'add' rule of AP with the 'multiply' rule of GP. Always double-check which type of progression you are dealing with before applying any formula. Practice makes perfect!
