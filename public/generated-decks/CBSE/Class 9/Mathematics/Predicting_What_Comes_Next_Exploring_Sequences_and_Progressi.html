Understanding a Sequence

  • An ordered list of numbers or objects.
  • Each item is called a 'term'.

Special Sequences: Progressions

  • They have a very specific and consistent rule.

🎙️ While all progressions are sequences, not all sequences are progressions. Progressions are like the VIPs of sequences – they follow a very strict mathematical rule that allows us to predict any term easily.

What is an Arithmetic Progression (AP)?

  • A sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant.
  • This constant difference is called the 'common difference'.

🎙️ An Arithmetic Progression, or AP, is super predictable! To get the next term, you just add the same fixed number to the current term, every single time. It's like climbing stairs where each step is the same height.

➕

How to Identify an AP

  • Subtract any term from its succeeding term.
  • If the difference is the same for all pairs, it's an AP.

🎙️ To check if a sequence is an AP, you just need to do a simple subtraction. Take any term and subtract the term before it. If this difference is always the same throughout the sequence, then you've found an AP!

The Common Difference (d)

  • The constant difference between consecutive terms in an AP.
  • Represented by the letter 'd'.
  • d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = ...

🎙️ The 'common difference', denoted by 'd', is the heart of an AP. It's that fixed number you add or subtract to get from one term to the next. It can be positive, negative, or even zero!

The Terms of an AP

  • First term: 'a'
  • Second term: a + d
  • Third term: a + 2d
  • And so on...

🎙️ Every AP starts with a 'first term', usually called 'a'. Then, to get the next term, you just keep adding the common difference 'd'. This creates a clear pattern for all the terms in the sequence.

Worked Example: Generating an AP

  • If a = 5 and d = 3,
  • First term = 5
  • Second term = 5 + 3 = 8
  • Third term = 8 + 3 = 11
  • The AP is: 5, 8, 11, 14, ...

🎙️ Let's put our knowledge into practice! If we know the starting point 'a' and the constant jump 'd', we can easily build the entire Arithmetic Progression, term by term. See how simple it is?

15 / 25

Worked Example: Finding 'd' or 'a'

  • Given AP: 10, 7, 4, 1, ...
  • a = 10 (first term)
  • d = 7 - 10 = -3
  • If a₁=5, a₂=12, then d = 12-5 = 7

🎙️ Sometimes, you're given the AP and need to find 'a' or 'd'. Remember, 'a' is always the very first term. To find 'd', just subtract any term from the one that immediately follows it. It's that easy!

The General Term of an AP (aₙ)

  • The formula to find any 'nth' term of an AP.
  • aₙ = a + (n - 1)d
  • Where 'a' is the first term, 'd' is the common difference, and 'n' is the term number.

🎙️ This is a super important formula! The general term, 'a sub n', allows us to find any term in an AP without having to list all the terms before it. Just plug in the values for 'a', 'd', and 'n', and you'll get your answer!

Worked Example: Finding the 10th Term

  • AP: 2, 5, 8, 11, ...
  • Find the 10th term (a₁₀).
  • a = 2, d = 3, n = 10
  • a₁₀ = 2 + (10 - 1)3 = 2 + (9)3 = 2 + 27 = 29

🎙️ Let's use our new formula! For this AP, we can quickly identify 'a' and 'd'. Then, we just substitute 'n' as 10 into the formula. See how quickly we can find a term far down the sequence?

20 / 25

Worked Example: How Many Terms?

  • AP: 7, 10, 13, ..., 55
  • Which term is 55?
  • a = 7, d = 3, aₙ = 55
  • 55 = 7 + (n - 1)3
  • Solve for n: 48 = (n-1)3 => 16 = n-1 => n = 17

🎙️ Sometimes, we know the last term and need to find its position. In this case, 'a sub n' becomes the last term, and we need to solve the equation for 'n'. It's like finding which step you are on, given its height!
