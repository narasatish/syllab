What is 'Saving'?

  • Setting aside money for future use.
  • Usually for short-term goals or emergencies.
  • Kept in a safe place, like a bank account.

🎙️ Now, let's dive into saving. Saving is simply putting a portion of your current income aside instead of spending it immediately. Think of it as reserving money for something specific you want or need later, or for unexpected situations.

What is 'Investing'?

  • Putting money into assets to generate returns.
  • Aims for long-term growth, often over many years.
  • Involves some level of risk for higher potential rewards.

🎙️ Now, let's move to investing. Investing is a more active way to make your money grow. Instead of just keeping it safe, you use it to buy assets like stocks or property, hoping they will increase in value over time and give you more money back.

Key Difference: 'Saving' vs 'Investing'

  • Saving: Low risk, low return, short-term goals.
  • Investing: Higher risk, higher potential return, long-term goals.
  • Both are important for financial well-being.

🎙️ This slide highlights the core distinction. Saving is about security and accessibility for near-future needs. Investing is about growth and wealth creation over a longer period, accepting more risk for potentially greater rewards.

The 'Power' of Compounding

  • Earning returns not just on your initial money, but also on the accumulated returns.
  • Often called 'interest on interest'.
  • Makes your money grow exponentially over time.

🎙️ Compounding is a magical concept in finance. It means your earnings themselves start earning money. Over time, this creates a snowball effect, making your investment grow much faster than simple interest alone.

➕
Growth Multiplier
15 / 25

Example: 'Compounding' Interest

  • If you invest ₹1,000 at 10% interest for 1 year, you get ₹100.
  • Next year, you earn 10% on ₹1,100 (original + interest), which is ₹110.
  • Your money grows faster each year because the base amount increases.

🎙️ Let's see how compounding works. Instead of just earning interest on the original ₹1,000, you start earning interest on the ₹1,100. This small difference adds up significantly over many years, making your money work harder for you.
