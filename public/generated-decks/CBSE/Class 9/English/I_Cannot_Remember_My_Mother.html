Literary Device: Understanding Simile - Like or As

  • A figure of speech comparing two different things directly
  • Uses 'like' or 'as' to make the comparison explicit
  • Adds vividness and helps the reader visualize

🎙️ Now let's explore some literary devices used by Tagore. A simile is like a direct bridge between two different things, using 'like' or 'as' to show how they are similar. It makes descriptions much more lively.

↔️

Literary Device: Unveiling Metaphor - Implied Comparison

  • A figure of speech that directly states one thing is another
  • Does NOT use 'like' or 'as'; comparison is implied
  • Creates a stronger, more imaginative connection between ideas

🎙️ A metaphor is like a stronger, more direct comparison than a simile. Instead of saying something is 'like' something else, it says it 'is' that other thing, creating a powerful imaginative link and deeper meaning.

🎭
Implied Identity
19 / 25

Worked Example: Identifying Metaphor in the Poem

  • 'The sky is the mother's face' (implied)

🎙️ While not explicitly stated as 'the sky is her face', the child's imagination makes this connection. When he looks at the sky and feels his mother's presence, the sky *becomes* her face for him, an implied metaphor for her omnipresent love.

1Look for a statement where one thing IS another
2Identify the two seemingly unrelated things
3Interpret the deeper meaning of their identity
