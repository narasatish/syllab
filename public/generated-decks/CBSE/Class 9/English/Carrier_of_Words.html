What is Reported Speech?

  • It's about sharing what someone said.
  • Two main ways: Direct and Indirect Speech.
  • Like a news reporter delivering information.

🎙️ Reported Speech is simply how we convey a message or information given by another person. Think of it like a news reporter delivering information – they tell you what happened or what someone said. We have two main ways to do this: either by quoting the exact words or by paraphrasing them.

Sharing Messages
5 / 30

Direct Speech: The Original Words

  • Uses the exact words spoken.
  • Enclosed in quotation marks ('...').
  • A comma separates the reporting verb from the quote.

🎙️ Direct speech is like taking a photograph of someone's words. You capture them exactly as they were spoken. We always put these exact words inside quotation marks, and usually, a comma comes before the opening quotation mark to separate it from the rest of the sentence.

💬
Exact Quote
6 / 30

Example: Direct Speech

  • She said, 'I am going to Delhi tomorrow.'
  • Rohan exclaimed, 'What a brilliant shot!'
  • My teacher asked, 'Have you completed your homework?'

🎙️ Here are a few examples. Notice how the exact words are in quotation marks. The first one is a statement, the second an exclamation, and the third a question. In all cases, the words inside the quotes are precisely what was uttered.

7 / 30

Indirect Speech: Reporting the Gist

  • Reports what was said, without exact words.
  • No quotation marks.
  • Changes often occur in tense, pronouns, and time/place words.

🎙️ Indirect speech, on the other hand, is like summarizing a story. You tell what was said, but you don't use the exact words. Because we're not quoting directly, there are no quotation marks. This usually means we have to make some changes to the original sentence, especially to the tense, pronouns, and certain adverbs.

📝
Summarizing
8 / 30

Example: Indirect Speech

  • Direct: She said, 'I am going to Delhi tomorrow.'
  • Indirect: She said that she was going to Delhi the next day.
  • Direct: My teacher asked, 'Have you completed your homework?'
  • Indirect: My teacher asked if I had completed my homework.

🎙️ Look at these transformations. 'I am going' becomes 'she was going', 'tomorrow' becomes 'the next day'. And for the question, 'Have you completed' becomes 'if I had completed'. See how many things change? We'll learn the rules for these changes step-by-step.

Key Changes: The Reporting Verb

  • `Said` remains `said` (if no object).
  • `Said to` changes to `told` (with an object).
  • Other verbs: `asked`, `exclaimed`, `advised`, `ordered`.

🎙️ The reporting verb is the first thing we look at. If there's no object after 'said', it usually stays 'said'. But if 'said to' is followed by a person, like 'said to me', it changes to 'told me'. For questions, we use 'asked', and for commands, we might use 'ordered' or 'advised'.

Key Changes: The Conjunction

  • Use `that` for statements.
  • Use `if` or `whether` for 'Yes/No' questions.
  • No conjunction for 'Wh- questions' (who, what, where).

🎙️ The conjunction acts like a bridge connecting the reporting clause to the reported clause. For simple statements, we usually add 'that'. For questions that can be answered with 'yes' or 'no', we use 'if' or 'whether'. Interestingly, for questions starting with 'who', 'what', 'where', etc., we don't add an extra conjunction; the 'wh- word' itself acts as the connector.

🔗
Joining Words

Key Changes: Pronouns

  • Pronouns change according to the speaker and listener.
  • `I` becomes `he/she`.
  • `My` becomes `his/her`.
  • `You` becomes `I/we/he/she/they` (context dependent).

🎙️ This is a crucial change! When you report someone else's words, the pronouns shift to reflect the new speaker's perspective. For example, if Rohan said 'I am happy', and you are reporting it, 'I' refers to Rohan, so it becomes 'he'. Similarly, 'my' becomes 'his' or 'her' depending on the original speaker. Always think: who is speaking now, and who was speaking then?

Whose Perspective?

Example: Pronoun Changes

  • Direct: He said to me, 'I will lend you my book.'
  • Indirect: He told me that he would lend me his book.
  • Direct: She said, 'My brother is taller than me.'
  • Indirect: She said that her brother was taller than her.

🎙️ See how 'I' changes to 'he' and 'my' changes to 'his' in the first example? In the second, 'my' becomes 'her' and 'me' becomes 'her'. The key is to understand who the pronoun refers to in the original direct speech and then adjust it to fit the reported context.

Key Changes: Tense Shift (Part 1)

  • If the reporting verb is in the past tense (e.g., `said`), the tense of the reported speech changes.
  • Simple Present changes to Simple Past.
  • Example: `I play` becomes `he played`.

🎙️ This is one of the most important rules! When you report something that was said in the past, the verb tense in the reported part usually shifts backward in time. The most basic change is from Simple Present to Simple Past. For instance, if someone said 'I eat', you report it as 'he ate'.

⏳
Time Travel for Verbs
16 / 30

Example: Simple Present to Simple Past

  • Direct: He said, 'I live in Delhi.'
  • Indirect: He said that he lived in Delhi.
  • Direct: She said, 'The sun rises in the east.'
  • Indirect: She said that the sun rises in the east. (Universal truths don't change!)

🎙️ Notice how 'live' becomes 'lived'. However, there's an exception! If the direct speech states a universal truth, a habitual fact, or a scientific fact, the tense does NOT change in indirect speech, even if the reporting verb is in the past. The sun still rises in the east, no matter when it was reported!

17 / 30

Key Changes: Tense Shift (Part 2)

  • Present Continuous changes to Past Continuous.
  • Example: `I am playing` becomes `he was playing`.
  • Applies when reporting verb is in past tense.

🎙️ Continuing our tense journey, if the original statement was about an ongoing action in the present, it shifts to an ongoing action in the past. So, 'I am reading' becomes 'he was reading'. Remember, this shift only happens if your reporting verb, like 'said' or 'told', is in the past tense.

➡️
Continuous Action Shift
18 / 30

Example: Present Continuous to Past Continuous

  • Direct: She said, 'I am watching a movie.'
  • Indirect: She said that she was watching a movie.
  • Direct: They said, 'We are planning a trip.'
  • Indirect: They said that they were planning a trip.

🎙️ Here, 'am watching' correctly transforms to 'was watching', and 'are planning' becomes 'were planning'. It's all about moving the action backward in time from the moment it was originally spoken.

19 / 30

Key Changes: Tense Shift (Part 3)

  • Present Perfect changes to Past Perfect.
  • Example: `I have finished` becomes `he had finished`.
  • This shift also applies to Present Perfect Continuous (to Past Perfect Continuous).

🎙️ For actions completed in the present that have a connection to the present, the Present Perfect tense is used. When reporting this, it shifts to the Past Perfect. So, 'I have eaten' becomes 'he had eaten'. The same logic applies to Present Perfect Continuous becoming Past Perfect Continuous.

✅
Completed Action Shift
20 / 30

Example: Present Perfect to Past Perfect

  • Direct: He said, 'I have completed my work.'
  • Indirect: He said that he had completed his work.
  • Direct: She said, 'They have been waiting for an hour.'
  • Indirect: She said that they had been waiting for an hour.

🎙️ You can clearly see 'have completed' changing to 'had completed', and 'have been waiting' to 'had been waiting'. These shifts ensure that the reported action correctly reflects its timing relative to when it was originally spoken and when it is being reported.

Key Changes: Time & Place Words

  • Words indicating proximity change to distance.
  • `Now` becomes `then`.
  • `Today` becomes `that day`.
  • `Here` becomes `there`.
  • `Tomorrow` becomes `the next day` / `the following day`.

🎙️ When you report something, the 'now' of the original speaker might be 'then' for the reporter. Similarly, 'here' becomes 'there'. These adverbs of time and place need to shift to reflect the new context of reporting. It's like moving the conversation to a different point in time and space.

📍
Where and When?

Example: Time & Place Changes

  • Direct: He said, 'I will meet you here tomorrow.'
  • Indirect: He said that he would meet me there the next day.
  • Direct: She said, 'I am busy now.'
  • Indirect: She said that she was busy then.

🎙️ Observe how 'here' changes to 'there' and 'tomorrow' becomes 'the next day'. Also, 'now' shifts to 'then'. These small words are crucial for making your indirect speech accurate and grammatically correct.

Transforming Questions

  • Reporting verb changes to `asked`, `inquired`, `wondered`.
  • No inversion (Subject-Verb order) in indirect questions.
  • Use `if` / `whether` for Yes/No questions.
  • Use 'Wh-word' (who, what, where) for Wh-questions.

🎙️ Transforming questions is slightly different. The reporting verb changes to 'asked' or similar. Crucially, the indirect question becomes a statement, so the subject comes before the verb (no inversion). For Yes/No questions, 'if' or 'whether' is used. For Wh-questions, the Wh-word itself acts as the conjunction.

❓
Asking Indirectly
25 / 30

Example: Transforming Questions

  • Direct: She asked, 'Are you coming to the party?'
  • Indirect: She asked if I was coming to the party.
  • Direct: He asked, 'What is your name?'
  • Indirect: He asked what my name was.

🎙️ In the first example, 'Are you coming' becomes 'if I was coming', notice the subject 'I' before 'was'. In the second, 'What is your name?' becomes 'what my name was' – again, 'my name' (subject) before 'was' (verb). And remember, no question mark at the end of indirect questions!

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting tense shifts.
  • Incorrect pronoun changes.
  • Using quotation marks in indirect speech.
  • Not changing time/place words.
  • Maintaining question structure in indirect questions.

🎙️ Students often make mistakes with tense shifts, especially forgetting to change them when the reporting verb is in the past. Another common error is incorrect pronoun usage. Remember, no quotation marks in indirect speech! Also, be mindful of changing words like 'today' and 'here', and always convert indirect questions into statement form.

Recap: Carrying Words Effectively

  • Direct Speech: Exact words, quotation marks.
  • Indirect Speech: Reported words, no quotes.
  • Key changes: Reporting verb, conjunction, pronouns, tense, time/place.
  • Questions: Use `asked`, `if/whether` or Wh-word, no inversion.

🎙️ Let's quickly recap the main points. Direct speech uses the exact words and quotes, while indirect speech reports the gist without quotes. Remember to change the reporting verb, use appropriate conjunctions, adjust pronouns, shift tenses backwards, and modify time and place words. For questions, use 'asked' and ensure the reported question is in statement form.
