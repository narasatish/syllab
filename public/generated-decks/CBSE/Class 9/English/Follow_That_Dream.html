Literary Lens: Unpacking Metaphor

  • A figure of speech directly comparing two unlike things.
  • It says one thing *is* another, without 'like' or 'as'.
  • Example: 'Her hope was a lighthouse guiding her through the storm.'

🎙️ Now, let's look at how authors use language to enrich stories. A metaphor is a powerful literary device where you state that one thing *is* another, even though it's not literally true. For example, if the chapter says, 'Her hope was a lighthouse,' it means her hope guided her, like a lighthouse guides ships, without using 'like' or 'as'.

Literary Lens: Exploring Simile

  • A figure of speech comparing two unlike things using 'like' or 'as'.
  • It makes descriptions more vivid and relatable.
  • Example: 'His determination was as strong as a mountain.'

🎙️ Similar to a metaphor, a simile also compares two different things. However, the key difference is that a simile *always* uses the words 'like' or 'as' to draw the comparison. For instance, 'Her determination was *as strong as* a mountain,' or 'His dream shone *like* a bright star in the night sky.' This makes the comparison explicit.

Literary Lens: Painting with Imagery

  • Language that appeals to our five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch).
  • Helps readers visualize and feel the story.
  • Example: 'The crisp morning air carried the scent of jasmine and the distant temple bells.'

🎙️ Imagery is all about creating a picture in the reader's mind, engaging their senses. When an author uses imagery, they're not just telling you what happened; they're showing you, letting you hear, smell, taste, and feel it. It makes the story come alive and feel more real.
