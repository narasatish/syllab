Why Do We Need Elections?

  • In a large country, direct democracy is impossible.
  • Citizens choose representatives to make laws and govern.
  • Elections provide a mechanism for choice and accountability.

🎙️ Imagine if every single decision in India, from a new law to a local road repair, had to be voted on by 1.4 billion people! It's simply not feasible. That's why we need elections – they allow us to choose a few individuals who represent our interests and make decisions on our behalf.

What is an Election?

  • A mechanism by which people can choose their representatives.
  • Allows people to change representatives if dissatisfied.
  • Ensures representatives are accountable to the people.

🎙️ An election is essentially a formal and organised process. It's not just about selecting someone; it's about holding them accountable. If our chosen representatives don't perform well, elections provide us with the power to replace them in the next cycle, ensuring they remain responsive to public needs.

Democratic vs. Non-Democratic Elections

  • Democratic: Universal adult suffrage, free choice, fair counting.
  • Non-Democratic: Limited choice, no real competition, outcomes manipulated.
  • Example: India vs. some authoritarian regimes where only one party's candidates can win.

🎙️ Not all elections are truly democratic. In a democratic election, everyone has a real choice, and the results genuinely reflect the people's will. In contrast, non-democratic elections might appear to happen, but they lack genuine competition and often have predetermined outcomes, making them a mere formality.

Is Political Competition Good?

  • Pros: Forces parties to perform, offers voters choice, prevents complacency.
  • Cons: Can lead to divisions, 'dirty tricks', focus on winning over governance.
  • In India, competition ensures diverse voices are heard.

🎙️ Just like competition in sports or business can bring out the best, political competition pushes parties to work harder for public support. However, it also has a downside, sometimes leading to mud-slinging and a focus on short-term gains rather than long-term vision. The challenge is to manage this competition constructively.

What is Our System of Elections?

  • India follows a 'First Past the Post' system.
  • Country divided into constituencies.
  • Candidate with most votes in a constituency wins.

🎙️ India, being a vast and diverse country, has a well-defined electoral system. We primarily use the 'First Past the Post' system, which means the candidate who secures the highest number of votes in their specific area, or constituency, is declared the winner, even if they don't get an absolute majority.

🗺️

Electoral Constituencies

  • The country is divided into specific geographical areas for election purposes.
  • Each constituency elects one representative to the Lok Sabha (Parliament).
  • Currently, there are 543 Lok Sabha constituencies in India.

🎙️ Think of constituencies as electoral districts. Each one is like a distinct zone, ensuring that every part of India has a voice in the national parliament. The 543 Lok Sabha constituencies are crucial because they determine who forms the central government.

Reserved Constituencies

  • Seats reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST).
  • Ensures fair representation for historically disadvantaged groups.
  • Currently, 84 seats for SC and 47 for ST in Lok Sabha.

🎙️ To uphold the principle of equality and ensure that no community is left behind, our Constitution provides for reserved constituencies. This mechanism guarantees that even the most marginalised sections of society have their representatives in Parliament, ensuring their concerns are voiced and addressed.

Voter's List (Electoral Roll)

  • Official list of all eligible voters for an election.
  • Universal Adult Franchise: Everyone 18 years or older can vote.
  • Ensures transparency and prevents fraudulent voting.

🎙️ Before any election, an updated voter's list, also known as the electoral roll, is published. This list is fundamental because it defines who is eligible to cast a vote. India's commitment to 'Universal Adult Franchise' means that every citizen, regardless of their background, has the right to vote once they turn 18.

Nomination of Candidates

  • Any citizen above 25 years of age can contest elections.
  • Candidates fill a nomination form and pay a security deposit.
  • Must declare assets, liabilities, and criminal records (if any).

🎙️ Becoming a candidate is a formal process. It's not just about wanting to stand for election; there are specific requirements. The declaration of assets and criminal records is a crucial step introduced to enhance transparency and allow voters to make informed decisions about their potential representatives.

Educational Qualifications for Candidates?

  • In India, there are NO educational qualifications required to be a candidate.
  • Why? Democracy is about representation, not just academic degrees.
  • Focus on understanding public needs, integrity, and leadership skills.

🎙️ This often surprises students! Unlike many jobs, contesting an election in India doesn't require specific educational degrees. The framers of our Constitution believed that democracy is for everyone, and the ability to represent people effectively doesn't solely depend on academic qualifications but on wisdom, experience, and commitment to public service.

Model Code of Conduct

  • Set of guidelines for political parties and candidates during elections.
  • Issued by the Election Commission to ensure fair play.
  • Restrictions: No bribing, no appeals to caste/religion, no use of government resources.

🎙️ To prevent unfair practices and ensure a level playing field, the Election Commission enforces a 'Model Code of Conduct'. This code lays down strict rules that all parties and candidates must adhere to during the campaign period, aiming to maintain the sanctity and fairness of the electoral process.

Polling and Counting of Votes

  • Polling Day: Citizens cast their votes at designated polling booths.
  • Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) are widely used for efficiency and accuracy.
  • Counting Day: Votes are counted, and results are declared.

🎙️ Polling day is when citizens exercise their most fundamental democratic right. The use of EVMs has significantly streamlined the process, making it faster and more secure. On counting day, the collective will of the people is revealed as votes are tallied and the winners are announced, marking the conclusion of the electoral battle.

The Election Commission (ECI)

  • An independent and powerful body responsible for conducting elections.
  • Appointed by the President, but largely immune from government interference.
  • Powers: Announce schedule, implement code of conduct, order re-election, punish non-compliance.

🎙️ The Election Commission of India (ECI) is arguably one of the most powerful and respected institutions in the country. Its independence is vital, as it allows the ECI to act without fear or favour, ensuring that elections are conducted fairly and impartially, thereby protecting the integrity of our democratic process.

Challenges to Free and Fair Elections

  • Money Power: Use of large sums of money to buy votes or influence results.
  • Muscle Power: Intimidation of voters or candidates.
  • Criminalisation of Politics: Candidates with criminal backgrounds contesting.
  • Family Politics: Dominance of certain families in political parties.

🎙️ Despite our robust system, challenges persist. Money and muscle power can distort the electoral playing field. The entry of individuals with criminal records and the prevalence of dynastic politics also pose significant threats to the fairness and integrity of our elections, requiring continuous vigilance and reform.

Common Mistakes Students Make!

  • Confusing Election Commission's role with the government.
  • Not understanding 'First Past the Post' vs. Proportional Representation.
  • Forgetting the importance of reserved constituencies.
  • Mixing up Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha elections.

🎙️ Be careful not to confuse the Election Commission's independent role with that of the ruling government; they are distinct. Also, remember the specific details of our 'First Past the Post' system and why reserved constituencies are crucial. Pay attention to the differences between national and state elections.

  • State funding of elections to curb money power.
