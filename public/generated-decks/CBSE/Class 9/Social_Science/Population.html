Factors Affecting Distribution: Physical Features

What is Population Growth? The Changing Numbers

  • The change in the number of inhabitants of a country/territory
  • Can be absolute growth (total increase) or annual growth rate (percentage)
  • Analogy: Your bank balance changing over time

🎙️ Population growth isn't just about 'more people.' It's about how that number changes over time, whether increasing or decreasing. A positive growth rate means more people are added, while a negative rate would mean a decline. This change is driven by three key processes.

📈
Population Growth
11 / 30

Processes of Population Change: Birth Rate (B.R.)

  • Number of live births per 1000 persons in a year
  • High birth rates contribute to rapid population growth
  • Factors: cultural norms, lack of family planning awareness, early marriage

🎙️ The birth rate is a fundamental driver of population change. In India, historically, birth rates have been high due to various social and cultural reasons, including the preference for male children and limited access to reproductive health services in many rural areas.

👶
New Lives
12 / 30

Processes of Population Change: Death Rate (D.R.)

  • Number of deaths per 1000 persons in a year
  • Decline in death rates significantly contributes to population growth
  • Factors: improved healthcare, sanitation, nutrition, disease control

🎙️ While high birth rates add to population, declining death rates mean people are living longer. India has seen remarkable improvements in healthcare since independence, leading to a sharp fall in the death rate, which is a major reason for our sustained population growth.

⚰️
Mortality

Processes of Population Change: Migration

  • Movement of people across regions and territories
  • Can be internal (within a country) or international (between countries)
  • Affects population size and distribution both at origin and destination

🎙️ Migration is the third crucial factor. It doesn't change the global population, but it significantly alters the population of specific regions or countries. Think of people moving from rural Bihar to urban Delhi for work – this changes the population composition of both places.

🚶‍♀️
Movement of People

Why Do People Migrate? Push and Pull Factors

  • **Push Factors:** Reasons people leave a place (e.g., poverty, unemployment)
  • **Pull Factors:** Reasons people are attracted to a new place (e.g., jobs, education)
  • These forces drive internal and international migration

🎙️ Imagine you're deciding where to live. 'Push' factors are like being pushed out of your current home – perhaps due to lack of jobs in your village. 'Pull' factors are like being pulled towards a city by the promise of better education or higher wages. Both are powerful motivators.

Push Factors (Rural Distress)
Pull Factors (Urban Opportunities)
15 / 30

Impact of Migration on Population

  • Increases population in destination areas (e.g., cities)
  • Decreases population in origin areas (e.g., rural villages)
  • Leads to changes in age and sex composition in both regions

🎙️ Migration has a profound impact. While it doesn't change India's total population, it redistributes it. This often leads to rapid urbanisation, with cities swelling and rural areas sometimes facing labour shortages as young people move away.

Rural-Urban Shift

Age Composition: The Structure of a Nation

  • The number of people in different age groups in a population
  • Crucial for understanding a country's workforce and dependency burden
  • Divided into children (0-14), working age (15-59), and aged (60+)

🎙️ Age composition is like a snapshot of a country's population by age. A large proportion of children means more investment in education and healthcare, while a large working-age population is a 'demographic dividend' for economic growth, as seen in India currently.

Sex Ratio: Balancing the Genders

  • Number of females per 1000 males in the population
  • Important social indicator reflecting equality between males and females
  • India's sex ratio has historically been a concern (943 females/1000 males in 2011)

🎙️ The sex ratio tells us about gender balance. A ratio below 1000 indicates fewer females than males, which can point to issues like female foeticide, infanticide, and discrimination against girl children, despite government initiatives like 'Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao'.

National Population Policy (NPP) 2000

  • A comprehensive framework for population stabilisation
  • Aimed at improving quality of life, not just controlling numbers
  • Focus on reproductive health, child health, and women empowerment

🎙️ Recognising the challenges of a large population, the Indian government formulated the NPP 2000. It's a forward-looking policy that shifted focus from mere population control to a holistic approach of improving the well-being and health of families.

🏛️
Government Policy
25 / 30

Key Initiatives of NPP 2000

  • Free and compulsory school education up to 14 years
  • Reducing infant mortality rate below 30 per 1000 live births
  • Achieving universal immunisation of children against all vaccine-preventable diseases
  • Promoting delayed marriage for girls, and making family welfare a people-centred programme

🎙️ The NPP 2000 outlined several strategic interventions. These included improving access to education for girls, reducing child deaths through immunisation, and providing better reproductive health services, all aimed at empowering individuals to make informed choices about family size.

1Education for All
2Child Health
3Reproductive Health
4Women Empowerment

Achievements and Challenges of NPP

  • **Achievements:** Decline in total fertility rate, improved health indicators
  • **Challenges:** Persistent regional disparities, gender inequality, unmet needs for contraception
  • Ongoing efforts are crucial for sustainable development

🎙️ While the NPP has led to significant progress, like a notable decline in the total fertility rate, challenges remain. We still see disparities in health and education outcomes across different states and communities, requiring continued focus and adaptive strategies.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Confusing 'population growth' with 'population change'
  • Not knowing the difference between birth rate and death rate
  • Mixing up 'push' and 'pull' factors of migration

🎙️ It's easy to get tangled in the terms! Remember, growth is usually an increase, while change can be an increase or decrease. Pay close attention to the definitions and specific examples we discussed to avoid these common pitfalls in your exams.
