🎙️ Hello, little scientists! Today, we're going on an exciting journey to explore all the wonderful things around us. Have you ever wondered what everything is made of?

What are Materials?

  • Anything we see or touch is a material.
  • Like wood, plastic, or metal.

🎙️ Imagine your toy car. It's made of plastic and metal. That plastic and metal are called materials. Your clothes are made of fabric, which is also a material.

Many Types of Materials

  • Some are hard, some are soft.
  • Some are shiny, some are dull.

🎙️ Just like we have different kinds of fruits, we have different kinds of materials. A rock is hard, but a pillow is soft. A spoon is shiny, but a brick is dull.

Materials have Different Forms

  • These forms are called 'states'.
  • There are three main states.

🎙️ It's like water can be ice, or liquid water, or steam! These are different forms of the same material. We call these forms 'states of matter'.

States of Matter

Meet the Solids!

  • Solids have a fixed shape.
  • They do not change their shape easily.

🎙️ Think of a brick or a book. They always keep their shape, no matter where you put them. They are strong and hold their form.

Shapes are all around us.
6 / 20

Examples of Solids

  • Your pencil, a chair, an apple.
  • They all have their own shape.

🎙️ Look around your classroom! Your desk, your bag, the blackboard – all are solids. They stay in their shape and don't spread out.

🍎🍎🍎

Next, the Liquids!

  • Liquids do not have a fixed shape.
  • They take the shape of their container.

🎙️ Imagine pouring water into a glass. The water takes the shape of the glass. If you pour it into a bowl, it takes the shape of the bowl. That's a liquid!

🥛
Takes Container Shape
8 / 20

Examples of Liquids

  • Water, milk, juice, oil.
  • They flow easily.

🎙️ When you drink juice, you're drinking a liquid. When it rains, the water falling is a liquid. Liquids can be poured and they flow.

Water moves in a never-ending cycle.

And finally, the Gases!

  • Gases do not have a fixed shape or volume.
  • They spread out to fill any space.

🎙️ You cannot always see gases, but they are all around us! The air we breathe is a mixture of gases. Gases like to spread out everywhere.

🎈
Fills the space

Examples of Gases

  • Air, steam from hot water, gas in balloons.
  • We can't always see them!

🎙️ When you inflate a balloon, you are putting gas inside. When you boil water, you see steam, which is water in its gas form. Gases are invisible most of the time.

Changing States: Water

  • Water can be solid (ice), liquid (water), or gas (steam).
  • Heat or cold makes it change.

🎙️ Water is a wonderful example because we can see it in all three states easily. When it's very cold, water becomes ice. When it's warm, it's liquid. And when it's very hot, it becomes steam!

🧊
Water changes form

Solid to Liquid: Melting!

  • When ice gets warm, it melts.
  • It changes from solid to liquid.

🎙️ Think of an ice cream cone on a sunny day. The solid ice cream starts to turn into a liquid. This change from solid to liquid is called melting.

Liquid to Gas: Evaporation!

  • When water gets very hot, it boils.
  • It turns into steam, a gas.

🎙️ When your mom boils water for tea, you see steam rising. That's the liquid water turning into a gas. This is called evaporation or boiling.

2Gets Hot

Gas to Liquid: Condensation!

  • When steam gets cold, it turns back into tiny water drops.
  • Like water on a cold glass.

🎙️ Have you ever seen tiny water drops on the outside of a cold drink glass? That's condensation! The gas in the air gets cold near the glass and turns back into liquid water.
