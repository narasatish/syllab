The Journey of Water

  • Water keeps moving all the time.
  • It goes up to the sky and comes down to Earth.
  • This continuous journey is called the Water Cycle.

🎙️ Imagine water as a little explorer, always on the move! It travels from the earth to the sky and then back again. This continuous journey, like a big circle, is what we call the Water Cycle.

The Sun's Warm Hug

  • The sun is very powerful and warm.
  • It makes the water in rivers, lakes, and oceans warm.

🎙️ Our friendly sun plays a very important role. Just like the sun makes us feel warm, it also makes the water in big oceans, rivers, and even small puddles warm. This warmth is the first step in water's big adventure.

Evaporation - Water Goes Up!

  • When water gets warm, it turns into a gas.
  • This gas is called water vapour.
  • Water vapour is very light and invisible. It goes up into the sky.

🎙️ When water gets warm enough, something magical happens! It changes from liquid water into an invisible gas called water vapour. Think of it like water going 'poof!' and flying up into the sky. This process is called Evaporation.

Water moves in a never-ending cycle.

Wet Clothes Dry

  • When we hang wet clothes, they become dry.
  • The water from the clothes goes into the air.
  • This is an example of evaporation.

🎙️ Have you ever seen your mom hang wet clothes out in the sun? After some time, the clothes become dry. Where did all the water go? It evaporated! The sun's warmth turned the water into vapour, and it went up into the air.

Puddles Vanish!

  • After rain, puddles form on the road.
  • When the sun shines, the puddles disappear.
  • The water evaporates and goes up.

🎙️ Remember when it rains, and you see puddles everywhere? But if the sun comes out, the puddles slowly shrink and then disappear. That's evaporation at work again! The sun helps the water in the puddles turn into vapour and rise.

🏞️
Disappearing puddle
7 / 20

Condensation - Vapour Turns Cold

  • High up in the sky, it is very cold.
  • The warm water vapour gets cold there.
  • It turns back into tiny water droplets.

🎙️ Now, imagine our water vapour traveling very high up in the sky. It's like going to a very cold place! When the warm vapour meets the cold air, it gets chilly and changes back into tiny, tiny drops of water. This process is called Condensation.

☁️
8 / 20

Drops on a Cold Glass

  • Take a cold drink out of the fridge.
  • You see tiny water drops on the outside of the glass.
  • This is an example of condensation.

🎙️ Here's a fun way to see condensation! If you take a very cold glass of water out on a warm day, you'll soon see tiny water droplets forming on the outside of the glass. The water vapour in the air touched the cold glass and turned back into liquid water.

🥛

Clouds are Born!

  • Many tiny water drops come together.
  • They form big, fluffy clouds in the sky.

🎙️ When many, many of these tiny water droplets come together, they form what we see as clouds! So, clouds are not fluffy cotton candy; they are actually made of millions of tiny water droplets floating in the sky.

☁️☁️☁️
Clouds forming

Clouds Get Full

  • As more and more water drops join, the clouds get bigger.
  • They become heavy with a lot of water.

🎙️ Imagine a cloud like a sponge. As it collects more and more water droplets, it gets heavier and heavier. It's like the cloud is slowly filling up with water, waiting for the right moment.

⚖️
11 / 20

Precipitation - Water Falls Back

  • When clouds get too heavy, they cannot hold the water anymore.
  • The water falls back to Earth.
  • This is called precipitation.

🎙️ Finally, when the clouds become too heavy to hold all the water, they release it! The water falls back to the Earth in different forms. This falling of water from the sky is called Precipitation.

🌧️
12 / 20

Different Kinds of Precipitation

  • Water can fall as rain, snow, or hail.
  • We mostly see rain in our areas.

🎙️ Precipitation can be many things! Most often, we see it as rain. But in very cold places, it can fall as snow, which looks like white flakes. Sometimes, it can even fall as hard ice balls called hail. All these are forms of precipitation.

Water Collects on Earth

  • The fallen water collects in rivers, lakes, and oceans.
  • Some water also goes into the ground.

🎙️ Once the water falls back to Earth, it doesn't just disappear. It collects in big bodies of water like rivers, lakes, and the vast oceans. Some of it also soaks into the ground, becoming groundwater.

🌊

The Cycle Never Stops!

  • The collected water gets warm again.
  • It evaporates and goes up to the sky.
  • The journey starts all over again!

🎙️ And guess what? The water that collected in rivers and oceans gets warmed by the sun again! It evaporates, goes up, forms clouds, falls as rain, and collects again. This amazing cycle keeps going on and on, forever!

Recap: Our Water Cycle Journey

  • Evaporation: Water goes up to the sky.
  • Condensation: Clouds form from water vapour.
  • Precipitation: Water falls down as rain.
  • Collection: Water gathers in rivers and oceans.

🎙️ Let's quickly remember our journey! First, water goes up (Evaporation). Then, it forms clouds (Condensation). Next, it falls down as rain (Precipitation). And finally, it gathers in rivers and lakes (Collection). This is our amazing Water Cycle!

Why is the Water Cycle Important?

  • The water cycle gives us fresh water to drink.
  • It helps plants and trees grow all around us.
  • It keeps our Earth alive and green!

🎙️ Why is this cycle so important? Because it brings us fresh water to drink and helps all the plants and trees grow. Without the water cycle, life on Earth would not be possible! So, let's appreciate this wonderful cycle.
