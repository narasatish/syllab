Step 1: Evaporation - Water Becomes Vapour!

  • The sun heats water in rivers and lakes.
  • Water turns into a gas called vapour and goes up.

🎙️ Our water story begins with the sun! When the sun shines brightly, it heats up the water in rivers, lakes, and oceans. This heat makes the water change into a gas, like steam, which we call water vapour. This vapour is very light and floats up into the sky.

Water goes up!

Puddles Disappear!

  • After rain, puddles dry up.
  • The sun's heat makes water turn into vapour.

🎙️ Have you ever seen a puddle after it rains? After some time, the puddle disappears! Where did the water go? The sun's heat made it evaporate, turning into vapour and rising into the air. This is evaporation happening right before your eyes!

Wet Clothes Dry Up!

  • When we hang wet clothes, they dry.
  • Water evaporates and goes into the air.

🎙️ Think about your wet clothes after they are washed. When Mummy hangs them out in the sun, they become dry. The water from the clothes evaporates because of the sun's heat and the wind, leaving your clothes dry and fresh.

Step 2: Condensation - Vapour Becomes Water Again!

  • Vapour rises high in the sky.
  • It gets cold and turns back into tiny water drops.

🎙️ As the water vapour goes higher and higher in the sky, it gets very cold. When it gets cold, the vapour doesn't want to be a gas anymore! It changes back into tiny, tiny drops of water. This process is called condensation.

☁️
Clouds form

How Clouds Are Made!

  • Tiny water drops join together.
  • They form big, fluffy clouds.

🎙️ Those tiny water drops, formed from condensation, start to stick together. Many, many drops join hands and form what we see as clouds in the sky! So, clouds are actually collections of millions of tiny water droplets.

Water on a Cold Glass!

  • See tiny drops on a cold glass?
  • Air vapour touches the cold glass and turns to water.

🎙️ Here's a fun experiment you can do! Pour a cold drink into a glass. After a while, you'll see tiny water drops on the outside of the glass. This is condensation! The water vapour in the air touches the cold glass and turns into liquid water.

Step 3: Precipitation - Water Falls Down!

  • Clouds get too heavy with water drops.
  • Water falls back to Earth as rain, snow, or hail.

🎙️ When the clouds gather too many water drops, they become very, very heavy. They can't hold all the water anymore! So, the water falls back down to Earth. This falling water is called precipitation. It can be rain, snow, or even hail.

🎙️ The most common type of precipitation we see is rain. Rain is so important! It waters our farms, fills our rivers and lakes, and helps all the plants and trees grow. We love the sound and smell of fresh rain!

Other Ways Water Falls!

  • In very cold places, water falls as snow.
  • Sometimes, it falls as small ice balls called hail.

🎙️ While rain is common, in very cold places, the water drops freeze and fall as soft, white snow. Sometimes, if the clouds are very cold, water can fall as tiny, hard balls of ice, which we call hail. All these are forms of precipitation.

Step 4: Collection - Water Gathers!

  • Fallen water collects in rivers.
  • It flows into lakes and oceans.

🎙️ After the water falls back to Earth as rain or snow, it doesn't just stay there. It starts to collect! It flows over the land, forming small streams that join to become bigger rivers. These rivers then carry the water to lakes and the vast oceans.

Water Finds Its Home!

  • Rainwater flows into streams and rivers.
  • Eventually, it all goes back to the big oceans.

🎙️ So, all the water that falls down finds its way back to its 'home' in the big water bodies. From streams to rivers, and finally to the oceans, the water gathers, ready to start its journey all over again. The cycle never truly ends!

The Water Cycle Story!

  • Evaporation: Water goes up.
  • Condensation: Clouds form.
  • Precipitation: Water falls down.
  • Collection: Water gathers.

🎙️ Let's quickly remember the whole story of the water cycle. First, water goes up (Evaporation). Then, clouds are made (Condensation). Next, water falls down (Precipitation). And finally, water gathers (Collection). And then, the cycle starts again!
