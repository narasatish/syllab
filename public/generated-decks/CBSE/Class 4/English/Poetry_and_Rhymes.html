What is a Poem?

  • A poem is a special way to tell a story.
  • Or share feelings and ideas.
  • It uses words beautifully.

🎙️ Imagine you want to tell someone about a beautiful butterfly you saw. You can just say it, or you can say it in a way that makes them feel like they are seeing the butterfly too. That's what a poem does – it paints pictures with words.

  • It's the beat or musical flow of a poem.
  • Like a gentle drum in the background.

🎙️ Just like songs have a beat, poems also have a rhythm! It's how the words 'dance' together, some sounding stronger, some softer. It makes the poem enjoyable to read aloud.

Strong and Soft Beats

  • Some words or parts of words are said with a strong beat.
  • Others are said softly.

🎙️ When we read a poem, we naturally put a little more emphasis on some words than others. This creates the 'strong' and 'soft' beats, giving the poem its unique rhythm, just like a heartbeat.

What is a Stanza?

  • A group of lines in a poem.
  • Like a paragraph in a story.

🎙️ Poems are often divided into sections, just like a story has paragraphs. Each of these sections in a poem is called a stanza. It helps to organize the poem's ideas.

🧱

A Stanza is Like a Room!

  • Each stanza holds a part of the poem's idea.
  • Together, they build the whole poem.

🎙️ Imagine a house. It has many rooms, right? Each room has a different purpose. Similarly, each stanza in a poem is like a room, holding a specific thought or image, and together all the stanzas make the complete poem.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Thinking words that start with the same letter rhyme.
  • Example: 'Sun' and 'Star' do NOT rhyme.

🎙️ A common mistake many children make is thinking that if words start with the same letter, they rhyme. Remember, it's the *ending sound* that matters for rhyming, not the starting letter!

❌
Don't Confuse!

Exam Tips for Poetry!

  • Read the poem carefully.
  • Listen for ending sounds to find rhymes.
  • Count lines and stanzas.

🎙️ When you see a poem in your exam, don't rush! Read it slowly, maybe even whisper it to yourself to hear the sounds. This will help you find the rhyming words and understand the rhythm. Also, remember to count the lines and stanzas if asked.
