What is a Tense?

  • A tense shows the time when an action happens.
  • It tells us if the action is in the past, present, or future.

🎙️ So, a tense is simply the form of a verb that tells us about the time of an action. It's super important for making our sentences clear and understandable.

Past Tense: Action DONE

  • Past tense tells us about actions that have already finished.
  • Keywords: yesterday, last week, last year, a long time ago.

🎙️ When an action is complete and finished, we use the past tense. It's like the sand in the hourglass has already fallen to the bottom. It's done!

Present Tense: Action HAPPENING

  • Present tense tells us about actions happening now.
  • It also tells about actions that happen every day or are true facts.
  • Keywords: now, every day, always, usually.

🎙️ The present tense is for actions that are happening right now, or things that happen regularly, like the sun rising every morning. It's about what is active and current.

Future Tense: Action WILL HAPPEN

  • Future tense tells us about actions that will happen later.
  • We often use 'will' or 'shall' with the verb.
  • Keywords: tomorrow, next week, soon, later.

🎙️ When an action has not happened yet but is going to happen, we use the future tense. It's like looking into a crystal ball to see what's coming next! We usually add 'will' before the action word.

Common Mistakes with Tenses

  • Forgetting 'will' for future tense (e.g., 'I go tomorrow' instead of 'I *will go* tomorrow').
  • Using 'ed' for all past tense verbs (e.g., 'I *eated*' instead of 'I *ate*').
  • Confusing 'is/am/are' with 'was/were'.

🎙️ Sometimes we make small errors. Remember to use 'will' for future actions. Also, some past tense words are special and don't just add 'ed', like 'eat' becomes 'ate'. Practice makes perfect!

Exam Tips for Tenses

  • Look for keywords like 'yesterday', 'now', 'tomorrow'.
  • Practice changing verbs from one tense to another.
  • Read the sentence carefully to understand WHEN the action happens.

🎙️ In your exams, you might be asked to fill in the blanks or identify tenses. Always look for the time words in the sentence. They are your clues! And practice, practice, practice!

Recap: Our Time Travel Summary

  • Past Tense: Action already happened (e.g., *played*, *ate*).
  • Present Tense: Action happening now or regularly (e.g., *play*, *eat*).
  • Future Tense: Action will happen later (e.g., *will play*, *will eat*).
