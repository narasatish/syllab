Meet the Full Stop (.)

  • Imagine you are playing a game.
  • When the game ends, you stop!
  • A Full Stop is like the "stop" signal for a sentence.

🎙️ Just like a stop sign tells cars to stop, a full stop tells our sentences to stop. It's a tiny dot, but it has a big job!

🛑
Stop Sign
5 / 24

When to Use a Full Stop

  • We use a Full Stop at the end of a sentence.
  • It tells us that the thought is complete.
  • It looks like a tiny dot (.).

🎙️ Every time you finish telling something, you put a full stop. It's like saying 'The End' to that one idea. It helps us take a little breath before the next sentence.

⚫
Tiny Dot

Full Stop in Action!

  • My name is Rohan.
  • I love to play cricket.
  • The sun shines brightly.

🎙️ See how each sentence tells a complete idea? After 'Rohan', the idea of telling your name is over. After 'cricket', the idea of what you love is over. And after 'brightly', the idea about the sun is over. Each needs a full stop.

Meet the Comma (,)

  • A Comma is like taking a short breath.
  • It tells us to pause for a moment.
  • It looks like a tiny tail (,).

🎙️ Imagine you are running a race. You can't run non-stop, right? You need tiny breaks. A comma gives our sentences tiny breaks, short pauses.

🌬️
Short Breath
9 / 24

Comma for Lists!

  • We use commas to separate things in a list.
  • This makes the list easy to read.
  • Example: apples, bananas, grapes.

🎙️ When you go shopping with your parents and buy many things, like apples, bananas, and grapes, you use commas to tell them apart. It's like putting a little space between each item in your shopping basket!

🍎🍎🍎
Fruits
10 / 24

Comma for Pauses!

  • We also use commas for a short pause in a sentence.
  • This helps us understand it better.
  • Example: Yes, I will come.

🎙️ Sometimes, we say something and then add a little extra information, or we just need a small break in the sentence. That's when the comma helps us. It's a tiny pause, not a full stop.

⏸️
Pause

Comma in Action!

  • I have a red, blue, and green pencil.
  • My favourite snacks are chips, biscuits, and chocolates.

🎙️ Look at the first sentence. We are listing the colours of the pencil. Red, blue, and green. The commas separate them. In the second sentence, we are listing snacks: chips, biscuits, and chocolates. The commas help us read the list clearly.

Meet the Question Mark (?)

  • A Question Mark is like asking for directions.
  • It tells you that someone is asking something.
  • It looks like a hook with a dot (?).

🎙️ When you don't know something, what do you do? You ask a question! This special mark tells us that the sentence is asking for information.

❓
Question Mark
14 / 24

When to Use a Question Mark

  • We use a Question Mark at the end of a question.
  • It shows that you are asking something.
  • Examples: What is your name? Are you happy?

🎙️ If a sentence starts with words like 'What,' 'Where,' 'When,' 'Why,' 'How,' or 'Are,' it's usually a question! And at the end of every question, we put a question mark.

🤔

Meet the Exclamation Mark (!)

  • An Exclamation Mark is like shouting with joy!
  • It shows strong feelings or surprise.
  • It looks like a straight line with a dot (!).

🎙️ Imagine you score a goal in football! How do you feel? You feel super excited, right? This mark helps us show those strong feelings in writing.

🤩
Wow!
18 / 24

When to Use an Exclamation Mark

  • We use an Exclamation Mark at the end of a sentence that shows strong emotion.
  • It can be joy, surprise, anger, or excitement!

🎙️ When you are very happy, or very surprised, or even a little bit scared, you use this mark. It makes your sentence sound loud and full of feeling!

Exclamation Mark in Action!

  • Hurray! We won the match!
  • What a beautiful flower!
  • Oh no! I dropped my ice cream!

🎙️ Look at these sentences! 'Hurray! We won the match!' shows great joy. 'What a beautiful flower!' shows surprise and admiration. 'Oh no! I dropped my ice cream!' shows sadness or disappointment. All strong feelings need an exclamation mark.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting full stops at the end.
  • Mixing up question marks and exclamation marks.
  • Not using commas in lists.

🎙️ It's easy to make small mistakes, but now you know! Always check if your sentence is a statement, a question, or showing strong feeling. And remember those tiny pauses for commas!
