What are Fractions?

  • It's about sharing equally.
  • Like dividing a cake.

🎙️ Imagine you have a yummy cake. If you want to share it with your friend, how would you do it? You would cut it into equal pieces, right? That's what fractions help us do!

A pizza cut into slices shows fractions — each slice is a part of the whole!

Making Equal Parts

  • Each piece must be the same size.
  • No fighting over bigger pieces!

🎙️ When we talk about fractions, it's super important that all the pieces are exactly the same size. If you cut a cake into two parts for you and your friend, both parts should be equal. This is called 'half'.

More Equal Parts

  • We can cut into many equal parts.
  • Like 4 parts, or 8 parts.

🎙️ You can cut the cake into more equal parts too! Maybe you have three friends, so you cut it into four equal pieces. Each piece is now one-fourth of the whole cake.

Numerator and Denominator

  • Top number: how many parts you have.
  • Bottom number: total equal parts.

🎙️ Fractions have two special numbers. The 'Numerator' is the number on top, telling us how many pieces we are looking at. The 'Denominator' is the number at the bottom, telling us how many total equal pieces the whole thing was cut into.

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

Example: Half (1/2)

  • You have 1 part out of 2 total parts.
  • Written as 1/2.

🎙️ If you cut an apple into two equal parts and take one part, you have 'one-half' of the apple. We write this as 1/2. Here, 1 is the numerator and 2 is the denominator.

🎙️ Imagine a big chocolate bar cut into four equal pieces. If you get one piece, you have 'one-quarter' of the chocolate. We write this as 1/4. Here, 1 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator.

🎙️ It's easy to write fractions! Just remember: the number of parts you have goes on top, then a small line, and then the total number of equal parts goes at the bottom. Try writing 3/4 now!

  • We share food all the time!
  • Like pizzas, sandwiches, and fruits.

🎙️ Think about your lunchbox! If you share your sandwich with a friend, you are using fractions. You give them half, and you keep half. Or if you cut a pizza into 8 slices and eat 2, you've eaten 2/8 of the pizza.

Real-life Fractions 2: Time

  • Clocks use fractions too.
  • Half past, quarter past.

🎙️ Look at a clock! When the big hand is on the 6, we say 'half past' the hour. That means half of the hour has passed. When it's on the 3, it's 'quarter past' because a quarter of the hour has passed.

What are Decimals?

  • Another way to show parts.
  • Especially parts of 10 or 100.

🎙️ Now let's meet decimals! Decimals are like cousins to fractions. They also help us talk about parts of a whole, but they are super good at showing parts when something is divided into 10 or 100 equal pieces.

Tenths - Small Parts

  • Imagine dividing into 10 equal parts.
  • Each part is one-tenth.

🎙️ Think of a chocolate bar that has 10 small squares. If you eat one square, you've eaten one-tenth of the bar. If you eat three squares, you've eaten three-tenths. Decimals help us write these easily.

The Decimal Point

  • It's a tiny dot in numbers.
  • It separates whole numbers from parts.

🎙️ Decimals have a special friend called the 'decimal point'. It's a small dot that tells us 'everything after me is a part of a whole'. For example, in 2.5, the 2 is a whole number, and the 5 is a part.

⚫
A small dot

Writing Decimals

  • 0.1 means one-tenth.
  • 0.5 means five-tenths (or half!).

🎙️ To write one-tenth, we write '0.1'. The '0' means no whole numbers, and the '1' after the point means one part out of ten. If you have five-tenths, you write '0.5'.

Real-life Decimals 1: Money

  • Rupees and Paisa use decimals.
  • Rs 5.50 means 5 rupees and 50 paisa.

🎙️ We use decimals every day when we talk about money! For example, if something costs 'five rupees and fifty paisa', we write it as 'Rs 5.50'. The decimal point separates the rupees from the paisa.

Learn money using real coins.

Real-life Decimals 2: Measuring Length

  • Metres and centimetres use decimals.
  • 1.5 metres means 1 metre and 50 centimetres.

🎙️ When you measure things, like your height or the length of your desk, you often use decimals. A measurement like '1.5 metres' means 1 whole metre and 5 parts out of 10, or 50 centimetres.

Fractions and Decimals are Friends!

  • They both show parts of a whole.
  • We can change fractions to decimals!

🎙️ Fractions and decimals are like two different ways to say the same thing – parts of a whole! For example, one-half (1/2) is the same as 0.5. Isn't that cool? They are just written differently.
