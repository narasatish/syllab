What is a Fraction?

  • It's a part of a whole thing.
  • When we cut something into equal pieces, each piece is a fraction.

🎙️ Imagine you have a yummy pizza. If you cut it into pieces to share with friends, each piece is a fraction of the whole pizza. A fraction tells us how many parts we have out of the total parts.

A pizza cut into slices shows fractions — each slice is a part of the whole!

Sharing a Pizza

  • Mom cuts the pizza into 8 equal slices.
  • You eat 1 slice. That's 1 out of 8 slices.

🎙️ See this pizza? If it has 8 equal slices, and you take one, you've taken one-eighth of the pizza. We write this as 1/8. It's like a story of sharing!

Numerator and Denominator

  • The top number (Numerator) tells us how many parts we have.
  • The bottom number (Denominator) tells us how many equal parts are in the whole.

🎙️ Every fraction has two special numbers. The number on top is called the 'Numerator' – it counts the parts we are looking at. The number at the bottom is the 'Denominator' – it tells us how many total equal parts make up the whole thing.

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

An Apple Story

  • Rani cuts an apple into 2 equal halves.
  • Each half is 1 out of 2 parts, written as 1/2.

🎙️ Let's take an apple. If Rani cuts it into two equal pieces, each piece is one half. We write this as 1/2. The '1' tells us we have one piece, and the '2' tells us there were two equal pieces in total.

Writing Fractions

  • We use a line between the numerator and denominator.
  • Like 1/2 (one-half) or 3/4 (three-fourths).

🎙️ It's easy to write fractions! We just put the number of parts we have on top, draw a small line, and then put the total number of equal parts at the bottom. This line means 'out of' or 'divided by'.

What is a Decimal?

  • Decimals are another way to show parts of a whole.
  • They are used for very small parts, like when you measure length.

🎙️ Now, let's meet decimals! Decimals are like cousins to fractions. They also help us talk about parts of a whole, especially when we divide things into 10, 100, or 1000 equal parts. Think of zooming in very close!

🔍
Zooming into small parts

The Decimal Point

  • A decimal uses a special dot called a 'decimal point'.
  • It separates the whole number part from the fractional part.

🎙️ The most important thing in a decimal number is a tiny dot called the 'decimal point'. It's like a fence that separates the whole number (like 1, 2, 3) from the small parts (like 0.5, 0.25). Everything after the dot is less than one whole.

Tenths

  • When we divide something into 10 equal parts, each part is a 'tenth'.
  • We write one-tenth as 0.1.

🎙️ Imagine a long strip divided into 10 small, equal squares. If you colour one square, you have coloured one-tenth of the strip. We write this as 0.1. The '1' after the decimal point means one-tenth.

Using a Ruler

  • Look at your ruler! It has big lines for centimetres.
  • Between centimetres, there are 10 small lines for millimetres, which are tenths.

🎙️ Do you use a ruler in school? The small markings between two big numbers on your ruler show tenths of a centimetre. For example, 3.5 cm means 3 whole centimetres and 5 tenths of another centimetre. Decimals help us measure things very precisely!

A clock helps us read hours and minutes.

Writing Decimals

  • We write the whole number, then the decimal point, then the fractional part.
  • Example: 0.5 (zero point five) for one-half.

🎙️ Writing decimals is also simple. First, write any whole numbers you have. Then, put the decimal point. After the point, write the numbers for the parts. For example, if you have half of something, you can write it as 0.5.

Comparing Fractions

  • Which slice of cake is bigger: 1/2 or 1/4?
  • 1/2 is bigger because it's a larger part of the same cake!

🎙️ Sometimes we need to know which fraction is bigger. Imagine two cakes of the same size. If you get 1/2 of one cake and your friend gets 1/4 of the other, who gets more? You do! Because 1/2 is a bigger piece than 1/4.

Comparing Decimals

  • Which is longer: 0.5 metres or 0.3 metres?
  • 0.5 metres is longer because 5 tenths is more than 3 tenths.

🎙️ Comparing decimals is similar. If you have a rope that is 0.5 metres long and another that is 0.3 metres long, which one is longer? The 0.5-metre rope is longer. Just look at the number after the decimal point – a bigger number means a bigger part.

Fractions and Decimals are Friends!

  • Fractions and decimals can show the same amount.
  • 1/2 is the same as 0.5.

🎙️ Guess what? Fractions and decimals are like two different ways to say the same thing! For example, if you have half of an apple (1/2), you can also say you have 0.5 of the apple. They both mean the same amount!

Money Matters

  • 1 Rupee has 100 Paise.
  • 50 Paise is 50/100 of a Rupee, which is 0.50 Rupees.

🎙️ We use decimals every day when we talk about money! One rupee has 100 paise. So, 50 paise is half of a rupee, which we write as 0.50 rupees. It's a perfect example of decimals in our daily life!

What We Learned Today

  • Fractions are parts of a whole (like 1/2).
  • Decimals are another way to show parts, using a decimal point (like 0.5).
  • They both help us understand sharing and small measurements.

🎙️ Wow, we learned so much today! We discovered fractions, which are about dividing things into equal parts. And we met decimals, which are special numbers with a dot to show smaller parts. Both help us understand the world around us better!
