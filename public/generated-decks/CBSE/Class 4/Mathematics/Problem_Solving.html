Step 1: Read Carefully

  • Read the whole question slowly.
  • Understand what the story is about.

🎙️ The very first step is super important: read the problem carefully. Don't rush! Understand the story it's telling and what characters or things are involved.

Step 2: Spot the Numbers!

  • Find all the numbers in the story.
  • Circle them or write them down.

🎙️ After reading, look for all the numbers given in the problem. These numbers are like clues! Write them down so you don't forget them.

Step 3: What's the Question?

  • Find out what you need to calculate.
  • What is the problem asking you to find?

🎙️ This step is about understanding what the problem wants from you. Is it asking for a total, or how many are left, or how many in each group? The last sentence often tells you this.

  • Choose the right math operation.
  • Will you Add, Subtract, Multiply, or Divide?

🎙️ Now that you know the numbers and the question, you need to pick the correct math tool. These tools are addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Each tool helps with a different kind of problem.

When to ADD (+)

  • When you need to find the 'total' or 'in all'.
  • When things are 'put together'.

🎙️ We use addition when we want to combine things or find a total. Look for words like 'altogether', 'sum', 'total', or 'in all'. It means things are joining up!

➕
Plus sign

When to SUBTRACT (-)

  • When you need to find 'how many left'.
  • When you find the 'difference' or 'take away'.

🎙️ Subtraction is used when you take away things or want to find how many are remaining. Words like 'left', 'difference', 'take away', or 'how many more' are clues for subtraction.

➖
Minus sign

When to MULTIPLY (x)

  • When you have 'groups of' the same thing.
  • It's like fast addition of the same number.

🎙️ Multiplication is useful when you have several groups of the same size and want to find the total. If you see words like 'times', 'product', or 'groups of', think multiplication.

✖️
Multiplication sign

When to DIVIDE (÷)

  • When you 'share equally' into groups.
  • When you find 'how many in each group'.

🎙️ Division is for sharing things equally or splitting a big group into smaller, equal parts. Look for words like 'share equally', 'distribute', or 'how many in each'.

Example: Sharing Candies (Addition)

  • Ria has 3 candies. Her friend gives her 2 more.
  • How many candies does Ria have 'in all'?

🎙️ Here's an example! Ria has 3 candies, and gets 2 more. The words 'in all' tell us to add. So, 3 + 2 = 5 candies. Ria has 5 candies in all.

Example: Balloons Fly Away (Subtraction)

  • There were 7 balloons. 3 balloons flew away.
  • How many balloons are 'left'?

🎙️ Let's try another one. You had 7 balloons, but 3 flew away. The word 'left' tells us to subtract. So, 7 - 3 = 4 balloons. There are 4 balloons left.
