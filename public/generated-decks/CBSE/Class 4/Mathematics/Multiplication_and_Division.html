Division: Subtracting Again and Again!

  • Division is also like taking away the same number repeatedly.
  • How many times can you take 3 from 9?
  • 9 - 3 = 6, 6 - 3 = 3, 3 - 3 = 0. That's 3 times!
  • So, 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

🎙️ Just like multiplication is repeated addition, division is repeated subtraction. We keep taking away the same number until we reach zero or can't subtract anymore. It tells us how many groups we can make.

Division with a Little Leftover: Remainder!

  • Sometimes, when we share, there's a little bit left over.
  • This leftover is called the Remainder.
  • Example: 7 ÷ 2 = 3 with 1 left over. (R=1)

🎙️ What if you have 7 cookies and want to share them equally between 2 friends? Each friend gets 3 cookies, but one cookie is left. That one cookie is the remainder! It's the part that couldn't be shared equally.

Let's Divide: 17 ÷ 5

  • Step 1: How many 5s are in 17?
  • 5 x 3 = 15 (closest without going over)
  • Step 2: 17 - 15 = 2. This is the remainder.
  • Answer: 3 with Remainder 2.

🎙️ Now, what if the number doesn't divide perfectly? We find the closest multiple without going over, and whatever is left is our remainder. It's like sharing, but with a few things left in your hand!

Fact Families: Math's Own Family!

  • A fact family uses the same three numbers.
  • Example: Numbers 3, 4, 12.
  • 3 x 4 = 12, 4 x 3 = 12
  • 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 12 ÷ 4 = 3

🎙️ Just like your own family, numbers can form families too! A fact family shows how three numbers are related through multiplication and division. It makes solving problems even easier!
