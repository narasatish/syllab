Reading the Clock: Hours

  • The short hand shows the hour.
  • It moves slowly, from one big number to the next.

🎙️ Look at the clock! The short hand is called the hour hand. It points to the big numbers to tell us the hour. When the short hand is on 3, it's 3 o'clock. It takes one whole hour to move from one number to the next.

Reading the Clock: Minutes

  • The long hand shows the minutes.
  • Each big number is 5 minutes.

🎙️ Now, see the long hand? That's the minute hand! It moves faster than the hour hand. When it points to 1, it's 5 minutes past the hour. When it points to 2, it's 10 minutes past, and so on. Each big number represents 5 minutes.

⏱️⏱️⏱️⏱️⏱️
Minute Hand
7 / 24

Reading the Clock: Seconds

  • The very thin, fast hand shows seconds.
  • 60 seconds make 1 minute.

🎙️ Some clocks have a very thin hand that moves super fast! That's the second hand. It helps us measure very short periods of time. Every time it completes one full round, 60 seconds have passed, which means one minute has gone by.

Calendar: Months and Years

  • There are 12 months in one year.
  • A year has 365 days (sometimes 366 for a leap year).

🎙️ Just like weeks, months also have names, and there are 12 of them in a year! January, February, March... all the way to December. A whole year has 365 days, but sometimes, every four years, we get an extra day in February, making it 366 days! That's called a leap year.

Adding Money: Buying Together

  • If you buy two things, you add their prices.
  • Example: A pencil costs ₹5, an eraser costs ₹3.

🎙️ When you want to buy more than one thing, you add their prices to find out the total cost. For example, if a pencil costs 5 rupees and an eraser costs 3 rupees, you add 5 and 3 to get 8 rupees. You need 8 rupees in total!

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

Subtracting Money: Making Change

  • When you pay more, you get some money back.
  • Example: You pay ₹10 for a ₹7 chocolate.

🎙️ Sometimes, you give the shopkeeper more money than the item costs. The money you get back is called 'change'. If you give 10 rupees for a 7-rupee chocolate, the shopkeeper will subtract 7 from 10 and give you 3 rupees back as change.

1Paid: ₹10
2Chocolate: ₹7
3Change: ₹10 - ₹7 = ₹3

Measurement: Length (cm, m, km)

  • Centimeters (cm) for small things like pencils.
  • Meters (m) for bigger things like a room.
  • Kilometers (km) for very long distances, like between cities.

🎙️ When we measure how long something is, we use units of length. For small things like your pencil, we use centimeters. For the length of your classroom, we use meters. And for the distance from your home to another city, we use kilometers!

Measurement: Weight (g, kg)

  • Grams (g) for light things like a chocolate bar.
  • Kilograms (kg) for heavier things like a bag of rice.

🎙️ To know how heavy something is, we use units of weight. For something very light, like a small chili or a chocolate bar, we use grams. But for heavier things, like a big bag of rice or a watermelon, we use kilograms.

Measurement: Capacity (ml, l)

  • Milliliters (ml) for small amounts of liquid, like medicine.
  • Liters (l) for larger amounts, like milk or water in a bottle.

🎙️ Capacity tells us how much liquid a container can hold. For a tiny amount, like a spoon of cough syrup, we use milliliters. For a big bottle of water or a carton of milk, we use liters. Remember these units when you're pouring liquids!

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing AM and PM for time.
  • Not aligning decimal points when adding/subtracting money.
  • Using wrong units for measurement (e.g., cm instead of m).

🎙️ Even grown-ups make mistakes sometimes! Here are some common errors to watch out for. Always remember that AM is for morning and PM is for afternoon/night. Be careful with decimal points in money, and always choose the correct unit for measuring length, weight, or capacity.
