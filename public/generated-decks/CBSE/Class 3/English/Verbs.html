  • These are 'Be-Verbs': IS, AM, ARE.
  • They tell us about the state or condition of someone or something.

🎙️ Sometimes, verbs don't show an action, but they tell us what someone or something 'is' or 'are'. These are very special verbs called 'Be-verbs'.

Using IS, AM, ARE

  • I AM a student.
  • He IS happy.
  • They ARE friends.

🎙️ We use 'am' with 'I'. We use 'is' with one person or thing (like 'he', 'she', 'it'). We use 'are' with many people or things (like 'they', 'we', 'you').

  • She HAS a beautiful doll.
  • We HAVE many toys.

🎙️ We use 'have' with 'I', 'you', 'we', 'they', and plural nouns. We use 'has' with 'he', 'she', 'it', and singular nouns. Remember this rule carefully!

20 / 24

Don't make these mistakes!

  • Forgetting the verb: 'The bird in the sky.' (Needs 'flies')
  • Confusing 'is' and 'are': 'He are happy.' (Should be 'He is happy.')
  • Confusing 'has' and 'have': 'She have a doll.' (Should be 'She has a doll.')

🎙️ It's easy to make small mistakes, but we can learn from them! Always check if your sentence has a verb and if you are using 'is/am/are' and 'has/have' correctly with singular and plural subjects.

⚠️
Watch out!

How Verbs are asked in exams

  • 1. Identify the verb in the given sentence.
  • 2. Fill in the blanks with the correct verb (e.g., is/are, has/have).
  • 3. Change the verb to past or future tense (simple forms).

🎙️ In your exams, you might be asked to find the verb, or choose the right verb to complete a sentence. Pay attention to whether the subject is one or many, and when the action happens.
