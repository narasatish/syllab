  • They are like a dark copy of an object.

🎙️ Now, let's talk about those mysterious dark shapes! Shadows are formed when something stands in the way of light. Imagine you are playing in the sun. Your body blocks the sunlight, and behind you, a dark patch appears on the ground. That's your shadow!

How Are Shadows Formed?

  • You need a source of light (like the sun or a lamp).
  • You need an object that blocks the light.
  • You need a surface (like the ground or a wall) for the shadow to fall on.

🎙️ For a shadow to form, three things must be present. First, you need light, like from the sun or a torch. Second, you need something to block that light, like your hand or a book. Third, you need a surface, such as the ground or a wall, for the shadow to appear on. If any of these are missing, no shadow will form!

Opaque Objects Make Clear Shadows

  • Objects that do not let any light pass through them are called opaque.
  • They make clear and dark shadows.
  • Examples: a book, a wall, your body.

🎙️ Some objects are very good at blocking light. We call them opaque objects. Think of a brick wall or a thick book. When light hits them, it cannot pass through, so a very clear and dark shadow is formed behind them. Your own body is opaque, which is why you have a shadow!

🧱🚫💡

11 / 24

Transparent Objects Make No Shadows

  • Objects that let all light pass through them are called transparent.
  • They do not make any shadow.
  • Examples: clear glass, clean water, air.

🎙️ Now, think about a clear glass window or a bottle of clean water. Light can pass right through them! Objects that allow all light to pass are called transparent. Since they don't block the light, they don't form any shadows. It's like the light doesn't even know they are there!

🥛✅💡
Glass lets light pass
12 / 24

Translucent Objects Make Faint Shadows

  • Objects that let only some light pass through them are called translucent.
  • They make faint or blurry shadows.
  • Examples: butter paper, frosted glass.

🎙️ There are some objects that are in between opaque and transparent. They let some light pass through, but not all of it. We call them translucent objects. Think of a piece of butter paper or a frosted bathroom window. They make shadows that are not very dark or clear, but rather faint and blurry.

How Shadow Size Changes

  • When an object is close to the light source, its shadow is big.
  • When an object is far from the light source, its shadow is small.

🎙️ This is a fun trick! If you hold your hand very close to a torch, you will see a huge shadow on the wall. But if you move your hand far away from the torch, the shadow becomes much smaller. So, the distance between the object and the light source changes the shadow's size!

Shadow's Direction

  • Your shadow is always on the opposite side of the light.
  • If the sun is in front of you, your shadow is behind you.

🎙️ Another interesting thing about shadows is their direction. They always appear on the side opposite to the light source. If the sun is shining from your right, your shadow will be on your left. If the sun is behind you, your shadow will be in front of you. Try it out!

Shadows During the Day

  • In the morning, the sun is low, so shadows are long.
  • At noon, the sun is right above, so shadows are short.
  • In the evening, the sun is low again, making long shadows.

🎙️ Have you noticed your shadow changing throughout the day? In the morning, when the sun is low in the sky, your shadow is very long. At noon, when the sun is directly above your head, your shadow is the shortest. And in the evening, as the sun goes down, your shadow becomes long again. It's like nature's clock!

Common Mistakes About Shadows

  • Thinking shadows are actual objects you can touch.
  • Confusing transparent (no shadow) with translucent (faint shadow).
  • Forgetting that light travels in straight lines.

🎙️ Sometimes, students make a few common mistakes about shadows. Remember, shadows are not solid things you can pick up; they are just areas where light is blocked. Also, don't mix up transparent objects, which let all light pass, with translucent objects, which let only some light pass. And always remember, light travels straight!
