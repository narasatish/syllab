Sharing Things Equally

  • You want to share it with your friend.
  • How do you make sure both get the same amount?

🎙️ When we share something, like this delicious cake, it's important that everyone gets an equal piece. No one should get more or less. This idea of 'equal sharing' is the beginning of fractions.

Whole and Its Parts

  • A 'whole' is one full thing.
  • When we cut it into pieces, those are 'parts'.
  • Fractions help us talk about these parts.

🎙️ Think of a whole pizza, like the one you see. It's one complete thing. If we cut it, each slice becomes a part of that whole pizza. Fractions give names to these parts.

Cutting into Equal Pieces

  • Fractions are only for EQUAL parts.
  • Each part must be exactly the same size.
  • Like cutting a sandwich fairly!

🎙️ This is very important! When we talk about fractions, all the pieces must be equal in size. If you cut a sandwich for two friends, both pieces should be the same size, right? That's what 'equal parts' means.

Names for Our Parts

  • Fractions have two special numbers.
  • One tells us how many parts we are looking at.
  • The other tells us how many equal parts the whole has.

🎙️ Just like you have a name, the numbers in a fraction also have names. These names help us understand what part of the whole we are talking about. Let's learn these two important names.

Denominator: Total Equal Parts

  • The bottom number is called the DENOMINATOR.
  • It tells us the total number of equal pieces.
  • How many slices is the whole cake cut into?

🎙️ The denominator is like the total number of friends you are sharing with, or the total number of pieces you cut your food into. If you cut a cookie into 4 equal pieces, the denominator is 4.

Numerator: Parts We Choose

  • The top number is called the NUMERATOR.
  • It tells us how many parts we are talking about.
  • How many slices did you eat?

🎙️ The numerator is the number of pieces you take, or the number of pieces you are pointing to. If you take 1 piece from the cookie that had 4 pieces, then the numerator is 1. It's the 'part of interest'.

Sharing Half: One Out of Two (1/2)

  • When you divide something into 2 equal parts.
  • Each part is called 'half' or 'one-half'.
  • We write it as 1/2.

🎙️ If you cut an apple into two equal pieces, each piece is a half. You get one half, and your friend gets the other half. So, 1 out of 2 equal parts is 1/2.

One-Third: One Out of Three (1/3)

  • When you divide something into 3 equal parts.
  • Each part is called 'one-third'.
  • We write it as 1/3.

🎙️ Imagine sharing a cake with two friends, so there are three of you. If you cut the cake into 3 equal slices, each person gets one-third of the cake. That's 1 out of 3 equal parts.

One-Fourth: One Out of Four (1/4)

  • When you divide something into 4 equal parts.
  • Each part is called 'one-fourth' or 'a quarter'.
  • We write it as 1/4.

🎙️ If you cut an apple into four equal pieces, each piece is one-fourth. Like cutting a pizza into four slices. Each slice is 1/4 of the whole pizza. We also call it a quarter.

Pizza Time! Fractions on Your Plate

  • A whole pizza has 8 slices.
  • If you eat 2 slices, you eat 2/8 of the pizza.
  • Your friend eats 3 slices, that's 3/8.

🎙️ Pizza is a great example of fractions! Each slice is an equal part. If there are 8 slices in total, the denominator is 8. If you eat 2 slices, the numerator is 2, so you ate 2/8 of the pizza.

Sharing a Chocolate Bar

  • A chocolate bar has 4 equal squares.
  • If you break off 1 square, you have 1/4 of the bar.
  • If you break off 2 squares, you have 2/4.

🎙️ Think of a chocolate bar with clear lines for breaking. Each square is an equal part. If there are 4 squares in total, the denominator is 4. If you eat 1 square, you ate 1/4 of the chocolate bar.

How to Write Fractions

  • We write fractions like this: Numerator / Denominator.
  • The top number is what we have.
  • The bottom number is the total.

🎙️ Always remember, the number of parts you are interested in goes on top, and the total number of equal parts goes at the bottom, with a line in between. For example, 1/2, 1/3, 2/4.

Fractions on a Number Line

  • We can also show fractions on a number line.

What We Learned Today!
