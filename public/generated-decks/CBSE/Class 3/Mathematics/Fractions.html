What are Fractions?

  • Fractions tell us about parts of a whole.
  • When we cut something into equal pieces.
  • Each piece is a 'fraction' of the whole.

🎙️ Imagine you have a yummy pizza. If you cut it into pieces, each piece is a part of that whole pizza. Fractions help us talk about these parts. They are special numbers that show how much of something we have.

A pizza cut into slices shows fractions — each slice is a part of the whole!

Sharing a Pizza Equally

  • Mom brought a pizza for you and your friend.
  • How can you share it so both get the same?
  • You cut it into two 'equal' parts!

🎙️ Think about sharing. If you and your friend want to eat a pizza, you'd want to make sure you both get the same amount, right? So, you cut the pizza exactly in the middle. Now you have two pieces, and each piece is a part of the whole pizza.

Equal Parts are Important!

  • Fractions are always about 'equal' parts.
  • The parts must be exactly the same size.
  • If parts are not equal, it's not a fraction.

🎙️ This is a very important rule! For something to be a fraction, all the pieces must be the same size. Like when you share sweets, you make sure everyone gets the same number. If one part is big and another is small, we cannot use fractions to describe them.

The Numerator: How Many Parts Do We Have?

  • The top number in a fraction is the Numerator.
  • It tells us how many parts we are looking at.
  • It's the 'number of parts taken'.

🎙️ Let's learn about the parts of a fraction. The number written on top is called the Numerator. It tells us how many pieces we are focusing on or how many pieces we have picked up. Remember, 'N' for Numerator and 'N' for 'Number of parts'.

The Denominator: Total Equal Parts

  • The bottom number is the Denominator.
  • It tells us the total number of equal parts.
  • It's the 'total number of parts in the whole'.

🎙️ Now, the number at the bottom is called the Denominator. It tells us how many equal pieces the whole thing was cut into in total. So, if you cut a cake into 4 pieces, the denominator would be 4. Remember, 'D' for Denominator and 'D' for 'Down' and 'Total'.

Writing Fractions: Numerator over Denominator

  • We write fractions like this: Numerator / Denominator.
  • A line separates the two numbers.
  • Example: 1/2 means 1 part out of 2 total parts.

🎙️ When we write a fraction, we put the Numerator on top, draw a small line, and then put the Denominator below it. This line means 'out of' or 'divided by'. So, 1/2 means one part out of two equal parts.

Let's See 'Half' (1/2)

  • When something is cut into 2 equal parts.
  • Each part is called 'one-half' or 1/2.
  • Numerator is 1, Denominator is 2.

🎙️ Imagine an apple cut into two equal pieces. If you take one piece, you have 'one-half' of the apple. We write this as 1/2. Here, 1 is the part you took, and 2 is the total number of equal parts the apple was cut into.

Understanding 1/2: How many halves make a whole?

  • One whole thing has two halves.
  • 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 (a whole).
  • Like two half-rotis make one full roti.

🎙️ If you have a sandwich and cut it into two equal halves, you have two pieces. If you put those two pieces back together, you get the whole sandwich again. So, two halves always make one whole.

Understanding 1/3: How many thirds make a whole?

  • Like three parts of a flag make a whole flag.

🎙️ Our Indian flag has three equal coloured stripes! Each stripe is one-third of the flag. If you put all three stripes together, you get the whole flag. So, three one-thirds make one whole.

Writing Fractions for Shaded Parts

  • Numerator = number of shaded parts.
  • Denominator = total number of equal parts.
  • Example: If 2 out of 4 parts are shaded, it's 2/4.

🎙️ Now that you know what a numerator and denominator are, you can write any fraction! Just count the parts that are coloured or taken, that's your numerator. Then count all the equal parts, that's your denominator. Put them together with a line in between.

Recap: What We Learned About Fractions!

  • Fractions are parts of a whole.
  • Parts must always be equal.
  • Numerator = parts we take (top number).
  • Denominator = total equal parts (bottom number).
