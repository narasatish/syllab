Meet Numerator and Denominator

  • A fraction has two special numbers.
  • The top number tells us how many parts we have.
  • The bottom number tells us how many equal parts make the whole.

🎙️ Every fraction has two numbers, one on top and one at the bottom, separated by a line. The top number is called the 'Numerator'. It tells us how many parts we are talking about or have. The bottom number is called the 'Denominator'. It tells us the total number of equal parts the whole thing was divided into.

One-half (1/2)

  • When something is divided into 2 equal parts, each part is one-half.
  • Half of a chocolate bar is 1/2.

🎙️ If you cut something into two exactly equal pieces, each piece is called 'one-half'. We write it as 1/2. For example, if you break a chocolate bar exactly in the middle, each piece is one-half of the chocolate bar. It means 1 part out of 2 total equal parts.

How to Find Fractions of a Group

  • Step 1: Divide the total number by the denominator.
  • Step 2: Multiply that answer by the numerator.

🎙️ Here are two simple steps to find a fraction of a group. First, take the total number of items in the group and divide it by the denominator of the fraction. This tells you the size of one 'part'. Second, take that answer and multiply it by the numerator. This tells you how many of those parts you have. Let's try an example!

1Total ÷ Denominator
2Answer × Numerator

Example: 1/2 of 8 Balloons

  • Total balloons = 8.
  • Denominator = 2. So, 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
  • Numerator = 1. So, 4 × 1 = 4.
  • 1/2 of 8 balloons is 4 balloons!

🎙️ Let's say you have 8 balloons, and you want to find out what is 1/2 of them. Following our steps: First, divide the total (8 balloons) by the denominator (2). 8 divided by 2 is 4. Second, multiply that answer (4) by the numerator (1). 4 multiplied by 1 is 4. So, 1/2 of 8 balloons is 4 balloons!

Example: 1/3 of 9 Flowers

  • Total flowers = 9.
  • Denominator = 3. So, 9 ÷ 3 = 3.
  • Numerator = 1. So, 3 × 1 = 3.
  • 1/3 of 9 flowers is 3 flowers!

🎙️ Here's another example. You have 9 beautiful flowers, and you want to give 1/3 of them to your teacher. First, divide the total number of flowers (9) by the denominator (3). 9 divided by 3 is 3. Then, multiply that answer (3) by the numerator (1). 3 multiplied by 1 is 3. So, 1/3 of 9 flowers is 3 flowers. You would give 3 flowers to your teacher.

Comparing Fractions

  • Which piece is bigger: 1/2 of a cake or 1/4 of the same cake?
  • The smaller the denominator, the bigger the part (if numerator is 1).

🎙️ Sometimes you might need to compare fractions and see which one is bigger. Look at the visual. If you cut a cake into 2 equal pieces (1/2), those pieces are much bigger than if you cut the same cake into 4 equal pieces (1/4). So, for fractions with 1 as the numerator, the smaller the denominator, the bigger the fraction. 1/2 is bigger than 1/4.

Common Mistakes

  • Mistake 1: Not making equal parts when drawing fractions.
  • Mistake 2: Confusing numerator and denominator.
  • Mistake 3: Thinking 1/4 is bigger than 1/2.

🎙️ It's okay to make mistakes, but let's learn from common ones! Always remember that for fractions, parts must be equal. Don't mix up the numerator (top, parts you have) and denominator (bottom, total parts). And remember, 1/2 is a bigger piece than 1/4, because when you divide something into fewer parts, each part is larger!
