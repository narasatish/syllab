Place Value: The Number House

  • Every digit in a number has a special 'place'.
  • This place tells us its value.
  • Think of a house with three rooms: Ones, Tens, Hundreds.

🎙️ Numbers are like people living in a house. Each person has their own room, and the room tells us how important they are! In our number house, we have three rooms for 3-digit numbers: the Ones room, the Tens room, and the Hundreds room. Let's see who lives where!

Inside the Number House: 325

  • The last digit is in the 'Ones' place.
  • The middle digit is in the 'Tens' place.
  • The first digit is in the 'Hundreds' place.

🎙️ Let's take the number 325. The '5' is in the Ones place, so it means 5 ones. The '2' is in the Tens place, so it means 2 tens, which is 20. And the '3' is in the Hundreds place, so it means 3 hundreds, which is 300. See how each digit has a different value depending on its place?

Reading 3-digit Numbers

Let's write "Two hundred forty-five"

  • 'Two hundred' means 2 in the Hundreds place.
  • 'Forty-five' means 4 in the Tens place and 5 in the Ones place.
  • So, the number is 245.

🎙️ If someone tells you 'Two hundred forty-five', you can write it down easily! 'Two hundred' tells you the first digit is 2. 'Forty-five' tells you the next two digits are 4 and 5. Put them together, and you get 245. Practice makes perfect!

Expanded Form: Opening Up Numbers

  • Expanded form is like taking a number apart.
  • We show the value of each digit separately.
  • Example: 325 = 300 + 20 + 5.

🎙️ Imagine you have a toy car, and you open it up to see all its parts. Expanded form is similar! We take a number like 325 and show what each digit truly means: 3 hundreds (300), 2 tens (20), and 5 ones (5). When you add them up, you get the original number!

Standard Form: Putting Numbers Together

  • Standard form is putting the expanded form back together.
  • It's the usual way we write numbers.
  • Example: 400 + 20 + 9 = 429.

🎙️ If expanded form is taking the toy car apart, standard form is putting it back together! When you have the values of hundreds, tens, and ones, you just combine them to get the normal number. So, 400 + 20 + 9 becomes 429. It's like building a number!

Before, After, Between

  • 'Before' means the number just smaller.
  • 'After' means the number just bigger.
  • 'Between' means the number in the middle of two numbers.

🎙️ Think of numbers standing in a line! The number 'before' 150 is 149. The number 'after' 150 is 151. And the number 'between' 149 and 151 is 150. This helps us understand the order of numbers.

Comparing 3-digit Numbers

  • Who is taller? Who has more?
  • To compare numbers, start with the Hundreds place.
  • If Hundreds are same, compare Tens. If Tens are same, compare Ones.

🎙️ Comparing numbers is like comparing two friends to see who is taller! Always start by looking at the digit in the Hundreds place. The number with the bigger Hundreds digit is the bigger number. If the Hundreds digits are the same, then look at the Tens digits, and so on. It's a fun game of finding the bigger one!

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing place value: 205 is not 25!
  • Forgetting the zero: 'Two hundred five' is 205, not 25.
  • Comparing only the first digit: 399 vs 401.

🎙️ Even the best mathematicians make small mistakes sometimes! A common one is forgetting that a zero in the tens or ones place is very important. 'Two hundred five' is 205, not 25. Also, remember to look at all the digits when comparing, not just the first one. Always be careful and double-check!
