The Home of 3-Digit Numbers

  • Every number has a special 'home' for its digits.
  • For 3-digit numbers, we have Ones, Tens, and Hundreds.
  • Hundreds is the newest, biggest place!

🎙️ Just like you have a home with different rooms, numbers have places for their digits. For numbers like 123, the '3' lives in the Ones place, the '2' lives in the Tens place, and the '1' lives in the Hundreds place. This helps us understand the value of each digit.

How to Build a 3-Digit Number?

  • We use digits from 0 to 9.
  • Put one digit in Hundreds, one in Tens, one in Ones.
  • For example, 2 hundreds, 3 tens, 4 ones makes 234.

🎙️ It's like building with blocks! You pick a block for the Hundreds place, a block for the Tens place, and a block for the Ones place. Remember, the hundreds digit cannot be zero, otherwise, it would not be a 3-digit number. Let's try an example together.

How to Read Big Numbers?

  • First, read the Hundreds digit and say 'hundred'.
  • Then, read the last two digits together as a 2-digit number.
  • Like 345 is 'Three hundred forty-five'.

🎙️ Reading 3-digit numbers is simple! You just say the hundreds part first, and then say the rest of the number as you usually would. For example, for the number 678, we say 'Six hundred' and then 'seventy-eight'. So, it's 'Six hundred seventy-eight'.

Stretching Numbers Apart (Expanded Form)

  • We can break a number into its parts.
  • Show the value of each digit.
  • 234 = 200 + 30 + 4.

🎙️ Expanded form is like pulling a number apart to see what it's made of. Each digit has a value based on its place. For 234, the '2' is 200, the '3' is 30, and the '4' is 4. When we add them up, we get back our original number!

Let's expand a number!

  • Take the number 456.
  • 4 is in Hundreds place, so 400.
  • 5 is in Tens place, so 50.
  • 6 is in Ones place, so 6.
  • So, 456 = 400 + 50 + 6.

🎙️ Let's try expanding the number 456. The 4 in the hundreds place means 400. The 5 in the tens place means 50. And the 6 in the ones place means just 6. So, 456 becomes 400 + 50 + 6. It's like seeing all the ingredients of a number.

Who is Bigger? Comparing Numbers!

  • We use > (greater than), < (less than), or = (equal to).
  • Start comparing from the Hundreds place.
  • If Hundreds are same, compare Tens, then Ones.

🎙️ Comparing numbers helps us know which number is bigger or smaller. Always start by looking at the Hundreds digit first. If the hundreds are the same, then move to the Tens digit. If those are also the same, then look at the Ones digit. It's like finding the tallest person in a line!

Let's compare 543 and 571.

  • Hundreds: 5 and 5 (same).
  • Tens: 4 and 7 (7 is bigger!).
  • So, 571 is greater than 543.

🎙️ Let's compare 543 and 571. Both have 5 in the hundreds place, so we look at the tens. 543 has 4 tens, and 571 has 7 tens. Since 7 tens is bigger than 4 tens, 571 is the bigger number. So, 543 < 571.

Putting Numbers in Order

  • We can arrange numbers from smallest to largest (ascending).
  • Or largest to smallest (descending).
  • Use comparison rules to help!

🎙️ Ordering numbers is like arranging your toys from smallest to biggest, or tallest to shortest. Ascending order means going up, from small to big. Descending order means going down, from big to small. We use the same comparison rules we just learned to help us.

Order these: 215, 198, 230.

  • Smallest Hundreds: 198 (1 Hundred).
  • Next: 215 (2 Hundreds, 1 Ten).
  • Largest: 230 (2 Hundreds, 3 Tens).
  • Order: 198, 215, 230.

🎙️ Let's order the numbers 215, 198, and 230 from smallest to largest. First, we look at the hundreds digit. 198 has 1 hundred, which is the smallest. Then, both 215 and 230 have 2 hundreds, so we look at the tens. 215 has 1 ten, and 230 has 3 tens. So, 215 comes before 230. The order is 198, 215, 230.
