What is Geometry?

  • Geometry is the study of shapes.
  • It helps us understand the world.
  • Look around you, what shapes do you see?

🎙️ Geometry is a special part of maths that teaches us about different shapes and sizes. From your toys to your house, everything has a shape. It's like a secret language of shapes!

Shapes are all around us.

Meet the 'Point'!

  • A point is a tiny dot.
  • It shows an exact place.
  • It has no size, just a position.

🎙️ Imagine poking your pencil on a paper, that tiny mark is a point. It's like a starting spot or a specific location. It's the smallest thing in geometry!

Straight Lines

  • A straight line goes in one direction.
  • It never bends or curves.
  • Like the edge of your ruler.

🎙️ Think about walking straight from one end of the room to the other, that's a straight line. We use rulers to draw straight lines. Can you find anything straight in your classroom?

📏
A straight path

Curved Lines

  • A curved line is not straight.
  • It bends and turns.
  • Like a wavy road or a rainbow.

🎙️ Now, imagine a snake moving or a river flowing, they make curved lines. Curved lines are fun because they can go in any direction and make beautiful patterns. Can you draw a curved line with your finger in the air?

〰️
A wavy path

Line Segment

  • A line segment is a part of a straight line.
  • It has a start point and an end point.
  • You can measure its length.

🎙️ If you take a long straight line and cut a piece from it, that piece is a line segment. It has a definite beginning and a definite end. Think of the edge of your desk, it's a line segment.

✂️
A cut piece of line

What is a Ray?

  • A ray starts at one point.
  • It goes on forever in one direction.
  • Like a ray of sunshine from the sun.

🎙️ Imagine the sun sending out light, it starts at the sun and goes on and on. That's a ray! It has a starting point but no end point. The light from a torch is also like a ray.

Eight planets orbit the Sun.

Plane Shapes (2D Shapes)

  • These are flat shapes.
  • You can draw them on paper.
  • They only have length and width.

🎙️ Now let's talk about plane shapes, also called 2D shapes. These are like pictures, they are flat and don't have any thickness. We see many of these shapes in our books and on walls.

Round and Round: The Circle!

  • A circle is perfectly round.
  • It has no straight sides or corners.
  • Like a yummy roti or a car wheel.

🎙️ The circle is one of the most common shapes. Think of a bangle, a coin, or the clock on the wall. They are all circles! It's fun to draw a circle freehand or with a compass.

A perfect circle

Four Equal Sides: The Square!

  • A square has four straight sides.
  • All four sides are exactly the same length.
  • It also has four corners.

🎙️ Next is the square! Imagine a chessboard, a slice of bread, or a carrom board. They are squares. All their sides are equal, which makes them look very balanced.

🟥
A red square

Long and Short: The Rectangle!

  • A rectangle also has four straight sides.
  • It has two long sides and two short sides.
  • Like your classroom door or a book.

🎙️ The rectangle is a bit like a stretched square. Your mobile phone, a blackboard, or your school bag are often rectangles. Its opposite sides are equal in length.

🟦
A blue rectangle

Three Sides: The Triangle!

  • A triangle has three straight sides.
  • It also has three corners.
  • Like a yummy samosa or a mountain peak.

🎙️ The triangle is a strong shape! Think of a slice of pizza, a traffic sign, or the roof of a house. They are all triangles. It's a very stable shape.

🔺
A pointed triangle

Counting Sides and Corners!

  • Sides are the straight lines of a shape.
  • Corners (vertices) are where two sides meet.
  • Let's count for square, rectangle, triangle!

🎙️ Every flat shape has sides and corners. For a square, there are 4 sides and 4 corners. For a rectangle, there are also 4 sides and 4 corners. And for a triangle, there are 3 sides and 3 corners. Good job counting!

Count and add using real objects like apples 🍎

Solid Shapes (3D Shapes)

  • These shapes are not flat.
  • You can hold them in your hand.
  • They have length, width, and height!

🎙️ Now let's move to solid shapes, also called 3D shapes. These are not flat like drawings; they are real objects you can touch and feel. They take up space, like your toys or a fruit.

The Cube!

  • A cube is like a dice.
  • All its faces are squares.
  • It has 6 flat faces.

🎙️ A cube is a very special 3D shape. Think of an ice cube or a Rubik's cube. All its sides, called faces, are perfect squares. It's a very balanced and strong shape.

🧊
An ice cube

The Cuboid!

  • A cuboid is like a shoebox.
  • Its faces are rectangles.
  • It also has 6 flat faces.

🎙️ The cuboid is very similar to a cube, but its faces are rectangles, not necessarily squares. Think of a brick, a book, or a matchbox. These are all examples of cuboids.

🎁
A gift box

The Cylinder!

  • A cylinder is like a can of juice.
  • It has two flat circular faces.
  • And one curved face.

🎙️ A cylinder is a shape that can roll! Imagine a water bottle, a battery, or a gas cylinder. It has a circular top and bottom, and a curved surface all around.

🥫
A food can

The Sphere!

  • A sphere is perfectly round.
  • It has only one curved face.
  • Like a football or an orange.

🎙️ The sphere is the roundest of all 3D shapes. Think of a globe, a marble, or a plum. It's completely curved and can roll in any direction. It has no flat faces, no edges, and no corners.

⚽
A round ball

The Cone!

  • A cone has one flat circular face.
  • It has one curved face.
  • It comes to a sharp point at the top.

🎙️ Finally, let's look at the cone! Imagine an ice cream cone or a birthday cap. It has a flat circular base and then tapers up to a single point. It's a fun shape!
