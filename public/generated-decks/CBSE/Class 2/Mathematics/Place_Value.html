The Ones Place: Smallest Seat!

  • The seat on the right is the 'Ones Place'.
  • It holds single things, like one yummy mango.
  • Numbers from 0 to 9 sit here alone.

🎙️ Think of the Ones Place as where you keep all your single items. If you have 5 mangoes, all 5 mangoes are in the Ones Place. It's the simplest seat!

The Tens Place: A Bigger Seat!

  • The seat next to the Ones Place (on its left) is the 'Tens Place'.
  • It holds groups of ten things.
  • Like 10 fingers, or 10 pencils bundled together!

🎙️ The Tens Place is special because it doesn't count single items. It counts groups of ten! Imagine tying 10 pencils together – that's one group of ten.

Place Value vs. Face Value

  • Face Value: What the digit *looks* like (e.g., 5 is always 5).
  • Place Value: What the digit is *worth* because of its seat.

🎙️ Face Value is easy; it's just the number itself. But Place Value is like a superpower that changes the number's worth depending on its position!

  • Face Value of 6 is 6. Place Value of 6 is 60 (Tens place).
  • Face Value of 8 is 8. Place Value of 8 is 8 (Ones place).

🎙️ See how the digit '6' has a different value depending on its place, but its face value remains the same, always '6'. This is a key difference!

Stretching Numbers: Expanded Form!

  • Expanded Form shows a number as a sum of its place values.
  • It's like stretching a number out to see its parts clearly.

🎙️ When we write a number in expanded form, we are just writing down the value of each digit and adding them together. It helps us understand the number's structure.

↔️
Stretch it out!
19 / 24

Let's Expand the Number 75!

  • In 75, 7 is in the Tens Place, so its value is 70.
  • 5 is in the Ones Place, so its value is 5.
  • Expanded Form: 70 + 5.

🎙️ This shows us clearly that 75 is made up of seventy and five. It's a great way to understand how numbers are built from their place values.

Watch Out for These Mistakes!

  • Confusing Face Value with Place Value.
  • Writing 2 + 3 instead of 20 + 3 for 23.
  • Not knowing which is the Tens and which is the Ones place.

🎙️ It's okay to make mistakes, but knowing what they are helps us learn better! Always double-check your Tens and Ones places and their values.
