Meet Our Helper: The Sun!

  • The Sun is very big and hot.
  • It gives us light and warmth.

🎙️ Our big, bright Sun plays a super important role in the water's journey. Its warmth and heat are what make the water move and change. Without the Sun, the water cycle wouldn't happen!

Step 1: Evaporation – Water Goes Up!

  • The Sun heats up water in rivers and oceans.
  • Warm water turns into tiny, invisible mist.
  • This mist is called water vapour and it floats up!

🎙️ Imagine the Sun acting like a giant heater! When it shines on water, the water gets warm. This warm water then changes into a very light, invisible mist called water vapour, which gently rises into the sky. This first step is called Evaporation.

2Gets Hot
3Goes Up (Vapour)

Example: Puddles Dry Up!

  • After rain, you see puddles on the road.
  • When the Sun shines, the puddles get smaller.
  • The water has evaporated and gone into the air!

🎙️ Think about a rainy day. After the rain stops, you see many puddles. But if the Sun comes out, the puddles slowly disappear. That's because the Sun's heat makes the water evaporate, turning it into vapour that goes up.

Example: Wet Clothes Dry!

  • When your clothes are wet, we hang them in the sun.
  • The Sun's heat makes the water leave the clothes.
  • The water evaporates, and clothes become dry!

🎙️ Another great example is when your clothes get wet. Mummy hangs them out in the sun, right? The sun helps the water in the clothes evaporate, making them dry and ready to wear again.

Step 2: Condensation – Clouds Are Made!

  • The water vapour goes very high up in the sky.
  • It gets very cold there!
  • The cold makes the vapour turn back into tiny water drops, forming clouds.

🎙️ When the water vapour goes high up, it's like going to a very cold place! The cold air makes the tiny vapour particles huddle together and turn back into tiny, tiny water drops. Millions of these tiny drops come together to form the clouds we see in the sky. This step is called Condensation.

1Vapour Up
2Gets Cold
3Forms Clouds

Example: Water on a Cold Glass!

  • Take a glass of very cold water.
  • You will see tiny water drops on the outside of the glass.
  • The air around the cold glass turns into water drops!

🎙️ Here's a fun way to see condensation at home! Fill a glass with ice-cold water. After some time, you'll see small water drops on the outside of the glass. The water vapour in the air gets cold when it touches the glass and turns back into liquid water.

Example: Foggy Mirror!

  • After a hot bath, your bathroom mirror gets foggy.
  • The warm water vapour from the bath touches the cold mirror.
  • It turns into tiny water drops, making the mirror foggy!

🎙️ Another example is when you take a hot bath or shower. The steam, which is water vapour, fills the bathroom. When it touches the cooler mirror, it condenses and forms tiny water droplets, making the mirror foggy.

Step 3: Precipitation – Water Falls Down!

  • Clouds get very heavy with all the water drops.
  • They can't hold the water anymore!
  • The water falls back to Earth as rain, snow, or hail.

🎙️ As more and more water drops join together in the clouds, the clouds become very heavy. When they get too heavy to hold the water, the water falls back down to Earth. This falling water is called Precipitation, and we usually see it as rain.

Example: Watching Rain!

  • The most common example is seeing rain fall.
  • Water from the clouds comes down to Earth.
  • It waters plants and fills rivers.

🎙️ You've all seen rain, haven't you? That's precipitation! It's the water completing its journey from the sky back to the ground. Rain is so important because it gives fresh water to everything on Earth.

Step 4: Collection – Water Back on Earth!

  • The rain that falls collects in different places.
  • It fills rivers, lakes, ponds, and oceans.
  • Some water also soaks into the ground.

🎙️ Once the rain falls, it doesn't just sit there! It collects in all the water bodies we know – rivers, lakes, and oceans. Some of it also goes deep into the ground, ready to be used by plants or to fill up underground water sources. This is the Collection step.

The Whole Water Cycle Story!

  • 1. Sun heats water (Evaporation).
  • 2. Water vapour forms clouds (Condensation).
  • 3. Water falls as rain (Precipitation).
  • 4. Water collects on Earth (Collection).

🎙️ So, the water cycle is a continuous loop! Water goes up, forms clouds, falls down, and collects, only to start its journey again. It's like a never-ending merry-go-round for water!
