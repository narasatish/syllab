Evaporation: Water Disappearing!

  • The sun heats the water.
  • Water turns into tiny, invisible 'water vapour'.
  • This vapour floats up into the sky.

🎙️ This process is called Evaporation. It's when liquid water gets warm and changes into a gas, which we call water vapour. This vapour is so light that it rises up, up, up into the sky, just like a balloon.

Condensation: Clouds Forming!

  • Cold air turns vapour into tiny water droplets.
  • These tiny droplets join together.
  • They form clouds!

🎙️ This amazing process is called Condensation! When the warm water vapour meets the cold air, it changes back into tiny, tiny drops of liquid water. These little drops stick together and become big enough for us to see them as clouds in the sky!

Precipitation: Rain Falls Down!

  • When clouds get too heavy, the water falls.
  • It falls back to Earth as rain.
  • Sometimes it's snow or hail!

🎙️ This is called Precipitation! When the clouds can't hold any more water, the water falls down from the sky. Most of the time, it falls as rain. But in very cold places, it can also fall as beautiful snow or tiny ice balls called hail.

The Water Cycle Story

  • It's a never-ending story of water!
  • Evaporation: Water goes up.
  • Condensation: Clouds are made.
  • Precipitation: Rain comes down.
  • Collection: Water gathers.

🎙️ So, the Water Cycle is like a beautiful, continuous story with four main parts! First, water goes up (Evaporation), then clouds are formed (Condensation), then rain falls (Precipitation), and finally, water gathers (Collection). And then it all starts again!

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Don't confuse evaporation (water going up) with precipitation (water coming down).
  • Remember water doesn't disappear, it changes!
  • Each step is important and linked.

🎙️ Sometimes, children get a little confused between evaporation and precipitation. Remember, evaporation is when water goes up, and precipitation is when it comes down. Also, water never truly disappears; it just changes its form and location in the cycle.
