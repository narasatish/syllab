The Sun is a Big Heater!

  • The Sun gives us light and heat.
  • It warms up the water in rivers.
  • It's like a giant stove!

🎙️ The Sun is very powerful! When it shines brightly, it heats up everything, especially the water in rivers, lakes, and oceans. This heat starts our water journey.

Water Becomes Vapour (Evaporation)

  • When water gets hot, it turns into a gas.
  • This gas is called water vapour.
  • We cannot see water vapour.

🎙️ When the sun heats the water, the water changes. It becomes so light that it floats up into the air, like magic! This invisible form of water is called water vapour.

Puddles Disappear!

  • After rain, we see puddles.
  • The Sun shines on them.
  • Soon, the puddles are gone!

🎙️ Have you ever noticed how puddles on the road vanish after a while? This is because the sun's heat turns the water into vapour, and it rises into the sky. That's evaporation in action!

Wet Clothes Dry!

  • When we wash clothes, they are wet.
  • We hang them in the sun.
  • The water goes into the air, and clothes dry!

🎙️ This is another example you see every day. The water from your wet clothes also turns into vapour and goes up into the air, leaving your clothes dry and ready to wear.

Vapour Goes Up, Up, Up!

  • Water vapour is very light.
  • It floats high in the sky.
  • It goes higher and higher!

🎙️ Just like a hot air balloon, water vapour is lighter than the air around it. So, it keeps rising higher and higher into the sky, away from the ground.

Vapour rising
9 / 20

Clouds Are Made of Vapour

  • High up, it is very cold.
  • The water vapour gets cold too.
  • It turns into tiny water drops and makes clouds.

🎙️ When the water vapour reaches very high, it gets chilly! The cold air makes the tiny vapour particles huddle together and turn back into very small water droplets. Millions of these droplets form clouds.

Clouds bring us rain.
10 / 20

Vapour Becomes Drops (Condensation)

  • When vapour gets cold, it changes back.
  • It becomes tiny water drops.
  • This is called condensation.

🎙️ So, when the warm vapour meets the cold air up high, it changes its form again. It condenses, meaning it turns back into tiny, tiny liquid water droplets. This is the opposite of evaporation.

Cold Glass Gets Wet!

  • Take a glass with cold water.
  • See tiny drops outside the glass?
  • This is condensation!

🎙️ Here's a fun experiment! The air around the glass has water vapour. When it touches the cold surface of the glass, it cools down and turns into visible water drops. Just like clouds form in the sky!

Fog and Mist

  • Sometimes, clouds are very low.
  • We call this fog or mist.
  • It's tiny water drops in the air!

🎙️ On some mornings, you might see a thick white blanket close to the ground. That's fog! It's basically a cloud that has formed very close to us, showing us those tiny water droplets.

Clouds Get Heavy!

  • More and more tiny drops join together.
  • The clouds become bigger and heavier.
  • They can't hold all the water anymore!

🎙️ Imagine many small drops joining hands to become one big drop. When millions of these tiny water droplets in a cloud come together, the cloud becomes very heavy and dark. It's full of water!

Water Falls Back (Precipitation)

  • When clouds are too heavy, water falls.
  • It falls as rain, snow, or hail.
  • This is called precipitation.

🎙️ When the clouds get too heavy to hold all the water, the water falls back to Earth. Most often, it falls as rain. Sometimes, in very cold places, it can fall as snow or tiny ice balls called hail.

Water Flows Back Home!

  • Rainwater flows into small streams.
  • Streams join to form rivers.
  • Rivers flow into the big oceans.

🎙️ After the rain falls, the water doesn't just stay in one place. It starts its journey again, flowing from higher places to lower places, eventually making its way back to the vast oceans, ready for the cycle to begin once more.

The Water Cycle Never Stops!

  • Sun heats water (Evaporation).
  • Vapour forms clouds (Condensation).
  • Water falls as rain (Precipitation).
  • Water collects in rivers/oceans.

🎙️ This whole journey of water – from evaporating, forming clouds, falling as rain, and flowing back – is called the Water Cycle. It's a continuous process that keeps water moving around our Earth.
