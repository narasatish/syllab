The Cartesian System: The Foundation

  • Invented by René Descartes.
  • Uses two perpendicular lines to define a plane.
  • Forms the basis of coordinate geometry.

🎙️ The entire system is named after the French mathematician René Descartes. He came up with this brilliant idea of using a grid to locate points, which is super helpful!

The Axes: X-axis and Y-axis

  • The horizontal line is the X-axis.
  • The vertical line is the Y-axis.
  • They intersect at the origin.

🎙️ Think of the X-axis as moving left and right, and the Y-axis as moving up and down. These two lines are our main reference points.

The Origin: The Starting Point (0,0)

  • The point where the X-axis and Y-axis intersect.
  • Its coordinates are always (0, 0).
  • It's our main reference point for all other locations.

🎙️ The origin is like the 'home base' or 'starting line' for all our measurements. Every point's location is described relative to this origin.

🎯
The central point

Quadrants: Dividing the Plane

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • Each region is called a quadrant.
  • They are numbered counter-clockwise.

🎙️ When the X and Y axes cross, they create four distinct sections. We call these quadrants, and they are always numbered starting from the top-right and moving anti-clockwise.

1Quadrant I (+,+)

Coordinates: (x, y) - How to write them

  • A pair of numbers (x, y) that specifies a point's location.
  • The first number, 'x', is the x-coordinate (abscissa).
  • The second number, 'y', is the y-coordinate (ordinate).

🎙️ This (x, y) pair is like a unique address for every point. The 'x' tells us how far left or right, and the 'y' tells us how far up or down from the origin.

(x,y)

Plotting Points: Step-by-step

  • Start at the origin (0,0).
  • Move horizontally along the X-axis (x-coordinate).
  • Then, move vertically along the Y-axis (y-coordinate).
  • Mark the point.

🎙️ Plotting points is like following directions on a map. Always remember to move horizontally first (along X) and then vertically (along Y).

1Start at (0,0)
2Move X-units
3Move Y-units
4Mark Point

Worked Example 1: Plotting A(3, 2)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 3 units right along the X-axis.
  • Then, move 2 units up parallel to the Y-axis.
  • Mark the point A.

🎙️ For A(3, 2), both numbers are positive. This means we move right on the X-axis and up on the Y-axis, placing the point in Quadrant I.

📈
Positive coordinates

Worked Example 2: Plotting B(-4, 1)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 4 units left along the X-axis (negative X).
  • Then, move 1 unit up parallel to the Y-axis (positive Y).
  • Mark the point B.

🎙️ Here, the x-coordinate is negative, so we move left. The y-coordinate is positive, so we move up. This point will be in Quadrant II.

📉
Negative X, Positive Y

Worked Example 3: Plotting C(-2, -3) and D(5, -1)

  • For C(-2, -3): Move 2 left, then 3 down.
  • For D(5, -1): Move 5 right, then 1 down.
  • Practice plotting these on your graph paper!

🎙️ C is in Quadrant III (both negative), and D is in Quadrant IV (positive X, negative Y). It's important to be careful with the signs!

Points on X-axis: (x, 0)

  • Any point on the X-axis has its y-coordinate as 0.
  • Examples: (3, 0), (-5, 0).
  • They don't move up or down from the X-axis.

🎙️ If the y-coordinate is zero, it means the point lies directly on the X-axis. There's no vertical movement from the origin.

➡️
Only horizontal movement

Points on Y-axis: (0, y)

  • Any point on the Y-axis has its x-coordinate as 0.
  • Examples: (0, 4), (0, -2).
  • They don't move left or right from the Y-axis.

🎙️ Similarly, if the x-coordinate is zero, the point lies directly on the Y-axis. There's no horizontal movement from the origin.

⬆️
Only vertical movement

Signs of Coordinates in Quadrants

  • Quadrant I: (+, +)

  • Quadrant III: (-, -)
  • Quadrant IV: (+, -)
