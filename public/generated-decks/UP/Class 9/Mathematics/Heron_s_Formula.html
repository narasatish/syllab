Area of a Rectangle

  • Formula: Length × Breadth
  • All angles are 90 degrees
  • Opposite sides are equal

🎙️ We all know rectangles, right? To find the area of a rectangle, we simply multiply its length by its breadth. For example, a floor is often rectangular, and its area tells us how much carpet we need.

Heron's Formula: The Solution

  • Finds area using only the three side lengths
  • No need for the height
  • Applicable to all types of triangles
  • Very powerful and versatile

🎙️ Heron's Formula is a powerful tool that allows us to find the area of any triangle if we know the lengths of all three of its sides. This means we don't need to calculate the height, simplifying many real-world problems.

Understanding 's': The Semi-perimeter

  • First step in Heron's Formula
  • 's' stands for semi-perimeter
  • Semi-perimeter = half of the perimeter
  • Formula: s = (a + b + c) / 2

🎙️ Before we look at the full formula, we need to understand a key term: 'semi-perimeter'. 'Semi' means half, so semi-perimeter is simply half of the triangle's perimeter. We denote it by 's', and it's calculated by adding all three side lengths and dividing by two.

📏
Half Perimeter

Heron's Formula: The Complete Formula

  • Area = √[s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)]
  • 'a', 'b', 'c' are side lengths
  • 's' is the semi-perimeter
  • Remember the square root!

🎙️ Here it is, the complete Heron's Formula! The area of a triangle is the square root of 's' multiplied by (s-a), then by (s-b), and finally by (s-c). Make sure you calculate 's' first, then the terms in the brackets, and finally multiply them all before taking the square root.

Worked Example 2: Equilateral Triangle

  • Side length = 6 cm
  • Step 1: s = (6+6+6)/2 = 9 cm
  • Step 2: (s-a)=3, (s-b)=3, (s-c)=3
  • Step 3: Area = √[9 * 3 * 3 * 3] = √243

🎙️ Let's apply Heron's Formula to an equilateral triangle with a side of 6 cm. Remember, all sides are equal. Calculate 's', then the (s-a) terms. You'll notice they are all the same. Finally, substitute into the formula. The area will be √243, which simplifies to 9√3 cm².

Beyond Triangles: Quadrilaterals

  • Can Heron's Formula be used for quadrilaterals?
  • Yes! Divide into two triangles
  • Find area of each triangle
  • Add them up for total area

🎙️ Heron's Formula is designed for triangles, but we can extend its use to more complex shapes like quadrilaterals. The trick is to divide the quadrilateral into two non-overlapping triangles by drawing a diagonal. Then, we find the area of each triangle and add them up.

Worked Example 3: Area of a Quadrilateral

  • Given a quadrilateral ABCD
  • Diagonal AC divides it into ΔABC and ΔADC
  • Find sides of both triangles
  • Calculate areas using Heron's Formula, then add

🎙️ Let's say we have a quadrilateral ABCD with sides AB=7m, BC=24m, CD=15m, DA=20m and diagonal AC=25m. We divide it into ΔABC and ΔADC. For ΔABC (7,24,25), area is 84 m². For ΔADC (25,15,20), area is 150 m². Total area = 84 + 150 = 234 m².

Recap: Key Takeaways from Heron's Formula

  • Finds area of triangle with only side lengths
  • First step: calculate semi-perimeter 's'
  • Formula: Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Useful for complex shapes by dividing

🎙️ To summarize what we've learned today: Heron's Formula is a fantastic tool for finding the area of any triangle when only the side lengths are known. Remember the two main steps: calculating 's' and then applying the main formula. It's also versatile for quadrilaterals!
