Meet the Cuboid

  • A solid with 6 rectangular faces
  • Opposite faces are identical
  • Examples: brick, shoebox, textbook

What is Surface Area?

  • Total area of all the faces of a 3D object
  • Imagine wrapping paper needed
  • Measured in square units (cm², m²)

🎙️ Surface area is simply the total area of all the outer surfaces of a 3D shape. Think about how much paint you'd need to cover a box, or how much wrapping paper for a gift.

Calculating Cuboid Surface Area

  • A cuboid has 3 pairs of identical faces
  • Area of front/back: l × h
  • Area of top/bottom: l × w
  • Area of sides: w × h
  • Total SA = 2(lb + bh + hl)

🎙️ To find the total surface area of a cuboid, we sum the areas of all six faces. Since opposite faces are equal, we can use the formula 2(lb + bh + hl), where l, b, and h are length, breadth, and height.

Example: Painting a Room

  • Room dimensions: L=5m, B=4m, H=3m
  • Area to paint = walls + ceiling
  • SA = 2(LH + BH) + LB (no floor)

🎙️ Imagine painting a room. You need to calculate the area of the four walls and the ceiling, but not the floor. This is a real-life application of finding the surface area of a cuboid.

Introducing the Cube

  • A special type of cuboid
  • All sides (edges) are equal in length
  • All 6 faces are identical squares

🎙️ A cube is a very special cuboid where all its edges are of the same length. Think of a dice or an ice cube – all its faces are perfect squares.

🎲
8 / 25

Surface Area of a Cube

  • Each face is a square with side 'a'
  • Area of one face = a²
  • Total 6 faces, so SA = 6a²

🎙️ Since a cube has six identical square faces, and the area of one square face is side × side (a²), the total surface area of a cube is simply 6 times a².

Example: Wrapping a Gift Box

  • A cube-shaped gift box with side 10 cm
  • How much wrapping paper is needed?
  • SA = 6 × (10 cm)² = 600 cm²

🎙️ If you have a cube-shaped gift box and want to wrap it, you'd calculate its surface area to know how much paper you need. It's a direct application of the formula.

Understanding Cylinders

  • Has two circular bases
  • A curved surface connecting them
  • Examples: can, pipe, battery

🎙️ A cylinder is a familiar shape, like a tin can or a water bottle. It has a top and bottom that are circles, and a smooth, curved side connecting them.

🥫

Curved Surface Area (CSA)

  • Area of only the curved part
  • Imagine unrolling the side of a can
  • Forms a rectangle

🎙️ The curved surface area is just the area of the side part, without the top and bottom circles. If you cut and unroll the curved part of a cylinder, it forms a rectangle.

CSA of a Cylinder

  • Rectangle's length = circumference of base (2πr)
  • Rectangle's width = height (h)
  • CSA = 2πrh

🎙️ When you unroll the cylinder, the length of the rectangle is the circumference of the circular base (2πr), and its width is the height (h). So, the CSA is found by multiplying these two.

📏
14 / 25

Total Surface Area (TSA)

  • CSA + Area of two circular bases
  • Area of one base = πr²
  • TSA = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h + r)

🎙️ The total surface area includes the curved surface area plus the areas of the top and bottom circular bases. Since each base is a circle with area πr², we add 2πr² to the CSA.

🎙️ If you need to paint a cylindrical water tank, you'll need to calculate its surface area. If the tank has an open top, you'd only paint the curved surface and the bottom base; otherwise, you'd use the total surface area.

What is Volume?

  • The amount of space a 3D object occupies
  • How much it can hold (capacity)
  • Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³)

🎙️ Volume is about how much space a 3D object fills up, or how much liquid or material it can hold. It's measured in cubic units, which tells us how many unit cubes can fit inside.

Volume of a Cuboid

  • Base area × Height
  • Base area = length × width (l × b)
  • Volume = l × b × h

🎙️ To find the volume of a cuboid, you simply multiply its length, breadth, and height. It's like stacking layers of the base area up to its height.

📦
19 / 25

Volume of a Cube

  • A special cuboid with all sides equal ('a')
  • Volume = a × a × a = a³

🎙️ Since all sides of a cube are equal, its volume is simply the side length multiplied by itself three times, or 'a' cubed. This is a very straightforward calculation.

🧊
20 / 25

Volume of a Cylinder

  • Base area × Height
  • Base area = πr² (area of circle)
  • Volume = πr²h

🎙️ Similar to a cuboid, the volume of a cylinder is its base area multiplied by its height. Here, the base is a circle, so its area is πr², making the formula πr²h.

🥛
21 / 25

Example: Filling a Tank

  • How much water can a cylindrical tank hold?
  • Tank radius 1m, height 3m
  • Volume = π(1)²(3) = 3π m³

🎙️ Calculating volume is crucial when you need to know how much a container can hold, like a water tank, a swimming pool, or even a glass of milk. It tells us the capacity.
