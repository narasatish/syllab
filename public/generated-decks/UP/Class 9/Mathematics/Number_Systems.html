Rational Numbers (Q)

  • Numbers that can be expressed as p/q.
  • Where p and q are integers, and q ≠ 0.
  • Examples: 1/2, -3/4, 5 (which is 5/1).

🎙️ Rational numbers are a big step forward! They are any number that can be written as a fraction, where both the top and bottom numbers are integers, and the bottom number is not zero. This includes all integers, as any integer 'p' can be written as 'p/1'.

➗
Division in p/q form

Properties of Rational Numbers

  • Closure: Sum, difference, product, quotient (q≠0) are rational.
  • Commutativity: a+b = b+a, a×b = b×a.
  • Associativity: (a+b)+c = a+(b+c), (a×b)×c = a×(b×c).

🎙️ Rational numbers behave nicely under basic arithmetic operations. For instance, if you add two rational numbers, the result will always be another rational number. The order of addition or multiplication doesn't change the result, and grouping them differently also gives the same answer.

Irrational Numbers

  • Numbers that cannot be expressed as p/q.
  • Their decimal expansions are non-terminating and non-repeating.
  • Examples: √2, √3, π (Pi).

🎙️ Irrational numbers are the opposite of rational numbers. You cannot write them as a simple fraction. When you try to write them as decimals, they go on forever without any repeating pattern. Famous examples include the square root of 2 and Pi.

  • Diagonal of a square with side 1 unit (√2).
  • Circumference of a circle (involves π).

🎙️ Irrational numbers might seem abstract, but they appear naturally in geometry. For instance, the length of the diagonal of a square with sides of 1 unit is exactly √2. Also, the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter is always Pi, an irrational number.

Real Numbers (R)

  • The collection of all Rational and Irrational Numbers.
  • Represent all points on the number line.
  • Every number you can think of that isn't 'imaginary' is a Real Number.

🎙️ Real numbers encompass all the number types we've discussed so far – natural, whole, integers, rational, and irrational numbers. They fill up the entire number line without any gaps. If you can place a number on a number line, it's a real number.

Worked Example 2: Locating √2 on Number Line

  • Use Pythagoras theorem to construct √2.

🎙️ Locating irrational numbers like √2 on a number line requires a geometric construction. We can use the Pythagorean theorem. By creating a right-angled triangle with sides of 1 unit, the hypotenuse will be √2 units long. Then, we transfer this length to the number line using a compass.

1Draw a number line, mark 0 and 1.
2At 1, draw a perpendicular of 1 unit length.
3Connect 0 to the top of the perpendicular. This length is √2.
4Using a compass, draw an arc from 0 with radius √2 to intersect the number line.

Properties of Real Numbers

  • Commutative Property: a+b = b+a, a×b = b×a.
  • Associative Property: (a+b)+c = a+(b+c), (a×b)×c = a×(b×c).

🎙️ Real numbers also possess several important properties that simplify calculations and algebraic manipulations. These include commutativity, associativity, and distributivity, which you've likely encountered with other number types.

Decimal Expansions

  • Terminating: Decimals that end (e.g., 0.5, 0.25).
  • Non-terminating Repeating: Decimals that don't end but repeat a pattern (e.g., 0.333...).
  • Non-terminating Non-repeating: Decimals that don't end and don't repeat (e.g., √2, π).

🎙️ Decimal expansions are key to identifying rational and irrational numbers. Rational numbers have either terminating or non-terminating repeating decimal expansions. Irrational numbers, on the other hand, always have non-terminating and non-repeating decimal expansions.

Recap: The Number System Hierarchy

  • Natural Numbers ⊂ Whole Numbers ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational Numbers ⊂ Real Numbers.
  • Irrational Numbers are also part of Real Numbers but separate from Rational.
  • Every real number has a place on the number line.

🎙️ To summarize, we started with natural numbers, added zero to get whole numbers, then included negatives for integers. Rational numbers are fractions, and irrationals are non-repeating, non-terminating decimals. All these together form the vast set of real numbers, which perfectly fill our number line.
