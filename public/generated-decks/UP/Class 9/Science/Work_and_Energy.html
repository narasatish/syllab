Scientific Definition of Work

  • Work is done when a force causes displacement.
  • Both force and movement are essential.
  • The displacement must be in the direction of the force.

🎙️ Scientifically, work is done only when a force acts on an object and causes it to move. The movement, or displacement, must happen along the direction in which the force is applied.

➡️
Force & Displacement
4 / 25

Calculating Work Done

  • Work (W) is Force (F) multiplied by Displacement (s).
  • W = F × s
  • Force is measured in Newtons (N).
  • Displacement is measured in meters (m).

🎙️ The formula for calculating work is quite simple: W equals F multiplied by s. Remember, 's' here represents the distance moved in the direction of the force.

✖️
W = F × s

Units of Work

  • The SI unit of work is Joule (J).
  • 1 Joule = 1 Newton-meter (1 J = 1 N·m).
  • One Joule is the work done when a force of 1 Newton moves an object by 1 meter.

🎙️ Just like we measure length in meters, we measure work in Joules. Understanding what one Joule represents helps us grasp the magnitude of work being done.

⚛️

When is Work Done?

  • A force must act on the object.
  • The object must be displaced.
  • The force must have a component along the direction of displacement.

🎙️ For work to be done in the scientific sense, both a force and a displacement are necessary. Furthermore, the displacement must be a direct result of the applied force, or at least have a component in that direction.

Positive Work

  • Work is positive when force and displacement are in the same direction.
  • Example: Pushing a trolley forward.
  • The force helps the movement.

🎙️ When you push a box and it moves in the direction you're pushing, you are doing positive work. This means the force applied is aiding the object's movement.

⬆️
Force & Displacement same direction
8 / 25

Negative Work

  • Work is negative when force and displacement are in opposite directions.
  • Example: Friction acting on a moving car.
  • The force opposes the movement.

🎙️ Sometimes, a force acts against the direction of motion, like friction slowing down a rolling ball. In such cases, the work done by that force is considered negative work.

⬇️
Force & Displacement opposite direction

Zero Work

  • No displacement occurs (e.g., pushing a wall).
  • Force is perpendicular to displacement (e.g., carrying a bag horizontally).
  • No work is done by that specific force.

🎙️ There are two main scenarios where scientific work is zero. Either there is no movement despite a force, or the force is acting at a 90-degree angle to the direction of movement.

🚫
No displacement or Perpendicular force

Kinetic Energy

  • Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
  • All moving objects have kinetic energy.
  • Examples: A moving car, a flying bird, flowing water.

🎙️ Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it's moving. The faster an object moves, and the heavier it is, the more kinetic energy it possesses.

🏃
Energy of Motion
16 / 25

Calculating Kinetic Energy

  • Kinetic Energy (KE) depends on mass (m) and velocity (v).
  • KE = ½ mv²
  • Mass is in kilograms (kg), velocity in meters per second (m/s).
  • The unit of energy is also Joule (J).

🎙️ The formula for kinetic energy shows us that it's directly proportional to both mass and the square of velocity. This means that speed has a very significant impact on an object's kinetic energy.

➗
KE = ½ mv²

Example: A Moving Car

  • Its kinetic energy depends on its mass and speed.
  • A faster or heavier car has more kinetic energy.

🎙️ A classic example of kinetic energy is a moving car. The car has energy because it is in motion, and this energy can be used to do work, for instance, by crashing into something and causing damage.

Potential Energy

  • Energy stored in an object due to its position or state.
  • It's 'stored' energy, ready to be released.
  • Examples: A stretched rubber band, water held in a dam.

🎙️ Potential energy is like hidden energy, stored within an object due to its arrangement or position. It has the 'potential' to do work once it's released or allowed to change.

Stored Energy

Gravitational Potential Energy

  • Energy stored in an object due to its height above the ground.
  • Depends on mass, gravity, and height.
  • The higher an object, the more gravitational potential energy it has.

🎙️ One common type of potential energy is gravitational potential energy. This is the energy an object has simply because it's elevated above a certain level, like the ground.

⛰️
Energy due to Height

Calculating Potential Energy

  • Potential Energy (PE) = mass (m) × gravity (g) × height (h).
  • PE = mgh
  • g is the acceleration due to gravity (approx. 9.8 m/s²).
  • The unit of energy is Joule (J).

🎙️ The formula for gravitational potential energy is mgh. This means a heavier object, or an object at a greater height, will possess more stored energy ready to be converted into other forms.

✖️
PE = mgh

🎙️ Water held behind a dam is a fantastic example of potential energy. The water is at a significant height, storing a vast amount of energy that can be released to flow downwards and turn turbines to produce electricity.

Energy Transformation

  • Energy can change from one form to another.
  • Example: A falling apple converts potential energy to kinetic energy.
  • Energy is never lost, it just changes form.

🎙️ Energy is constantly transforming around us. Think of a light bulb: it converts electrical energy into light and heat energy. This ability to change forms is crucial in many processes.

Law of Conservation of Energy

  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed.
  • It can only be transformed from one form to another.
  • The total energy in an isolated system remains constant.

🎙️ This is one of the most fundamental laws in physics. It tells us that we cannot 'make' energy from nothing, nor can we 'lose' it. Energy simply shifts from one type to another, always maintaining its total amount.

🔒
Energy is conserved

Chapter Recap

  • Work: Force causes displacement in its direction (W = F × s).
  • Energy: Capacity to do work (Units: Joule).
  • Kinetic Energy: Due to motion (KE = ½ mv²).
  • Potential Energy: Stored energy (PE = mgh).
  • Energy can transform but is always conserved.
