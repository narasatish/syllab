What is Matter?

  • Anything that occupies space.
  • Anything that has mass.
  • Perceived by our senses.

🎙️ In simple terms, matter is anything that takes up space and has weight. You, me, this desk, the air – all are examples of matter. We can usually see, touch, or smell matter.

Ancient Indian View of Matter

  • Panch Tatva: Air, Earth, Fire, Sky, Water.
  • All matter believed to be made of these five.
  • Still relevant in some traditional beliefs.

🎙️ Long ago, Indian philosophers believed that all matter, living or non-living, was made up of five basic elements, which they called 'Panch Tatva'. This was a very early way of understanding the composition of the universe.

Matter is Made of Particles

  • Matter is not continuous.
  • It is made up of tiny, discrete particles.
  • Like grains of sand making a beach.

🎙️ Imagine breaking a piece of chalk into smaller and smaller pieces. Eventually, you'd reach a point where you can't break it further. This suggests that matter is not a continuous block, but rather composed of tiny particles.

How Small are These Particles?

  • Dissolve 2-3 crystals of Potassium Permanganate in 100ml water.
  • Take 10ml of this solution, add to 90ml fresh water.
  • Repeat 5-8 times; colour is still visible!

🎙️ This experiment shows just how incredibly small the particles of matter are. Even after repeated dilution, a single crystal of potassium permanganate can color a large volume of water, meaning millions of tiny particles spread out.

Particles Have Space Between Them

  • Particles are not tightly packed.
  • There are gaps or voids.
  • This allows other particles to fit in.

🎙️ Think of a crowded room, but with some small gaps between people. Similarly, the particles that make up matter are not always right next to each other; there's space between them, which is very important.

🔵🔵🔵🔵🔵🔵
Particles with Gaps
8 / 25

Demonstrating Space Between Particles

  • Take 100ml water.
  • Add 2-3 spoons of sugar/salt.
  • Water level does not rise significantly.

🎙️ When you dissolve sugar in water, the sugar particles fit into the spaces between the water particles. This is why the total volume doesn't increase much, proving that there are spaces between water particles.

🥛
Water & Sugar

Particles are Continuously Moving

  • Particles possess kinetic energy.
  • They vibrate, rotate, and move randomly.
  • Movement increases with temperature.

🎙️ Even when a substance appears still, its particles are constantly in motion. They have kinetic energy, which means energy due to motion. This movement is what causes things to mix or spread out over time.

🏃
Moving Particles

Evidence of Particle Movement

  • Light an incense stick in a corner.
  • Its smell spreads throughout the room.
  • Drop a crystal of copper sulphate in water; it spreads.

🎙️ The fragrance of an incense stick spreading through a room, or a drop of ink spreading in water, are classic examples. The moving particles of the scent or ink collide and mix with air or water particles, spreading out.

Particles Attract Each Other

  • Particles hold onto each other.
  • There are forces of attraction.
  • Strength of attraction varies in different states.

🎙️ Just like magnets attract each other, particles of matter also have forces of attraction between them. These forces are what hold matter together and determine its shape and strength.

Attractive Forces

Strength of Attraction

  • Try breaking a piece of chalk.
  • Try breaking a block of ice.
  • Try breaking an iron nail.

🎙️ You'll find it easiest to break chalk, harder to break ice, and very difficult to break an iron nail. This shows that the forces of attraction between particles are strongest in iron, moderate in ice, and weakest in chalk.

Three States of Matter

  • Solid
  • Liquid
  • Gas
  • Differentiated by particle arrangement and forces.

🎙️ Based on the characteristics of their particles, especially how much space is between them and how strongly they attract each other, matter generally exists in three main states: solid, liquid, and gas.

Matter exists in three states: solid, liquid, and gas. Each state has different properties
15 / 25

The Solid State

  • Definite shape and fixed volume.
  • Particles are tightly packed.
  • Strong forces of attraction; particles only vibrate.

🎙️ In solids, particles are very close together and held by strong forces. This gives solids their definite shape and volume. They are rigid and cannot be compressed easily because there's very little space between particles.

Examples of Solids

  • A book on your desk.
  • The chair you are sitting on.
  • A piece of rock.

🎙️ Look around you! Your school bag, a pen, a stone – all are solids. They maintain their shape unless an external force is applied, and their volume remains constant.

The Liquid State

  • No definite shape, but fixed volume.
  • Particles are less tightly packed.
  • Weaker forces; particles can slide past each other.

🎙️ Liquids have a definite volume but no definite shape. They take the shape of their container. Their particles are further apart than in solids and can move and slide past each other, which is why liquids can flow.

💧💧💧💧💧💧💧

Examples of Liquids

  • Water in a glass.
  • Milk in a carton.
  • Oil for cooking.

🎙️ Think about water, juice, or cooking oil. These are all liquids. You can pour them, and they will adapt to the shape of whatever vessel you put them in, but the amount (volume) remains the same.

The Gaseous State

  • No definite shape, no fixed volume.
  • Particles are far apart.
  • Very weak forces; particles move freely and rapidly.

🎙️ Gases have neither a definite shape nor a definite volume. Their particles are very far apart and move randomly at high speeds. This is why gases can fill any container completely and are highly compressible.

Beyond Three States: Plasma and BEC

  • Plasma: Super-energetic, ionized gases (e.g., in stars, neon signs).
  • Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC): Super-cooled gas (e.g., in labs).
  • These are advanced states of matter.

🎙️ While solid, liquid, and gas are the most common states we observe, scientists have identified other states of matter under extreme conditions. Plasma is found in stars, and BEC is created in very cold lab environments.

Chapter Recap: Matter in Our Surroundings

  • Matter has mass and occupies space.
  • Made of tiny particles with space, motion, and attraction.
  • Exists as Solids, Liquids, and Gases.

🎙️ To sum up, we learned that everything around us is matter, composed of tiny particles that are always moving and attracting each other. These characteristics define whether matter exists as a solid, liquid, or gas.
