Formulating the Cell Theory

  • Schleiden (1838): All plants are made of cells.
  • Schwann (1839): All animals are made of cells.
  • Together, they proposed the Cell Theory.

🎙️ In the late 1830s, two scientists, Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann, independently observed cells in plants and animals. Their combined work led to the foundational concept known as the Cell Theory.

Extending the Cell Theory: Rudolf Virchow

  • Added a crucial point in 1855.
  • Proposed: 'Omnis cellula e cellula'.
  • Meaning: All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

🎙️ Rudolf Virchow further refined the Cell Theory. His famous statement, 'Omnis cellula e cellula,' established that new cells don't just appear spontaneously, but instead come from the division of existing cells.

The Plasma Membrane: The Cell's Outer Boundary

  • Outermost covering of the cell.
  • Made of lipids and proteins.
  • Selectively permeable: controls entry/exit.

🎙️ The plasma membrane acts like a gatekeeper, deciding what goes in and out of the cell. This selective nature is crucial for maintaining the cell's internal environment and communicating with its surroundings.

Movement Across Membrane: Diffusion

  • Movement from high to low concentration.
  • No energy required (passive).
  • Example: gases (O2, CO2) moving in/out of cells.

🎙️ Diffusion is a natural process where particles spread out from an area where they are crowded to an area where they are less crowded. This is how essential gases like oxygen enter our cells and carbon dioxide leaves.

The Cell Wall: Extra Protection for Plant Cells

  • Found only in plant cells (and some others).
  • Located outside the plasma membrane.
  • Provides rigidity and structural support.

🎙️ Unlike animal cells, plant cells have an additional tough outer layer called the cell wall. This wall gives plants their strength and helps them stand upright, protecting the cell from external stresses.

A plant cell is a eukaryotic cell with a rigid cell wall, large vacuole, and chloroplasts.

Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells: Two Major Types

  • Prokaryotic: no true nucleus, simple.
  • Eukaryotic: true nucleus, complex.
  • Organelles are membrane-bound in eukaryotes.

🎙️ Cells are broadly divided into two main categories. Prokaryotic cells are simpler and lack a well-defined nucleus, while eukaryotic cells are more complex, possessing a true nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
