Green Revolution Example

  • Introduction of high-yielding varieties (HYV) of wheat and rice
  • Led to a significant increase in food grain production in India
  • Saved millions from hunger in the 1960s and 70s

🎙️ A great example of crop improvement is the Green Revolution in India. By introducing new, high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice, our country dramatically increased its food production and became self-sufficient in food grains.

Organic Farming

  • Farming without chemical fertilizers, pesticides, or herbicides
  • Uses manure, compost, bio-fertilizers, and biological pest control
  • Promotes environmental health and sustainable agriculture

🎙️ Organic farming is a method that focuses on natural ways to grow crops. It avoids synthetic chemicals, relying instead on natural inputs to maintain soil fertility and control pests, which is good for both us and the environment.

Watering Our Crops (Irrigation)

  • Supplying water to crops artificially
  • Methods: wells, canals, river lift systems, tanks
  • Modern methods: drip irrigation, sprinkler systems

🎙️ Irrigation is simply giving water to crops when rainfall isn't enough. Farmers use various methods, from traditional wells and canals to modern, water-saving techniques like drip and sprinkler systems.

Smart Cropping Patterns

  • Mixed Cropping: Growing two or more crops together on the same land (e.g., wheat + gram)
  • Inter-cropping: Growing two or more crops in definite rows (e.g., maize + lobia)
  • Reduces risk, increases yield

🎙️ Farmers use clever cropping patterns to make the most of their land. Mixed cropping is planting different crops together, while inter-cropping arranges them in specific rows, both helping to utilize resources better and reduce risk.

Crop Rotation

  • Growing different crops on the same land in a pre-planned succession
  • Helps maintain soil fertility
  • Controls pests and diseases naturally
  • Example: Legumes followed by non-legumes

🎙️ Crop rotation means changing the type of crop grown in a field each season. This practice is excellent for keeping the soil healthy, preventing the build-up of specific pests, and reducing the need for chemical treatments.

Storing Harvested Grains

  • Protecting grains after harvest
  • Biotic factors: insects, rodents, fungi, bacteria
  • Abiotic factors: moisture, temperature, humidity
  • Can lead to significant losses if not managed well

🎙️ After all the hard work of growing and harvesting, storing grains properly is vital. Pests like rats and insects, along with factors like moisture and heat, can spoil huge amounts of food.

Safe Grain Storage

  • Proper drying of grains before storage
  • Maintaining hygiene in storage godowns
  • Chemical treatment (fumigation) to kill pests
  • Storing in airtight containers or silos

🎙️ To prevent spoilage, grains must be thoroughly dried. Storage areas need to be clean, and sometimes fumigation is used to eliminate pests. Large quantities are often stored in specialized silos.
