Laws of Reflection

  • Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.
  • Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in the same plane.

🎙️ These two laws are fundamental to understanding reflection. The angle at which light hits a surface is always equal to the angle at which it bounces off. Also, all three key lines involved stay on the same flat surface.

Image Formation by Plane Mirror

  • Image is virtual and erect.
  • Image is laterally inverted.
  • Image distance equals object distance.

🎙️ When you look into a plane mirror, your image appears to be behind the mirror, which means it's virtual. It's also upright and the same size as you, but your left appears right and vice-versa, which is lateral inversion.

Spherical Mirrors: Concave & Convex

  • Concave mirror: Reflecting surface curves inwards.
  • Convex mirror: Reflecting surface curves outwards.

🎙️ Spherical mirrors are part of a hollow sphere. If the inner surface is reflecting, it's concave, like a spoon's inside. If the outer surface is reflecting, it's convex, like the back of a spoon.

Key Terms for Spherical Mirrors

  • Pole (P): Center of the mirror's reflecting surface.
  • Centre of Curvature (C): Center of the sphere it's part of.
  • Principal Axis: Line joining P and C.
  • Focal Length (f): Distance between Pole and Principal Focus (F).

🎙️ Understanding these terms is crucial for drawing ray diagrams and applying mirror formulas. The principal axis is like the mirror's backbone, and the focal point is where parallel rays converge or appear to diverge from.

Mirror Formula and Magnification

  • Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
  • Magnification (m): h'/h = -v/u
  • f = focal length, v = image distance, u = object distance
  • h' = image height, h = object height

🎙️ These are the mathematical tools to precisely calculate image positions and sizes without drawing ray diagrams. Remember the sign conventions carefully for 'u', 'v', and 'f' when solving problems.

Worked Example: Concave Mirror

  • An object is placed 10 cm from a concave mirror.
  • Focal length is 15 cm.
  • Find the position and nature of the image.

🎙️ Let's apply the mirror formula. Here, u = -10 cm (object left of mirror), f = -15 cm (concave mirror). Using 1/v = 1/f - 1/u, we get v = 30 cm. Since v is positive, the image is virtual and formed behind the mirror.

Laws of Refraction

  • Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
  • Snell's Law: sin i / sin r = constant (refractive index).
  • Constant depends on the pair of media and light's colour.

🎙️ Similar to reflection, the rays and normal lie in the same plane. Snell's Law quantifies the bending, showing the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction through a constant called the refractive index.

Refractive Index

  • Measure of how much light bends when entering a medium.
  • Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed in medium (n = c/v).
  • Higher refractive index means denser medium, more bending.

🎙️ The refractive index tells us how optically dense a material is. A higher refractive index means light slows down more in that medium, causing it to bend more significantly when passing into it from a rarer medium.

Lenses: Concave & Convex

  • Concave lens: Thinner in the middle, diverges light.

🎙️ Lenses are transparent materials that refract light. Convex lenses are like magnifying glasses, bringing light rays together. Concave lenses spread light rays out, making objects appear smaller.

Lens Formula and Magnification

  • Lens Formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
  • Magnification (m): h'/h = v/u
  • Note the minus sign difference from mirror formula for 'u' in the lens formula.

🎙️ The lens formula is very similar to the mirror formula, but pay close attention to the sign between 1/v and 1/u. Magnification also helps us determine if the image is enlarged or reduced, and erect or inverted.
