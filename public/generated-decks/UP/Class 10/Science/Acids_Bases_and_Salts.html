What are Acids?

  • Substances that taste sour.
  • Turn blue litmus paper red.
  • Produce H+ ions in water.

🎙️ Acids are chemical compounds that have distinct properties. Think of the sour taste of lemon or vinegar – that's due to acids. They are also corrosive and react with certain metals.

Strong vs. Weak Acids

  • Strong acids fully dissociate in water (e.g., HCl).
  • Weak acids partially dissociate in water (e.g., Acetic Acid).
  • Strong acids are more corrosive and dangerous.

🎙️ The strength of an acid depends on how much it breaks apart into ions in water. Strong acids release many H+ ions, while weak acids release fewer.

How to Identify Acids: Indicators

  • Litmus paper: Blue to Red.
  • Methyl Orange: Orange to Red.
  • Phenolphthalein: Remains colourless.

🎙️ Indicators are special substances that change colour in the presence of an acid or a base. They help us identify if a solution is acidic without tasting it, which can be dangerous.

What are Bases?

  • Substances that taste bitter and feel soapy.
  • Turn red litmus paper blue.
  • Produce OH- ions in water.

🎙️ Now let's move to the opposite end of the spectrum: Bases. They have a distinct bitter taste and a slippery, soapy feel. Many cleaning products contain bases.

  • Strong bases fully dissociate in water (e.g., NaOH).

  • Strong bases are corrosive.

🎙️ Similar to acids, bases also vary in strength. Strong bases release many OH- ions in water, making them highly reactive and corrosive.

The pH Scale: Measuring Acidic or Basic

  • Measures the concentration of H+ ions.
  • Scale from 0 to 14.
  • 0-7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, 7-14 is basic.

🎙️ The pH scale is a universal way to measure how acidic or basic a solution is. It gives us a numerical value, making comparisons easy.

📏

pH in Everyday Life

  • Our blood pH is around 7.4 (slightly basic).
  • Acid rain has a low pH, damaging environment.
  • Soil pH affects crop growth.

🎙️ pH is vital for many natural processes and our survival. Maintaining the correct pH is crucial for health, agriculture, and environmental balance.

Neutralization Reaction

  • Acid reacts with a base.
  • Forms salt and water.
  • H+ ions combine with OH- ions.

🎙️ When an acid and a base react, they cancel out each other's properties in a process called neutralization. The products are always a salt and water.

Worked Example: Neutralization

  • Hydrochloric acid reacts with Sodium Hydroxide.
  • Forms Sodium Chloride (common salt) and water.

What are Salts?

  • Ionic compounds formed from neutralization.
  • Consist of a cation from a base and an anion from an acid.
  • Many are soluble in water.

🎙️ Salts are not just the table salt we use; they are a broad class of compounds formed when acids and bases react. They are essential in many chemical processes and industries.

🧂
Table Salt
18 / 25

Types of Salts

  • Neutral Salts: Formed from strong acid + strong base (e.g., NaCl).
  • Acidic Salts: Formed from strong acid + weak base (e.g., NH4Cl).
  • Basic Salts: Formed from weak acid + strong base (e.g., Na2CO3).

🎙️ The pH of a salt solution depends on the strength of the acid and base from which it was formed. This determines if the salt itself will be neutral, acidic, or basic.
