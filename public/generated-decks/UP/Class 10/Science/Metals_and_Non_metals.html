Why Study Metals and Non-metals?

  • Understand materials around us
  • Learn their properties and uses
  • Foundation for advanced chemistry

🎙️ Everything you see, from the pen you write with to the phone you use, is made of either metals, non-metals, or their compounds. Knowing their properties helps us choose the right material for the right job.

What are Metals?

  • Elements that readily form positive ions
  • Typically shiny, hard, and good conductors
  • Found on the left side of the periodic table

🎙️ Metals are a very important group of elements. Think of common items like gold, silver, iron, and copper. They share many common physical properties which we will discuss.

⚙️
Building Blocks
4 / 25

Metals are Lustrous

  • Possess a shiny surface
  • Reflect light well
  • Example: Gold, Silver, Copper

🎙️ The shiny appearance of metals is called metallic luster. This property makes them useful for making jewellery and decorative items, as they look attractive.

✨
Shiny Surface
5 / 25

Metals are Hard

  • Generally solid and strong
  • Resist scratching and bending
  • Example: Iron, Steel (alloy)

🎙️ Most metals are hard and strong, which is why they are used in construction and for making tools. However, there are exceptions like sodium and potassium, which are soft enough to be cut with a knife.

💪
Strong Material
6 / 25

Metals are Malleable

  • Can be hammered into thin sheets
  • Without breaking
  • Example: Aluminium foil, Iron sheets

🎙️ Malleability is the property that allows metals to be flattened. This is why you can make thin aluminium foils for wrapping food or iron sheets for roofing.

🥞
Thin Sheets
7 / 25

Metals are Ductile

  • Can be drawn into thin wires
  • Without breaking
  • Example: Copper wires, Gold jewellery wire

🎙️ Ductility is another fascinating property of metals. It allows us to make long, thin wires from metals like copper for electrical circuits and gold for intricate jewellery designs.

🧵
Thin Wires
8 / 25

Metals are Good Conductors of Heat

  • Allow heat to pass through easily
  • Feel hot quickly when heated
  • Example: Cooking utensils made of copper/aluminium

🎙️ Because metals conduct heat well, they are ideal for making cooking pots and pans. The heat from the stove quickly transfers to the food through the metal utensil.

🔥
Heat Transfer
9 / 25

Metals are Good Conductors of Electricity

  • Allow electric current to flow through easily
  • Have free electrons
  • Example: Copper wires in electrical circuits

🎙️ This is a very important property. The free electrons in metals allow electricity to pass through them, which is why copper and aluminium are used extensively in electrical wiring.

Metals are Sonorous

  • Produce a ringing sound
  • When struck with a hard object
  • Example: School bell, Temple bells

🎙️ Have you ever noticed the sound a metal bell makes? This ringing sound is due to the sonorous property of metals. Non-metals do not produce such a sound.

🔔
Ringing Sound

Metals have High Melting and Boiling Points

  • Require a lot of energy to melt or boil
  • Strong metallic bonds
  • Example: Iron melts at 1538°C

🎙️ Most metals have very high melting and boiling points, meaning they remain solid at room temperature. This makes them suitable for applications requiring high temperatures, like industrial furnaces.

🌡️

What are Non-metals?

  • Elements that readily form negative ions
  • Typically dull, brittle, and poor conductors
  • Found on the right side of the periodic table

🎙️ Now let's shift our focus to non-metals. These elements have properties quite opposite to metals. They exist as solids, liquids, or gases at room temperature.

☁️
Diverse Forms
14 / 25

Non-metals are Non-lustrous

  • Generally have a dull appearance
  • Do not reflect light well
  • Example: Sulphur, Carbon (coal)

🎙️ Unlike metals, non-metals usually lack that characteristic shine. They appear dull and powdery or gaseous. There are a few interesting exceptions, which we will see later.

🌫️
Dull Surface
15 / 25

Non-metals are Soft

  • Generally soft or brittle solids
  • Break easily when struck
  • Example: Sulphur, Phosphorus

🎙️ Most solid non-metals are soft and brittle, meaning they will break into pieces if you try to hammer them. They don't have the strength of metals.

💔
Easily Broken
16 / 25

Non-metals are Non-malleable and Non-ductile

  • Cannot be hammered into sheets
  • Cannot be drawn into wires
  • They are brittle and break instead

🎙️ If you try to hammer a piece of coal, it will simply break into powder. This shows that non-metals are neither malleable nor ductile, unlike metals.

🔨
Brittle
17 / 25

Non-metals are Poor Conductors of Heat and Electricity

  • Do not allow heat to pass through easily
  • Do not allow electric current to flow
  • Example: Wood, Plastic (insulators)

🎙️ Non-metals generally act as insulators, meaning they resist the flow of heat and electricity. This is why electrical wires are covered with plastic, which is a non-metal.

🚫⚡
No Flow
18 / 25

Non-metals are Non-sonorous

  • Do not produce a ringing sound
  • When struck with a hard object
  • Example: Sulphur powder, Carbon chunk

🎙️ If you hit a piece of chalk or a lump of coal, it will just make a dull thud, or break. This confirms that non-metals do not possess the sonorous property.

🔇
Silent
19 / 25

Non-metals have Low Melting and Boiling Points

  • Require less energy to melt or boil
  • Often gases or liquids at room temperature
  • Example: Oxygen, Nitrogen (gases)

🎙️ Many non-metals exist as gases (like oxygen, nitrogen) or liquids (like bromine) at room temperature. The solids (like sulphur, phosphorus) have relatively low melting points.

🧊
Low Temperature
20 / 25

Interesting Exceptions in Metals

  • Mercury: Liquid metal at room temperature (used in thermometers)
  • Sodium & Potassium: Soft metals, can be cut with a knife
  • Gallium & Caesium: Very low melting points, melt in your palm

🎙️ While we discussed general properties, nature always has its exceptions! Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. Sodium and potassium are surprisingly soft for metals.

💧
Liquid Metal
21 / 25

Interesting Exceptions in Non-metals

  • Diamond: Hardest known natural substance (form of Carbon)
  • Graphite: Good conductor of electricity (form of Carbon)
  • Iodine: Lustrous non-metal (shiny crystals)

🎙️ Non-metals also have their unique exceptions. Diamond, a form of carbon, is extremely hard. Graphite, another form of carbon, is an excellent electrical conductor, unlike most non-metals. Iodine even has a metallic luster!

💎
Hard & Shiny

Everyday Uses of Metals

  • Construction: Iron, Steel for buildings, bridges
  • Electrical: Copper, Aluminium for wires
  • Utensils & Jewellery: Aluminium, Stainless Steel, Gold, Silver

🎙️ Metals are indispensable in our daily lives. From the sturdy buildings we live in to the cars we drive and the jewellery we wear, metals are everywhere. Their unique properties make them highly valuable.

Everyday Uses of Non-metals

  • Life Support: Oxygen for breathing
  • Fertilizers: Nitrogen, Phosphorus for plant growth
  • Fuel: Carbon (coal), Hydrogen (fuels)

🎙️ Non-metals are equally vital, though often less visible. Oxygen is essential for respiration, nitrogen and phosphorus are crucial for agriculture, and carbon is a primary component of fuels.

Recap: Metals and Non-metals

  • Metals: Lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors, high MP/BP.
  • Non-metals: Dull, soft/brittle, non-malleable, non-ductile, non-sonorous, poor conductors, low MP/BP.
  • Remember important exceptions like Mercury, Diamond, Graphite, Iodine.
