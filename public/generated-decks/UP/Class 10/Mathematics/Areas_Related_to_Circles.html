Revisiting the Circle: Basic Concepts

  • A set of points equidistant from a fixed point (center).
  • It's a two-dimensional closed curve.
  • Every point on the boundary is the same distance from the center.

🎙️ Before we dive into new concepts, let's quickly recall what a circle is. Remember, it's defined by its center and all points that are an equal distance away from it.

Important Circle Terms: Radius, Diameter, Circumference

  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on the circle.
  • Diameter (d): Distance across the circle through the center (d = 2r).
  • Circumference (C): The total distance around the circle (C = 2πr).

🎙️ These terms are fundamental to understanding circles. The radius, diameter, and circumference are key measurements we'll use repeatedly in our calculations today.

🎙️ What exactly is 'area'? It's simply the measure of the flat space enclosed by a shape. For a circle, it's the entire region within its boundary.

Area of a Circle: The Formula

  • The area (A) of a circle is given by A = πr².
  • Here, 'r' is the radius of the circle.
  • 'π' (Pi) is a mathematical constant, approximately 22/7 or 3.14.

🎙️ This is the most important formula for today! The area of a circle depends only on its radius and the constant Pi. Remember, Pi is an irrational number, so we use approximations for calculations.

Worked Example 1: Finding Area of a CD

  • Problem: Find the area of a CD with a radius of 6 cm.
  • Step 1: Write the formula: A = πr².
  • Step 2: Substitute values: A = (22/7) × 6².
  • Step 3: Calculate: A = (22/7) × 36 ≈ 113.14 cm².

Introducing the Sector of a Circle

  • A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • It looks like a slice of pizza or a piece of cake.
  • The angle between the two radii is called the 'angle of the sector'.

🎙️ Imagine cutting a slice out of a round cake – that's a sector! It's defined by two radii and the curved part of the circle between them. The size of the slice depends on the angle at the center.

Area of a Sector: The Formula

  • Area of Sector = (θ/360°) × πr².
  • 'θ' is the angle of the sector in degrees.
  • This formula calculates a fraction of the total circle's area.

🎙️ The area of a sector is proportional to the angle it subtends at the center. If the angle is 90 degrees, it's one-fourth of the total circle's area. This formula helps us find that exact fraction.

Worked Example 2: Finding Sector Area

  • Problem: Find the area of a sector with radius 7 cm and angle 60°.
  • Step 1: Formula: A = (θ/360°) × πr².
  • Step 2: Substitute: A = (60/360) × (22/7) × 7².
  • Step 3: Calculate: A = (1/6) × (22/7) × 49 = (1/6) × 22 × 7 ≈ 25.67 cm².

🎙️ Let's work through this example step-by-step. Remember to simplify fractions where possible to make your calculations easier. The angle is a fraction of the full circle, so the area will be a fraction of the full circle's area too.

Understanding the Segment of a Circle

  • A segment is the region bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • It's like a 'slice' of a circle, but cut straight across.
  • There can be a minor segment and a major segment.

🎙️ Now, let's talk about segments. Imagine drawing a straight line, called a chord, across a circle. The area enclosed by this chord and the curved part of the circle (the arc) is a segment. It's different from a sector!

Area of a Segment: The Formula

  • Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle.
  • The triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord.
  • Area of triangle = (1/2)r²sinθ (for acute/obtuse angle) or (1/2) × base × height.

🎙️ To find the area of a segment, we take the area of the entire sector and subtract the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord. This leaves us with just the crescent-shaped segment.

Worked Example 3: Finding Segment Area

  • Problem: Find the area of a segment if r = 10 cm, θ = 90°.
  • Step 1: Area of Sector = (90/360) × π(10)² = (1/4) × 3.14 × 100 = 78.5 cm².
  • Step 2: Area of Triangle = (1/2) × 10 × 10 = 50 cm² (since θ=90°, it's a right triangle).
  • Step 3: Area of Segment = 78.5 - 50 = 28.5 cm².

🎙️ This example shows how to calculate the segment area for a 90-degree angle, where the triangle is a right-angled triangle. Remember, for other angles, you might need trigonometry to find the triangle's area.

Worked Example 4: Area of a Shaded Region

  • Problem: Find the shaded area of a square (side 14 cm) with an inscribed circle.
  • Step 1: Area of Square = side² = 14² = 196 cm².
  • Step 2: Radius of inscribed circle = side/2 = 14/2 = 7 cm.
  • Step 3: Area of Circle = πr² = (22/7) × 7² = 154 cm².
  • Step 4: Shaded Area = Area of Square - Area of Circle = 196 - 154 = 42 cm².

🎙️ Here's an example of finding a shaded area. We calculate the area of the larger shape (the square) and then subtract the area of the smaller shape (the circle) that is removed from it. This is a common type of problem.

The Significance of Pi (π)

  • Pi is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.
  • It's a constant value for ALL circles, regardless of size.
  • Its approximate value is 3.14159... or 22/7.

🎙️ Pi is a magical number in mathematics! It connects the circumference and diameter of every circle. Its constant nature makes it incredibly useful in all calculations involving circles.
