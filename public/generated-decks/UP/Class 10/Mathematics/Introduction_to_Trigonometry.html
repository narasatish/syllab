What is Trigonometry?

  • Trigonon (Greek: triangle) + Metron (Greek: measure).
  • It's the study of relationships between the sides and angles of triangles.
  • Mainly focuses on right-angled triangles.

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's a branch of mathematics that helps us understand how the lengths of sides and the measures of angles in a triangle are connected. Our main focus will be on a special type of triangle: the right-angled triangle.

Revisiting the Right-Angled Triangle

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The longest side is always the 'Hypotenuse'.
  • The other two sides are 'legs'.

🎙️ Before we go further, let's quickly review what we know about right-angled triangles. Remember, it has one angle that's a perfect 90 degrees. The side opposite this 90-degree angle is always the longest side, and we call it the hypotenuse.

Identifying Sides: Opposite & Adjacent

  • These sides depend on the 'reference angle' (θ).
  • Opposite Side: Directly across from the reference angle.
  • Adjacent Side: Next to the reference angle, not the hypotenuse.

🎙️ Now, this is crucial for trigonometry. The names of the other two sides, 'opposite' and 'adjacent', are not fixed. They change depending on which acute angle you are focusing on in the triangle. The opposite side is the one furthest from your chosen angle, and the adjacent side is the one next to it, but not the hypotenuse.

Introducing Trigonometric Ratios

  • These are ratios of the lengths of the sides of a right triangle.
  • They relate an angle to the side lengths.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are simply fractions formed by dividing the lengths of two sides of a right-angled triangle. These ratios have specific names and values that depend only on the angle, not the size of the triangle itself. Let's learn the first three fundamental ratios.

🔗
Angle-Side Connections
7 / 25

The Sine Ratio (sin θ)

  • Defined as the ratio of the 'Opposite side' to the 'Hypotenuse'.
  • Formula: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse.
  • Often abbreviated as 'sin'.

🎙️ The first ratio is called Sine, written as 'sin'. When we talk about the sine of an angle theta, we are referring to the length of the side opposite to that angle, divided by the length of the hypotenuse. Remember this formula: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse.

⬆️
Opposite / Hypotenuse
8 / 25

The Cosine Ratio (cos θ)

  • Defined as the ratio of the 'Adjacent side' to the 'Hypotenuse'.
  • Formula: cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.
  • Often abbreviated as 'cos'.

🎙️ Next up is Cosine, abbreviated as 'cos'. For an angle theta, the cosine is the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to that angle, divided by the length of the hypotenuse. So, cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse. Keep these definitions clear in your mind.

➡️
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
9 / 25

The Tangent Ratio (tan θ)

  • Defined as the ratio of the 'Opposite side' to the 'Adjacent side'.
  • Formula: tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • Often abbreviated as 'tan'.

🎙️ Finally, we have the Tangent ratio, or 'tan'. The tangent of an angle theta is the ratio of the length of the side opposite to the angle, divided by the length of the side adjacent to the angle. This means tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent. Notice this ratio doesn't involve the hypotenuse directly.

🪜
Opposite / Adjacent

Mnemonic: Remembering the Ratios

  • SOH CAH TOA is a popular way to remember.
  • SOH: Sin = Opposite / Hypotenuse.
  • CAH: Cos = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.
  • TOA: Tan = Opposite / Adjacent.

🎙️ Here's a handy trick to remember these three ratios: SOH CAH TOA. Say it aloud a few times. SOH helps you recall Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse. CAH reminds you Cosine is Adjacent over Hypotenuse. And TOA tells you Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent. Practice this!

Introducing Reciprocal Ratios

  • Each of the three main ratios has a reciprocal.
  • These are Cosecant (cosec), Secant (sec), and Cotangent (cot).
  • They are simply 1 divided by the primary ratio.

🎙️ Just like how every number has a reciprocal (like 2 and 1/2), each trigonometric ratio also has a reciprocal. These reciprocal ratios are equally important and will complete our set of six trigonometric functions. Let's look at each one.

🔄
Inverse Relationships
13 / 25

The Cosecant Ratio (cosec θ)

  • Reciprocal of Sine: cosec θ = 1 / sin θ.
  • Formula: cosec θ = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • Often abbreviated as 'csc' or 'cosec'.

🎙️ Cosecant, or 'cosec', is the reciprocal of sine. So, if sin θ is Opposite over Hypotenuse, then cosec θ is Hypotenuse over Opposite. It's like flipping the fraction. Remember this pairing: sine and cosecant are reciprocals.

⬇️
Hypotenuse / Opposite
14 / 25

The Secant Ratio (sec θ)

  • Reciprocal of Cosine: sec θ = 1 / cos θ.
  • Formula: sec θ = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.
  • Often abbreviated as 'sec'.

🎙️ Next, Secant, or 'sec', is the reciprocal of cosine. So, if cos θ is Adjacent over Hypotenuse, then sec θ is Hypotenuse over Adjacent. Again, just flip the fraction. Cosine and secant are a reciprocal pair.

⬅️
Hypotenuse / Adjacent
15 / 25

The Cotangent Ratio (cot θ)

  • Reciprocal of Tangent: cot θ = 1 / tan θ.
  • Formula: cot θ = Adjacent / Opposite.
  • Often abbreviated as 'cot'.

🎙️ Lastly, Cotangent, or 'cot', is the reciprocal of tangent. Since tan θ is Opposite over Adjacent, cot θ is Adjacent over Opposite. These three reciprocal ratios complete our set of six fundamental trigonometric ratios.

🪨
Adjacent / Opposite

Important Relations Between Ratios

  • tan θ = sin θ / cos θ.
  • cot θ = cos θ / sin θ (since cot is reciprocal of tan).
  • These identities are very useful in solving problems.

🎙️ Beyond just reciprocals, there are other important relationships. One key identity is that tangent of an angle is equal to the sine of the angle divided by its cosine. And naturally, cotangent is the cosine divided by the sine. These relations simplify many calculations.

Example 2: Using Reciprocal Ratios

  • If sin A = 3/5, find cosec A.
  • cosec A = 1 / sin A = 1 / (3/5) = 5/3.
  • Reciprocal ratios make calculations quicker.

🎙️ Let's try another example. If you are given that sin A is 3/5, and you need to find cosec A, you don't need to draw a triangle again. Simply remember that cosec A is the reciprocal of sin A. So, you just flip the fraction to get 5/3. Simple, right?

Real-Life Application: Measuring Heights

  • Imagine you want to find the height of a tree.

🎙️ Here’s how trigonometry helps in real life. Suppose you want to find the height of a tall tree. You can measure your distance from the tree and use a device to measure the angle from the ground to the top of the tree. This forms a right triangle, and with the tangent ratio, you can easily find the height!

Angle of Elevation

  • The angle formed when you look UP at an object.
  • It's between your line of sight and the horizontal line.
  • Think of looking up at a kite or a bird.

🎙️ The 'angle of elevation' is a common term in trigonometry. It's the angle created when you're looking upwards from a horizontal line to an object above you. For example, if you're looking up at the top of a pole, the angle your gaze makes with the ground is the angle of elevation.

Angle of Depression

  • The angle formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • It's between your line of sight and the horizontal line.
  • Think of looking down from a cliff at a boat.

🎙️ Conversely, the 'angle of depression' is the angle formed when you look downwards from a horizontal line to an object below you. Imagine you are on top of a building looking down at a car. The angle between your horizontal line of sight and your line of sight to the car is the angle of depression.
