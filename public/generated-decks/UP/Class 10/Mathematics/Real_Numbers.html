Euclid's Division Lemma (EDL)

  • Given positive integers 'a' and 'b'.
  • There exist unique integers 'q' and 'r'.
  • Such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b.

🎙️ Euclid's Division Lemma is a fundamental rule about division. It simply states that when you divide one positive integer by another, you get a unique quotient and a unique remainder.

➗
Division Rule

EDL: A Real-Life Scenario

  • You have 17 sweets and 5 friends.
  • You divide 17 by 5: 17 = 5 × 3 + 2.
  • Each friend gets 3 sweets, and 2 sweets are left over.

🎙️ Think about sharing sweets! If you have 17 sweets for 5 friends, each friend gets 3, and you're left with 2. Here, a=17, b=5, q=3, and r=2. The remainder (2) is less than the divisor (5).

Euclid's Division Algorithm (EDA)

  • A technique to compute the HCF (Highest Common Factor).
  • Based on Euclid's Division Lemma.
  • Involves a series of well-defined steps.

🎙️ The algorithm is a step-by-step procedure to find the HCF of two numbers. You keep applying the lemma until you get a remainder of zero, and the divisor at that stage is your HCF.

1Apply EDL (a=bq+r)
2If r=0, HCF is b
3If r≠0, replace a with b, and b with r
4Repeat until remainder is 0
11 / 25

Finding HCF using EDA: Example

  • Find HCF of 135 and 225.
  • Step 1: 225 = 135 × 1 + 90
  • Step 2: 135 = 90 × 1 + 45
  • Step 3: 90 = 45 × 2 + 0. HCF is 45.

🎙️ Let's work through an example. We apply the division lemma repeatedly. Notice how the divisor becomes the new dividend and the remainder becomes the new divisor in each step, until the remainder is zero.

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (FTA)

  • Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes.
  • This factorisation is unique.
  • The order of the prime factors does not matter.

🎙️ This theorem is very powerful! It says that every number greater than 1 is either a prime number itself, or it can be broken down into a unique set of prime numbers multiplied together, like building blocks.

🌳
Prime Factor Tree
13 / 25

FTA: Building Blocks of Numbers

  • Consider the number 12.
  • 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 (or 2² × 3).
  • No other combination of prime numbers will multiply to 12.

🎙️ Just like a building is made of unique bricks, every composite number is made of a unique set of prime numbers. For example, 12 is always 2 multiplied by 2 and then by 3, no matter how you arrange them.

🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱🧱
Primes Make Up Numbers

Applications of FTA: HCF and LCM

  • HCF: Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor.
  • LCM: Product of the greatest power of all prime factors.
  • Prime factorization simplifies finding HCF and LCM.

🎙️ The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic makes finding HCF and LCM very systematic. Once you have the prime factors, it's easy to pick out the common ones for HCF and all factors with their highest powers for LCM.

✖️
Multiplying Factors
15 / 25

Calculating HCF & LCM: Example

  • Find HCF and LCM of 6 and 20.
  • Prime factors: 6 = 2 × 3; 20 = 2² × 5.
  • HCF (6, 20) = 2¹ = 2.
  • LCM (6, 20) = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.

🎙️ Let's apply it: 6 is 2 times 3, and 20 is 2 squared times 5. The common prime factor is 2, with the lowest power being 2 to the power of 1, so HCF is 2. For LCM, we take 2 squared, 3, and 5, multiplying them to get 60.

Relationship between HCF and LCM

  • For any two positive integers 'a' and 'b'.
  • HCF (a, b) × LCM (a, b) = a × b.
  • This formula is very useful for calculations.

🎙️ There's a beautiful relationship between HCF, LCM, and the numbers themselves. The product of the HCF and LCM of two numbers is always equal to the product of the two numbers.

🤝
Product Relationship
17 / 25

Verifying the Relationship: Example

  • Numbers: 6 and 20.
  • HCF (6, 20) = 2.
  • LCM (6, 20) = 60.
  • HCF × LCM = 2 × 60 = 120. And a × b = 6 × 20 = 120. It matches!

🎙️ Using our previous example of 6 and 20, we found HCF to be 2 and LCM to be 60. Their product is 120. The product of the numbers 6 and 20 is also 120. The relationship holds true!

Revisiting Irrational Numbers (Brief Proof)

  • We can prove numbers like √2 are irrational.
  • Method of proof by contradiction.

🎙️ How do we know for sure that numbers like root 2 are irrational? We use a powerful technique called proof by contradiction. We assume the opposite, and if it leads to an impossible situation, our initial assumption must be false.

Decimal Expansions of Rational Numbers

  • Terminating: Denominator (q) has only 2 or 5 as prime factors (2^m × 5^n).
  • Non-terminating Repeating: Denominator has prime factors other than 2 or 5.
  • This helps classify rational numbers without actual division.

🎙️ Remember how rational numbers have terminating or repeating decimals? We can actually tell which type it will be just by looking at the prime factors of the denominator of the fraction in its simplest form.

➡️
Fraction to Decimal
20 / 25

Predicting Decimal Type: Example

  • Consider 3/8: Denominator 8 = 2³. (Only factor 2). This will terminate (0.375).
  • Consider 1/7: Denominator 7. (Factor 7). This will be non-terminating repeating (0.142857...).
  • This rule applies only when the fraction is in simplest form.

🎙️ Let's test this. For 3/8, the denominator is 8, which is 2 cubed. Since it only has 2 as a prime factor, its decimal expansion terminates. For 1/7, the denominator is 7, which is not 2 or 5, so its decimal expansion will repeat.

1/7 (Repeating)
