Surface Area vs. Volume

  • Surface Area: Total area of all surfaces (like painting).
  • Volume: Space occupied by an object (like filling).

🎙️ Surface area is about the outside, like the wrapper on a gift. Volume is about the inside, like the gift itself. Both are crucial for understanding 3D objects and their properties.

Surface Area: Cuboid and Cube

  • Cuboid Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl).
  • Cube Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6a².

🎙️ A cuboid is like a brick, and a cube is like a dice. Remember, TSA means Total Surface Area, covering all sides. 'a' is the side length of the cube, and 'l, b, h' are dimensions for the cuboid.

Worked Example: Cuboid SA

  • Room (cuboid) l=5m, b=4m, h=3m.
  • Area to paint (4 walls + ceiling) = 2(lh + bh) + lb
  • = 2(5x3 + 4x3) + (5x4) = 74 m².

🎙️ When painting a room, you usually don't paint the floor! So, we adapt the TSA formula. We calculate the area of the four walls (Lateral Surface Area) and add the ceiling area. This shows a real-world application.

Surface Area: Right Circular Cylinder

  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh (curved part).
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr(r + h) (curved + 2 bases).

🎙️ Think of a can of juice. The curved part is the label, and the two circles are the top and bottom. The total surface area includes all these parts. 'r' is the radius and 'h' is the height.

Worked Example: Cylinder SA

  • Find CSA of a can with r=3.5cm, h=10cm.
  • CSA = 2 x (22/7) x 3.5 x 10 = 220 cm².

🎙️ If you're designing a label for a cylindrical can, you need to know its curved surface area. Remember to use the correct value of π and be careful with your calculations.

Surface Area: Right Circular Cone

  • Slant height l = √(r² + h²).
  • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = πrl.
  • Total Surface Area (TSA) = πr(l + r).

🎙️ A cone has a pointy top and a circular base. The slant height 'l' is important here, and we find it using Pythagoras theorem. TSA includes the base, CSA only the curved part.

Worked Example: Cone SA

  • A conical funnel has r=7cm, h=24cm. Find CSA.
  • l = √(7²+24²) = 25cm.
  • CSA = π x 7 x 25 = 175π cm².

🎙️ If you're making a funnel, you're essentially creating its curved surface. This example shows how to calculate the material needed. Don't forget to calculate the slant height first!

Surface Area: Sphere and Hemisphere

  • Sphere Surface Area (SA) = 4πr².
  • Hemisphere Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πr².
  • Hemisphere Total Surface Area (TSA) = 3πr².

🎙️ A sphere is a perfectly round ball. A hemisphere is half a sphere. Notice that the hemisphere's TSA includes its flat circular base, which is why it's 3πr² and not just half of the sphere's SA.

Worked Example: Hemisphere SA

  • A hemispherical dome has diameter 14m. Find CSA to paint.
  • r = 14/2 = 7m.
  • CSA = 2π(7)² = 2 x (22/7) x 49 = 308 m².

🎙️ If you're painting the outer surface of a hemispherical dome, you need its curved surface area. Always double-check if the problem gives radius or diameter before calculating!

Volume: Cuboid and Cube

  • Cuboid Volume = l x b x h.
  • Cube Volume = a³.

🎙️ Volume tells us how much space an object occupies or how much it can hold. For cuboids and cubes, it's a simple multiplication of their dimensions: length, breadth, and height for cuboid, and side cubed for a cube.

Worked Example: Cuboid Volume

  • Find volume of a rectangular water tank l=2m, b=1.5m, h=1m.
  • Volume = 2 x 1.5 x 1 = 3 m³.
  • (1 m³ = 1000 Litres). So, 3000 Litres.

🎙️ This is a very practical application! Knowing the volume of a water tank helps us understand how much water it can store. Remember the common conversion from cubic meters to litres.

🎙️ Think of a cylindrical glass or a water pipe. The volume is the area of its base (πr²) multiplied by its height. This makes intuitive sense as you're stacking circular layers.

Volume: Right Circular Cone

🎙️ The volume of a cone is directly related to the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height. It's exactly one-third of the cylinder's volume. This is a very interesting relationship to remember!

Volume: Sphere and Hemisphere

  • Sphere Volume = (4/3)πr³.
  • Hemisphere Volume = (2/3)πr³.

🎙️ These formulas might seem a bit more complex, but they are just as important. A hemisphere's volume is simply half of a sphere's volume, as expected, as it's literally half the shape.

Example Idea: A Tent (Combined Shape)

  • A tent might be a cylinder with a conical top.
  • To find canvas needed (SA), we add CSA of cylinder + CSA of cone.

🎙️ For a tent, we would typically only need the curved surfaces for the canvas. We wouldn't include the base if it's open to the ground. This shows how we adapt our thinking for combined shapes.
