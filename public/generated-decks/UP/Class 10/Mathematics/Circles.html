Understanding the Basics: What is a Circle?

  • A set of points equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.
  • The constant distance is called the 'radius'.

🎙️ Imagine drawing a perfect round shape with a compass. The point where you place the compass is the center, and the distance from that point to the pencil tip is the radius. Every point on the circle is exactly that distance from the center.

Parts of a Circle: Radius & Diameter

  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on circle.
  • Diameter (d): Line segment passing through the center.
  • Diameter is twice the radius (d = 2r).

🎙️ The radius is like the arm of a clock, extending from the center to the edge. The diameter is the longest chord possible in a circle, cutting it exactly in half through the center. Remember, the diameter is always double the radius.

Parts of a Circle: Chord & Arc

  • Chord: Line segment joining any two points on the circle.
  • Arc: A continuous piece or segment of the circle's circumference.
  • A diameter is the longest chord of a circle.

🎙️ Imagine taking a bite out of a circular cookie; the curved edge of your bite is an arc, and if you drew a straight line across the bite, that would be a chord. The diameter is a special type of chord that passes through the center.

Parts of a Circle: Segment & Sector

  • Segment: Region bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • Sector: Region bounded by two radii and the arc between them.
  • Think of a pizza slice as a sector!

🎙️ If you cut a slice of pizza, that's a perfect example of a sector – it has two straight edges (radii) and a curved crust (arc). A segment, on the other hand, is the area you get if you draw a straight line across the pizza and look at one of the pieces.

Measuring the Boundary: Circumference

  • The total distance around the circle.
  • Similar to the perimeter of other shapes.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd.
  • π (pi) is a constant, approximately 3.14 or 22/7.

🎙️ If you were to walk along the edge of a circular park, the total distance you cover would be its circumference. It's the 'length' of the circle. The value of pi is crucial here, linking the diameter or radius to the circumference.

Measuring the Space: Area of a Circle

  • The amount of surface enclosed by the circle.
  • Formula: A = πr².
  • Units are always squared (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ While circumference measures the boundary, the area measures the space inside the boundary. Think of a circular field; the area tells you how much grass covers it. Remember, area is always expressed in square units.

Introducing Tangents: Touching Just Once

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • This point is called the 'point of contact'.
  • It never enters the interior of the circle.

🎙️ Imagine a straight road just barely touching the edge of a perfectly round pond. That road is like a tangent line – it meets the circle at only one single point, without crossing into it. This is a very important concept in circles.

Secants: Crossing Through the Circle

  • A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.
  • It passes through the interior of the circle.
  • A chord is a part of a secant within the circle.

🎙️ Unlike a tangent, a secant line cuts right through the circle, touching it at two different places. Think of cutting a piece of paper with scissors; the path of the scissors is like a secant. The segment of the secant inside the circle is a chord.

Key Property: Tangent and Radius

  • The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius.
  • The radius passes through the point of contact.
  • This forms a 90-degree angle.

🎙️ This is a fundamental property! If you draw a radius to the exact point where a tangent touches the circle, they will always meet at a perfect right angle, 90 degrees. This property is vital for solving many problems related to circles and tangents.

How Many Tangents from a Point?

  • Point Inside: Zero tangents.
  • Point On: Exactly one tangent.
  • Point Outside: Exactly two tangents.

🎙️ The number of tangents you can draw to a circle depends entirely on where your starting point is. If you're inside, you can't draw any. If you're right on the circle, you can draw one. But from an external point, you can always draw two distinct tangents.

Theorem 1: Tangent is Perpendicular to Radius

  • Statement: The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
  • Proof involves showing it's the shortest distance.
  • This theorem is crucial for many geometric proofs.

🎙️ This theorem formally states what we just discussed. It's not just a rule, it's a proven fact. The shortest distance from the center to the tangent line is along the radius, and the shortest distance is always perpendicular.

Worked Example 1: Using the Perpendicular Property

  • A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the center O at a point Q so that OQ = 12 cm. Find the length PQ.

🎙️ Let's apply Theorem 1. Since OP is the radius and PQ is the tangent, angle OPQ is 90 degrees. This forms a right-angled triangle OPQ, allowing us to use the Pythagorean theorem to find the unknown length PQ.

11. Draw diagram and identify right triangle.
22. Apply Pythagoras Theorem: OP² + PQ² = OQ².
33. Substitute values: 5² + PQ² = 12².
44. Calculate PQ: 25 + PQ² = 144 => PQ² = 119 => PQ = √119 cm.

Theorem 2: Equal Tangent Lengths

  • Statement: The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
  • If PA and PB are tangents from point P, then PA = PB.
  • This creates congruent triangles with the center.

🎙️ This is another very important theorem. If you stand outside a circle and draw two tangents to it, the distance from your point to where each tangent touches the circle will always be exactly the same. This property is often used in geometric proofs.

Worked Example 2: Using Equal Tangent Lengths

  • In a figure, if TP and TQ are two tangents to a circle with center O so that ∠POQ = 110°, then find ∠PTQ.

🎙️ Here we combine Theorem 1 (perpendicularity) with the properties of a quadrilateral. Since OP and OQ are radii, and TP and TQ are tangents, we have right angles at P and Q. Knowing the sum of angles in a quadrilateral helps us find the unknown angle.

11. Identify properties: OP ⊥ PT and OQ ⊥ QT (Theorem 1).
22. Sum of angles in quadrilateral OPTQ is 360°.
33. Substitute known angles: 90° + 90° + 110° + ∠PTQ = 360°.
44. Calculate ∠PTQ: 290° + ∠PTQ = 360° => ∠PTQ = 70°.

Circles Can Touch: Externally or Internally

  • Externally: The distance between centers is sum of radii (C1C2 = r1 + r2).
  • Internally: The distance between centers is difference of radii (C1C2 = |r1 - r2|).
  • They share exactly one common tangent at the point of contact.

🎙️ Circles don't always exist in isolation. They can touch each other in two ways. When they touch externally, their centers are far apart. When they touch internally, one circle is inside the other, and their centers are closer.

Common Tangents: Shared Lines

  • Maximum 4 common tangents (when circles are separate).
  • 3 common tangents (when circles touch externally).
  • 2 common tangents (when circles intersect).
  • 1 common tangent (when circles touch internally).
  • 0 common tangents (when one circle is inside another, not touching).
