What is Trigonometry?

  • Study of relationships between sides and angles of a right-angled triangle.
  • Key ratios: Sine, Cosine, Tangent.
  • Helps find unknown sides or angles.

🎙️ Before we jump into applications, let's quickly remember what trigonometry is. It's all about right-angled triangles and the special relationships between their angles and sides. We use ratios like sine, cosine, and tangent to solve for missing parts.

🎙️ You might be wondering, 'Why do we need to learn this?' Well, trigonometry helps us find heights and distances that are difficult or impossible to measure directly. Imagine measuring the height of a tall building without climbing it – that's what we'll learn!

Key Term 1: Line of Sight

  • The line from the eye of an observer to the object being viewed.
  • It's an imaginary line.
  • Forms the hypotenuse of our right triangle.

🎙️ Our first important term is 'Line of Sight'. This is simply the straight line connecting your eye to whatever you are looking at. Think of it as an invisible laser beam going from your eye to the object.

Key Term 2: Angle of Elevation

  • Angle formed when you look UP at an object.
  • Between the line of sight and the horizontal level.
  • The object is ABOVE the observer's eye level.

🎙️ When you look up at something, like a bird in the sky or the top of a building, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal ground is called the Angle of Elevation. Remember, 'elevation' means going higher.

Key Term 3: Angle of Depression

  • Angle formed when you look DOWN at an object.
  • Between the line of sight and the horizontal level.
  • The object is BELOW the observer's eye level.

🎙️ Now, let's consider the opposite. If you are on a tall building and look down at a car on the road, the angle your line of sight makes with the horizontal line *from your eye* is the Angle of Depression. 'Depression' means going lower.

Trigonometric Ratios: A Quick Look

  • Sine (sin) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Cosine (cos) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Tangent (tan) = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ Let's quickly refresh our memory on the three main ratios. Remember SOH CAH TOA. Sine uses Opposite and Hypotenuse. Cosine uses Adjacent and Hypotenuse. And Tangent uses Opposite and Adjacent. These are our tools!

Worked Example 1: Height of a Tower

  • From a point 15m away, the angle of elevation to the top of the tower is 60°.
  • Find the height of the tower.

🎙️ Let's try our first example. We have a tower, and we're standing 15 meters away from its base. When we look up, the angle of elevation to the top is 60 degrees. We need to find the height of this tower.

Solution 1: Height of a Tower

  • Diagram: Right triangle with base 15m, angle 60°.
  • To find: Opposite side (height). Known: Adjacent side (15m).
  • Ratio: tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • tan(60°) = height / 15
  • height = 15 * √3 meters.

🎙️ First, draw your triangle. The height is opposite to the 60° angle, and 15m is adjacent. So, we use tan. tan(60°) is √3. Therefore, height = 15 * √3 meters. Remember to include units!

Solution 2: Distance from a Lighthouse

  • Diagram: Horizontal line from top, angle 30° below.
  • Angle of depression = Angle of elevation (alternate interior angles).
  • To find: Adjacent side (distance). Known: Opposite side (75m).
  • Ratio: tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.
  • tan(30°) = 75 / distance
  • distance = 75√3 meters.

🎙️ Draw the lighthouse and the ship. The angle of depression is 30°. Remember, the angle of elevation from the ship to the top of the lighthouse will also be 30° (alternate interior angles). Now, we have the opposite side (height) and need the adjacent side (distance), so we use tan again. distance = 75√3 meters.

Worked Example 3: Two Objects

  • From the top of a 7m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60°.
  • The angle of depression of its foot is 45°.
  • Determine the height of the tower.

🎙️ This problem involves both elevation and depression. We are on a building, looking up at a tower and down at its base. This will create two right-angled triangles that share some information. We need to find the total height of the tower.

🏢
Building & Tower

Solution 3: Two Objects

  • 1. Draw two triangles: one for elevation, one for depression.
  • 2. From depression (45°), find distance to tower (base of building). tan(45°) = 7 / distance => distance = 7m.
  • 3. From elevation (60°), find tower height above building. tan(60°) = height_above / 7 => height_above = 7√3m.
  • 4. Total height = building height + height_above = 7 + 7√3 meters.

🎙️ This problem requires two steps. First, use the angle of depression to find the distance between the building and the tower. Since tan(45°) is 1, the distance is 7m. Then, use the angle of elevation and this distance to find the part of the tower above the building. Finally, add the building's height to get the total tower height.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Angle of Elevation and Depression.
  • Incorrectly identifying Opposite, Adjacent, Hypotenuse.
  • Using the wrong trigonometric ratio.
  • Calculation errors (especially with square roots).

🎙️ It's easy to make small errors in these problems. Always double-check if you're using the correct angle (elevation or depression). Make sure you identify the opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides correctly for your chosen angle. And, of course, be careful with your calculations!

Tips for Drawing Diagrams

  • Always draw a right-angled triangle.
  • Horizontal line represents ground/eye level.
  • Vertical line represents height.
  • Mark all known angles and lengths clearly.
  • Use dotted lines for horizontal eye level for depression angles.

🎙️ A clear diagram is half the battle won! Always start by drawing a right-angled triangle. Remember that the horizontal line is usually the ground or your eye level, and vertical lines are heights. Label everything neatly, especially the angles, to avoid confusion.
