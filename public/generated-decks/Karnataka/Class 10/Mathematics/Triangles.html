What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides
  • Has three vertices and three angles
  • Sum of interior angles is always 180°

🎙️ A triangle is the simplest polygon, defined by three straight lines connecting three points. Remember, the sum of its internal angles is always 180 degrees, a very important property.

Types of Triangles: By Sides

  • Equilateral: All 3 sides are equal
  • Isosceles: 2 sides are equal
  • Scalene: All 3 sides are different

🎙️ Triangles can be classified based on the lengths of their sides. An equilateral triangle is perfectly symmetrical, an isosceles has two equal sides, and a scalene has all unique side lengths.

Congruent Figures

  • Figures with exactly the same shape and size
  • One can be placed exactly over the other
  • Symbol: ≅

🎙️ Congruent figures are like identical twins; they are perfect copies of each other. If you can superimpose one figure onto another perfectly, they are congruent.

Similar Figures

  • Figures with the same shape but possibly different sizes
  • Corresponding angles are equal
  • Corresponding sides are proportional
  • Symbol: ~

🎙️ Similar figures maintain their shape even if their size changes. Imagine zooming in or out on an image; the objects remain similar. This concept is vital for understanding maps and models.

Congruent vs. Similar Figures

  • Congruent: Same shape AND same size
  • Similar: Same shape, DIFFERENT size possible
  • All congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent

🎙️ The key difference is size. Congruent means identical, while similar means proportional. Think of it this way: if two figures are congruent, they are also similar, but the reverse isn't always true.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem)

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle
  • It intersects the other two sides at distinct points
  • Then it divides the two sides in the same ratio

🎙️ This fundamental theorem, also known as Thales Theorem, helps us understand how parallel lines interact within a triangle. It establishes a crucial relationship between the segments formed on the sides.

Balancing ratios

BPT: Visual Explanation

  • Draw a triangle ABC
  • Draw line DE parallel to BC
  • D is on AB, E is on AC
  • Then AD/DB = AE/EC

🎙️ Imagine drawing a line inside a triangle that is perfectly parallel to its base. The BPT tells us that this parallel line will cut the other two sides into segments that maintain the same proportion.

2Line DE || BC
3Ratios are Equal

Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio
  • Then the line must be parallel to the third side

🎙️ The converse of BPT is equally important. It allows us to prove that a line is parallel to a side if we can show that it divides the other two sides proportionally. It's the 'if and only if' relationship.

Criteria for Similarity of Triangles

  • How do we prove two triangles are similar?
  • There are three main criteria:
  • 1. AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle)
  • 2. SSS (Side-Side-Side)
  • 3. SAS (Side-Angle-Side)

🎙️ Just like with congruence, we have specific rules or criteria to prove if two triangles are similar. These criteria simplify the process of identifying similar figures without measuring every single element.

✨
Ways to prove similarity
16 / 25

AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) Similarity

  • If corresponding angles of two triangles are equal
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ The AAA criterion is straightforward: if all three angles of one triangle are equal to the corresponding three angles of another, the triangles are similar. This is often shortened to AA similarity, as two angles imply the third.

1∠A = ∠P
2∠B = ∠Q
3∠C = ∠R
4ΔABC ~ ΔPQR
17 / 25

SSS (Side-Side-Side) Similarity

  • If the ratios of corresponding sides of two triangles are equal
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ For SSS similarity, we don't need to know the angles. If the corresponding sides are all in the same proportion, meaning their ratios are equal, then the triangles are similar.

1AB/PQ = k
2BC/QR = k
3CA/RP = k
4ΔABC ~ ΔPQR
18 / 25

SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Similarity

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other triangle
  • And the sides including these angles are proportional
  • Then the triangles are similar

🎙️ SAS similarity requires one pair of equal angles and the proportionality of the two sides that 'include' that angle. It's a powerful criterion for proving similarity with less information.

1∠A = ∠P
2AB/PQ = AC/PR
3ΔABC ~ ΔPQR

Worked Example: Using Similarity

  • Given ΔABC and ΔPQR
  • If ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q
  • Then ΔABC ~ ΔPQR (by AA similarity)
  • This means AB/PQ = BC/QR = AC/PR

🎙️ Let's see how these criteria work in practice. If we know two angles are equal, we can immediately conclude similarity. This then allows us to find unknown side lengths using the proportionality of sides.

Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles
  • Is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides

🎙️ This theorem provides a direct link between the side lengths and the areas of similar triangles. If the sides are in a ratio of 'k', then their areas will be in a ratio of 'k squared'.

🏞️
Area relationship

Pythagoras Theorem

  • In a right-angled triangle
  • The square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle)
  • Is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides
  • a² + b² = c²

🎙️ The Pythagoras Theorem is one of the most famous and useful theorems in mathematics. It only applies to right-angled triangles and helps us find unknown side lengths if two are known.

📐
Right triangle rule
