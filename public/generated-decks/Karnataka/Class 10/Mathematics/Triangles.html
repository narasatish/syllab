What is a Triangle?

  • A polygon with three sides and three vertices.
  • Sum of interior angles is always 180°.
  • Forms the simplest closed shape.

🎙️ At its core, a triangle is a three-sided polygon. It has three vertices, three sides, and three angles. A key property you already know is that the sum of its internal angles always adds up to 180 degrees. It's the simplest shape that can enclose an area.

Congruent Figures: Identical Twins

  • Two figures are congruent if they have the exact same shape and size.
  • They perfectly overlap each other.
  • Think of two ₹10 coins.

🎙️ Imagine two identical ₹10 coins. If you place one on top of the other, they match perfectly. This is what we mean by congruent figures – they are exact copies of each other, having the same shape and the same size. There's no difference between them.

Similar Figures: Same Shape, Different Size

  • Two figures are similar if they have the same shape but possibly different sizes.
  • One is an enlarged or reduced version of the other.
  • Think of a photo and its enlarged print.

🎙️ Now, think about a small photograph and its enlarged version. They both show the same image, meaning they have the same shape, but one is bigger than the other. These are called similar figures – they look alike, but their sizes can be different. All congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent.

Similarity of Triangles: The Core Idea

  • Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal.
  • AND their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).
  • Both conditions must be met for similarity.

🎙️ When we talk about similar triangles, it means they have the same 'form' but not necessarily the same 'dimensions'. This implies two crucial conditions: all their corresponding angles must be equal, and the ratio of their corresponding sides must be constant. These two properties go hand-in-hand.

Criterion 1: AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) Similarity

  • If two triangles have corresponding angles equal, then they are similar.
  • If ∠A=∠D, ∠B=∠E, ∠C=∠F, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.
  • This also implies sides are proportional.

🎙️ The AAA criterion is very powerful. If you can show that all three corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, then you've automatically proven they are similar. This also means that their corresponding sides will be in proportion, even if you haven't measured them yet.

📐
Angles Match
10 / 30

AAA Similarity: Worked Example

  • Given ΔABC with ∠A=50°, ∠B=60°.
  • Given ΔPQR with ∠P=50°, ∠Q=60°.
  • Are ΔABC and ΔPQR similar?

🎙️ In this example, we're given two angles for each triangle. First, we find the third angle using the angle sum property. Once we see that all three corresponding angles are equal, we can confidently say that the triangles are similar by the AAA criterion.

1Find ∠C and ∠R (180°-angles)
2∠C = 180-(50+60) = 70°
3∠R = 180-(50+60) = 70°
4Since ∠A=∠P, ∠B=∠Q, ∠C=∠R, Yes, by AAA.

Criterion 2: SSS (Side-Side-Side) Similarity

  • If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then they are similar.
  • If AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.
  • This also implies corresponding angles are equal.

🎙️ The SSS criterion focuses on the sides. If the ratio of all three pairs of corresponding sides is the same, then the triangles are similar. This means one triangle is a scaled version of the other. If this condition is met, their angles must also be equal.

📏
Sides in Proportion
12 / 30

SSS Similarity: Worked Example

  • Given ΔABC sides: AB=3, BC=4, CA=5.
  • Given ΔPQR sides: PQ=6, QR=8, RP=10.
  • Are ΔABC and ΔPQR similar?

🎙️ Here, we compare the lengths of the corresponding sides. We find the ratio for each pair. If all these ratios are identical, then the triangles are similar by the SSS criterion. Notice how ΔPQR is simply twice the size of ΔABC.

1Calculate ratios of corresponding sides:
2AB/PQ = 3/6 = 1/2
3BC/QR = 4/8 = 1/2
4CA/RP = 5/10 = 1/2
5Since all ratios are equal (1/2), Yes, by SSS.

Criterion 3: SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Similarity

  • If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other triangle,
  • AND the sides including these angles are proportional, then they are similar.
  • If ∠A=∠D and AB/DE = CA/FD, then ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.

🎙️ The SAS criterion is a combination. You need to identify one pair of equal corresponding angles, and then check if the two sides that 'form' or 'include' that angle are proportional. If both conditions hold true, the triangles are similar. This is very useful when you don't have all angles or all sides.

✂️
Angle between Proportional Sides
14 / 30

SAS Similarity: Worked Example

  • Given ΔABC with AB=4, AC=6, ∠A=70°.
  • Given ΔPQR with PQ=6, PR=9, ∠P=70°.
  • Are ΔABC and ΔPQR similar?

🎙️ In this example, we first confirm that the included angles are equal. Then, we calculate the ratios of the sides that form these angles. Since both the angle is equal and the sides are proportional, we can conclude similarity using the SAS criterion. Remember, the angle must be *between* the proportional sides.

1Check angles: ∠A = ∠P = 70° (Equal)
2Check ratios of including sides:
3AB/PQ = 4/6 = 2/3
4AC/PR = 6/9 = 2/3
5Since ∠A=∠P and AB/PQ = AC/PR, Yes, by SAS.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT) / Thales Theorem

  • If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle,
  • It intersects the other two sides at distinct points,
  • Then it divides the two sides in the same ratio.

🎙️ The Basic Proportionality Theorem, also known as Thales Theorem, is a cornerstone of this chapter. It states that if you draw a line inside a triangle that's parallel to one of its sides, it will cut the other two sides in such a way that the segments created are proportional. This theorem has many practical applications.

↔️
Parallel Line Divides Sides
17 / 30

BPT: Worked Example

  • In ΔABC, DE || BC.
  • AD = 3 cm, DB = 6 cm, AE = 4 cm.
  • Find EC.

🎙️ Let's apply BPT to find an unknown length. We set up the proportion based on the theorem. By substituting the given values and solving the simple equation, we can easily find the missing length. This demonstrates the direct application of BPT in problem-solving.

1Apply BPT: AD/DB = AE/EC
2Substitute values: 3/6 = 4/EC
3Simplify: 1/2 = 4/EC
4Cross-multiply: EC = 4 * 2 = 8 cm
18 / 30

Converse of Basic Proportionality Theorem

  • If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio,
  • Then the line must be parallel to the third side.
  • It's the reverse logic of BPT.

🎙️ Just as important as the BPT itself is its converse. The converse states that if you find a line segment within a triangle that divides two sides proportionally, then that line segment *must* be parallel to the third side. This is useful for proving lines are parallel.

🔄
Proportional Sides Implies Parallel

Areas of Similar Triangles Theorem

  • The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles,
  • Is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  • Area(ΔABC) / Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)² = (BC/EF)² = (CA/FD)².

🎙️ This theorem provides a powerful link between the side lengths and areas of similar triangles. It tells us that if one triangle is, say, twice as big in terms of side length, its area will be four times larger. This squared relationship is crucial for many problems.

🎨
Area Ratio Squared
21 / 30

Areas of Similar Triangles: Worked Example

  • Given ΔABC ~ ΔDEF.
  • AB = 4 cm, DE = 6 cm.
  • Area(ΔABC) = 24 cm². Find Area(ΔDEF).

🎙️ Here, we use the theorem to find the area of the larger triangle. We set up the ratio of areas equal to the square of the ratio of corresponding sides. After substituting the known values and solving, we find the unknown area. This shows how the theorem helps in scaling areas.

1Area(ΔABC)/Area(ΔDEF) = (AB/DE)²
224 / Area(ΔDEF) = (4/6)²
324 / Area(ΔDEF) = (2/3)² = 4/9
4Area(ΔDEF) = 24 * (9/4) = 6 * 9 = 54 cm²

Pythagoras Theorem: The Right Angle Rule

  • In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle)
  • Is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse).

🎙️ The Pythagoras Theorem is a fundamental theorem that you've likely encountered before. It's exclusively for right-angled triangles and provides a powerful relationship between the lengths of its three sides. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the 90-degree angle.

🏠
Right Triangle Property
23 / 30

Pythagoras Theorem: Worked Example

  • In a right-angled triangle ABC, with right angle at B.
  • AB = 5 cm, BC = 12 cm.
  • Find AC (hypotenuse).

🎙️ Let's use the theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse. We square the lengths of the two shorter sides, add them, and then take the square root to find the length of the hypotenuse. This is a direct application of the theorem to find an unknown side in a right triangle.

1Apply Pythagoras Theorem: AB² + BC² = AC²
2Substitute values: 5² + 12² = AC²
3Calculate: 25 + 144 = AC²
4169 = AC² => AC = √169 = 13 cm
24 / 30

Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

  • If in a triangle, the square of one side is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides,
  • Then the angle opposite the first side is a right angle (90°).
  • Used to determine if a triangle is right-angled.

🎙️ The converse of Pythagoras Theorem is equally important. It allows us to prove if a given triangle is a right-angled triangle, simply by checking if the relationship a² + b² = c² holds true for its side lengths. This is a powerful tool for classifying triangles based on their angles.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing congruent and similar triangles.
  • Incorrectly identifying corresponding sides/angles.
  • Not squaring ratios for areas of similar triangles.
  • Applying BPT or Pythagoras to non-relevant triangles.

🎙️ Students often make these common errors. Always double-check if you're dealing with congruence or similarity, ensure you're matching the correct corresponding parts, remember to square the ratio for areas, and only apply Pythagoras to right-angled triangles. Careful attention to detail will prevent these mistakes.
