What is Trigonometry?

  • From Greek words: 'Trigon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).
  • It's the study of relationships between sides and angles of triangles.
  • Mainly deals with right-angled triangles.

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's a powerful tool that helps us understand how the lengths of sides in a triangle are connected to its angles. We'll focus primarily on right-angled triangles.

Sides Relative to an Acute Angle

  • For an acute angle (θ) in a right triangle:
  • Opposite Side: The side directly across from angle θ.
  • Adjacent Side: The side next to angle θ, not the hypotenuse.

🎙️ This is crucial! The terms 'opposite' and 'adjacent' are not fixed; they depend on which acute angle you are referring to. The hypotenuse, however, always remains the same.

Trigonometric Ratios: Sine (sin)

  • The ratio of the length of the side Opposite to the angle to the length of the Hypotenuse.
  • sin(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Think of it as the 'stretch' of the opposite side relative to the hypotenuse.

🎙️ Our first ratio is Sine. It tells us how much the side opposite to our angle 'stretches' compared to the hypotenuse. Remember, it's always a ratio of two lengths.

📈
Opposite / Hypotenuse

Trigonometric Ratios: Cosine (cos)

  • The ratio of the length of the side Adjacent to the angle to the length of the Hypotenuse.
  • cos(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Relates the 'closeness' of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse.

🎙️ Next, we have Cosine. This ratio compares the adjacent side to the hypotenuse. It gives us a different perspective on how the sides are related to the angle.

📉
Adjacent / Hypotenuse
12 / 30

Trigonometric Ratios: Tangent (tan)

  • The ratio of the length of the side Opposite to the angle to the length of the side Adjacent.
  • tan(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent
  • Also, tan(θ) = sin(θ) / cos(θ).

🎙️ Our third main ratio is Tangent. It's different because it doesn't involve the hypotenuse directly. It compares the opposite side to the adjacent side, and it can also be found by dividing sine by cosine.

Reciprocal Ratios: Cosecant (cosec)

  • The reciprocal of the Sine ratio.
  • cosec(θ) = 1 / sin(θ)
  • cosec(θ) = Hypotenuse / Opposite

🎙️ Now for the reciprocal ratios! Cosecant is simply the inverse of sine. If you know sine, you automatically know cosecant by flipping the fraction.

🔄
Inverse of Sine

Reciprocal Ratios: Secant (sec)

  • The reciprocal of the Cosine ratio.
  • sec(θ) = 1 / cos(θ)
  • sec(θ) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent

🎙️ Similarly, Secant is the reciprocal of Cosine. Knowing one helps you find the other effortlessly. Just remember which ratio is the reciprocal of which.

↩️
Inverse of Cosine

Reciprocal Ratios: Cotangent (cot)

  • The reciprocal of the Tangent ratio.
  • cot(θ) = 1 / tan(θ)
  • cot(θ) = Adjacent / Opposite

🎙️ And finally, Cotangent is the reciprocal of Tangent. So, we have a total of six trigonometric ratios, three main ones and their three reciprocals.

Easy Way to Remember Ratios!

  • SOH CAH TOA:
  • S - Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • C - Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • T - Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

Worked Example: Finding All Ratios

  • Given a right triangle with Opposite = 4, Adjacent = 3.
  • First, find Hypotenuse using Pythagoras Theorem: 3² + 4² = H² => H = 5.
  • Now, calculate all 6 ratios for the angle.

🎙️ Let's try an example. If we have the opposite and adjacent sides, we first use the Pythagorean theorem to find the hypotenuse. Once all three sides are known, finding all six ratios is straightforward.

3cos(θ) = 3/5

Worked Example: Finding a Specific Ratio

  • If tan(A) = 3/4, find sin(A) and cos(A).
  • Draw a right triangle. If tan(A) = Opposite/Adjacent = 3/4, let Opposite = 3k, Adjacent = 4k.
  • Using Pythagoras: Hypotenuse = √( (3k)² + (4k)² ) = √(9k² + 16k²) = √(25k²) = 5k.
  • sin(A) = Opposite/Hypotenuse = 3k/5k = 3/5.
  • cos(A) = Adjacent/Hypotenuse = 4k/5k = 4/5.

🎙️ Sometimes you'll be given one ratio and asked to find others. The best approach is to draw a right-angled triangle, assign values to the sides based on the given ratio, use Pythagoras to find the third side, and then calculate the required ratios.

2Assign Sides
3Find Hypotenuse
4Calculate sin(A)

Deriving Ratios for 45°

  • Consider an isosceles right-angled triangle.
  • If two angles are 45°, then the opposite sides are equal (let them be 'a').
  • Hypotenuse = √(a² + a²) = a√2.
  • sin(45°) = a / (a√2) = 1/√2.
  • cos(45°) = a / (a√2) = 1/√2.
  • tan(45°) = a / a = 1.

🎙️ We can derive the values for 45 degrees using a simple isosceles right triangle. Since two angles are 45 degrees, the sides opposite to them must be equal. Then, apply Pythagoras theorem and the ratio definitions.

2Sides: a, a, a√2

Deriving Ratios for 30° and 60°

  • Start with an equilateral triangle of side '2a'. All angles are 60°.
  • Draw an altitude from one vertex, which bisects the opposite side and the angle.
  • This creates two 30-60-90 right triangles.
  • Using Pythagoras, find the altitude: √( (2a)² - a² ) = a√3.

🎙️ For 30 and 60 degrees, we use an equilateral triangle. By drawing an altitude, we split it into two congruent 30-60-90 triangles. From these, we can easily find all the ratios for both 30 and 60 degrees.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing Opposite/Adjacent sides: Always relative to the chosen angle.
  • Incorrectly applying Pythagoras Theorem.
  • Mixing up reciprocal ratios (e.g., sin and cosec).
  • Forgetting the values of special angles.

🎙️ Many students make these common errors. Always double-check which angle you are referring to for opposite and adjacent sides. Practice will help you avoid mixing up the ratios and remembering special angle values.

Exam Tips for Trigonometry

  • Draw diagrams for problems to visualize relationships.
  • Write down SOH CAH TOA or mnemonic for reference.
  • Practice derivations for special angles to understand, not just memorize.
  • Questions often involve combining ratios and using algebraic simplification.

🎙️ To ace trigonometry in your exams, always start by drawing a clear diagram. Use mnemonics to recall ratios. Understanding how special angle values are derived helps you rebuild the table if you forget it. Be prepared for multi-step problems!
