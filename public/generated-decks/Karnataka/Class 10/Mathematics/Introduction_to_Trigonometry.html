What is Trigonometry?

  • Derived from Greek words 'Trigon' (triangle) and 'Metron' (measure).
  • It's the study of relationships between the sides and angles of triangles.
  • Mainly focuses on right-angled triangles.

🎙️ Trigonometry literally means 'measuring triangles'. It's all about how the lengths of a triangle's sides relate to the sizes of its angles. We'll primarily work with right-angled triangles.

Angles - A Quick Revision

  • An angle is formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint.
  • Measured in degrees (°).
  • Types: acute (<90°), right (=90°), obtuse (>90°).

🎙️ Before we dive deeper, let's quickly revise angles. Remember, angles are fundamental to trigonometry, and we'll be dealing with them constantly, especially the 90-degree angle.

The Right-Angled Triangle - Our Focus

  • A triangle with one angle exactly 90 degrees.
  • The sum of all angles in any triangle is 180 degrees.
  • The other two angles are always acute.

🎙️ Our entire study of basic trigonometry revolves around the right-angled triangle. It's crucial to identify the 90-degree angle and understand its properties.

Identifying Sides: The Hypotenuse

  • The longest side of a right-angled triangle.
  • Always opposite the 90-degree angle.
  • Its length never changes, regardless of which acute angle we consider.

🎙️ The hypotenuse is easy to spot! It's always the side directly across from the right angle and it's the longest of the three sides. Remember this, as it's a fixed reference point.

↗️
Longest Side
7 / 25

Identifying Sides: Opposite and Adjacent

  • These sides depend on the 'reference angle' chosen.
  • The 'Opposite' side is directly across from the reference angle.
  • The 'Adjacent' side is next to the reference angle (not the hypotenuse).

🎙️ This is where it gets interesting! Unlike the hypotenuse, the opposite and adjacent sides change depending on which acute angle you are looking 'from'. It's all about perspective.

↔️
Relative Sides

Example 1: Identifying Sides

  • Consider triangle ABC, right-angled at B.
  • If we choose angle A as the reference angle:
  • Side BC is Opposite to angle A. Side AB is Adjacent to angle A.

🎙️ Let's look at an example. Imagine you are standing at angle A. The side you are looking directly at is BC, so it's the opposite. The side next to you, AB, is the adjacent side.

Example 2: Identifying Sides (Different Angle)

  • Consider triangle ABC, right-angled at B.

  • Side AB is Opposite to angle C. Side BC is Adjacent to angle C.

Trigonometric Ratios - The Core Idea

  • These are ratios of the lengths of a right triangle's sides.
  • They relate an acute angle to the ratio of two sides.
  • There are six main trigonometric ratios.

🎙️ Trigonometric ratios are simply fractions formed by dividing the length of one side by another. These ratios are constant for a given angle, no matter the size of the triangle.

➗
Side Ratios
11 / 25

The Sine Ratio (sin)

  • Defined as: Opposite side / Hypotenuse.
  • Abbreviated as 'sin'.
  • sin(angle) = Opposite / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ The first important ratio is Sine. Think of it as relating the 'height' of the triangle (opposite side) to its 'slant' (hypotenuse). It's always Opposite divided by Hypotenuse.

↕️
Height Ratio
12 / 25

The Cosine Ratio (cos)

  • Defined as: Adjacent side / Hypotenuse.
  • Abbreviated as 'cos'.
  • cos(angle) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse.

🎙️ Next up is Cosine. This ratio connects the 'base' of the triangle (adjacent side) to its 'slant' (hypotenuse). Remember, it's always Adjacent divided by Hypotenuse.

↔️
Base Ratio
13 / 25

The Tangent Ratio (tan)

  • Defined as: Opposite side / Adjacent side.
  • Abbreviated as 'tan'.
  • tan(angle) = Opposite / Adjacent.

🎙️ Finally, we have Tangent. This ratio compares the opposite side to the adjacent side. You can think of it as a measure of the 'steepness' or 'slope' of the angle within the triangle.

🪜
Slope Ratio
14 / 25

Mnemonic: SOH CAH TOA

  • A helpful way to remember the main three ratios.
  • SOH: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ This little phrase, SOH CAH TOA, is a lifesaver! It summarises the definitions of sine, cosine, and tangent. Practice saying it until it sticks in your mind, it will be very useful.

🧠
Memory Aid

Reciprocal Ratios: Cosecant (csc)

  • The reciprocal of Sine.
  • csc(angle) = 1 / sin(angle) = Hypotenuse / Opposite.
  • Often denoted as 'cosec'.

🎙️ Besides the main three, there are three reciprocal ratios. Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine, meaning you just flip the fraction. Hypotenuse divided by Opposite.

🔄
Sine's Reciprocal
16 / 25

Reciprocal Ratios: Secant (sec)

  • The reciprocal of Cosine.
  • sec(angle) = 1 / cos(angle) = Hypotenuse / Adjacent.

🎙️ Secant is the reciprocal of Cosine. Again, just flip the cosine ratio. So, it's Hypotenuse divided by Adjacent. These reciprocal ratios are equally important.

↩️
Cosine's Reciprocal
17 / 25

Reciprocal Ratios: Cotangent (cot)

  • The reciprocal of Tangent.
  • cot(angle) = 1 / tan(angle) = Adjacent / Opposite.

🎙️ And finally, Cotangent is the reciprocal of Tangent. This means it's Adjacent divided by Opposite. Now you know all six fundamental trigonometric ratios!

🔃
Tangent's Reciprocal

Worked Example: Finding Ratios (1)

  • Given a right triangle with sides: Opposite = 3, Adjacent = 4, Hypotenuse = 5.
  • sin(angle) = Opposite/Hypotenuse = 3/5.
  • cos(angle) = Adjacent/Hypotenuse = 4/5.

🎙️ Let's try an example. If we have a triangle with these side lengths, we can directly apply our definitions. Sine is 3/5 and Cosine is 4/5. Simple, right?

Worked Example: Finding Ratios (2)

  • Given a right triangle with sides: Opposite = 3, Adjacent = 4, Hypotenuse = 5.
  • tan(angle) = Opposite/Adjacent = 3/4.
  • csc(angle) = 5/3, sec(angle) = 5/4, cot(angle) = 4/3.

🎙️ Continuing with the same triangle, Tangent is 3/4. And for the reciprocal ratios, we just flip them: Cosecant is 5/3, Secant is 5/4, and Cotangent is 4/3. Practice makes perfect!

Trigonometry in Real Life: Measuring Heights

  • Imagine measuring the height of a tree without climbing it!
  • Use the angle of elevation (from ground to top of tree).
  • Apply tangent ratio: tan(angle) = Height / Distance from base.

🎙️ Here's a practical application. By knowing the angle you look up at a tree (angle of elevation) and your distance from its base, you can calculate its height using the tangent ratio. It's truly amazing!
