Concept: Dividing a Line Segment in a Given Ratio

  • To divide a line segment into two parts in a specific ratio.
  • For example, dividing a line AB in the ratio m:n.
  • This is a fundamental construction in Class 10.

🎙️ One of the first important constructions we'll tackle is dividing a line segment into a given ratio. This means if you have a line segment of a certain length, you want to find a point on it that splits the segment into two smaller segments whose lengths are in a particular proportion. Let's explore how to do this accurately.

Steps: Dividing a Line Segment (Method 1)

  • Draw line segment AB.
  • Draw a ray AX making an acute angle with AB.
  • Mark m+n points on AX at equal distances.
  • Join B to the (m+n)th point.
  • Draw a parallel line to divide AB.

🎙️ This method uses the concept of parallel lines and basic proportionality theorem. First, we draw our line segment and an auxiliary ray. Then, we mark points on the ray, corresponding to the sum of the ratio parts. The trick is to draw a line parallel to the one connecting the last point to B, passing through the 'm'th point.

1Draw AB & Ray AX
2Mark m+n points
3Join last point to B
4Draw parallel line

Example: Divide 7.6 cm Line in Ratio 5:8

  • Draw AB = 7.6 cm.
  • Draw ray AX, mark 13 points (A1 to A13).
  • Join B to A13.
  • Draw A5C || A13B, where C is on AB.

🎙️ Let's apply the first method to a concrete example. We need to divide a 7.6 cm line into a 5:8 ratio. This means we'll mark 5+8=13 points on our ray. The point C that divides AB will be found by drawing a line parallel to A13B from A5. This ensures AC:CB = 5:8.

Steps: Dividing a Line Segment (Method 2)

  • Draw line segment AB.
  • Draw rays AX and BY parallel and opposite.
  • Mark m points on AX, n points on BY (equal distances).
  • Join Am to Bn. This line intersects AB at C.

🎙️ This second method is equally effective and involves drawing two parallel rays from points A and B, but in opposite directions. We then mark 'm' points on one ray and 'n' points on the other. Connecting the 'm'th point on AX to the 'n'th point on BY will give us the point C on AB that divides it in the desired ratio.

1Draw AB & parallel rays
2Mark m points on AX
3Mark n points on BY
4Join Am to Bn

Example: Divide 6 cm Line in Ratio 3:2 (Method 2)

  • Draw AB = 6 cm.
  • Draw ray AX and parallel ray BY (opposite sides).
  • Mark A1, A2, A3 on AX; B1, B2 on BY.
  • Join A3 to B2. This line cuts AB at C.

🎙️ For this example, we'll divide a 6 cm line segment in the ratio 3:2 using Method 2. This means we'll mark 3 points on ray AX and 2 points on ray BY. The intersection of the line joining A3 and B2 with AB will be our desired point C, ensuring AC:CB = 3:2.

Concept: Constructing Similar Triangles

  • Triangles with same shape but possibly different sizes.
  • Corresponding angles are equal.
  • Corresponding sides are in the same ratio (scale factor).

🎙️ Now, let's move on to constructing similar triangles. Remember, similar triangles look alike but can be scaled up or down versions of each other. This means their angles are identical, but their side lengths are proportional. We'll learn how to construct a new triangle that is similar to a given one, based on a specific scale factor.

Understanding Scale Factor

  • The ratio of corresponding sides of similar triangles.
  • If scale factor > 1, the new triangle is larger.
  • If scale factor < 1, the new triangle is smaller.

🎙️ The scale factor tells us how much larger or smaller the new triangle will be compared to the original. A scale factor greater than 1 means enlargement, while less than 1 means reduction. For example, a scale factor of 2 means each side of the new triangle will be twice as long as the original.

Steps: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor < 1)

  • Draw the given triangle ABC.
  • Draw a ray BX making an acute angle with BC.
  • Mark 'm' points on BX (m = denominator of scale factor).
  • Join Bm to C. Draw a parallel line from B(numerator) to intersect BC at C'.
  • Draw a parallel line from C' to intersect AB at A'.

🎙️ When constructing a smaller similar triangle (scale factor less than 1), we'll use a method similar to dividing a line segment. We start by drawing the original triangle. Then, we construct a new base segment on one side, scaled down by the given factor. Finally, we complete the triangle by drawing a parallel line to the third side.

1Draw original triangle
2Draw ray & mark points
3Construct base segment
4Construct final side

Example: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor 2/3)

  • Construct a triangle ABC with sides 4, 5, 6 cm.
  • Construct another triangle whose sides are 2/3 of corresponding sides of ΔABC.
  • The new triangle ΔA'BC' will be smaller.

🎙️ Let's work through an example where the scale factor is 2/3. First, construct the initial triangle ABC with the given side lengths. Then, follow the steps to create a smaller triangle A'BC'. The key is to correctly use the ratio 2/3 to divide the base and then draw parallel lines to form the new triangle.

Steps: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor > 1)

  • Draw the given triangle ABC.
  • Draw a ray BX making an acute angle with BC.
  • Mark 'm' points on BX (m = numerator of scale factor).
  • Join B(denominator) to C. Draw a parallel line from B(numerator) to intersect extended BC at C'.
  • Draw a parallel line from C' to intersect extended AB at A'.

🎙️ For a larger similar triangle (scale factor greater than 1), the process is quite similar, but we'll be extending the sides of the original triangle. The denominator of the scale factor will be used to connect to point C, and the numerator will guide the parallel line construction on the extended ray. This will result in a larger triangle A'BC' that encloses the original triangle.

1Draw original triangle
2Draw ray & mark points
3Extend base segment
4Extend final side

Example: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor 5/3)

  • The new triangle ΔA'BC' will be larger.

🎙️ Here, we'll construct a triangle with a scale factor of 5/3, which means it will be larger than the original. After drawing the initial triangle, extend the base BC and ray BX. The construction involves joining B3 to C and then drawing a parallel line from B5 to the extended BC, and similarly for the other side. This will give us the larger similar triangle.

Concept: Constructing Tangents to a Circle

  • A tangent is a line that touches a circle at exactly one point.
  • The point of contact is called the point of tangency.
  • Important in geometry and real-world applications.

🎙️ Our final major construction involves tangents to a circle. A tangent is a very special line that 'kisses' the circle at precisely one point without entering its interior. Understanding how to construct tangents is crucial and has many practical applications, such as in designing gears or understanding motion.

🎯
Touching at One Point

Tangent Property: Radius Perpendicular to Tangent

  • The radius drawn to the point of tangency is always perpendicular to the tangent.
  • This property is key for constructing tangents.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle at the point of contact.

🎙️ The most fundamental property of a tangent is its relationship with the circle's radius. At the exact point where the tangent touches the circle, the radius drawn to that point will always form a perfect 90-degree angle with the tangent line. This perpendicularity is the secret sauce for constructing tangents accurately.

Steps: Tangent at a Point on the Circle

  • Draw a circle with center O and desired radius.
  • Mark a point P on the circle.
  • Draw the radius OP.
  • Construct a line perpendicular to OP passing through P.

🎙️ To draw a tangent at a specific point on the circle, we first draw the circle and mark the point P. Then, we draw the radius connecting the center O to P. The next step is to construct a line that is perpendicular to this radius OP, right at point P. This perpendicular line will be our tangent.

1Draw circle & point P
2Draw radius OP
3Construct perpendicular

Example: Tangent at P on 4cm Circle

  • Draw a circle with radius 4 cm.
  • Take any point P on the circle.
  • Draw radius OP.

🎙️ Let's apply these steps to construct a tangent. Draw a circle of 4 cm radius. Choose any point P on its circumference. Draw the radius OP. Now, using your compass and ruler, construct a line perpendicular to OP that passes through P. This line is the tangent to the circle at point P.

Steps: Tangents from an External Point to a Circle

  • Draw a circle with center O and radius r.
  • Mark an external point P.
  • Join OP and bisect it to find its midpoint M.
  • With M as center and MO as radius, draw a new circle.
  • The new circle intersects the original circle at T1 and T2. Join PT1 and PT2.

🎙️ To draw tangents from a point outside the circle, we use a clever trick involving another circle. First, draw the original circle and the external point P. Join O to P and find the midpoint M of OP. Using M as the center, draw a circle that passes through O and P. The points where this new circle intersects the original circle are our points of tangency. Joining P to these points gives the two tangents.

1Draw circle & point P
2Bisect OP
3Draw new circle
4Join P to intersections

Example: Tangents from 7cm Away to 3cm Radius Circle

  • Draw a circle of radius 3 cm.
  • Mark a point P, 7 cm away from the center O.
  • Find the midpoint of OP (let's say M).
  • Draw a circle with center M and radius MO.
  • Join P to the intersection points on the 3 cm circle.

🎙️ In this example, we have a circle of 3 cm radius and an external point P located 7 cm from the center. Follow the steps: find the midpoint of OP, then draw a 'helper' circle. This helper circle will show you exactly where the tangents touch the original circle. Connect P to these points, and you have your two tangents.
