Dividing a Line Segment

  • To split a line segment into two parts.
  • The ratio of the lengths of these parts is predetermined.
  • Uses properties of similar triangles.

🎙️ We use a geometric method to divide a line segment AB into a ratio m:n, meaning the two new segments will have lengths in that proportion. This method relies on the concept of similar triangles, which we'll revisit later.

📏
Line Segment

Steps: Divide a Line Segment (m:n)

🎙️ Here are the key steps. First, draw the line segment. Then, draw an acute ray from one endpoint. Mark points on this ray and connect the last point to the other endpoint of the segment. Finally, draw a parallel line to divide the segment.

1Draw AB
2Ray AX
3Mark m+n points
4Join B to (m+n)th point
5Draw parallel line
7 / 30

Example: Divide 7.6 cm in 5:8

  • Draw AB = 7.6 cm.
  • Draw ray AX, mark 5+8=13 points.
  • Join B to A13.
  • Draw A5C parallel to A13B.

🎙️ Let's apply these steps. Draw your line segment AB of 7.6 cm. Then draw an acute ray AX. Mark 13 equal divisions on AX. Join B to the 13th point. From the 5th point on AX, draw a line parallel to BA13 intersecting AB at C. AC:CB will be 5:8.

What is a Tangent to a Circle?

  • A line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • This point is called the point of contact.
  • It never enters the interior of the circle.

🎙️ A tangent is a special kind of line. It's like a gentle kiss between the line and the circle, happening at only one unique point. It's crucial not to confuse it with a secant, which cuts through the circle at two points.

⭕
Circle & Line

Key Property of a Tangent

  • The radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • This means they form a 90-degree angle.
  • Fundamental for tangent constructions.

🎙️ This property is incredibly important! Always remember that the radius and the tangent meet at a perfect right angle at the point of contact. This principle is the foundation for constructing tangents.

📐
Right Angle

Steps: Tangent from a Point on the Circle

🎙️ To draw a tangent at a point P on the circle, first draw the radius OP. Then, extend this radius to a point Q. Now, using a compass, construct a perpendicular line to OQ passing through point P. This perpendicular line is your tangent.

1Draw circle & radius OP
2Extend OP to Q
3Draw perpendicular at P
14 / 30

Example: Tangent at a Point P

  • Draw a circle of radius 3 cm.
  • Mark a point P on it.
  • Construct a tangent through P.

🎙️ Let's try this. Draw a 3 cm radius circle. Pick any point P on its circumference. Draw the radius OP. Now, extend OP and construct a 90-degree angle at P. The line forming this angle will be your tangent.

Steps: Tangents from an External Point

🎙️ When the point P is outside the circle, we first join OP and find its midpoint M. Then, draw a circle with M as the center and MO (or MP) as the radius. This new circle will intersect the original circle at two points. Connect P to these two points to get your two tangents.

1Draw circle & point P
2Join OP, bisect it
3Draw circle with M as center
4Join P to intersection points

Example: Tangents from External Point

  • Draw circle radius 4 cm.
  • Point P is 7 cm from center.
  • Construct two tangents from P.

🎙️ Here, draw a 4 cm radius circle. Locate point P 7 cm away from the center O. Join OP, bisect it to find M. Draw a circle with center M and radius MO. The intersection points with the original circle, say T1 and T2, will be the points of contact. Join PT1 and PT2.

What are Similar Triangles?

  • Triangles with the same shape but different sizes.
  • Corresponding angles are equal.
  • Corresponding sides are in the same ratio (scale factor).

🎙️ Similar triangles are not identical, but they look alike. Their angles match up perfectly, and their sides are proportional. This 'scale factor' is key to understanding how one triangle relates to another.

🔺
Triangle Shapes
20 / 30

Steps: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor < 1)

🎙️ To construct a smaller similar triangle, we start with the original. Draw a ray from one vertex. Mark points based on the scale factor. Connect a point on the ray to a vertex, then draw a parallel line to create the smaller triangle.

1Draw original triangle ABC
2Draw ray BX
3Mark points on BX (max of ratio)
4Join C to appropriate point
5Draw parallel line
21 / 30

Example: Scale Factor 2/3

  • Construct ΔABC.
  • Construct ΔA'BC' similar to ΔABC.
  • Scale factor 2/3.

🎙️ Let's say you have a triangle ABC. Draw a ray BX. If the scale factor is 2/3, mark 3 points on BX. Join C to B3. Then draw a line from B2 parallel to CB3, intersecting BC at C'. From C', draw a line parallel to AC, intersecting AB at A'. You'll get ΔA'BC'.

✅
Solution

Steps: Similar Triangle (Scale Factor > 1)

🎙️ For a larger similar triangle, the initial steps are similar, but we'll need to extend the sides of the original triangle. The parallel line construction will then define the vertices of the larger triangle.

1Draw original triangle ABC
2Draw ray BX
3Mark points on BX (max of ratio)
4Extend sides of triangle
5Join C to appropriate point
6Draw parallel line
23 / 30

Example: Scale Factor 5/3

  • Construct ΔABC.
  • Construct ΔA'BC' similar to ΔABC.
  • Scale factor 5/3.

🎙️ Given ΔABC, draw ray BX. Mark 5 points. Join C to B3. Extend BC and AB. Draw a line from B5 parallel to CB3, intersecting extended BC at C'. From C', draw a line parallel to AC, intersecting extended AB at A'. You'll get the larger ΔA'BC'.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not drawing acute angles for rays.
  • Incorrectly marking points on the ray (m+n or denominator).
  • Not drawing parallel lines accurately.
  • Forgetting to extend lines/segments for scale factor > 1.

🎙️ These are some common pitfalls. Always ensure your construction lines are neat and accurate. Pay close attention to the number of divisions and the parallel lines – they are critical for correct results.
