What is Probability?

  • It's the measure of how likely an event is to occur.
  • Expressed as a number between 0 and 1.
  • 0 means impossible, 1 means certain.

🎙️ In simple terms, probability tells us 'how likely' something is to happen. If you toss a coin, what are the chances of getting a 'Heads'? Probability gives us a mathematical way to answer such questions. It's always a value between 0 and 1.

Sample Space: All Possibilities

  • The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.
  • Represented by 'S'.
  • Example: Tossing a coin, S = {Heads, Tails}.

🎙️ Let's say you roll a die. What are all the numbers that can possibly show up? 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6. The collection of all these possible outcomes is called the 'Sample Space'. It's like listing every single thing that *could* happen.

Event: A Specific Outcome

The Probability Formula

  • P(E) = (Number of Favourable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Outcomes)
  • P(E) is the probability of event E.
  • Favourable outcomes are the results we are interested in.

🎙️ This is the most important formula in probability! To find the probability of an event, you count how many ways your desired outcome can happen, and then divide that by the total number of all possible outcomes. Remember this formula well!

Example: Tossing a Coin

  • Question: What is the probability of getting a Head?
  • Sample Space (S) = {Heads, Tails}. Total outcomes = 2.
  • Favourable outcome (E) = {Heads}. Number of favourable outcomes = 1.

🎙️ Let's apply the formula. When you toss a coin, there are two possibilities: Heads or Tails. If we want a Head, that's one favourable outcome. So, using the formula, P(Head) = 1/2. Simple, right?

Example: Rolling a Die

  • Question: What is the probability of getting an even number?
  • Sample Space (S) = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. Total outcomes = 6.
  • Favourable outcomes (E) = {2, 4, 6}. Number of favourable outcomes = 3.

🎙️ For rolling a die, we have 6 total outcomes. If we want an even number, we can get 2, 4, or 6. That's 3 favourable outcomes. So, P(Even Number) = 3/6, which simplifies to 1/2. Always simplify your fractions!

Range of Probability: 0 to 1

  • The probability of any event E, P(E), is always between 0 and 1 (inclusive).
  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  • P(E) = 0 for an impossible event, P(E) = 1 for a certain event.

🎙️ It's crucial to remember that a probability can never be less than 0 or greater than 1. If you calculate a probability and get a negative number or something like 1.5, you know you've made a mistake! It's always a fraction or decimal between 0 and 1.

Complementary Events: Happening or Not Happening

  • If E is an event, then 'not E' (or E') is its complementary event.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • Example: If P(rain) = 0.3, then P(not rain) = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7.

🎙️ Every event has a 'complement' – the event that it *doesn't* happen. The sum of the probability of an event happening and the probability of it *not* happening is always 1. This is a very useful property for solving many problems!

🔄
Opposite Events

Example: Not Getting a 6

  • Question: What is the probability of NOT getting a 6 when rolling a die?
  • P(getting a 6) = 1/6.
  • P(not getting a 6) = 1 - P(getting a 6) = 1 - 1/6 = 5/6.

🎙️ We know the probability of getting a 6 is 1/6. Instead of counting all the outcomes that are not 6 (which are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 - 5 outcomes), we can simply use the complementary event formula. P(not 6) = 1 - 1/6 = 5/6. Much faster!

Example: Sum of 7 with Two Dice

  • Question: What is the probability of getting a sum of 7 when rolling two dice?
  • Total outcomes = 36.
  • Favourable outcomes: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1). Number of favourable outcomes = 6.

🎙️ To get a sum of 7, we need to list all the pairs that add up to 7. There are 6 such pairs. Since the total outcomes are 36, the probability of getting a sum of 7 is 6/36, which simplifies to 1/6.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Not identifying the correct sample space.
  • Miscounting favourable outcomes.
  • Forgetting to simplify the probability fraction.
  • Stating probability as a negative number or greater than 1.

🎙️ Students often make a few common errors. Always double-check your sample space, especially for multiple events like two dice. Count your favourable outcomes carefully. And remember, probability must always be between 0 and 1!
