Cuboid's Skin: Surface Area & Formula

  • A cuboid has 6 rectangular faces.
  • Opposite faces are identical (l×b, l×h, b×h).
  • TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl). LSA = 2h(l + b).

🎙️ A cuboid is like a stretched cube. To find its surface area, we add up the areas of all its six faces. Since opposite faces are equal, we can simplify the calculation into the given formulas.

Example: Painting a Room

  • Room: length=5m, breadth=4m, height=3m.
  • Calculate the area to be painted (walls & ceiling).
  • Area = LSA + Area of ceiling (l×b).
  • Area = 2(3)(5+4) + (5)(4) = 54 + 20 = 74 m².

🎙️ Here, we need to paint the four walls and the ceiling, but not the floor. So, we use the Lateral Surface Area and add the area of the ceiling. Pay attention to what surfaces need to be covered!

Cube's Coat: Surface Area & Formula

  • A special cuboid with all sides equal (length=a).
  • All 6 faces are identical squares (area=a²).
  • TSA = 6a². LSA = 4a².

🎙️ The cube is a very symmetrical shape. Since all its faces are squares of the same size, finding its surface area is simply multiplying the area of one face by 6 for TSA, or by 4 for LSA.

Example: Gift Box Wrapping

  • A cubical gift box with side 10 cm.
  • How much wrapping paper is needed?
  • TSA = 6a² = 6 × (10 cm)² = 6 × 100 cm² = 600 cm².

🎙️ If you want to completely wrap a cubical gift box, you need enough paper to cover all its surfaces. This is a direct application of the total surface area formula for a cube.

Cuboid's Capacity: Volume & Formula

  • The space inside a cuboid.
  • Imagine stacking unit cubes inside.
  • Volume (V) = length × breadth × height = l × b × h.

🎙️ Volume tells us how much 'stuff' can fit inside a 3D object. For a cuboid, it's simply the product of its three dimensions. Think of how many small boxes can fit into a larger container.

Example: Water Tank Capacity

  • A rectangular water tank: L=3m, B=2m, H=1.5m.
  • How much water can it hold?
  • Volume = L × B × H = 3 × 2 × 1.5 = 9 m³.
  • Since 1 m³ = 1000 litres, it holds 9000 litres.

🎙️ This is a practical application. Knowing the volume of a tank allows us to calculate its capacity in litres, which is very useful in daily life and engineering.

Cube's Inner Space: Volume & Formula

  • A cuboid with all sides equal (a).
  • Volume = side × side × side.
  • Volume (V) = a³.

🎙️ Just like with surface area, the cube's simplicity makes its volume calculation straightforward. It's the side length multiplied by itself three times. This is why we say 'cubed'.

Example: Melting Ice Cube

  • An ice cube with side 4 cm.
  • What is its volume?
  • Volume = a³ = (4 cm)³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 cm³.

🎙️ If this ice cube melts, it will produce 64 cubic centimeters of water. This simple calculation helps us understand the amount of substance an object contains.

Cylinder's Wrap: Surface Area & Formulas

  • Two circular bases and a curved surface.
  • CSA = 2πrh (curved part).
  • TSA = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h+r) (curved + 2 bases).

🎙️ Cylinders are everywhere! When we talk about their surface area, we need to consider the top and bottom circles, and the curved part that connects them. CSA is just the curved part.

Example: Painting a Cylindrical Pillar

  • Pillar: radius=0.7m, height=5m.
  • Calculate area to be painted (curved surface only).
  • CSA = 2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 0.7 × 5 = 22 m².

🎙️ If you're painting only the curved part of a pillar, you only need the Curved Surface Area. Remember to use the value of π (usually 22/7 or 3.14) and be careful with units.

Cylinder's Fill: Volume & Formula

  • The amount of space a cylinder occupies.
  • Imagine stacking many circular discs.
  • Volume (V) = Area of base × height = πr²h.

🎙️ Just like the cuboid, the volume of a cylinder is found by multiplying its base area by its height. The base here is a circle, so its area is πr². This formula is crucial for liquid capacities.

Example: Filling a Water Drum

  • Cylindrical drum: diameter=70cm, height=100cm.
  • Find its capacity in litres.
  • r = 35cm = 0.35m, h = 1m.
  • V = πr²h = (22/7) × (0.35)² × 1 = 0.385 m³ = 385 litres.

🎙️ Here, we convert dimensions to metres to get volume in m³, then convert to litres (1 m³ = 1000 litres). Always double-check units and conversions in such problems.

Cone's Cover: Surface Area & Formulas

  • A circular base and a curved surface tapering to a point (vertex).
  • Slant height (l) is important: l = √(r² + h²).
  • CSA = πrl. TSA = πrl + πr² = πr(l+r).

🎙️ Think of an ice cream cone or a birthday cap. It has a circular base and a curved surface. The slant height 'l' is the distance from the vertex to any point on the circumference of the base, and it's essential for cone calculations.

Example: Birthday Cap Material

  • Conical cap: radius=7cm, height=24cm.
  • Find the area of paper needed (no base).
  • l = √(7²+24²) = √(49+576) = √625 = 25cm.
  • CSA = πrl = (22/7) × 7 × 25 = 550 cm².

🎙️ For a birthday cap, we only need the curved surface area, as it doesn't have a base. First, calculate the slant height 'l' using Pythagoras theorem, then apply the CSA formula.

Cone's Capacity: Volume & Formula

  • The space occupied by a cone.
  • It's 1/3rd the volume of a cylinder with same base & height.
  • Volume (V) = (1/3)πr²h.

🎙️ A fascinating fact about cones is their relationship with cylinders. If a cone and a cylinder have the exact same base radius and height, the cone's volume will be exactly one-third of the cylinder's volume.

Example: Filling a Conical Flask

  • Conical flask: radius=3cm, height=7cm.
  • Find its volume.
  • V = (1/3)πr²h = (1/3) × (22/7) × 3² × 7.
  • V = (1/3) × 22 × 9 × 1 = 66 cm³.

🎙️ This is a straightforward application of the cone volume formula. Remember the (1/3) factor, which is unique to cones and pyramids.

Watch Out! Common Mistakes

  • Confusing LSA/CSA with TSA.
  • Incorrect units (cm² vs cm³, m vs cm).
  • Calculation errors (especially with π and 'l').
  • Not identifying the correct shape/formula for the problem.

🎙️ Many students lose marks due to these errors. Always read the question carefully, write down the given values and the formula, and check your units at every step. Practice is key to avoiding these!
