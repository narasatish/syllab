Quick Recall: What is a Circle?

  • A circle is a set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point.
  • The fixed point is called the 'center'.
  • The constant distance is called the 'radius'.

🎙️ Before we dive into areas, let's quickly refresh our memory on what a circle truly is. Remember, every point on the boundary of a circle is the same distance from its center. This fundamental definition is key to understanding all related concepts.

Key Terms of a Circle: Radius, Diameter, Circumference

  • Radius (r): Distance from center to any point on the circle.
  • Diameter (d): Distance across the circle through the center (d = 2r).
  • Circumference (C): The distance around the circle (C = 2πr or πd).

🎙️ These three terms – radius, diameter, and circumference – are the building blocks for all our calculations. Make sure you clearly understand the relationship between them, especially how the diameter is twice the radius. The circumference is essentially the perimeter of the circle.

The Space Within: Area of a Circle

  • Area is the amount of surface enclosed by the boundary of a circle.
  • It tells us how much space the circle occupies.
  • Measured in square units (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ Imagine a circular field or a circular table. The area of the circle tells us the total amount of land in the field or the surface of the table. It's about the two-dimensional space that the circle covers, which is different from its boundary length, the circumference.

Formula for Area of a Circle

  • The area (A) of a circle with radius 'r' is given by A = πr².
  • Here, π (pi) is a mathematical constant, approximately 22/7 or 3.14.
  • Real-life example: How much dough is needed for a circular pizza?

🎙️ The formula A = πr² is fundamental. Remember, 'r' is the radius, and π is a constant value. Think about a pizza: the area tells you how much pizza there is to eat! If you increase the radius, the area increases much faster because it's squared.

Let's Calculate: Area of a Circular Park

  • Problem: Find the area of a circular park with a radius of 21 meters.
  • Step 1: Identify the given radius, r = 21 m.
  • Step 2: Use the formula A = πr².
  • Step 3: Substitute values: A = (22/7) × (21)² = 22/7 × 21 × 21.

🎙️ Let's work through an example together. If a park has a radius of 21 meters, applying the formula is straightforward. We use the approximate value of π as 22/7 for easier calculation. Notice how the units become square meters for area.

1Given r = 21m
2Formula A = πr²
3A = 22/7 × 21²
4A = 1386 m²

Around the Edge: Circumference of a Circle

  • Circumference is the total distance around the boundary of a circle.
  • It's like the perimeter of other shapes.
  • Formula: C = 2πr or C = πd, where 'r' is radius and 'd' is diameter.

🎙️ While area tells us the space inside, circumference tells us the length of the boundary. Imagine walking around a circular track; the distance you cover in one round is its circumference. Remember the two forms of the formula, using either radius or diameter.

Perimeter of a Circular Track

  • Problem: A circular track has a diameter of 70 meters. Find its circumference.
  • Step 1: Identify the given diameter, d = 70 m.
  • Step 2: Use the formula C = πd.
  • Step 3: Substitute values: C = (22/7) × 70.

🎙️ Here's an example using the diameter. If a track is 70 meters across, its circumference is easily found using the πd formula. This is useful for knowing how much fencing is needed around a circular garden or the length of a running track.

1Given d = 70m
2Formula C = πd
3C = 22/7 × 70
4C = 220 m

Slicing the Circle: What is a Sector?

  • A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.
  • Think of it as a 'slice' of pizza or a piece of a pie.
  • It has a specific central angle (θ).

🎙️ Now we move to parts of a circle. A sector is like a wedge cut from the center. It's defined by two straight lines (radii) extending from the center to the edge, and the curved part of the circle between them, called an arc. The angle at the center is crucial.

Area of a Sector of a Circle

  • The area of a sector depends on the central angle and the radius.
  • Formula: Area of Sector = (θ/360°) × πr².
  • Real-life example: The area of a fan's sweep or a clock's minute hand movement.

🎙️ The area of a sector is a fraction of the total area of the circle. The fraction is determined by the central angle (θ) divided by 360 degrees, which represents the full circle. This formula is vital for calculating space in situations like a rotating sprinkler's coverage.

Calculating Area of a Sector

  • Problem: Find the area of a sector with radius 7 cm and central angle 90°.
  • Step 1: Given r = 7 cm, θ = 90°.
  • Step 2: Use the formula Area = (θ/360°) × πr².
  • Step 3: Substitute values: Area = (90/360) × (22/7) × (7)².

🎙️ Let's apply the sector area formula. A 90-degree angle means it's a quarter of the circle, so we expect the area to be a quarter of the full circle's area. Always remember to simplify the fraction (θ/360°) first for easier calculation.

1Given r=7cm, θ=90°
2Formula (θ/360)πr²
3(90/360) × 22/7 × 7²
4Area = 38.5 cm²

Another Slice: What is a Segment?

  • A segment is a region of a circle bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • It's like the crust of a pizza slice cut straight across.
  • It can be a 'minor segment' (smaller) or 'major segment' (larger).

🎙️ Now, let's look at segments. Unlike a sector, which is defined by radii, a segment is cut off by a straight line called a chord. Think of the shape of a crescent moon; that's a segment. We usually focus on finding the area of the minor segment.

Area of a Segment of a Circle

  • Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle.
  • The triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord.
  • Formula: Area = (θ/360°)πr² - ½r²sinθ (for a minor segment).

🎙️ To find the area of a segment, we use a clever trick: we find the area of the sector that contains the segment, and then subtract the area of the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord. This leaves us with just the segment's area. Remember your trigonometry for the triangle area!

Finding Area of a Segment

  • Problem: Find the area of the minor segment of a circle with radius 10 cm and central angle 90°.
  • Step 1: Calculate Area of Sector = (90/360) × π(10)² = 78.5 cm² (using π=3.14).
  • Step 2: Calculate Area of Triangle = ½ × base × height = ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 cm² (for 90°).
  • Step 3: Area of Segment = Area of Sector - Area of Triangle.

🎙️ Let's walk through finding a segment's area. For a 90-degree angle, the triangle is a right-angled isosceles triangle, making its area calculation simple. Subtracting the triangle's area from the sector's area gives us the segment's area. Pay attention to using the correct value for π.

1Sector Area = 78.5 cm²
2Triangle Area = 50 cm²
3Segment Area = 28.5 cm²

Combining Shapes: A Tablecloth Design

Worked Example: Area of Shaded Region

  • Problem: Find the area of the shaded region in a square of side 14 cm, where a circle is inscribed.
  • Step 1: Area of Square = side² = 14² = 196 cm².
  • Step 2: The diameter of the inscribed circle is equal to the side of the square, so d = 14 cm, r = 7 cm.
  • Step 3: Area of Circle = πr² = (22/7) × 7² = 154 cm².

🎙️ This common type of problem asks for the area of the region outside an inscribed shape. Here, the shaded area is the square's area minus the circle's area. It's crucial to correctly identify the relationship between the dimensions of the square and the circle.

1Square Area = 196 cm²
2Circle Area = 154 cm²
3Shaded Area = 42 cm²
