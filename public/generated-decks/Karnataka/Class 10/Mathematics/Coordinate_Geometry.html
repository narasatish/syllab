What is Coordinate Geometry?

  • It's a branch of mathematics that uses a coordinate system.
  • We use numbers (coordinates) to locate points in space.
  • It connects algebra and geometry.

🎙️ Just like a map uses a grid to help you find places, coordinate geometry uses numbers to precisely locate points. It's like giving every point its own unique address!

The Cartesian Plane: Your Math Grid

  • Named after René Descartes.
  • Two perpendicular lines: X-axis (horizontal) and Y-axis (vertical).
  • They intersect at the Origin (0,0).
  • Divides the plane into four Quadrants.

🎙️ Imagine two number lines crossing each other at zero. That's your Cartesian plane! The horizontal line is the X-axis, and the vertical is the Y-axis. Their meeting point is the very important 'Origin'.

Plotting Points: Finding Your Way

  • A point is represented by an ordered pair (x, y).
  • The first number 'x' is the x-coordinate (horizontal distance from Y-axis).
  • The second number 'y' is the y-coordinate (vertical distance from X-axis).

🎙️ Think of it like giving directions: 'Go X steps right or left, then Y steps up or down'. The x-coordinate tells you how far left or right from the Y-axis, and the y-coordinate tells you how far up or down from the X-axis.

Distance Formula: How Far Apart?

  • How do we find the straight-line distance between two points?
  • Imagine measuring the distance between two towns on a map.
  • Coordinate geometry provides a precise formula for this.

🎙️ If you have two points, say your home and your school, on a coordinate grid, how would you calculate the direct distance between them? This is where our distance formula comes in handy!

📏
Measuring Distance
10 / 30

Deriving the Distance Formula

  • We can form a right-angled triangle using the two points.
  • The horizontal side is |x2 - x1|.
  • The vertical side is |y2 - y1|.
  • Apply Pythagoras theorem: hypotenuse² = side1² + side2².

🎙️ The beauty of coordinate geometry is how it connects to other concepts. By creating a right-angled triangle, we can use the famous Pythagoras theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse, which is our distance!

📐
Pythagoras Rule
11 / 30

The Distance Formula Defined

  • The distance 'd' between two points P(x1, y1) and Q(x2, y2) is:
  • d = √((x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²)
  • This formula always gives a positive distance.

🎙️ This is the core formula you need to remember for finding distances. Make sure to square the differences in x and y coordinates, add them, and then take the square root. The order of (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) doesn't matter for the squared differences.

Section Formula: Dividing a Line

  • What if a point divides a line segment into a specific ratio?
  • Imagine cutting a string into two pieces with a certain proportion.
  • The section formula helps us find that dividing point's coordinates.

🎙️ Sometimes, a point doesn't just lie on a line segment; it divides it into two parts in a particular ratio. The section formula is our tool to find the exact coordinates of such a point.

🍰
Cutting a Slice
15 / 30

Deriving the Section Formula

  • Consider a point P(x, y) dividing line segment AB in ratio m1:m2.
  • Using similar triangles, we can establish relationships between coordinates.
  • The ratio of corresponding sides will be equal.

🎙️ The derivation involves drawing perpendiculars from the points to the axes, creating similar triangles. This geometric insight leads us directly to the algebraic formula for the dividing point.

➗
Ratio Division
16 / 30

The Section Formula Defined

  • If P(x, y) divides the line segment joining A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2) internally in the ratio m1:m2, then:
  • x = (m1x2 + m2x1) / (m1 + m2)
  • y = (m1y2 + m2y1) / (m1 + m2)

🎙️ This is the section formula for internal division. Notice how m1 is multiplied by x2 and m2 by x1. This is a common point of confusion, so be careful! The denominator is simply the sum of the ratios.

✂️
Internal Division

Midpoint Formula: The Exact Middle

  • A special case of the section formula.
  • When a point divides a line segment into two equal halves (ratio 1:1).
  • The midpoint M(x, y) of a segment joining A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2) is:

🎙️ If the ratio m1:m2 is 1:1, it means the point is exactly in the middle. This simplifies our section formula greatly, giving us the midpoint formula.

🎯
Center Point
19 / 30

The Midpoint Formula Defined

  • x = (x1 + x2) / 2
  • y = (y1 + y2) / 2
  • It's simply the average of the x-coordinates and the average of the y-coordinates.

🎙️ This formula is very intuitive. To find the middle, you just add the corresponding coordinates and divide by two. It's like finding the average position.

⚖️
Equal Halves

Area of a Triangle: Using Coordinates

  • How can we find the area of a triangle if we only know its vertices?
  • No need for base and height!
  • Coordinate geometry offers a direct formula using the coordinates of the vertices.

🎙️ Traditionally, you calculate triangle area using base times height. But what if you only have the coordinates of the corners? Coordinate geometry gives us a neat formula to do just that!

🔺
Triangle Area
23 / 30

The Area of a Triangle Formula Defined

  • For a triangle with vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), and (x3, y3), the area is:
  • Area = 1/2 |x1(y2 - y3) + x2(y3 - y1) + x3(y1 - y2)|
  • The absolute value ensures the area is always positive.

🎙️ This formula might look complex, but it's very systematic. A good way to remember it is '123, 231, 312' for the y-subscripts within the brackets. The absolute value is crucial as area cannot be negative.

Collinearity: Points on a Line

  • Three or more points are collinear if they lie on the same straight line.
  • If three points are collinear, they cannot form a triangle.
  • Therefore, the area of the triangle formed by collinear points is zero.

🎙️ If three points are in a perfectly straight line, they can't enclose any space to form a triangle. This means their 'triangle area' will be zero. This is a very useful test for collinearity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sign Errors: Be careful with positive/negative values in formulas.
  • Mixing up (x1, y1) and (x2, y2): Consistency is key.
  • Calculation Errors: Double-check arithmetic, especially squares and square roots.
  • Forgetting Absolute Value: Area must always be positive.

🎙️ These are the typical pitfalls students face. A small sign error can change your entire answer. Always be systematic and recheck your steps, especially with negative numbers.
