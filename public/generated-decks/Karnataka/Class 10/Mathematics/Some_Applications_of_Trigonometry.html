The Path of Your Gaze: Line of Sight

  • Imagine you are looking at a kite flying high in the sky.
  • The imaginary line from your eyes to the kite is your 'Line of Sight'.

🎙️ It's simply the direct path your eyes follow when you look at an object. Whether you look up, down, or straight ahead, that's your line of sight.

Defining the Line of Sight

  • The Line of Sight is the straight line joining the eye of an observer to the object being viewed.
  • It's a fundamental concept in understanding how we perceive objects in space.

🎙️ This line is crucial because it forms one side of the imaginary right-angled triangle we'll use to solve problems. Remember, it's always from the observer's eye to the object.

What is the Angle of Elevation?

  • The Angle of Elevation is formed when the object is above the horizontal level.
  • It is the angle between the Line of Sight and the horizontal line.

🎙️ Imagine a horizontal line from your eye straight ahead. When you look up at an object, the angle between this horizontal line and your line of sight is the angle of elevation. It's always measured upwards from the horizontal.

What is the Angle of Depression?

  • The Angle of Depression is formed when the object is below the horizontal level.
  • It is the angle between the Line of Sight and the horizontal line.

🎙️ Similarly, if you draw a horizontal line from your eye straight ahead, the angle between this horizontal line and your line of sight, when looking down, is the angle of depression. Remember, it's always measured downwards from the horizontal.

Revisiting Our Trigonometric Ratios

  • SOH CAH TOA is our memory aid!
  • Sine (Sin) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Cosine (Cos) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Tangent (Tan) = Opposite / Adjacent

🎙️ A quick refresh! These three ratios are the backbone of solving problems in this chapter. Make sure you remember which sides correspond to which ratio.

Finding Heights: The Tall Tower

  • Imagine you want to find the height of a tower.
  • You know your distance from the tower and the angle of elevation to its top.
  • Using Tangent, we can calculate the height!

🎙️ If you know the base distance (adjacent) and the angle, and you want to find the height (opposite), the Tangent ratio is your best friend. Tan(angle) = Opposite/Adjacent.

Example: The Shadow of a Pole

  • A pole 15m high casts a shadow 15√3 m long on the ground.
  • Find the angle of elevation of the sun.
  • Steps: 1. Draw diagram. 2. Identify knowns/unknowns. 3. Choose ratio. 4. Solve.

🎙️ Here, the height is opposite to the angle, and the shadow length is adjacent. So, tan(θ) = 15 / (15√3) = 1/√3. We know tan(30°) = 1/√3. So, the angle of elevation is 30 degrees.

Your Problem-Solving Roadmap

Finding Distances: The Lighthouse

  • Imagine you are on top of a lighthouse, observing a ship.
  • You know the height of the lighthouse and the angle of depression to the ship.
  • Using Tangent, we can calculate the ship's distance from the lighthouse!

🎙️ Here, the height of the lighthouse is opposite to the angle, and the distance to the ship is adjacent. Again, Tangent is useful. Remember, the angle of depression from the observer is equal to the angle of elevation from the object due to alternate interior angles.

Example: Ship from a Lighthouse

  • A lighthouse is 75m high. From its top, the angle of depression of a ship is 30°.
  • Find the distance of the ship from the foot of the lighthouse.
  • Remember: Angle of depression = Angle of elevation.

🎙️ Draw the diagram. The angle of depression is 30°. This means the angle of elevation from the ship to the top of the lighthouse is also 30°. Tan(30°) = 75 / distance. So, distance = 75 / tan(30°) = 75√3 m.

Avoid These Common Mistakes!

  • Confusing Angle of Elevation with Angle of Depression (and vice-versa).
  • Incorrectly labelling sides (Opposite, Adjacent, Hypotenuse) relative to the angle.
  • Using the wrong trigonometric ratio for the given sides/angle.
  • Not drawing a clear diagram first.

🎙️ These are the pitfalls students often fall into. A careful approach, especially with diagramming and identifying angles, will save you many marks.
