Introducing Arithmetic Progression (AP)

  • A special type of sequence.
  • Each term is obtained by adding a fixed number to the preceding term.
  • Think of it like adding the same amount of money to your piggy bank every day.

🎙️ An Arithmetic Progression, or AP, is a sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is always the same. It's like a steady increase or decrease, just as if you add the same fixed amount to your savings daily.

📈
Consistent Growth

The Defining Feature: Constant Difference

  • In an AP, the difference between any term and its preceding term is constant.
  • This constant value is called the 'common difference'.
  • Example: 2, 5, 8, 11... (Difference is 3)

🎙️ The most important characteristic of an AP is this 'constant difference'. If you pick any two consecutive terms and subtract the first from the second, you'll always get the same number. This number is very important!

Adding the same

Common Difference (d)

  • Represented by 'd'.
  • Calculated as: d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = ...
  • Can be positive, negative, or zero.
  • Example: In 10, 8, 6, 4..., d = 8 - 10 = -2.

🎙️ The 'common difference', denoted by 'd', is found by subtracting any term from its succeeding term. Remember, 'd' can be positive (increasing AP), negative (decreasing AP), or even zero (all terms are the same).

🎙️ Every AP has a beginning, and that's what we call the 'first term'. It's the number from which the progression starts, and we usually denote it by the letter 'a' or 'a₁'.

General Form of an AP

  • If 'a' is the first term and 'd' is the common difference, an AP looks like:
  • a, a + d, a + 2d, a + 3d, ...
  • Each term is built upon the previous one by adding 'd'.

🎙️ This is the general way we represent an AP. Starting with the first term 'a', we keep adding the common difference 'd' to get the next term. Notice the pattern in the coefficient of 'd'.

11st term: a
22nd term: a + d
33rd term: a + 2d
44th term: a + 3d

Finding the 'nth' Term (aₙ)

  • What if you want to find the 50th term without listing all of them?
  • The formula: aₙ = a + (n - 1)d
  • Here, 'aₙ' is the nth term, 'a' is the first term, 'n' is the term number, and 'd' is the common difference.

🎙️ This formula is incredibly powerful! It allows us to find any term in an AP without having to write out the entire sequence. Imagine wanting the 100th term – this formula makes it easy.

Measuring any term

Deriving the 'nth' Term Formula

  • a₁ = a
  • a₂ = a + d
  • a₃ = a + 2d
  • a₄ = a + 3d
  • Notice the pattern: for a_n, 'd' is multiplied by (n-1).

🎙️ Let's see how the formula aₙ = a + (n-1)d comes about. By observing the pattern for the first few terms, we can clearly see that the coefficient of 'd' is always one less than the term number 'n'. This leads us directly to the general formula.

1a₁ = a + (1-1)d
2a₂ = a + (2-1)d
3a₃ = a + (3-1)d
4aₙ = a + (n-1)d

Worked Example 1: Finding a Specific Term

  • Problem: Find the 10th term of the AP: 2, 7, 12, ...
  • Step 1: Identify a = 2, d = 7 - 2 = 5, n = 10.
  • Step 2: Use formula aₙ = a + (n - 1)d.
  • Step 3: a₁₀ = 2 + (10 - 1) × 5 = 2 + 9 × 5 = 2 + 45 = 47.

🎙️ Here's a step-by-step example. We first identify the given values: the first term, the common difference, and the term number we need to find. Then, we simply substitute these values into our formula and calculate.

Worked Example 2: Finding the Term Number

  • Problem: Which term of the AP: 21, 18, 15, ... is -81?
  • Step 1: Identify a = 21, d = 18 - 21 = -3, aₙ = -81.
  • Step 2: Use formula aₙ = a + (n - 1)d.
  • Step 3: -81 = 21 + (n - 1)(-3).
  • Step 4: -81 - 21 = -3n + 3 => -102 = -3n + 3 => -105 = -3n => n = 35.

🎙️ This time, we know the value of a term, but we need to find its position. We use the same formula, but we'll be solving for 'n'. Be careful with the signs, especially when 'd' is negative.

Deriving the Sum Formula (Sₙ)

  • Legend says young Gauss quickly summed 1 to 100!
  • He wrote the sum forwards and backwards, then added them.
  • Sₙ = a + (a+d) + ... + (a+(n-1)d)
  • Sₙ = (a+(n-1)d) + ... + (a+d) + a
  • 2Sₙ = n × [2a + (n-1)d]
  • Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]

🎙️ This formula has a fascinating origin story involving the brilliant mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss. By writing the sum twice, once forwards and once backwards, and then adding them, he discovered this elegant formula. Each pair sums to the same value!

🧠
Gauss's Trick
19 / 30

Alternative Sum Formula (Sₙ)

  • If the last term (l or aₙ) is known:
  • Sₙ = n/2 [a + l]
  • This is a simpler version when you already know the first and last terms.
  • Remember, l = aₙ = a + (n-1)d.

🎙️ We have two formulas for the sum of an AP. The second one is particularly useful when the last term of the progression is already given. It saves a step of calculation, making it quicker to find the sum.

Sₙ = n/2[2a+(n-1)d]
Sₙ = n/2[a+l]

Worked Example 3: Sum of 'n' Terms

  • Problem: Find the sum of the first 20 terms of the AP: 1, 3, 5, 7, ...
  • Step 1: Identify a = 1, d = 3 - 1 = 2, n = 20.
  • Step 2: Use formula Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d].
  • Step 3: S₂₀ = 20/2 [2(1) + (20 - 1)2].
  • Step 4: S₂₀ = 10 [2 + 19 × 2] = 10 [2 + 38] = 10 × 40 = 400.

🎙️ Let's apply the sum formula. First, identify 'a', 'd', and 'n'. Then, carefully substitute these values into the formula and perform the calculations. Double-check your arithmetic to avoid errors.

Worked Example 4: Sum with Last Term

  • Problem: Find the sum of the AP: 7, 10, 13, ..., 85.
  • Step 1: Identify a = 7, d = 3, l = 85.
  • Step 2: First, find 'n' using aₙ = a + (n - 1)d: 85 = 7 + (n - 1)3 => 78 = (n - 1)3 => 26 = n - 1 => n = 27.
  • Step 3: Now use Sₙ = n/2 [a + l]: S₂₇ = 27/2 [7 + 85].
  • Step 4: S₂₇ = 27/2 [92] = 27 × 46 = 1242.

🎙️ This problem requires two steps. Since 'n' (the number of terms) is not given, we first use the nth term formula to find 'n'. Once 'n' is known, we can use the simpler sum formula with the first and last terms.

Worked Example 5: Real-Life Application

  • Problem: A man saves ₹1000 in the first month, ₹1200 in the second, ₹1400 in the third, and so on. How much will he save in 10 months?
  • Step 1: Identify AP: 1000, 1200, 1400... a = 1000, d = 200, n = 10.
  • Step 2: Find total savings (S₁₀) using Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d].
  • Step 3: S₁₀ = 10/2 [2(1000) + (10 - 1)200].
  • Step 4: S₁₀ = 5 [2000 + 9 × 200] = 5 [2000 + 1800] = 5 × 3800 = ₹19000.

🎙️ Here's a practical example of how APs can help manage finances. By recognizing the pattern of savings, we can easily calculate the total amount saved over a period using our sum formula.

Arithmetic Mean

  • If a, b, c are in AP, then 'b' is the Arithmetic Mean of 'a' and 'c'.
  • This means: b - a = c - b
  • So, 2b = a + c, or b = (a + c) / 2.
  • Example: For 3, 5, 7, the Arithmetic Mean of 3 and 7 is 5.

🎙️ The concept of Arithmetic Mean is closely related to APs. If three numbers are in an AP, the middle number is the arithmetic mean of the other two. It's essentially the average of the two numbers.

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Forgetting '(n-1)' in aₙ or Sₙ formulas.
  • Sign errors, especially with negative common differences.
  • Confusing 'n' (term number) with 'aₙ' (value of the nth term).
  • Using the wrong formula (e.g., sum formula for nth term).
  • Calculation errors in basic arithmetic.

🎙️ Even experienced students make these common errors. Always double-check your formula, pay close attention to positive and negative signs, and be clear about what each variable represents in the problem.
