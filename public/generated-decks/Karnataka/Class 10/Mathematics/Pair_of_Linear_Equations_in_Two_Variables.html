Definition: Linear Equation in Two Variables

  • An equation of the form ax + by + c = 0.
  • Here, 'a', 'b', 'c' are real numbers, and a ≠ 0, b ≠ 0.
  • 'x' and 'y' are the two variables.

🎙️ This is the standard form for a linear equation in two variables. The 'linear' part means that when you graph it, it forms a straight line. The coefficients 'a' and 'b' cannot be zero simultaneously.

🎙️ Here, 'x' is the number of apples and 'y' is the number of bananas. This equation shows how the cost of apples and bananas adds up to your total bill. Can you think of possible values for x and y?

Solution of a Linear Equation in Two Variables

  • A pair of values (x, y) that satisfies the equation.
  • When substituted, the Left Hand Side equals the Right Hand Side.
  • A linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions.

🎙️ For the equation 10x + 5y = 50, (3, 4) is a solution because 10(3) + 5(4) = 30 + 20 = 50. But (1, 8) is also a solution! There are many such pairs.

Graphical Representation

🎙️ When we plot all the possible solutions of a linear equation in two variables on a graph, they all lie on a single straight line. This is why they are called 'linear' equations.

Introducing 'Pair' of Linear Equations

  • Sometimes, one equation isn't enough to find unique values for x and y.
  • We need two equations that relate the same two variables.
  • This forms a 'pair' of linear equations.

🎙️ If you just know 10x + 5y = 50, you have many options for x and y. But if you have another condition, like 'total number of items is 7' (x + y = 7), then there might be only one specific combination of x and y that works for both.

General Form of a Pair of Linear Equations

  • Equation 1: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
  • Equation 2: a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0
  • a₁, b₁, c₁, a₂, b₂, c₂ are real numbers.

🎙️ Here, the subscripts 1 and 2 distinguish the coefficients of the first and second equations. Remember that a₁, b₁, a₂, b₂ cannot be zero simultaneously in their respective equations.

Methods to Solve a Pair of Linear Equations

  • We can solve them using graphical methods.
  • We can also solve them using algebraic methods.
  • Both methods lead to the same solution.

🎙️ Just like there are different routes to reach a destination, there are different methods to solve these pairs of equations. We will explore both graphical and algebraic approaches.

Graphical Method: Steps

  • Step 1: Draw the graph for the first equation.
  • Step 2: Draw the graph for the second equation on the same plane.
  • Step 3: Observe the intersection point (or lack thereof).

🎙️ The graphical method involves visualizing the equations as lines. The point where these lines meet, if they do, is the common solution to both equations.

1Graph Eq 1
2Graph Eq 2
3Find Intersection

Graphical Method: Case 1 - Intersecting Lines

  • The two lines cross at exactly one point.
  • This point (x, y) is the unique solution to the pair.
  • The equations are called 'consistent' and 'independent'.

🎙️ When the graphs of two linear equations intersect, it means there's a single, specific pair of (x, y) values that satisfies both equations. This is the most common scenario for problems.

✖️
One common point
16 / 26

Graphical Method: Case 2 - Parallel Lines

  • The two lines never cross each other.
  • They maintain a constant distance apart.
  • There is no common solution; the pair is 'inconsistent'.

🎙️ If the lines are parallel, they will never intersect, no matter how far you extend them. This means there is no (x, y) pair that can satisfy both equations simultaneously.

🛤️
Never meeting
17 / 26

Graphical Method: Case 3 - Coincident Lines

  • The two lines lie exactly on top of each other.
  • Every point on one line is also on the other line.
  • There are infinitely many solutions; the pair is 'consistent' and 'dependent'.

🎙️ Coincident lines mean the two equations are essentially the same, perhaps one is just a multiple of the other. Since they share all their points, they have endless common solutions.

♾️
Overlapping lines

Worked Example: Graphical Method

  • Solve: x + y = 5 and x - y = 1.
  • For x + y = 5: Points (0,5), (5,0), (2,3).
  • For x - y = 1: Points (0,-1), (1,0), (3,2).
  • Plotting these lines, they intersect at (3,2).

🎙️ To solve this graphically, first find a few points for each equation by choosing values for x and calculating y. Then, plot these points and draw the lines. You'll see they cross at (3,2), which is the unique solution.

Algebraic Methods: Introduction

  • Solving equations without drawing graphs.
  • More precise and efficient for complex numbers.
  • Two main methods: Substitution and Elimination.

🎙️ While graphical methods give us a visual understanding, algebraic methods use calculations to find the exact solution. These are particularly useful when the intersection point is not an integer.

Algebraic Method 1: Substitution Method Steps

  • Step 1: Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation.
  • Step 2: Substitute this expression into the second equation.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting linear equation in one variable.

🎙️ The idea here is to reduce the problem of two variables into a simpler problem of one variable. We 'substitute' one variable's value to simplify the problem.

1Isolate a variable
2Substitute into other eq
3Solve for one variable
22 / 26

Worked Example: Substitution Method

  • Solve: 2x + 3y = 7 (1) and x - y = 1 (2).
  • From (2), x = 1 + y. (Step 1)
  • Substitute x in (1): 2(1+y) + 3y = 7. (Step 2)
  • 2 + 2y + 3y = 7 => 5y = 5 => y = 1. (Step 3)
  • Substitute y=1 into x=1+y => x = 1+1 = 2. Solution: (2,1).

🎙️ This example shows how we systematically find the value of one variable, then use it to find the other. Always check your solution by plugging the values back into both original equations.

23 / 26

Algebraic Method 2: Elimination Method Steps

  • Step 1: Make the coefficients of one variable equal in both equations.
  • Step 2: Add or subtract the equations to 'eliminate' that variable.
  • Step 3: Solve the resulting linear equation in one variable.

🎙️ The goal of elimination is to get rid of one variable by making its coefficients the same (or opposite) in both equations, then adding or subtracting them. This leaves you with an equation with only one variable.

1Equalize coefficients
2Add/Subtract equations
3Solve for one variable
24 / 26

Worked Example: Elimination Method

  • Solve: 3x + 4y = 10 (1) and 2x - 2y = 2 (2).
  • Multiply (2) by 2: 4x - 4y = 4 (3). (Step 1)
  • Add (1) and (3): (3x+4y) + (4x-4y) = 10+4 => 7x = 14 => x = 2. (Step 2)
  • Substitute x=2 into (1): 3(2) + 4y = 10 => 6 + 4y = 10 => 4y = 4 => y = 1. Solution: (2,1).

🎙️ Notice how multiplying the second equation by 2 allowed us to easily eliminate 'y' by adding the equations. This method is often quicker when coefficients can be easily matched.

Recap: Key Concepts

  • Linear equations in two variables: ax + by + c = 0.
  • A pair can have unique, no, or infinitely many solutions.
  • Graphical methods show intersection types.
  • Algebraic methods (Substitution, Elimination) find exact solutions.
