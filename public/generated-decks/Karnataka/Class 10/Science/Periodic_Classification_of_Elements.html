Dobereiner's Triads: Early Grouping

  • Proposed by Johann Dobereiner in 1817
  • Grouped elements in threes (triads)
  • Elements in a triad had similar properties

🎙️ Johann Dobereiner, in 1817, noticed that some elements with similar properties could be grouped in threes, which he called 'triads'. He found a peculiar relationship between their atomic masses.

1Identify 3 elements
2Similar properties
3Middle element's mass is avg of other two
4 / 25

Example: A Dobereiner Triad

  • Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), Potassium (K)
  • Atomic masses: Li ≈ 7, Na ≈ 23, K ≈ 39
  • Average of Li and K = (7+39)/2 = 23 (approx. Na's mass)

🎙️ Consider Lithium (atomic mass ~7), Sodium (~23), and Potassium (~39). Sodium's atomic mass (23) is roughly the average of Lithium and Potassium ((7+39)/2 = 23). All three are reactive metals.

⚛️⚛️⚛️
Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), Potassium (K)

Limitations of Dobereiner's Triads

  • Could only identify a few triads
  • Did not work for all known elements
  • Not a comprehensive classification system

🎙️ While a good start, Dobereiner could only identify a few such triads. Many known elements at the time did not fit into this pattern, making it an incomplete classification system.

❌
Not many elements fit this rule.

Newlands' Law of Octaves: Musical Harmony

  • Proposed by John Newlands in 1866
  • Arranged elements by increasing atomic mass
  • Properties of every eighth element were similar

🎙️ John Newlands, in 1866, arranged elements in increasing order of atomic mass and observed a fascinating pattern: the properties of every eighth element were similar to the first, just like in a musical octave.

🎶
Elements repeat properties like musical notes.
7 / 25

Example: Octave Pattern

  • H, Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F
  • Fluorine (F) has properties similar to Hydrogen (H)
  • Sodium (Na) has properties similar to Lithium (Li)

🎙️ If we start with Hydrogen, the eighth element, Fluorine, has similar properties. Similarly, after Lithium, the eighth element, Sodium, shares properties with Lithium.

Limitations of Newlands' Octaves

  • Only applicable up to Calcium
  • New elements didn't fit the rule
  • Did not account for undiscovered elements

🎙️ This law worked well only for lighter elements, up to Calcium. As more elements were discovered, and with heavier elements, the octave rule began to break down, making it unsuitable for a universal classification.

🛑
Only worked for lighter elements.

Mendeleev's Periodic Table: A Major Breakthrough

  • Developed by Dmitri Mendeleev in 1869
  • Arranged elements by increasing atomic mass
  • First comprehensive and widely accepted table

🎙️ Dmitri Mendeleev, a Russian chemist, is widely credited with developing the first widely accepted and comprehensive periodic table in 1869. His genius lay in his bold predictions.

The periodic table organizes elements by atomic number and electron configuration. Similar

Mendeleev's Periodic Law

  • Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses
  • Arranged elements into rows and columns
  • Focused on chemical properties like oxide and hydride formation

🎙️ Mendeleev's law stated that 'the properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses.' He arranged elements primarily based on increasing atomic mass, but also considered their chemical properties.

Achievements of Mendeleev's Table

  • Predicted properties of undiscovered elements
  • Left gaps for future elements (e.g., Eka-Aluminium/Gallium)
  • Corrected atomic masses of some elements

🎙️ Mendeleev bravely left gaps for undiscovered elements, predicting their properties (like Eka-Aluminium, later Gallium). He also corrected the atomic masses of some elements, showing remarkable foresight.

✨✨✨
Predicted new elements and left gaps.
12 / 25

Limitations of Mendeleev's Table

  • Position of isotopes was unclear
  • Anomalous pairs (e.g., Ar before K)
  • Position of Hydrogen was not fixed

🎙️ Despite its success, Mendeleev's table had issues. Isotopes (atoms of the same element with different atomic masses) posed a challenge. Also, the position of Hydrogen was ambiguous, sometimes placed with alkali metals, sometimes with halogens.

🚧
Couldn't explain isotopes or Hydrogen's position.

The Modern Periodic Table: A New Foundation

  • Proposed by Henry Moseley in 1913
  • Atomic number is the fundamental property
  • Resolved many issues of Mendeleev's table

🎙️ In 1913, Henry Moseley showed that atomic number (the number of protons) is a more fundamental property of an element than its atomic mass. This discovery revolutionized the periodic table.

💡
Atomic number is the fundamental property.
15 / 25

Modern Periodic Law

  • Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers
  • Elements arranged by increasing atomic number
  • Basis for the current periodic table

🎙️ The Modern Periodic Law states: 'The properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.' This corrected Mendeleev's law and resolved many of its limitations.

⚛️
Properties are periodic function of atomic number.

Features of the Modern Periodic Table: Periods

  • Horizontal rows are called periods
  • There are 7 periods
  • Elements in a period have the same number of shells

🎙️ The horizontal rows in the modern periodic table are called periods. There are 7 periods. Elements in a period have the same number of electron shells, but their valence electrons increase from left to right.

Features of the Modern Periodic Table: Groups

  • Vertical columns are called groups
  • There are 18 groups
  • Elements in a group have similar chemical properties

🎙️ The vertical columns are called groups. There are 18 groups. Elements within the same group have similar chemical properties because they have the same number of valence electrons.

Trends in the Modern Periodic Table: Valency

  • Combining capacity of an element
  • Across a period: increases then decreases
  • Down a group: remains the same

🎙️ Valency is the combining capacity of an element. In a period, valency first increases from 1 to 4 and then decreases to 0. In a group, all elements have the same valency as they have the same number of valence electrons.

🔗
Combining capacity of an element.

Trends: Atomic Size (Radius)

  • Across a period: decreases due to increased nuclear charge
  • Down a group: increases due to addition of new shells

🎙️ Atomic size generally decreases across a period from left to right due to increased nuclear charge pulling electrons closer. It increases down a group because new shells are added, increasing the distance from the nucleus.

Trends: Metallic and Non-metallic Character

  • Metallic character: tendency to lose electrons
  • Non-metallic character: tendency to gain electrons
  • Across a period: metallic decreases, non-metallic increases

🎙️ Metallic character decreases across a period (elements tend to lose electrons less easily). Non-metallic character increases across a period. Down a group, metallic character increases because electrons are further from the nucleus and easier to lose.
