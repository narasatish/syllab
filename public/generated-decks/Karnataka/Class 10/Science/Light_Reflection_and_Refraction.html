What is Light?

  • A form of energy that enables us to see.
  • Travels in straight lines (rectilinear propagation).
  • Does not require a medium to travel (can travel through vacuum).

🎙️ Light is a fundamental form of energy that makes our vision possible. It travels incredibly fast, always in straight lines, and can even cross the vast emptiness of space to reach us.

Laws of Reflection

  • 1. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
  • 2. Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.

🎙️ These two laws are fundamental to understanding how light behaves when it reflects. The first law tells us about the angles, and the second ensures everything happens on a flat surface, making it predictable.

Plane Mirrors

  • Flat reflecting surfaces.
  • Produce virtual, erect, and same-sized images.
  • Image is laterally inverted.

🎙️ Plane mirrors are the simplest type of mirror, like the ones you use at home. They create images that appear behind the mirror, are upright, and are the same size as the object, but left and right are swapped.

Image by Plane Mirror

  • Distance of image from mirror = distance of object from mirror.
  • Image is virtual and erect.
  • Image is laterally inverted.

🎙️ When you stand in front of a plane mirror, your image appears to be as far behind the mirror as you are in front. Remember, the image is virtual because light rays don't actually meet there.

Ray Diagram Rules: Concave Mirror

  • 1. Parallel ray passes through F after reflection.
  • 2. Ray through F becomes parallel after reflection.
  • 3. Ray through C reflects back along the same path.
  • 4. Ray incident at P reflects symmetrically.

🎙️ These four rules are crucial for drawing ray diagrams and understanding image formation by concave mirrors. By applying these, we can accurately locate the image for any object position.

Concave Mirror: Object at Infinity

  • Image is formed at the focus (F).

🎙️ When an object is very far away, like the sun, the light rays reaching the mirror are nearly parallel. A concave mirror converges these rays to form a tiny, real image at its focus.

  • 1. Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
  • 2. Snell's Law: (sin i) / (sin r) = constant (refractive index).

🎙️ Similar to reflection, refraction also has two key laws. The first is about the plane, and the second, known as Snell's Law, quantifies how much light bends based on the properties of the two media.

Refractive Index (n)

  • A measure of how much light bends when entering a medium.
  • Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in medium (n = c/v).
  • Higher refractive index means more bending.

🎙️ The refractive index is a very important concept. It tells us how optically dense a material is. A higher refractive index means light slows down more and bends more significantly when entering that medium.

Refraction: Pencil in Water

  • A pencil partially immersed in water appears bent.
  • Light rays from the submerged part bend as they leave water and enter air.
  • Our brain perceives the pencil in a different position.

🎙️ This is a classic demonstration of refraction. The light coming from the part of the pencil in water bends as it exits the water, making the pencil appear 'broken' or bent at the water's surface.
