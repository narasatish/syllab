What are Elements?

  • Pure substances made of only one type of atom
  • Cannot be broken down into simpler substances
  • Building blocks of all matter around us

🎙️ An element is a pure substance consisting only of atoms that all have the same numbers of protons in their atomic nuclei. Think of them as the fundamental building blocks from which everything in the universe is made.

Classifying Elements: Metals, Non-metals, and Metalloids

  • Elements are broadly categorized based on properties
  • Metals are generally shiny, hard, and good conductors
  • Non-metals are often dull, brittle, and poor conductors
  • Metalloids show properties of both metals and non-metals

🎙️ We can classify elements into three main groups: metals, non-metals, and metalloids. This classification helps us understand their general behavior and predict their reactions. Today, we'll dive deep into metals and non-metals.

  • Ability of a metal to be hammered into thin sheets
  • Without breaking or cracking
  • A key characteristic of most metals

🎙️ Malleability is a fascinating property of metals. Imagine taking a piece of metal and flattening it into a sheet without it breaking. This ability to be hammered into thin sheets is called malleability.

  • Metals are good conductors of heat
  • Metals are good conductors of electricity
  • Free electrons enable this excellent conductivity

🎙️ Metals are excellent conductors of both heat and electricity. This is why cooking utensils are made of metal and electrical wires use metals like copper. The presence of free electrons allows heat and charge to move easily through them.

Physical Properties of Non-metals: The Opposites

  • Generally non-lustrous (dull appearance)
  • Non-malleable and non-ductile (brittle)
  • Poor conductors of heat and electricity (insulators)
  • Not sonorous, often soft (except diamond)

🎙️ Non-metals typically exhibit properties that are opposite to those of metals. They are usually dull, brittle, and poor conductors. Think of carbon in the form of coal, which is dull and breaks easily.

Exceptions to the Rules: Unconventional Elements

  • Mercury: A metal, but liquid at room temperature
  • Iodine: A non-metal, but has a lustrous appearance
  • Carbon (Diamond): A non-metal, but very hard
  • Graphite (Carbon): A non-metal, but conducts electricity

🎙️ While general rules apply, science always has fascinating exceptions! Mercury is a metal but liquid, while non-metals like Iodine and Carbon (diamond, graphite) show metallic or conductive properties. These exceptions remind us to observe carefully.

Chemical Properties: Metals and Oxygen

  • Metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides
  • Metal + Oxygen → Metal Oxide
  • These oxides are generally basic in nature

🎙️ Now, let's explore how metals react chemically. When metals react with oxygen, they form metal oxides. For example, magnesium burns in air to form magnesium oxide, which is a basic oxide.

Chemical Properties: Non-metals and Oxygen

🎙️ Just like metals, non-metals also react with oxygen. However, the oxides formed by non-metals are typically acidic or neutral. For instance, carbon reacts with oxygen to form carbon dioxide, which is an acidic gas.

Chemical Properties: Metals and Water

  • Some metals react with water (cold/hot/steam)
  • Form metal oxide/hydroxide and hydrogen gas
  • Reactivity varies greatly among metals

🎙️ Metals show varying reactivity with water. Highly reactive metals like sodium react vigorously even with cold water, while others like iron react only with steam. This reaction produces metal oxides or hydroxides and hydrogen gas.

🎙️ When metals react with dilute acids, they typically produce a metal salt and hydrogen gas. This is a common laboratory reaction. However, very unreactive metals like gold do not react with dilute acids.
