How We See: A Step-by-Step Process

  • Light enters through cornea & pupil
  • Lens focuses light on retina
  • Retina forms inverted, real image
  • Optic nerve sends signals to brain
  • Brain interprets signals as upright image

🎙️ So, to summarize, light first enters the eye, gets focused by the lens onto the retina, which then sends signals to the brain. The brain is the ultimate interpreter, turning those signals into the clear, colourful world we perceive.

Myopia: Nearsightedness & Correction

  • Can see near objects clearly, distant objects blurred
  • Image forms in front of the retina
  • Corrected using a concave (diverging) lens

🎙️ In myopia, the eye lens converges light too strongly, or the eyeball is too long, causing the image to form before the retina. A concave lens helps to diverge the light slightly before it enters the eye, allowing it to focus correctly on the retina.

👀

Hypermetropia: Farsightedness & Correction

  • Can see distant objects clearly, near objects blurred
  • Image forms behind the retina
  • Corrected using a convex (converging) lens

🎙️ Hypermetropia occurs when the eye lens is too weak, or the eyeball is too short, causing the image to form behind the retina. A convex lens helps to converge the light more strongly, ensuring it focuses precisely on the retina.

👓

Presbyopia: Age-Related Vision Loss

  • Loss of accommodation power with age
  • Ciliary muscles weaken, lens becomes less flexible
  • Difficulty seeing near objects clearly
  • Corrected with bifocal or convex lenses

🎙️ Presbyopia is a natural part of aging, where the eye's ability to focus on close objects diminishes. This is why many older adults need reading glasses, which are typically convex lenses.

Why Do Stars Twinkle?

  • Due to atmospheric refraction
  • Light from stars passes through turbulent air layers
  • Appears to flicker or twinkle to our eyes

🎙️ Stars appear to twinkle because their light travels through many layers of the Earth's atmosphere, which are constantly moving and changing density. This causes the light to bend irregularly, making the star appear to flicker.

Why the Sky is Blue and Sun is Red

  • Blue light scatters more than red light
  • Short wavelengths (blue) scatter effectively by air molecules
  • Sun appears reddish at sunrise/sunset due to more scattering of blue light

🎙️ This is a direct consequence of scattering. Blue light, having a shorter wavelength, scatters much more efficiently by the tiny molecules in our atmosphere, making the sky appear blue. At sunrise or sunset, sunlight travels through more atmosphere, scattering away most of the blue light, leaving the reds and oranges.
