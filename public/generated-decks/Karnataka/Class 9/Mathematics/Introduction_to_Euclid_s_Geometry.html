What is Geometry?

  • From Greek words 'Geo' (Earth) and 'Metron' (Measure).
  • Deals with shapes, sizes, positions, and properties of figures.
  • It's the study of space and its dimensions.

🎙️ Geometry literally means 'Earth Measurement'. It's a branch of mathematics concerned with the properties and relations of points, lines, surfaces, solids, and higher dimensional analogs. Think of it as the science of shapes and space.

Euclid's Axiomatic Approach

  • Built geometry from a few basic, self-evident truths.
  • These truths are called Axioms or Postulates.
  • All other theorems are derived logically from these basics.

🎙️ Euclid's genius was in creating a logical system. He started with a small set of undeniable truths, called axioms and postulates, and then used logical deduction to prove all other geometric facts. This systematic approach is called the axiomatic method.

Undefined Terms: Point

  • It has no size, no dimension, only position.
  • Represented by a dot and labeled with a capital letter (e.g., A).

🎙️ In geometry, some terms are so fundamental that we don't define them formally; we just understand what they mean. A 'point' is one such term. It's like a tiny dot, representing a precise location, but having no physical size.

Undefined Terms: Line

Axioms vs. Postulates

  • Both are fundamental truths, assumed without proof.
  • Axioms are general truths, applicable across mathematics.
  • Postulates are specific to geometry.

🎙️ Euclid used both axioms and postulates. While both are self-evident truths we accept without proof, axioms are universal mathematical statements, whereas postulates are specific assumptions made within the realm of geometry.

Euclid's Axiom 1

  • Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
  • If A = C and B = C, then A = B.
  • Example: If your height equals your friend's, and your friend's height equals your cousin's, then your height equals your cousin's.

🎙️ This axiom is very intuitive. It simply states that if two different things are both equal to a third thing, then those two things must also be equal to each other. It's a basic principle of equality.

Euclid's Axiom 2

  • If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
  • If A = B, then A + C = B + C.
  • Example: If you have 5 candies and your friend has 5 candies, and both of you get 2 more, you still have an equal number of candies.

🎙️ Axiom 2 tells us that if we start with two equal quantities and add the same amount to both, they will remain equal. This holds true for numbers, lengths, areas, or any other measurable quantity.

Euclid's Axiom 3

  • If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
  • If A = B, then A - C = B - C.
  • Example: If two students score 90 marks each, and both lose 5 marks for a late submission, their final scores remain equal.

🎙️ Similar to addition, Axiom 3 states that if we subtract the same amount from two equal quantities, the remaining amounts will also be equal. This principle is fundamental in solving equations.

Euclid's Axiom 4

  • Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another.
  • If object X perfectly covers object Y, then X and Y are equal.
  • Example: If two identical coins are placed exactly one on top of the other, they are equal in size.

🎙️ Axiom 4 is about congruence. If two geometric figures can be placed one over the other such that they perfectly cover each other, then they are considered equal in all respects, meaning they are identical.

Euclid's Axiom 5

  • The whole is greater than the part.
  • If A is a part of B, then B > A.
  • Example: A whole pizza is always larger than any single slice of that pizza.

🎙️ This axiom states a very obvious truth: a complete entity is always larger than any one of its components. For instance, the entire length of a road is greater than any segment of that road.

Euclid's Axiom 6

  • Things which are double of the same things are equal to one another.
  • If A = B, then 2A = 2B.
  • Example: If the length of rope A is equal to rope B, then two ropes of length A combined will be equal to two ropes of length B combined.

🎙️ Axiom 6 extends the idea of equality to multiplication. If two quantities are equal, then doubling both of them will result in two new quantities that are also equal to each other.

Euclid's Axiom 7

  • Things which are halves of the same things are equal to one another.
  • If A = B, then A/2 = B/2.
  • Example: If two cakes are of the same size, then half of the first cake will be equal in size to half of the second cake.

🎙️ Similarly, Axiom 7 applies to division. If two quantities are equal, then taking half of each will result in two new quantities that are still equal. This is useful for understanding concepts like midpoints.

  • Form the basis for geometric constructions and proofs.

🎙️ Now let's look at postulates, which are the geometric-specific truths. These are statements that are so basic and self-evident within geometry that we accept them without needing a formal proof.

Euclid's Postulate 1

  • A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.
  • Through any two distinct points, there is exactly one unique straight line.
  • Example: If you mark two dots on a paper, you can only draw one straight line connecting them.

🎙️ Postulate 1 is very intuitive. It simply states that if you have two distinct points, you can always draw one, and only one, straight line that passes through both of them. This is a fundamental concept for defining lines.

Euclid's Postulate 2

  • A terminated line can be produced indefinitely.
  • A line segment can be extended infinitely in both directions to form a line.
  • Example: If you have a pencil line segment, you can imagine it continuing forever.

🎙️ Postulate 2 means that any line segment we draw, which has definite endpoints, can be extended without limit in either direction. This is how we get the concept of an infinite line from a finite segment.

Euclid's Postulate 3

  • A circle can be drawn with any center and any radius.
  • You can create a circle anywhere, of any size you wish.
  • Example: Using a compass, you can draw a circle with a chosen center point and any desired opening (radius).

🎙️ This postulate gives us the freedom to construct circles. It means that to draw a circle, all we need is a center point and a specific distance for the radius, and we can draw it anywhere and any size.

Euclid's Postulate 4

  • All right angles are equal to one another.
  • A right angle always measures 90 degrees.
  • Example: The corner of a square table and the corner of a door frame both form right angles, and they are equal.

🎙️ Postulate 4 simplifies our understanding of right angles. It states that no matter where or how a right angle is formed, its measure is always the same, 90 degrees. This is crucial for consistency in geometry.

Euclid's Postulate 5 (The Parallel Postulate)

  • If a line falling on two lines makes interior angles less than two right angles on one side,
  • then the two lines will meet on that side if produced indefinitely.
  • This postulate is foundational for understanding parallel lines.

🎙️ This is Euclid's most famous and complex postulate. It essentially describes what happens when two lines are not parallel. If the interior angles on one side add up to less than 180 degrees, those lines will eventually intersect on that side.
