The Origin (0,0)

  • Where the X-axis and Y-axis meet.
  • The starting point for all coordinates.
  • Its coordinates are (0,0).

🎙️ The point where the X-axis and Y-axis cross is very special. We call it the Origin, and it's our central reference point, like the center of a map. Its address is always (0,0).

Ordered Pairs (x,y): Our Address System

  • A point's location is given by two numbers.
  • Always written as (x-coordinate, y-coordinate).
  • The order matters! (2,3) is different from (3,2).

🎙️ Every point on our plane has a unique address, called an ordered pair. The first number tells us how far along the X-axis, and the second tells us how far along the Y-axis. Remember, the order is crucial!

Quadrants: Dividing Our Map

  • The axes divide the plane into four regions.
  • These regions are called quadrants.
  • Numbered I, II, III, IV in anti-clockwise direction.

🎙️ Our X and Y axes cut the entire plane into four distinct parts, like four different neighborhoods on our map. We call these Quadrants, and they are numbered in a specific counter-clockwise order.

Quadrant I: Top-Right Section

  • Both x and y coordinates are positive.
  • Example: (3, 5)
  • Moves right from origin, then up.

🎙️ Quadrant I is the top-right section. Here, both the x-coordinate and the y-coordinate will always be positive numbers. Think of it as moving right and then up from the origin.

Quadrant II: Top-Left Section

  • x-coordinate is negative, y-coordinate is positive.
  • Example: (-2, 4)
  • Moves left from origin, then up.

🎙️ Moving anti-clockwise, we reach Quadrant II, the top-left part. In this quadrant, the x-coordinate is negative, but the y-coordinate remains positive. We move left, then up.

Quadrant III: Bottom-Left Section

  • Both x and y coordinates are negative.
  • Example: (-6, -1)
  • Moves left from origin, then down.

🎙️ Quadrant III is the bottom-left section. Here, both the x-coordinate and the y-coordinate are negative. So, we move left from the origin and then move downwards.

Quadrant IV: Bottom-Right Section

🎙️ Finally, Quadrant IV is the bottom-right section. In this part, the x-coordinate is positive, but the y-coordinate is negative. We move right from the origin and then downwards.

Plotting Points: How to Find an Address

  • Start at the Origin (0,0).
  • Move horizontally (left/right) according to x.
  • Then move vertically (up/down) according to y.

🎙️ Let's learn how to plot a point. It's like following directions. Always start at the origin, then follow the x-direction, and finally the y-direction to mark your spot.

Worked Example: Plotting (2,3)

  • Start at (0,0).
  • Move 2 units right (positive x).
  • Move 3 units up (positive y).

🎙️ To plot (2,3), we start at the origin. Since x is 2 (positive), we move 2 steps to the right. Then, since y is 3 (positive), we move 3 steps upwards. That's our point!

Points on the X-axis: Always (x, 0)

  • Any point on the X-axis has its y-coordinate as 0.
  • Example: (5,0), (-3,0).
  • It does not move up or down from the axis.

🎙️ What about points that lie directly on the axes? If a point is on the X-axis, it means it hasn't moved up or down, so its y-coordinate is always zero, like (5,0).

↔️

Points on the Y-axis: Always (0, y)

  • Any point on the Y-axis has its x-coordinate as 0.
  • Example: (0,4), (0,-2).
  • It does not move left or right from the axis.

🎙️ Similarly, if a point is on the Y-axis, it means it hasn't moved left or right from the origin along the X-axis. So, its x-coordinate is always zero, like (0,4).
