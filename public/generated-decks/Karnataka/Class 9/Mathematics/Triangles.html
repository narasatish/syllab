What is a Triangle?

  • A closed figure made of three line segments.
  • It has three vertices, three sides, and three angles.
  • The word 'tri' means three, referring to its three angles.

🎙️ Simply put, a triangle is a polygon with three sides. Imagine connecting three points that are not on the same straight line; you'll always form a triangle. It's the simplest polygon!

Key Elements of a Triangle

  • Vertices: The three corner points.
  • Sides: The three line segments forming the triangle.
  • Angles: The three angles formed inside at each vertex.

🎙️ Every triangle has these three main components. For example, in triangle ABC, A, B, and C are the vertices. AB, BC, and CA are the sides, and the angles are formed at A, B, and C.

1Vertices (A, B, C)
2Sides (AB, BC, CA)
3Angles (∠A, ∠B, ∠C)

Equilateral Triangle: All Sides Equal

  • All three sides are of equal length.
  • All three interior angles are equal (and always 60°).
  • It's a perfectly balanced and symmetrical triangle.

🎙️ An equilateral triangle is very special. Because all its sides are equal, all its angles are also equal. Since the sum of angles in a triangle is 180°, each angle must be 60 degrees.

📏

Isosceles Triangle: Two Sides Equal

  • Exactly two sides are of equal length.
  • The angles opposite the equal sides are also equal.
  • Often seen in roof designs or certain types of flags.

🎙️ In an isosceles triangle, if you have two sides that are the same length, then the angles facing those sides will also be equal. This property is very useful for solving problems.

⚖️
Two equal sides

Scalene Triangle: No Sides Equal

  • All three sides have different lengths.
  • All three interior angles are also different.
  • It's the most general type of triangle.

🎙️ A scalene triangle has no special symmetry regarding its sides or angles. If you measure all three sides, you'll find they are all distinct. Consequently, all its angles will also be different.

Acute-angled Triangle: All Angles < 90°

  • All three interior angles are acute (less than 90 degrees).
  • An equilateral triangle is always an acute-angled triangle.
  • It looks 'sharp' or 'pointy' at all its corners.

🎙️ In an acute-angled triangle, every single angle inside the triangle is smaller than a right angle. This means it doesn't have any 'blunt' or 'square' corners. An equilateral triangle is a perfect example.

✨
All angles sharp

Right-angled Triangle: One Angle = 90°

  • Exactly one interior angle is a right angle (90 degrees).
  • The side opposite the 90° angle is called the hypotenuse.
  • This type is crucial for trigonometry and Pythagoras theorem.

🎙️ A right-angled triangle is very important in mathematics. The side opposite the 90-degree angle is always the longest side, and it has a special name: the hypotenuse. We will study the Pythagoras theorem for these triangles later.

📐

Obtuse-angled Triangle: One Angle > 90°

  • Exactly one interior angle is obtuse (greater than 90 degrees).
  • The other two angles must be acute.
  • It looks 'wide' or 'open' at one of its corners.

🎙️ In an obtuse-angled triangle, one of the angles is wider than a right angle. Because the sum of all angles must be 180 degrees, the other two angles have to be acute. It cannot have more than one obtuse angle.

The Angle Sum Property

  • The sum of all three interior angles of any triangle is always 180°.
  • This is a fundamental property of all triangles.
  • Helps find missing angles when others are known.

🎙️ This property is incredibly powerful and applies to every single triangle, no matter its shape or size. If you tear off the corners of any paper triangle and place them together, they will always form a straight line, which is 180 degrees.

➕➕➕

Example: Finding a Missing Angle

  • Given two angles: ∠A = 70°, ∠B = 50°.
  • Let the third angle be ∠C.
  • Using Angle Sum Property: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°.

🎙️ Let's apply the Angle Sum Property. We have 70° + 50° + ∠C = 180°. So, 120° + ∠C = 180°. Subtracting 120° from both sides gives ∠C = 60°. Simple, isn't it?

The Exterior Angle Property

  • An exterior angle of a triangle is formed by extending one of its sides.
  • It is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • This property is very useful in geometry problems.

🎙️ Imagine extending one side of a triangle. The angle formed outside the triangle, adjacent to an interior angle, is called an exterior angle. This exterior angle has a special relationship with the two interior angles that are not its neighbours.

Example: Using Exterior Angle Property

  • Interior angles: ∠A = 60°, ∠B = 70°.
  • Find the exterior angle at vertex C.
  • Exterior angle at C = ∠A + ∠B.

🎙️ If ∠A is 60° and ∠B is 70°, the exterior angle at C will be 60° + 70°, which is 130°. This property saves us from first finding the interior angle at C and then subtracting it from 180°.

What is Congruence?

  • Two figures are congruent if they have exactly the same shape and size.
  • They are perfect copies of each other.
  • If you superimpose one on the other, they will perfectly overlap.

🎙️ Congruence is a very important concept in geometry. Think of two identical coins or two identical stamps. They are congruent because they are exact replicas of each other, having the same dimensions and form.

Criteria for Congruence

  • We don't need to check all sides and angles for congruence.
  • Specific combinations of equal parts prove congruence.
  • These are the SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and RHS rules.

🎙️ To prove that two triangles are congruent, we don't need to show that all six corresponding parts (three sides and three angles) are equal. We have specific shortcuts, or criteria, that make this task easier and quicker.

1SSS (Side-Side-Side)
2SAS (Side-Angle-Side)
3ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)
4AAS (Angle-Angle-Side)
5RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side)

SSS Congruence Rule

  • If three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • This rule is simple: matching sides guarantee identical triangles.

🎙️ The SSS rule is quite intuitive. If you have two triangles and you can confirm that all three sides of the first triangle are exactly the same length as the corresponding three sides of the second triangle, then the triangles must be congruent.

📏📏📏
All three sides match

SAS Congruence Rule

  • If two sides and the included angle of one triangle are equal to the corresponding two sides and the included angle of another triangle.
  • Then the two triangles are congruent.
  • The 'included angle' is the angle *between* the two sides.

🎙️ For the SAS rule, it's crucial that the angle is *between* the two sides that you are comparing. If the angle is not included between the sides, then this rule cannot be applied. Remember: Side-Angle-Side in order.
