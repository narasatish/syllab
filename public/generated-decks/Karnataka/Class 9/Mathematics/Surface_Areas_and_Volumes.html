Surface Area - The Outer Skin

  • Total area of all faces
  • How much 'skin' a shape has
  • Measured in square units (cm², m²)

🎙️ Imagine you want to paint a gift box. The amount of paint you'd need to cover its entire outer surface is its surface area. It's the sum of the areas of all its individual flat or curved surfaces.

Volume - The Space Inside

  • Amount of space a shape occupies
  • How much 'stuff' fits inside
  • Measured in cubic units (cm³, m³)

🎙️ Now, imagine filling that same gift box with small candies. The total amount of candies it can hold represents its volume. Volume tells us how much capacity a 3D object has.

The Cuboid - A Familiar Box

  • Six rectangular faces
  • Opposite faces are identical
  • Examples: brick, matchbox

🎙️ A cuboid is a very common 3D shape, like a brick or a shoebox. It has three distinct dimensions: length, breadth, and height.

Cuboid - Lateral Surface Area (LSA)

  • Area of the four side walls
  • Excludes top and bottom faces
  • Formula: 2h(l + b)

🎙️ LSA is like painting only the walls of a room, not the ceiling or the floor. It's the sum of the areas of the four vertical faces.

Cuboid - Total Surface Area (TSA)

  • Area of all six faces
  • Includes top and bottom
  • Formula: 2(lb + bh + hl)

🎙️ TSA is what you'd calculate if you wanted to completely wrap a gift box. It covers every single surface of the cuboid.

🎁

Cuboid - Volume

  • Space occupied by the cuboid
  • Formula: l × b × h
  • Length × Breadth × Height

🎙️ To find out how much water a rectangular tank can hold, we use its volume. It's simply the product of its three dimensions.

Worked Example - Cuboid

  • Find TSA of a box
  • Dimensions: l=10cm, b=5cm, h=2cm
  • TSA = 2(10*5 + 5*2 + 2*10) = 2(50+10+20) = 2(80) = 160 cm²

The Cube - A Special Box

  • A cuboid with all sides equal
  • All six faces are squares
  • Example: dice, Rubik's cube

🎙️ A cube is a very special type of cuboid where all its edges are of equal length. This makes its calculations even simpler!

Cube - Lateral Surface Area (LSA)

  • Area of four side faces
  • Each face is a square (side × side)
  • Formula: 4a² (where 'a' is side length)

🎙️ For a cube, the LSA is the area of its four square walls. If each side is 'a', then the area of one face is a², so four faces would be 4a².

🧱
Four Walls

Cube - Total Surface Area (TSA)

  • Area of all six square faces
  • Formula: 6a² (where 'a' is side length)

🎙️ The TSA of a cube includes all six of its identical square faces. So, it's simply six times the area of one face.

🎁
Completely Wrapped

Cube - Volume

  • Space occupied by the cube
  • Formula: a³ (side × side × side)
  • Also written as 'a cubed'

🎙️ The volume of a cube is found by multiplying its side length by itself three times. This is why we often say 'cubed' when talking about powers of three.

The Cylinder - A Round Pipe

  • Two circular bases
  • A curved lateral surface
  • Examples: can, pipe, drum

🎙️ A cylinder is a familiar shape, like a soda can or a pipe. It has a circular top and bottom, connected by a smooth, curved side.

Cylinder - Curved Surface Area (CSA)

  • Area of the rounded side
  • Excludes top and bottom circles
  • Formula: 2πrh

🎙️ Imagine peeling the label off a can. The area of that label is the cylinder's curved surface area. It's the area of the curved part only.

📜
Unrolled Label
19 / 25

Cylinder - Total Surface Area (TSA)

  • Area of curved surface + two circular bases
  • Formula: 2πr(h + r)

🎙️ The TSA of a cylinder includes the curved side and both the top and bottom circular faces. It's the total area you'd need to paint the entire drum.

🛢️
Oil Drum

Cylinder - Volume

  • Space occupied by the cylinder
  • Formula: πr²h
  • Area of base × height

🎙️ To find out how much liquid a cylindrical container can hold, we calculate its volume. It's the area of its circular base multiplied by its height.

Worked Example - Cylinder

  • r=7cm, h=10cm
  • CSA = 2 * (22/7) * 7 * 10 = 440 cm²
