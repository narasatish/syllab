Introducing Heron's Formula!

  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Where 'a', 'b', 'c' are side lengths
  • 's' is the semi-perimeter

🎙️ This is Heron's Formula! It allows us to calculate the area of any triangle if we know the lengths of all three sides. The formula looks a bit complex at first, but we'll break it down step-by-step. Notice the 's' and 'a, b, c' terms.

Understanding the Semi-perimeter (s)

  • Half of the perimeter of the triangle
  • s = (a + b + c) / 2
  • First step in using Heron's Formula

🎙️ The term 's' in Heron's Formula stands for the semi-perimeter. 'Semi' means half, so the semi-perimeter is simply half of the total perimeter of the triangle. You calculate it by adding the lengths of all three sides and then dividing by two. This is always your first step!

Worked Example 1: Basic Triangle

  • Sides: a=3cm, b=4cm, c=5cm
  • Step 1: s = (3+4+5)/2 = 6cm
  • Step 2: Area = √[6(6-3)(6-4)(6-5)]
  • Area = √[6 × 3 × 2 × 1] = √36 = 6 cm²

🎙️ Let's work through a simple example. We have a triangle with sides 3cm, 4cm, and 5cm. First, calculate 's' which is 6cm. Then, substitute these values into Heron's formula. We get the square root of 36, which means the area is 6 square centimeters.

Key Takeaways from Heron's Formula

  • Area calculation using only side lengths
  • s = (a+b+c)/2 (semi-perimeter)
  • Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]
  • Applicable for triangles and quadrilaterals

🎙️ To summarize, Heron's Formula is a powerful method to find the area of any triangle when only the lengths of its three sides are known. Remember the semi-perimeter 's' and the main formula. It's a versatile tool for various geometric problems.
