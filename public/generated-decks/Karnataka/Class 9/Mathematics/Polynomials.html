What is a Polynomial? The Special Club!

  • Not all algebraic expressions are polynomials.
  • Polynomials are expressions where variables have ONLY non-negative integer powers.
  • Think of it as a 'special club' with strict entry rules!

🎙️ Imagine a very exclusive club. Only certain algebraic expressions are allowed in, and they are called 'polynomials'. The main rule for entry is that the powers of the variables must always be whole numbers – no fractions, no negatives!

🚪
Entry Rules

Polynomials: Formal Definition

  • An algebraic expression p(x) = a₀ + a₁x + a₂x² + ... + aₙxⁿ.
  • Here, a₀, a₁, ..., aₙ are real numbers (coefficients).
  • The powers of the variable 'x' (0, 1, 2, ..., n) must be non-negative integers.

🎙️ More formally, a polynomial in one variable 'x' looks like this general form. The key thing to remember is that the exponents on the variable 'x' must always be 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on – never negative, never fractions, and never decimals.

✅
Correct Form
12 / 31

Is it a Polynomial or Not?

  • Polynomial: 3x² + 5x - 2 (powers are 2, 1, 0)
  • Not a Polynomial: 4x⁻¹ + 7 (power is -1)
  • Not a Polynomial: √x + 3 (power is 1/2)
  • Not a Polynomial: 5/x + 1 (variable in denominator)

🎙️ Let's look at some examples to clarify. '3x² + 5x - 2' is a polynomial because all powers of 'x' are non-negative integers. However, expressions with negative powers, fractional powers, or variables in the denominator are NOT polynomials. This is a common point of confusion.

Degree of a Polynomial: The Highest Rank!

  • Imagine a hierarchy, like in a kingdom.
  • The 'degree' is the highest power of the variable in the polynomial.
  • It tells us the 'rank' or 'importance' of the polynomial.

🎙️ Every polynomial has a 'degree', which is like its rank. It's simply the highest exponent of the variable present in the entire polynomial. For example, if a polynomial has terms with x², x³, and x, the highest power is 3, so its degree is 3.

👑
Highest Power
14 / 31

Finding the Degree

  • In a polynomial with one variable, it's the highest exponent of that variable.
  • Example: For 5x⁴ - 2x² + 7, the degree is 4.
  • For a constant polynomial (e.g., 5), the degree is 0.
  • The degree of the zero polynomial (0) is undefined.

🎙️ To find the degree, simply scan all the terms and pick out the largest exponent on the variable. Remember, a constant like '7' can be thought of as '7x⁰', so its degree is 0. The zero polynomial, just '0', is a special case and its degree is not defined.

⬆️
Maximum Exponent

Worked Example: What's the Degree?

  • 1. Identify all terms.
  • 2. Find the power of the variable in each term.
  • 3. The highest power is the degree.

🎙️ Let's work through an example. For the polynomial '6y⁵ - 4y² + 1', first we look at the exponents on 'y'. They are 5, 2, and 0 (for the constant term). The largest among these is 5, so the degree of this polynomial is 5.

Monomial, Binomial, Trinomial

  • Monomial: One term (e.g., 5x, -7y², 10).
  • Binomial: Two terms (e.g., 2x + 3, y² - 4).
  • Trinomial: Three terms (e.g., x² + 2x - 1, a³ - 5a + 6).

🎙️ Monomials are the simplest, having just one term. Binomials have two terms joined by a plus or minus sign. Trinomials, as the name suggests, have three terms. Any polynomial with more than three terms is generally just called a 'polynomial'.

Linear, Quadratic, Cubic Polynomials

  • Linear Polynomial: Degree 1 (e.g., 2x + 5, y - 7).
  • Quadratic Polynomial: Degree 2 (e.g., x² + 3x - 1, 4y²).
  • Cubic Polynomial: Degree 3 (e.g., 3x³ - 2x² + x - 8).

🎙️ A polynomial of degree one is called linear. If the highest power is two, it's a quadratic polynomial. And if the highest power is three, it's a cubic polynomial. These names are very important and you'll use them frequently in higher classes.

Value of a Polynomial: The Input-Output Machine!

  • Think of a polynomial as a machine.
  • You 'input' a number for the variable.
  • The machine 'outputs' a new number, the value of the polynomial.

🎙️ A polynomial can be thought of as a function or a machine. When you feed a specific number into it (by substituting it for the variable), it processes that number according to its operations and gives you a single output number. That output is the 'value' of the polynomial at that specific input.

Finding the Value of p(x)

  • To find the value of a polynomial p(x) at x = a, substitute 'a' for 'x'.
  • This is denoted as p(a).
  • Example: If p(x) = x² + 2x, find p(2).

🎙️ The process is straightforward: wherever you see the variable 'x' in the polynomial, replace it with the given numerical value. Then, perform all the arithmetic operations to get your final answer. This is a fundamental skill in algebra.

➡️
Substitute and Solve

Worked Example: Evaluate p(x)

  • 1. Write down the polynomial.
  • 2. Substitute the given value for 'x'.
  • 3. Perform calculations carefully.

🎙️ Let's find the value of p(x) = 3x² - 5x + 7 when x = 1. We replace every 'x' with '1'. So, it becomes 3 times 1 squared, minus 5 times 1, plus 7. Calculating this step-by-step, we get 3 - 5 + 7, which simplifies to 5. So, p(1) = 5.

1Given p(x) = 3x² - 5x + 7
2Find p(1)
3p(1) = 3(1)² - 5(1) + 7
4p(1) = 3 - 5 + 7 = 5

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing 'x⁻¹' or '√x' as valid polynomial terms.
  • Incorrectly identifying the highest degree in a multi-variable polynomial.
  • Errors in calculation when substituting values (especially with negatives).

🎙️ Students often make mistakes like thinking an expression with a variable in the denominator or under a square root is a polynomial. Also, be careful when finding the degree, especially if there are multiple variables, and always double-check your arithmetic when substituting values.
