🎙️ Before we dive into new concepts, let's quickly remember what area means. Area is simply the measure of the space occupied by a flat shape. For example, the area of your notebook cover is the space it takes up on your desk.

Units of Area

  • Area is always measured in square units.
  • Common units include square centimetres (cm²), square metres (m²).
  • For larger areas, we use square kilometres (km²) or hectares.

🎙️ It's crucial to remember that area is always expressed in square units. If the sides are in centimetres, the area is in square centimetres. If they are in metres, the area is in square metres. Always include the correct unit!

Figures on the Same Base

  • Two figures are on the same base if they share a common side.
  • This common side must be the 'base' for both figures.
  • The vertices not on the base must be on opposite sides of the base line.

🎙️ Imagine two houses built right next to each other, sharing a common wall. In geometry, when two shapes share an entire side, we say they are on the same base. This shared side acts as the foundation for both figures.

Figures Between the Same Parallels

  • Two figures are between the same parallels if their bases lie on one parallel line.
  • And the vertices opposite to the bases lie on another parallel line.
  • The distance between these parallel lines is the height of the figures.

🎙️ Now, let's think about parallel railway tracks. If two shapes are 'sandwiched' between these two tracks, meaning their bases are on one track and their top points are on the other, they are said to be between the same parallels. The distance between the tracks is key here.

Theorem: Equal Areas for Parallelograms

  • Parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area.
  • This is a fundamental theorem in geometry.
  • It means their 'size' is the same, even if their shapes look slightly different.

🎙️ This theorem is incredibly powerful! It tells us that if parallelograms satisfy the conditions of being on the same base and between the same parallels, their areas will always be identical. It's like having two different shaped containers that hold the exact same amount of water because they have the same base and height.

Proof Idea: Shifting a Triangle

  • Imagine parallelogram ABCD and ABEF.
  • Triangle ADE can be 'shifted' to become triangle BCF.
  • This shows that the area of ABEF is equal to the area of ABCD.

🎙️ The proof for this theorem involves a clever trick! You can visualize taking a small triangular piece from one end of a parallelogram and moving it to the other end to transform it into the second parallelogram, without changing the total area. This demonstrates why their areas must be equal.

  • Find its area.

🎙️ Let's put our knowledge to practice. The area of any parallelogram is simply its base multiplied by its perpendicular height. Remember, the height is the perpendicular distance between the base and the opposite side.

The Parallelogram Area Formula

  • Area of Parallelogram = Base × Height
  • Base (b) is any side of the parallelogram.
  • Height (h) is the perpendicular distance from the opposite side to the base.

🎙️ This is the core formula you need to remember for parallelograms. Always make sure you're using the perpendicular height corresponding to the chosen base. The height is NOT the slanted side length!

▱
Area = b × h

Worked Example: Finding Height

  • A parallelogram has an area of 60 m².
  • Its base is 12 m.
  • What is its height?

🎙️ We can also use the area formula to find a missing dimension. If you know the area and the base, you can easily calculate the height by rearranging the formula. Always pay attention to the units!

1Formula:
2Area = base × height
3Substitute values:
460 m² = 12 m × height
5Solve for height:
6height = 60 m² / 12 m = 5 m

Theorem: Equal Areas for Triangles

  • Triangles on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area.
  • This theorem is very similar to the one for parallelograms.
  • It highlights the importance of base and perpendicular height in determining area.

🎙️ Another powerful theorem! If triangles meet these conditions – same base and between the same parallels – their areas will be equal. This is because, effectively, they share the same base and the same perpendicular height, which are the only factors determining their area.

Proof Idea: Completing a Parallelogram

  • Any triangle can be seen as half of a parallelogram.
  • If two triangles are on the same base and between the same parallels,
  • They are each half of parallelograms that are equal in area, hence they themselves are equal.

🎙️ The proof for this theorem often involves constructing a parallelogram from each triangle. Since each triangle is half of a parallelogram, and the two parallelograms would be equal in area (by our previous theorem), it logically follows that the two triangles must also be equal in area.

The Triangle Area Formula

  • Area of Triangle = ½ × Base × Height
  • Base (b) is any side of the triangle.
  • Height (h) is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the base.

🎙️ This is the fundamental formula for the area of a triangle. Notice the 'half' factor – it's because a triangle can always be thought of as half of a parallelogram with the same base and height.

△
Area = ½ b × h

Parallelogram & Triangle: A Special Link

  • If a triangle and a parallelogram are on the same base and between the same parallels,
  • Then the area of the triangle is half the area of the parallelogram.
  • Area(Triangle) = ½ × Area(Parallelogram)

🎙️ This is a very important relationship! It directly connects the areas of parallelograms and triangles under the specific conditions of sharing a base and being between the same parallels. It's a powerful shortcut for solving many problems.

Median of a Triangle and Area

  • A median of a triangle connects a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
  • A median divides the triangle into two triangles of equal areas.
  • This property holds true for any triangle.

🎙️ Remember the farmer's dilemma? A median is a simple line segment with a powerful property! It doesn't just divide the opposite side into two equal parts; it also divides the *entire area* of the triangle into two perfectly equal halves. This is very useful!

Worked Example: Median Property

  • In triangle ABC, AD is a median.
  • If the area of triangle ABC is 80 cm².
  • What is the area of triangle ABD?

🎙️ This example shows how straightforward it is to use the median property. If you know the total area of a triangle, finding the area of the two smaller triangles formed by a median is as simple as dividing by two. This is very helpful in complex problems.

1Apply Median Property:
2Area(ABD) = ½ × Area(ABC)
3Substitute:
4Area(ABD) = ½ × 80 cm²
5Calculate:
6Area(ABD) = 40 cm²

Common Mistakes to Avoid!

  • Confusing base with height (height must be perpendicular).
  • Forgetting the '½' in the triangle area formula.
  • Not using square units for area.
  • Assuming figures are between the same parallels without confirmation.

🎙️ Even the best students can make small errors. Pay close attention to these common pitfalls. Always double-check if the height is perpendicular and if you've included the 'half' for triangles. And never assume conditions are met; always verify them from the problem statement!
