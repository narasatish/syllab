Revisiting Area of a Rectangle

  • Area is the amount of space a 2D shape covers.
  • Formula: Length × Breadth (or Width).
  • Units are always squared (e.g., cm², m²).

🎙️ Before we dive into new shapes, let's quickly recall what we already know. Remember, the area of a rectangle is found by multiplying its length by its breadth. This gives us the total surface enclosed by the shape.

Area of a Square

  • A square is a special type of rectangle with all sides equal.
  • Formula: Side × Side (or Side²).
  • Simple and fundamental shape.

🎙️ Since a square has all its sides equal, its length and breadth are the same. So, the area of a square is simply the side length multiplied by itself. Very straightforward, isn't it?

Basic Area of a Triangle

  • Formula: ½ × Base × Height.
  • Height must be the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base.
  • Essential for many geometric calculations.

🎙️ And finally, the triangle! We know that the area of a triangle is half the product of its base and its corresponding height. Always remember that the height must be perpendicular to the base you choose.

Unveiling the Area of a Parallelogram

  • We can transform a parallelogram into a rectangle.
  • Cut a triangular part and move it to the other side.
  • Formula: Base × Height.

🎙️ The clever trick to finding a parallelogram's area is to imagine cutting off a right-angled triangle from one end and attaching it to the other. Voila! You get a rectangle! This shows us the area is simply base times height, just like a rectangle.

Theorem 1: Equal Areas!

  • Parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area.
  • This is a fundamental theorem in geometry.
  • The 'slant' does not affect the area if base and height are constant.

🎙️ This is a powerful result! It means that even if the parallelograms look different because they are 'slanted' differently, as long as they share the same base and are between the same parallel lines (meaning they have the same height), their areas will be identical.

Theorem 2: Equal Triangle Areas!

🎙️ Yes, you guessed it! Just like parallelograms, if two triangles share the same base and are situated between the same parallel lines, their areas will also be equal. This is because they will have the same base and the same perpendicular height.

Parallelogram and Triangle Connection

  • If a triangle and a parallelogram share the same base.
  • And are between the same parallel lines.
  • The area of the triangle is half the area of the parallelogram.

🎙️ Here's an even more interesting connection! If you have a parallelogram and a triangle, and they both stand on the same base and are between the same parallel lines, the area of that triangle will always be exactly half the area of the parallelogram. This is a very useful relationship!

Worked Example 2: Triangles/Parallelograms Relation

  • Given: Parallelogram ABCD has Area = 60 cm².
  • Triangle EFG shares the same base and is between the same parallels.
  • Area of Triangle EFG = ½ × Area of Parallelogram = 30 cm².

🎙️ Let's apply this relationship. If a parallelogram has an area of 60 square centimeters, and a triangle is constructed on the same base and between the same parallel lines, then the area of that triangle would simply be half of 60, which is 30 square centimeters. Simple, right?

Median's Magical Property

  • A median connects a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
  • It divides the triangle into two triangles of equal area.
  • A powerful property for area division.

🎙️ Now, let's look at another special property within triangles. A median is a line segment drawn from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. This median has a 'magical' effect: it perfectly divides the original triangle into two smaller triangles that have exactly the same area!

Worked Example 3: Median Area Calculation

  • Given: Triangle PQR has an area of 70 cm².
  • PS is a median to side QR.
  • Area of ΔPQS = Area of ΔPRS = ½ × 70 cm² = 35 cm².

🎙️ Let's see this in action. If a triangle PQR has a total area of 70 square centimeters, and PS is a median, then the two triangles formed, PQS and PRS, will each have an area of half of 70, which is 35 square centimeters. This property is very useful for dividing areas.

Key Formulas at a Glance
