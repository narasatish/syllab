Construction 1: Perpendicular Bisector

  • Divides a line segment into two equal parts.
  • Forms a 90-degree angle with the segment.
  • Every point on the bisector is equidistant from the segment's endpoints.

🎙️ Our first construction is the perpendicular bisector. Imagine you have a line segment, and you want to cut it exactly in half, forming a perfect right angle. That's what a perpendicular bisector does. It's a fundamental construction in geometry.

✂️
Dividing a Line

Steps: Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

  • Draw line segment AB.
  • With A as center, draw arcs above and below AB (radius > half AB).
  • With B as center, draw arcs intersecting the previous ones.
  • Join the intersection points to get the bisector.

🎙️ Let's walk through the steps. First, draw your line segment. Then, open your compass more than half the length of the segment and draw arcs from each endpoint. Where these arcs cross, you'll find two points. Connecting them gives you the perpendicular bisector.

2Arcs from Endpoints
3Intersect Arcs
4Join Points

Construction 2: Bisecting an Angle

  • Divides an angle into two equal angles.
  • The bisector passes through the vertex.
  • Every point on the bisector is equidistant from the angle's arms.

🎙️ Next, we'll learn to bisect an angle. This means drawing a ray that splits any given angle into two perfectly equal smaller angles. This is incredibly useful when you need precise angles for various designs or structures.

📐
Splitting an Angle
8 / 25

Steps: Bisecting an Angle

  • Draw an angle with vertex O.
  • With O as center, draw an arc intersecting both arms at A and B.
  • With A as center, draw an arc inside the angle.
  • With B as center, draw an arc intersecting the previous one at C.
  • Join O to C to get the angle bisector.

🎙️ To bisect an angle, first draw an arc from the vertex that crosses both arms of the angle. Then, from where this arc meets each arm, draw two more arcs inside the angle. The point where these two new arcs intersect, when connected to the vertex, forms your angle bisector.

1Draw Angle
2Arc from Vertex
3Arcs from Intersections
4Join Vertex to Arc

Constructing Specific Angles: 60°

  • Based on the properties of an equilateral triangle.
  • A fundamental angle for many other constructions.
  • Requires only a compass and straightedge.

🎙️ Now let's construct specific angles. The 60-degree angle is one of the most basic and important. It's derived from the fact that all angles in an equilateral triangle are 60 degrees. Mastering this will open doors to constructing many other angles.

⬆️
Base Angle
11 / 25

Steps: Constructing a 60° Angle

  • Draw a ray OA.
  • With O as center, draw an arc intersecting OA at P.
  • With P as center and same radius, draw an arc intersecting the first arc at Q.
  • Join O to Q. Angle QOA is 60°.

🎙️ Start with a ray. Draw an arc from the starting point of the ray. Then, keeping the compass open to the same radius, place the compass point where the arc crosses the ray and draw another arc that intersects the first one. Connect this intersection to the ray's origin, and you have a 60-degree angle.

1Draw Ray
2Arc from Origin
3Arc from Intersection
4Join Origin to Arc

Constructing Specific Angles: 90°

  • Also known as a right angle.
  • Can be constructed by bisecting a straight angle (180°).
  • Essential for perpendicular lines and right triangles.

🎙️ The 90-degree angle, or right angle, is crucial in many areas, from building construction to designing furniture. There are a few ways to construct it, but a common method involves creating a perpendicular to a line at a point on it, which is essentially bisecting a straight line's angle.

⏹️
Right Angle
14 / 25

Steps: Constructing a 90° Angle

  • Draw a ray OA.
  • With O as center, draw an arc intersecting OA at P.
  • With P as center, draw an arc cutting the first arc at Q (60°).
  • With Q as center, draw an arc cutting the first arc at R (120°).
  • With Q and R as centers, draw two arcs intersecting at S.
  • Join O to S. Angle SOA is 90°.

🎙️ To construct a 90-degree angle, start with a ray. Draw a large arc from the origin. Then, from where the arc crosses the ray, mark off 60 degrees and then 120 degrees on the arc. Finally, bisect the angle between the 60 and 120-degree marks to get your perfect 90-degree angle.

1Draw Ray & Arc
2Mark 60° & 120°
3Intersect Arcs from 60/120
4Join Vertex

Constructing Triangles: SSS Criterion

  • SSS stands for Side-Side-Side.
  • All three side lengths are given.
  • Unique triangle can be constructed.

🎙️ Now we move to constructing entire triangles! The SSS criterion means we are given the lengths of all three sides. With these three measurements, we can construct one unique triangle. This is a very common type of triangle construction.

△
Triangle from Sides
18 / 25

Steps: Constructing SSS Triangle

  • Draw base BC of given length.
  • With B as center, draw an arc with radius equal to side AB.
  • With C as center, draw an arc with radius equal to side AC.
  • The arcs intersect at A. Join A to B and A to C.

🎙️ To construct an SSS triangle, first draw the longest side as your base. Then, using your compass, draw an arc from one endpoint of the base with the length of the second side. Repeat from the other endpoint of the base with the length of the third side. Where these arcs intersect is your third vertex.

1Draw Base
2Arc from 1st Vertex
3Arc from 2nd Vertex
4Join Vertices

Constructing Triangles: SAS Criterion

  • SAS stands for Side-Angle-Side.
  • Two side lengths and the included angle are given.
  • The angle must be between the two given sides.

🎙️ The SAS criterion is when we know two sides and the angle *between* them. This is crucial: the angle must be the 'included' angle. Knowing this combination also allows us to construct a unique triangle. It's often used in design where angles and lengths are critical.

📐

Constructing Triangles: ASA Criterion

  • ASA stands for Angle-Side-Angle.
  • Two angles and the included side are given.
  • The side must be between the two given angles.

🎙️ With ASA, we are given two angles and the side that connects their vertices. Again, the 'included' side is key. This criterion is particularly useful in surveying and mapping, where angles can be measured easily from specific points.

📏
Side Between Angles

Constructing Triangles: RHS Criterion

  • RHS stands for Right-angle-Hypotenuse-Side.
  • Specific to right-angled triangles.
  • Given the right angle, the hypotenuse, and one other side.

🎙️ Finally, for right-angled triangles, we have the RHS criterion. This means if you know the right angle, the length of the hypotenuse, and the length of one of the other two sides, you can uniquely construct that right-angled triangle. This is fundamental in construction and engineering.
