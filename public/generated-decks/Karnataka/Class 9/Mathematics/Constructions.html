🎙️ Our first construction is the perpendicular bisector. Think of it like this: if you have a piece of string and you want to cut it into two pieces of exactly equal length, and you want that cut to be perfectly straight at a right angle to the string, you'd be creating a perpendicular bisector!

Steps: Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

  • 1. Draw line segment AB.
  • 2. With A as center, radius > half AB, draw arcs above & below AB.
  • 3. With B as center, same radius, draw arcs intersecting previous ones at P & Q.
  • 4. Join P and Q. PQ is the perpendicular bisector of AB.

🎙️ Let's walk through the steps. First, draw your line segment. Then, using your compass, draw arcs from both ends of the segment, ensuring the radius is more than half the segment's length. These arcs will intersect at two points. Connecting these two points gives you the perpendicular bisector.

Steps: Constructing an Angle Bisector

  • 1. Draw angle ABC.
  • 2. With B as center, draw an arc intersecting BA & BC at E & F.
  • 3. With E as center, draw an arc inside the angle.
  • 4. With F as center, same radius, draw an arc intersecting the previous one at D.
  • 5. Join B and D. BD is the angle bisector of ABC.

🎙️ Here are the steps for bisecting an angle. Start by drawing any angle. Then, from the vertex, draw an arc that cuts both arms of the angle. From these intersection points, draw two more arcs inside the angle. The line connecting the vertex to where these arcs meet is your angle bisector.

Steps: Constructing a 60° Angle

  • 1. Draw a ray AB.
  • 2. With A as center, draw an arc intersecting AB at C.
  • 3. With C as center, same radius, draw an arc intersecting the previous arc at D.
  • 4. Join A and D. Angle DAB is 60°.

🎙️ To construct a 60-degree angle: start with a ray. From its starting point, draw an arc. Then, from where that arc intersects the ray, draw another arc with the *same radius* that cuts the first arc. Connect the starting point of the ray to this intersection, and you've got your 60-degree angle!

Steps: Constructing a 90° Angle

  • 1. Draw a ray AB.
  • 2. With A as center, draw a semicircle intersecting AB at C and D.
  • 3. With C and D as centers, radius > half CD, draw arcs intersecting at E.
  • 4. Join A and E. Angle EAB is 90°.

🎙️ To construct a 90-degree angle: start with a ray. From its starting point, draw a semicircle. This semicircle will cut the ray at one point. From that point, and the other end of the semicircle, draw two arcs that intersect. Connecting the ray's endpoint to this intersection creates the 90-degree angle.

Steps: Constructing a 45° Angle

  • 1. Construct a 90° angle (e.g., angle PQR).
  • 2. With Q as center, draw an arc intersecting QP & QR at S & T.
  • 3. With S as center, draw an arc inside angle PQR.
  • 4. With T as center, same radius, draw an arc intersecting the previous one at U.
  • 5. Join Q and U. Angle RQU is 45°.

🎙️ First, you need to construct your 90-degree angle. Once that's done, treat it as any other angle you want to bisect. Use the angle bisector steps: draw an arc from the vertex, then from the points where it cuts the arms, draw two more intersecting arcs. Connect the vertex to this intersection, and you have 45 degrees.

Steps: Triangle XYZ (Base YZ, Angle Y, XY+XZ)

  • 1. Draw base YZ.
  • 2. At Y, construct angle MYZ equal to the given angle Y.
  • 3. On ray YM, cut off YP = XY+XZ.
  • 4. Join P and Z.
  • 5. Construct perpendicular bisector of PZ, intersecting YP at X.
  • 6. Join X and Z. Triangle XYZ is the required triangle.

🎙️ This construction is a bit longer, so follow carefully. First, draw the base and construct the given base angle. Then, on the ray of the angle, measure out the *sum* of the other two sides. Connect this point to the other end of the base. The key is then to draw the perpendicular bisector of this new line segment. Where it intersects the longer ray, that's your third vertex!

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Incorrect compass opening (e.g., radius less than half for bisector).
  • Not keeping compass radius fixed when needed.
  • Using ruler markings for measurements in constructions.
  • Not drawing faint construction lines clearly.
  • Inaccurate intersections of arcs.

🎙️ Even experienced students make mistakes! The most common ones include not opening the compass wide enough for a perpendicular bisector, or changing the compass radius when it should be fixed. Always remember not to use the ruler for measuring, only for drawing straight lines. Make sure your construction lines are light but visible, and arc intersections are precise.
