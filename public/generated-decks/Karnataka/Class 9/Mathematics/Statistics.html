What is Statistics?

  • The study of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data.
  • Helps us make sense of large amounts of information.
  • Used in many fields: sports, weather, business.

🎙️ Statistics is like being a detective for numbers. We gather facts, put them in order, look for patterns, and then understand what they mean. It's a powerful tool!

What is Data?

  • A collection of facts, figures, observations, or information.
  • Raw material for statistical analysis.
  • Can be numbers (like marks) or descriptions (like colours).

🎙️ Data is simply information! Imagine a list of your classmates' favourite colours or their heights. Each piece of information is a 'data point'.

Primary Data

  • Data collected by the investigator themselves for a specific purpose.
  • First-hand information.
  • More reliable as you collect it directly.

🎙️ Primary data is like going out and gathering information yourself. If you want to know the favourite sport of your classmates, you'd ask them directly. That's primary data.

  • How often something appears.
  • Helps us see patterns and popular choices.

🎙️ Frequency simply means 'how many times'. If you count how many students like apples, that count is the frequency of 'apples' as a favourite fruit.

Grouped Frequency Distribution

  • Used when the range of data is very large.
  • Data is divided into groups or 'class intervals'.
  • Each group has its own frequency.

🎙️ What if we have marks of 100 students, ranging from 0 to 100? Making a frequency table for each individual mark would be too long! So, we group them, like 0-10, 10-20, etc.

📦
Data in Groups
16 / 25

Class Interval

  • A range of values into which the data is grouped.
  • Example: 0-10, 10-20, 20-30.
  • Each interval should be of equal width.

🎙️ A class interval is simply a group. For example, '0-10' means all marks from 0 up to (but not including) 10. The 'width' is the size of this group, here it's 10.

↔️
Range of Values
17 / 25

Lower & Upper Class Limits

  • Lower limit: The smallest value in a class interval.
  • Upper limit: The largest value in a class interval.
  • For 10-20, 10 is the lower limit, 20 is the upper limit.

🎙️ Every class interval has a beginning and an end. The lower limit is where it starts, and the upper limit is where it ends. For '10-20', 10 is the floor and 20 is the ceiling of that group.

18 / 25

Class Mark (Mid-point)

  • The middle value of a class interval.
  • Calculated as: (Lower Limit + Upper Limit) / 2.
  • Used for calculations later on.

🎙️ The class mark is like finding the average value for a group. If your group is 10-20, the midpoint is (10+20)/2 = 15. It represents the entire group.

Bar Graphs

  • Used for categorical data or discrete frequency distributions.
  • Rectangular bars of equal width.
  • Height of the bar represents the frequency.
  • Gaps between bars.

🎙️ Bar graphs are great for comparing different categories. Each bar stands for a category, and its height tells you how much of it there is. Notice the space between the bars.

Histograms

  • Used for grouped frequency distributions (continuous data).
  • Bars are adjacent to each other (no gaps).
  • Width of the bar represents the class interval.
  • Area of the bar is proportional to frequency.

🎙️ Histograms look a bit like bar graphs, but they are used for continuous data, like heights or weights. The main difference is that the bars touch each other, showing that the data flows continuously.
