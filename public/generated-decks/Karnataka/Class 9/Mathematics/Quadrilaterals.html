What is a Quadrilateral?

  • A polygon with four sides.
  • It has four vertices and four angles.
  • The word 'quad' means four, and 'lateral' means side.
  • A closed figure formed by four line segments.

🎙️ A quadrilateral is simply any closed shape that has exactly four straight sides. Think of it as a basic building block for many structures. It always has four corners, which we call vertices, and four angles inside.

Elements of a Quadrilateral

  • Four Vertices (A, B, C, D)
  • Four Sides (AB, BC, CD, DA)
  • Four Angles (∠A, ∠B, ∠C, ∠D)
  • Two Diagonals (AC, BD)

🎙️ Just like any polygon, a quadrilateral has specific parts. The points where the sides meet are called vertices. The lines connecting opposite vertices are called diagonals. Understanding these elements is key to describing and analyzing quadrilaterals.

Angle Sum Property

  • The sum of all interior angles of a quadrilateral is 360°.
  • For any quadrilateral ABCD: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360°.
  • This property is true for ALL quadrilaterals.

🎙️ This is a very important property! No matter what kind of quadrilateral you have, if you add up all its interior angles, the total will always be 360 degrees. This is a fundamental rule we'll use often.

Worked Example 1: Finding an Angle

  • Given a quadrilateral with three angles: 80°, 100°, 120°.
  • Find the measure of the fourth angle.
  • Use the angle sum property: Sum = 360°.

🎙️ Let's apply the angle sum property with an example. If we know three angles of a quadrilateral, we can easily find the fourth. We just sum the known angles and subtract that total from 360 degrees to get the missing angle.

1Add known angles: 80+100+120 = 300°
2Subtract from 360°: 360 - 300 = 60°
3The fourth angle is 60°

Parallelogram

  • A quadrilateral with opposite sides parallel.
  • Properties: Opposite sides are equal.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other.

🎙️ A parallelogram is a very common type of quadrilateral where both pairs of opposite sides are parallel to each other. This parallelism gives rise to several important properties, such as equal opposite sides and angles, and diagonals that cut each other in half.

Rectangle

  • A parallelogram with all four angles equal to 90°.
  • Properties: All properties of a parallelogram.
  • All angles are right angles.
  • Diagonals are equal in length.

🎙️ A rectangle is a special kind of parallelogram. What makes it special is that all its interior angles are right angles, or 90 degrees. This additional property means its diagonals are not only bisecting each other but are also equal in length.

Square

  • A rectangle with all four sides equal.
  • Properties: All properties of a rectangle AND a rhombus.
  • All sides are equal.
  • Diagonals are equal and bisect each other at 90°.

🎙️ A square is the most 'perfect' quadrilateral in many ways. It combines the best features of a rectangle and a rhombus: all sides are equal, and all angles are 90 degrees. Its diagonals are also equal and intersect at right angles.

Rhombus

  • A parallelogram with all four sides equal.
  • Properties: All properties of a parallelogram.
  • All sides are equal.
  • Diagonals bisect each other at 90°.

🎙️ A rhombus is another special parallelogram, but instead of all angles being 90 degrees, all its sides are equal in length. Its diagonals are particularly interesting; they not only bisect each other but also cross at a perfect right angle.

Trapezium (Trapezoid)

  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  • The parallel sides are called bases.
  • The non-parallel sides are called legs.

🎙️ A trapezium is a bit different from the parallelograms we've seen. It only requires one pair of its opposite sides to be parallel. These parallel sides are known as the bases, and the other two non-parallel sides are called the legs.

Kite

  • A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of equal adjacent sides.
  • Properties: One pair of opposite angles are equal.
  • Diagonals are perpendicular to each other.
  • One diagonal bisects the other diagonal.

🎙️ A kite is named after the flying toy! Its defining feature is that it has two pairs of equal-length sides, but these equal sides are *adjacent* to each other, not opposite. Its diagonals always cross at a right angle.

Worked Example 2: Parallelogram Diagonals

  • In a parallelogram ABCD, diagonals AC and BD intersect at O.
  • If AO = 5 cm, find OC.
  • Recall the property of parallelogram diagonals.

🎙️ Let's apply a property of parallelograms. Since the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, the point where they intersect divides each diagonal into two equal parts. This makes solving for unknown lengths quite straightforward.

1Property: Diagonals bisect each other.
2This means O is the midpoint of AC.
3Therefore, AO = OC.
4So, OC = 5 cm.

Mid-point Theorem for Triangles (Revisit)

  • The line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.
  • And is half of the third side.
  • This theorem is crucial for proving quadrilateral properties.

🎙️ While this is a theorem about triangles, it's incredibly important when we work with quadrilaterals, especially when dealing with midpoints of sides. It allows us to prove relationships of parallelism and length within quadrilaterals by dividing them into triangles.

Recap: Key Takeaways

  • Quadrilaterals are 4-sided polygons with an angle sum of 360°.
  • Types include parallelograms, rectangles, squares, rhombuses, trapeziums, and kites.
